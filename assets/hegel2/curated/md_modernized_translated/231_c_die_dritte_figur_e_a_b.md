## c. The Third Figure: S-U-P

1. Of immediate premises this third syllogism retains not a single one; the reference S–U came to be mediated through the first syllogism, the reference P–U through the second. It has the two earlier syllogisms, therefore, for its presupposition; but {{{ 12.103 }}} they in turn presuppose it, as indeed each of the three presupposes the remaining two. With it, accordingly, the determining of the syllogism reaches completion on the whole. – What this mutual mediating contains is just this, that each syllogism, though for itself it be the mediation, is at the same time not in its own self the totality of that mediation, but carries about it an immediacy whose mediating is to be found outside it.

Taken in its own self, the syllogism S–U–P is the truth of the formal syllogism; what it enunciates is that the mediation belonging to the latter is the abstractly universal one, and that of the extremes their essential determinateness [is] not held in the middle term, which holds them only as regards their universality, so that just what ought to have been mediated is precisely what fails to be conjoined there. Posited here, accordingly, is that wherein the formalism of the syllogism lies: its terms carry an immediate content that stands indifferent to the form, or, which says the same, they are form determinations that have not yet reflected themselves into determinations of content.

2. True, the middle term of this syllogism is the unity of the extremes, but a unity that has abstracted from their determinateness, the _indeterminate_ universal. Yet so far as this universal, being the abstract one, stands at the same time distinguished from the extremes as the _determinate_, it too remains a _determinate_ as against them, and the whole is a syllogism whose bearing upon its own concept calls for examination. Being the universal, the middle term is towards _both_ of its extremes the subsuming one, or predicate, and on no occasion the subsumed one, or subject. If, then, this syllogism as _one_ _species_ of syllogism is to answer to what a syllogism is, that can come about only thus: while the one reference S–U already stands in the fitting relation, the other, P–U, must come to stand in it as well. Such is the case with a judgment where subject and predicate are related indifferently, with the _negative_ judgment. So the syllogism turns out legitimate; its conclusion, however, of necessity negative.

Herewith it also makes no difference which of the two determinations of this proposition should count as predicate and which as subject, and, within the syllogism, whether as the extreme of singularity or as that of particularity, hence whether as minor or as major term. Since on the received supposition it is by this that the major or the minor premise gets decided, the point has here lost all weight. – Herein lies the ground of that customary _fourth_ _figure_ of the syllogism, unknown to Aristotle, which moreover has to do with an utterly empty distinction void of all interest. The unmediated placing of the terms in it inverts the placing of the first figure; for since, on the formal view of the judgment, subject and predicate of a negative conclusion do not stand in the determinate relation of subject and predicate but {{{ 12.104 }}} either may occupy the position of the other, nothing turns on which of the terms is made subject and which predicate, and just as little on which premise is made major or minor. – Such indifference, helped along by the determination of particularity as well (above all where the remark is made that it may be taken in the comprehensive sense), reduces that fourth figure to something wholly idle.

3. What the syllogism having the universal for its middle term means objectively is that the mediating factor, being the unity of the extremes, is _essentially_ _universal_. Since, however, universality is at the outset only the qualitative or abstract sort, it does not harbour the determinateness of the extremes within it; should a conjoining of these come about, its ground must equally lie in a mediation situated outside this syllogism, and so far as this syllogism goes it is every bit as contingent as with the earlier forms of syllogism. Now since it is the universal that gets determined as middle term, and the determinateness of the extremes finds no place there, that determinateness stands posited as something altogether indifferent and external. – Thus by way of this bare abstraction a _fourth_ _figure_ of the syllogism does indeed come about, that of the _relationless_ syllogism, U–U–U: it abstracts from whatever qualitative difference the terms have, and so takes for its determination their merely external unity, namely their _equality_.
