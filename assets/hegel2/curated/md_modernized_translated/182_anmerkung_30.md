## Remark

Essence in its movement is, in general terms, the _becoming_ _into_ _the_ _concept_. What comes to the fore in the relation of inner and outer is the essential moment of that becoming, namely that its determinations are posited so as to stand in the negative unity in such fashion that each immediately is not only as its own other but also as the whole in its totality. This totality, though, is in the concept as such the _universal_; – a foundation which the relation of inner and outer does not yet have at hand. – Absent from the negative identity of inner and outer, which is the _immediate_ _inversion_ of one of these determinations into the other, {{{ 11.367 }}} is likewise the foundation that was called the _fact_ a moment ago. –

The unmediated _identity_ _of_ _form_, as it is posited here while still without the content-laden movement of the fact itself, is very much worth noting. It turns up in the fact as the fact stands at its _beginning_. So _pure_ _being_ is immediately the _nothing_. Everything real whatever is, at its beginning, an identity merely immediate in this way; for at its beginning it has not yet set its moments in opposition and developed them, having on the one side not yet _recollected_ itself out of externality, and on the other not yet _externalized_ itself out of inwardness and brought itself forth by its own activity; hence it is only the inner as _determinateness_ over against the outer, and only the outer as _determinateness_ over against the inner. So it is, in part, _only_ an immediate being; and in part, so far as it is quite as much the negativity which is to become the activity of development, it is as such essentially, to begin with, _only_ an inner. – Throughout all development whatever, natural, scientific and spiritual, this shows itself, and it is essential to recognize that a first, where a something is as yet only _inwardly_ or in its _concept_, is on that very account nothing but the immediate, passive existence of that something. So – to take at once the nearest example – the _essential_ _relation_ under consideration here, before it has moved through mediation, the relation of _force_, and thereby realized itself, is the relation only _in_ _itself_, its concept, or as yet only _inwardly_. But precisely on that account it is _only_ the _external_, immediate relation; that of the _whole_ with its _parts_, where the sides possess a subsistence indifferent towards each other. Their identity is not yet in their own selves; it is as yet only _inward_, and on that account they fall apart, their subsistence being an immediate and external one. – Thus the _sphere_ _of_ _being_ in general is as yet nothing but what is still, without qualification, _inner_, and on that account it is the sphere of the immediacy that is, of externality. – _Essence_ too is as yet only the _inner_; that is why it also gets taken for a wholly _external_ communality lacking all system; one says _Schulwesen_, _Zeitungswesen_, meaning by these a something held in common that is made by taking existing objects together from outside, so far as they are without any essential connection, without organization. – Or, with concrete objects: the germ of the plant, the child, is as yet only an _inner_ plant, an _inward_ human being. But just for that reason the plant, or the human being, as germ is something immediate, an outer, one that has not yet given itself the negative reference to its own self, _a_ _passive_ thing _surrendered_ to otherness. – So too _God_ in his _immediate_ concept is not spirit; for spirit is not the immediate, the counterpart set against mediation, but is rather the essence that eternally posits its immediacy and eternally comes back out of it into itself. _Immediately_ taken, then, God is _only_ nature. Or nature is _only_ {{{ 11.368 }}} God as inner, God not actual as spirit and thus not the genuine God. – Or God, in thinking taken as _first_ thinking, is only pure being, or else essence, the abstract absolute; not, however, God as absolute spirit, which alone is the genuine nature of God.

3. Of the identities of inner and outer under consideration, the _first_ is the foundation standing indifferent towards the difference of these determinations, as towards a form external to it, or it as _content_. The _second_ identity is the difference of the two held in unmediated identity, the immediate inversion of each into the one opposed to it; – or it as pure _form_. These two identities, however, are only _the_ _sides_ _of_ _one_ _totality_; or that totality is itself only the inversion of the one into the other. Totality as foundation and content is this immediacy reflected into itself solely by way of the presupposing reflection of form, which sublates the difference that form has and posits itself over against that difference as indifferent identity, as reflected unity. Or content is form itself, so far as form determines itself as diversity and makes itself into one of its own sides, as externality, but into the other as immediacy reflected into itself, or into the inner.

Conversely, then, inner and outer, the differences of form, are each of them posited in its own self as the totality of itself and of its other; the _inner_, being simple identity reflected into itself, is the immediate and hence being and externality quite as much as essence; and the _outer_, being the manifold, determinate being, is merely outer, that is, posited as unessential and as gone back into its ground, and thus as inner. This crossing over of the two into one another is their immediate identity, as foundation; but it is also their mediated identity; each, namely, is by way of its other precisely what it is in itself, the relation in its totality. Or the other way about: because either side has totality in it, its determinateness is mediated with the other determinateness; totality thus mediates itself with its own self through form, or determinateness, and determinateness mediates itself with itself through its simple identity.

Whatever a something is, it is that wholly in its externality; externality is its totality, and quite as much its unity reflected into itself. Its appearance is reflection not merely into an other but into itself, and its externality is on that account the expression of what it is in itself; and since in this way its content and its form are identical without qualification, it is nothing in and for itself _but_ _this_, _to_ _express_ _itself_. It is its essence's own revealing, so that this essence consists precisely and only in being what reveals itself.

With appearance thus identical with the inner, or with essence, the essential relation has determined itself into _actuality_.
