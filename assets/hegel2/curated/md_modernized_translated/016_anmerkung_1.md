## Remark 1

The customary counterpart set against _something_ is _nothing_; yet something is by now already a determinate being, one marked off from other somethings; hence the nothing standing opposed to something – the nothing belonging to some something or other – is equally a nothing that is determinate. In the present context, though, what we must take is the nothing in the simplicity of its indeterminateness. – Were one to hold it more correct that, instead of nothing, _non-being_ be opposed to being, there would be nothing to object to in view of the result, for in _non-being_ the reference to _being_ is contained; both, being and the negation of it, are enunciated in _One_, the nothing as it is in becoming. But at first it is not a matter of the form of opposition, that is, at the same time of _reference_, but of the abstract, immediate negation, nothing purely for itself, the relationless denial, – which one could also express, if one likes, by the mere: _not_.

The simple thought _of_ _pure_ _being_ was enunciated first by the _Eleatics_, above all by _Parmenides_, as the absolute and as the sole truth, and in the fragments that survive from him with the pure enthusiasm of a thinking that grasps itself for the first time in its absolute abstraction: _only_ _being_ _is_, _and_ _nothing_ _is_ _not_ _at_ _all_. – In oriental systems, essentially in Buddhism, the _nothing_, the void, is as is well known the absolute principle. – The profound _Heraclitus_ brought forward against that simple and one-sided abstraction the higher total concept of becoming, and said: _being_ _is_ _as_ _little_ _as_ _nothing_ _is_, or again everything _flows_, that is, everything is _becoming_. – The popular sayings, especially the oriental ones, that everything which is has the germ of its ceasing-to-be in its very birth, and that death conversely is the entry into new life, express at bottom the same uniting of being and nothing. Such formulations, however, rest upon a substratum where the passing over occurs; time keeps being and nothing asunder, since they are represented as alternating within it, and they are not thought in their abstraction, nor consequently in a way that lets them be in and for themselves the same.

Ex nihilo nihil fit – this ranks among the propositions that metaphysics invested with weighty {{{ 21.71 }}} significance. In it there is either only the vacuous tautology to be seen: nothing is nothing; or, if _becoming_ were to have an actual significance in it, then rather, since only _nothing_ _becomes_ out of _nothing_, no _becoming_ is in fact present in it, for nothing there remains nothing. Becoming contains this, that nothing does not remain nothing but passes over into its other, into being. – Later metaphysics, above all the Christian sort, threw out the proposition that nothing yields nothing, and in doing so claimed a transition from nothing over into being; no matter how synthetically, or how merely representationally, that proposition was taken there, even the most imperfect of unions still harbours a point at which being and nothing meet and cease to be distinguished. – Whatever genuine weight the proposition _out_ _of_ _nothing_, _nothing_ _comes_; _nothing_ _is_ _precisely_ _nothing_ carries, it carries by standing opposed to _becoming_ as such, and with that against the world's creation out of nothing. Whoever maintains, and even waxes passionate in maintaining, that nothing is precisely nothing, has no awareness of thereby endorsing the abstract _pantheism_ of the Eleatics, and in substance the Spinozist variety too. A philosophical outlook that takes as its principle: being is only being, nothing is only nothing, has earned the title system of identity; and pantheism has its essence in just this abstract identity.

Should this outcome – that being and nothing are one and the same – strike a reader as startling on its own account, or look paradoxical, there is no need to dwell on the matter; the astonishment itself is what deserves astonishment, an astonishment that presents itself as something so novel in philosophy and forgets that determinations occurring in this science are of a wholly different order from those of ordinary consciousness and of the so-called common human understanding, which is not exactly the sound understanding but rather the understanding trained up into abstractions and into belief – superstition, rather – in abstractions. Exhibiting the unity of being and nothing would be no hard task, in any example one likes, in _any_ actual thing whatever or any thought. Of _being_ and _nothing_ we have to repeat what was stated above regarding immediacy and mediation (mediation being what contains a reference _to_ _one_ _another_ and therewith _negation_), namely _that_ _neither_ _in_ _heaven_ _nor_ _on_ _earth_ _is_ _there_ _anything_ _whatever_ _which_ _does_ _not_ _contain_ _within_ _itself_ _both_ _being_ _and_ _nothing_. Granted, because what is spoken of in such cases is _some_ _something_ _or_ _other_ and something _actual_, the determinations no longer stand there in that consummate untruth which belongs to them as being and nothing, but stand rather under a further determination, taken {{{ 21.72 }}} say as _positive_ and _negative_ – the one a being that has been posited and reflected, the other a nothing posited and reflected; yet within positive and negative, the abstract groundwork of the one is being and of the other nothing. – Thus in God himself the quality, _activity_, _creation_, _power_, and so forth, essentially contains the determination of the negative, – they are a bringing forth of an _other_. But an empirical elucidation of that assertion by means of examples would here be altogether superfluous. Now that this unity of being and nothing underlies everything once and for all as the first truth and constitutes the element of all that comes after, becoming itself aside, every further determination of logic – existence, quality, indeed every concept of philosophy – furnishes an example of it. – But the self-styled common or sound human understanding may be referred, insofar as it rejects the unseparatedness of being and nothing, to the attempt to hunt out an example in which the one is to be found separated from the other (something from limit, restriction, or the infinite, God, as was just mentioned, from activity). Only the empty thought-things, being and nothing themselves, are these separated ones, and it is they that are preferred by that understanding to the truth, the unseparatedness of the two, which is everywhere before us.

One cannot wish to have the intention of meeting on all sides the confusions into which ordinary consciousness puts itself in the face of such a logical proposition, for they are inexhaustible. Only a few can be mentioned. Among the sources of such confusion is this, that consciousness approaches an abstract logical proposition of this kind carrying representations of some concrete something, and loses sight of the fact that nothing of that sort is under discussion, that what is under discussion is being and nothing in their pure abstraction alone, and that these are what must be kept firmly in view.

Being and non-being are one and the same; _hence_ it makes no difference whether I am or fail to be, whether this house stands or does not, whether these hundred thalers figure in my financial estate or are absent from it. – This inference, or application of that proposition, completely alters its sense. The proposition contains the pure abstractions of being and nothing; the application, however, makes of them a determinate being and a determinate nothing. But of determinate being, as has been said, there is no talk here. A determinate, a finite being is one that refers itself to an other; it is a content standing in a relation of necessity with other content, with the whole world. Having regard {{{ 21.73 }}} to the way the whole hangs together in reciprocal determination, metaphysics was able to advance its – fundamentally tautological – claim that the destruction of a single mote of dust would bring the entire universe down with it. Among the instances marshalled against the proposition at issue, a something turns up for which being or not being is no matter of indifference – yet what decides the case is never the being or the non-being as such, but rather its _content_, whereby it hangs together with other things. If a determinate content, some determinate existence, is _presupposed_, then this existence, because it is _determinate_, stands in manifold reference to other content; it is not indifferent to it whether a certain other content with which it stands in reference is or is not; for only through such reference is it essentially what it is. The same is the case in _representation_ (when we take non-being in the more determinate sense of representation as against actuality), in whose interconnection the being or the absence of a content that is represented as standing determinately in reference to other content is not indifferent. –

This consideration contains the same as what makes up a main moment in the Kantian critique of the ontological proof of the existence of God, a critique to which, however, regard is here given only with respect to the distinction occurring in it between being and nothing in general and _determinate_ being or non-being. – As is well known, in that so-called proof the concept of a being was presupposed to which all realities accrue, hence also concrete existence, which was likewise assumed to be one of the realities. What the Kantian critique fastened on above all was that _concrete existence_, or being (the two counting here as equivalent in meaning), is neither a _property_ nor a _real_ _predicate_ – meaning: it is no concept of anything capable of accruing to the _concept_ of a thing.[^1] – Kant means thereby to say that being is no determination of content. – Hence, he continues, the possible contains no more than the actual; a hundred actual thalers contain not the least bit more than a hundred possible ones; – namely, the former have no other determination of content than the latter. {{{ 21.74 }}} For this content considered as isolated it is in fact indifferent to be or not to be; there lies in it no difference of being or non-being, this difference does not touch it at all; the hundred thalers do not become fewer if they are not, nor more if they are. A difference must first come from somewhere else. – »On the other hand, Kant recalls, in my financial estate there is more with a hundred actual thalers than with the mere concept of them, or with their possibility. For where actuality is concerned the _object_ is not simply held analytically within my concept; rather it _accrues_ _synthetically_ to _my_ _concept_, (a concept that is one _determination_ of my _state_,) and yet this being outside my concept adds not the slightest to the hundred thalers as thought.«

[^1]: (Kant's Critique of Pure Reason, 2nd ed., p. 628 ff.)

Two sorts of states are here _presupposed_, to keep to the Kantian expressions, which are not without a confused ponderousness: the one, which Kant calls the concept, by which representation is to be understood, and another, the financial estate. For the one as for the other, the estate as well as the representing, a hundred thalers are a determination of content, or »they are added to such a determination, as Kant expresses it, _synthetically_;« I as _possessor_ of a hundred thalers, or as non-possessor of them, or again I as _representing_ a hundred thalers to myself, or not representing them, is indeed a different content. Put in more general terms: once being and nothing acquire a determinate content, the two abstractions stop being abstractions at all; being now amounts to reality, the determinate being of 100 thalers, and nothing to negation, the determinate non-being of those same thalers. This determinateness of content itself, the hundred thalers, taken also abstractly for itself, is unchanged the same in the one as in the other. But inasmuch as being is further taken as financial estate, the hundred thalers enter into reference to a state, and for this state such a determinateness as they are is not indifferent; their being or non-being is only _alteration_; they are transposed into the sphere of _existence_. So when someone presses against the unity of being and nothing the objection that it surely does matter whether this or that (the 100 thalers) is or is not, the deception lies in our pushing the difference off onto bare being and non-being, {{{ 21.75 }}} onto my _having_ or _not_ _having_ the hundred thalers – a deception resting, as was shown, upon the one-sided abstraction which drops out the _determinate_ _existence_ that these examples in fact contain and clings to being and non-being by themselves; conversely the same abstraction turns the abstract being and nothing that ought to be grasped into a determinate being and nothing, into an existence. Only _existence_ contains the real difference of being and nothing, namely a _something_ and an _other_. – It is this real difference that hovers before representation, instead of abstract being and pure nothing and their merely intended difference.

As Kant expresses it, »through concrete existence something comes into the context of the whole of experience,« »we thereby get one object of _perception_ more, but our _concept_ of the object is not thereby augmented.« – This means, as follows from what has been elucidated, this much: through concrete existence, essentially because something is determinate concrete existence, it stands in interconnection with an _other_, and among others also with something perceiving. – »The concept of the hundred thalers, says Kant, is not augmented by perceiving.« The _concept_ here means the hundred thalers noted before as represented in _isolation_. Isolated in that fashion they do count as an empirical content, yet a severed one, lacking interconnection and lacking determinateness as against an _other_; the form of self-identity strips from them their reference to an other and renders it a matter of indifference that they should be perceived or go unperceived. Yet what gets called the _concept_ of the hundred thalers is a spurious concept; the form of simple self-reference does not of itself belong to a content so bounded and finite; that form is imposed on it, loaned to it, by the subjective understanding; a hundred thalers are no self-relating item but something alterable and transient.

The thinking or representing before which only a determinate being, existence, {{{ 21.76 }}} hovers, is to be referred back to the mentioned beginning of science which Parmenides made, who purified and raised his representing, and with it the representing of later times, into _pure_ _thought_, into being as such, and thereby created the element of science. – What is the _first_ in _science_ has had to show itself _historically_ as the _first_. And the Eleatic _one_ or _being_ we are to regard as the first of the knowledge of thought; _water_ and material principles of that sort are indeed _supposed_ to be the universal, but as matters they are not pure thoughts; _numbers_ are neither the first simple thought nor the thought that remains with itself, but the thought wholly external to itself.

The referral of _particular_ _finite_ being back to being as such, in the complete abstractness of its universality, must count as the very first demand, theoretical and indeed practical alike. Suppose a great to-do is made over the hundred thalers, on the grounds that my financial estate is affected by my _having_ them or _not_, and all the more by whether I exist or fail to, whether an other exists or fails to; then – leaving aside that there will be financial estates to which possessing a hundred thalers makes no odds – one may be reminded that a human being is meant to lift his disposition up into this abstract universality, one where it genuinely makes no odds to him whether or not the hundred thalers are, whatever quantitative ratio they may stand in to his financial estate, and equally none whether he himself is or is not, that is, is or is not amid finite life (a state, a determinate being, being what is meant here), and so on – even si fractus illabatur orbis, impavidum ferient ruinae, as a Roman put it, and the Christian is supposed to dwell in such indifference still more fully.

There is still to be noted the immediate connection in which the elevation above the hundred thalers and above finite things in general stands with the ontological proof and with the cited Kantian critique of it. This critique has made itself universally plausible through its popular example; who does not know that a hundred actual thalers are different from a hundred merely possible thalers? that they make a difference in my financial estate? Because the hundred thalers display this diversity, concept – that is, determinateness of content taken as bare possibility – stands distinct from being; _hence_ God's concept likewise stands distinct from his being, and as little as I can extract the actuality of the hundred thalers out of their possibility, just as little can I »pick out« his concrete existence from the concept of God; and it is in this picking out {{{ 21.77 }}} of God's concrete existence from his concept that the ontological proof is supposed to consist. Granted the correctness of it, that concept differs from being, God differs still more widely from the hundred thalers and from the rest of finite things. What constitutes the _definition_ _of_ _finite_ _things_ is precisely that concept and being diverge in them, that concept and reality, soul and body, admit of being parted, and that finite things are on this account transient and mortal; God's abstract definition, by contrast, consists in just this, that with him concept and being stand _unseparated_ and _inseparable_. A genuine critique of the categories and of reason amounts to just this: bringing cognition to an understanding of that difference, and restraining it from carrying over onto God the determinations and relations belonging to the finite.
