## C. The Syllogism of Necessity

By now what mediates has determined itself 1) into _simple_ determinate universality, such as particularity is within the syllogism of existence; but 2) into _objective_ universality, meaning one that holds the entire determinateness of the distinguished extremes, such as allness is within the syllogism of reflection; a universality _filled_ and yet _simple_; – the _universal_ _nature_ belonging to the fact, the genus.

This syllogism is _full_ _of_ _content_, since the _abstract_ middle belonging to the syllogism of existence has set itself out into _determinate_ _difference_, as that middle is when it serves the syllogism of reflection, only for this difference to reflect itself back into simple identity. – Hence it is a syllogism of _necessity_, its middle being no further immediate content but rather the reflection into self of the {{{ 12.119 }}} determinateness belonging to the extremes. In the middle the extremes possess their inner identity, and its content-determinations are the extremes' determinations of form. – Whatever it is that tells the termini apart therefore counts as _external_, _unessential_ form, while they themselves count as _moments_ _of_ _one_ _necessary_ existence.

At first this syllogism is the immediate one, and formal in so far as the _connection_ _of_ _the_ _termini_ _is_ _the_ _essential_ _nature_, taken as _content_, which content is present at the distinguished termini in _differing_ _form_ merely, while the extremes on their own account are no more than an _unessential_ subsistence. – To realize this syllogism is to determine it so that the _extremes_ likewise get _posited_ as that _totality_ which at first the middle alone is, and so that the _necessity_ of the reference, at first nothing but substantial _content_, becomes a reference of _posited_ _form_.
