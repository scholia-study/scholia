## a. Something and an Other

1. Something and an other are both, _firstly_, existents or _somethings_.

_Secondly_, each is just as much an _other_. Which of the two gets named first and is called _something_ merely on that account makes no difference; (in Latin, when both occur in one sentence, each is called aliud, or the one the other, alius alium; where reciprocity obtains the analogous expression runs: alter alterum.) Should we call one existence A and the other B, then B counts at first as determined to be the other. But A is quite as much the other of B. In like manner both are _others_. For pinning down the difference, and the something to be taken affirmatively, the word _this_ serves. Yet _this_ voices precisely the point that such distinguishing and singling out of the one something is a subjective designating, one falling outside the something itself. Into this external act of pointing the whole determinateness falls; not even the expression _this_ harbours a difference; each and every something is a _this_ quite as much as it is also an other. One _supposes_ that with _this_ one utters something perfectly determinate; overlooked is the fact that language, being a work of the understanding, utters only what is universal, except in the _name_ of a singular object; the individual name, however, is a senseless thing in the sense that it voices nothing universal, and it looks like a merely posited, arbitrary affair for the same reason that individual names can be arbitrarily adopted, conferred, or likewise changed.

Otherness accordingly looks like a determination alien to the existence so determined, or like the other _outside_ the one existence; partly because an existence gets determined as other only through a third party's _comparing_, partly because it gets so determined only on account of the other lying outside it, and is not such for itself. Every existence, as was noted, simultaneously determines itself, for representation as well, quite as much as an other existence, so that no existence is left over that would be determined only as an existence, that would not lie outside an existence and hence not be itself an other.

{{{ 21.106 }}} Determined as _something_ no less than as _other_, the two are hence _the_ _same_, and no difference between them is yet on hand. This _selfsameness_ of the determinations falls, however, likewise only within external reflection, within a _comparing_ of the two; but on the way the _other_ is at first posited, it stands for itself indeed in relation to the something, yet also _for_ _itself_ _outside_ _of_ _it_.

_Thirdly_, then, the _other_ must be taken in isolation, in relation to itself; _abstractly_ as the other, Plato's τὸ ἕτερον, which he ranges among the moments of totality and sets over against _the_ _one_, thereby granting _the_ _other_ a _nature_ of its own. So grasped, the _other_ stands alone as such: not some thing's other, but the other in its very self, i.e. its own other. – An other of that kind, other by its very determination, is _physical_ _nature_; nature is the _other_ _of_ _spirit_; and so this determination of nature is initially sheer relativity, voicing no quality of nature itself but merely a relation external to nature. Since spirit, however, is the true something, and nature therefore in its very self only what it is over against spirit, nature's quality — taken for itself — consists precisely in being the other in its very self, the _being-outside-of-itself_ (in the determinations of space, of time, of matter).

The other for itself is the other in its very self, therefore its own other, and so an other of the other – hence what is downright unequal within itself, self-negating, what _alters_. Just as much, though, it stays identical with itself, since whatever it altered into is the _other_, which carries no further determination besides; and what alters is determined in no other manner, but in the very same, namely to be an other, whence in that other it _converges_ _only_ _with_ _itself_. Posited accordingly as inwardly reflected, with otherness sublated, it is a something _identical_ with itself, from which otherness — simultaneously a moment of it — is something distinct, something not accruing to it as the something itself.

2. Something _maintains_ itself within its non-existence; with that non-existence it is essentially _one_, and with it essentially _not_ _one_. So it stands in _relation_ to its otherness; purely its otherness it is not. Otherness is contained within it and withal still _severed_ from it; it is _being-for-other_.

Existence taken as such is immediate, relationless; or it stands under the determination of _being_. But existence, enclosing non-being within itself, is _determinate_ being, being inwardly negated, and thus at first an other – yet since throughout its negating it likewise maintains itself, it is merely _being-for-other_.

{{{ 21.107 }}} Within its non-existence it maintains itself, and is being; not being at large, however, but being as relation to self _over_ _against_ its relation to other, as self-equality over against its inequality. Being of that sort is _being-in-itself_.

The _two_ _moments_ of the something are made up by being-for-other and being-in-itself. _Two_ _pairs_ of determinations turn up here: 1) _something_ and an _other_. 2) _being-for-other_, _and_ _being-in-itself_. The first pair carries the relationlessness of its determinateness; something and other fall asunder. Their truth, though, lies in their relation; being-for-other along with being-in-itself are accordingly those same determinations, only posited as _moments_ of one selfsame thing, as determinations that are relations and that abide within their unity, the unity of existence. Each of them thereby contains upon itself, withal, its own moment that differs from it.

Within their unity, which is existence, being and nothing are being and nothing no longer; – only apart from that unity are they such; and so within their restless unity, in becoming, they amount to coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be. – Being within the something is _being-in-itself_. Being, the relation to self, self-equality, is now no longer immediate, but relation to self solely as the non-being of otherness, (as inwardly reflected existence). – Non-being likewise, as a moment of the something within this unity of being and non-being, is not non-existence at large but an other, and, more determinately, given the _distinguishing_ of being from it withal, a _relation_ to its non-existence, being-for-other.

_Being-in-itself_, accordingly, is firstly a negative relation to non-existence: otherness lies outside it and it stands opposed thereto; so far as something is _in_ _itself_, otherness and being-for-other have been taken away from it. Secondly, though, it bears non-being upon itself too; for it itself _is_ _the_ _non-being_ of being-for-other.

{{{ 21.108 }}} _Being-for-other_, however, is firstly the negation of that simple relation of being to itself which is supposed at first to be existence and something; so far as something lies within an other, or for an other, it goes without a being of its own. Secondly, though, it is not non-existence as pure nothing; it is a non-existence referring to being-in-itself as to its inwardly reflected being, exactly as, in reverse, being-in-itself refers to being-for-other.

3. The two moments are determinations of one selfsame thing, the something, namely. Something is _in_ _itself_ so far as it has come back into itself out of being-for-other. Yet something also carries a determination or circumstance _in_ _itself_ (the accent here falling on _in_) or _in_ _it_, so far as this circumstance stands externally _in_ _it_, a being-for-other.

A further determination follows from this. At first _being-in-itself_ and being-for-other stand diverse; but that something carries _in_ _it_ the very _same_ _that_ _it_ _is_ _in_ itself, and inversely that whatever it is as being-for-other it likewise is in itself – herein lies the identity of being-in-itself with being-for-other, on the determination that the something is itself the selfsame in both moments, which are therefore undivided within it. – Formally this identity turns up already within the sphere of existence, more expressly where essence comes under consideration and then the relation of _inwardness_ to _outwardness_, and most determinately of all where the idea comes under consideration, as unity of concept and actuality. – With the _in-itself_, as with the _inner_, one supposes oneself to be uttering something lofty; but whatever something is _merely_ _in_ _itself_ is equally _merely_ _in_ _it_; the in-itself is an abstract determination only, and therefore itself an external one. The phrases: there is nothing _in_ _him_, or there is something _in_ _it_, carry with them, darkly perhaps, the thought that whatever is _in_ _a_ _thing_ belongs likewise to its _being-in-itself_, to its inner and genuine worth.

One may remark that the sense of the _thing-in-itself_ emerges here — a very simple abstraction, though for a while a very important determination, a sort of noble possession, just as the dictum that what things are in {{{ 21.109 }}} themselves stays unknown to us once passed for prized wisdom. – Things get called in-themselves so far as one abstracts from every being-for-other, which means generally: so far as they are thought without any determination whatever, as nothings. In that sense, to be sure, _what_ the thing-in-itself is cannot be known. For the query: _what_? insists that _determinations_ be stated; but since the things about which they are to be stated are meanwhile supposed to be _things-in-themselves_, that is, precisely without determination, the impossibility of answering has been lodged thoughtlessly in the query itself, or else one produces merely a preposterous answer. – That absolute about which nothing is known save that everything within it is one: the thing-in-itself is the very same. Hence one knows perfectly well what is _in_ these things-in-themselves; as such they are nothing but truthless, empty abstractions. What the thing-in-itself is in truth, however, what is genuinely in itself — of that the logic is the exposition, where by _in-itself_ something better than abstraction is meant, namely what a thing is in its concept; and this concept is inwardly concrete, comprehensible as concept generally, and inwardly knowable as determinate and as the interconnection of its determinations.

Being-in-itself has being-for-other for its opposing moment at first; but _positedness_ also gets set over against it; that expression does harbour being-for-other too, yet it definitely holds within it the bending-back, already accomplished, of what is not in itself into what constitutes its being-in-itself, wherein it is _positive_. _Being-in-itself_ is customarily to be understood as an abstract manner of voicing the concept; _positing_ properly enters only within the sphere of essence, the sphere of objective reflection; the ground _posits_ what has its ground in it; the cause does still more, it _brings_ _forth_ an effect, an existence whose self-subsistence is _immediately_ negated and which bears upon it the sense of having its _matter_, its being, in an other. Existence, within the sphere of being, merely _issues_ _forth_ out of becoming; or, along with something, an other is posited, along with the finite the infinite — the finite, though, brings forth no infinite, does not _posit_ it. Within the sphere of being the concept's own _self-determining_ is as yet only _in_ _itself_, and so goes by the name of a transition; the reflecting determinations of being as well — something and other, say, or the finite and the infinite — count as _qualitative_, subsisting on their own, however essentially they refer to one another or exist as being-for-other; the _other_ _is_, and the finite counts, exactly like the infinite, as _immediately_ _being_ and as standing fast on its own; the sense of each looks {{{ 21.110 }}} complete apart from its other. Positive and negative, by contrast, cause and effect, however much they too get taken as existing in isolation, have withal no sense apart from one another; their shining into one another, the shining of its other within each, is on hand _upon_ _them_ _themselves_. – Across the various circles of determination, and especially in the advance of the exposition, or more closely in the concept's advance toward its exposition, it is a chief matter to keep distinguishing well what is still _in_ _itself_ from what is _posited_, how determinations stand as within the concept and how they stand once posited, i.e. as being-for-other. Such a difference belongs to dialectical development alone, and metaphysical philosophizing, which takes in the critical sort as well, does not know it; metaphysics' definitions, like its presuppositions, distinctions and inferences, mean to assert and bring forth only _what_ _is_, indeed only _what_ _is_ _in_ _itself_.

_Being-for-other_, within the unity of something with itself, is identical with its _in-itself_; being-for-other is thus _upon_ the something. Determinateness thus inwardly reflected is thereby once more _simple_ and _existent_, hence once more a quality – the _determination_.
