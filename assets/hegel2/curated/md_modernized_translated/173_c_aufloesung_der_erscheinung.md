## {{{ 11.351 }}} C. Dissolution of Appearance

The world in and for itself constitutes the _determinate_ ground of the appearing world, and constitutes it only insofar as it possesses in its own self the negative moment, and with it the totality of content-determinations and of the alterations they undergo, a totality answering to the appearing world while at the same time making up the side thoroughly opposed to it. The mutual bearing of the two worlds is accordingly such that what counts as positive in the appearing world counts as negative in the world in and for itself, while what is negative in the one is, conversely, positive in the other. In the appearing world the north pole is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ the south pole, and vice versa; electricity that is positive is _in_ _itself_ negative, and so forth. Whatever counts in appearing existence as evil, as misfortune and so forth, is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ something good and a happiness[^1].

[^1]: (Compare Phenomenology of Spirit, p. 96 ff.)

As a matter of fact _their_ _difference_ _has_ _vanished_ precisely within this opposition of the two worlds, and whatever was meant to be the world in and for itself is itself an appearing world, while conversely the appearing world is in its own self the essential one. – At first the _appearing_ _world_ stands determined as the reflection into otherness, so that whatever determinations and concrete existences belong to it have ground and subsistence in something other; but as this other is itself likewise something _reflected_ _into_ _an_ _other_, therein they refer merely to an other engaged in sublating itself, and thus _to_ _themselves_; herewith the appearing world is _in_ _its_ _own_ _self_ a self-equal law. – The world in and for itself, conversely, is at first the content identical with itself, taken out of otherness and change; but this content, being the appearing world's complete reflection into its own self, or because the diversity belonging to it is difference reflected into itself and absolute, harbours within it the negative moment and the reference to itself as to otherness; thereby it turns into content opposed to itself, content inverting itself, content void of essence. This content of the world in and for itself has furthermore therewith also received the form of _immediate_ _concrete_ _existence_. For at first that world is ground of the appearing one; but as it possesses the opposition in its own self, it is in equal measure sublated ground and immediate concrete existence.

Each of the two, the appearing world and the essential world, is herewith in its own self the totality of the reflection that is identical with itself and of reflection-into-other, or of being-in-and-for-itself as well as of appearing. Each of them is a self-subsistent {{{ 11.352 }}} whole of concrete existence; the one was meant to be reflected concrete existence only, the other immediate concrete existence only; yet each _continues_ itself within its other, and is on that score in its own self the identity of the two moments. What lies at hand, accordingly, is this totality, one repelling from itself two totalities, the one being the _reflected_ totality and the other the _immediate_ one. Both are in the first instance self-subsistent ones, but they are such only qua totalities, and they are such to this extent, that each essentially carries within it the moment belonging to the other. The differentiated self-subsistence of each, of the one determined as _immediate_ and of the one determined as _reflected_, is on that score now posited so as to be nothing but essential reference to the other, and so as to have its self-subsistence _within_ _this_ _unity_ _of_ _the_ _two_.

The beginning was made from the _law_ _of_ _appearance_; law is the identity of one diverse content with an other content, so fashioned that the one's positedness is the other's. Within law this difference is still at hand, namely that the identity of its sides is as yet an inner one only, and that these sides do not yet possess it in their own selves; herewith, for one part, that identity is not realized; law's content stands not as identical but as an indifferent, diverse content; – for another part, it is herewith determined only _in_ _itself_ so that the one's positedness is the other's; this is not yet at hand within law. By now, however, _law_ stands _realized_; its inner identity is at the same time an existent one, and conversely law's content is raised into ideality; for in its own self that content is a sublated one, reflected into itself, since each side carries its other within it and is thereby genuinely identical with that other and with its own self.

So law is _essential_ _relation_. At first the truth of the unessential world is a world in and for itself _other_ _to_ _it_; but that world is the totality, in that it is both itself and the first world; so the two are immediate concrete existences and hence reflections into their otherness, and by that same token genuinely reflected into themselves. _World_ expresses, generally, the formless totality of manifoldness; this world, essential as much as appearing, has foundered, inasmuch as manifoldness has ceased to be a merely diverse one; so it is still totality, or universe, yet as _essential_ _relation_. Within appearance two totalities of content have come to be; at first they stand determined over against each other as indifferent self-subsistent ones, and each does have form in its own self, though not over against the other; form, however, has shown itself to be their reference as well, and the essential relation is the consummation of their unity of form.
