## Remark

In the account given here of the syllogism's nature and of its various forms, notice has also been taken in passing of what makes up the chief interest in the customary consideration and treatment of syllogisms, namely how a correct inference can be drawn in each figure; in doing so, however, only the principal moment has been indicated, and those cases and entanglements have been passed over which arise once the difference between positive and negative judgments is drawn in along with the quantitative determination, particularity above all. – A few remarks upon the customary view of the syllogism in logic, and upon the manner of dealing with it, will not be out of place here. – As is well known, this doctrine was elaborated into such minute detail that its so-called subtleties became a general vexation and disgust. As _natural_ _understanding_ asserted itself against substanceless forms of reflection on every side of intellectual culture, it turned against that artificial acquaintance with the forms of reason as well, and supposed that such a science could be done without, for the reason that by nature, and with no special learning, it already performs of itself the individual operations of thought recorded there. Where rational thinking is concerned, the human being would in fact be as badly off, were the condition of it the toilsome study of the formulae of the syllogism, as he would be, (as was already remarked in the preface) if he could not walk and digest without having studied anatomy and physiology. Just as the study of those sciences may not be without profit for one's dietetic conduct, so too an influence more important still upon the correctness of thinking will doubtless have to be ascribed to the {{{ 12.107 }}} study of the forms of reason; but without entering here upon this side of the matter, which concerns the cultivation of subjective thinking and hence really belongs to pedagogy, it will have to be granted that a study whose object is how reason operates and what its laws are must in and for itself be of the very greatest interest, – of one at least no smaller than the knowledge of the laws of nature and of their particular shapes. If it is held to be no small thing to have discovered some sixty odd species of parrot, a hundred and thirty-seven kinds of veronica and so forth, then finding out the forms of reason must be held to be a far less trifling matter; is a figure of the syllogism not something infinitely higher than a parrot species or a veronica species?

Much as it is therefore to be reckoned nothing but crudity to despise acquaintance with the forms of reason altogether, just as much must one grant that the customary exposition of the syllogism and of its particular shapes yields no _rational_ cognition, no exhibition of these as _forms_ _of_ _reason_, and that the wisdom of syllogistic, through its own worthlessness, drew upon itself the low esteem that fell to it. Its defect consists in this, that it stops short absolutely at the _understanding's_ _form_ of the syllogism, according to which the determinations of the concept get taken for _abstract_ determinations of form. Holding fast to them as abstract qualities is all the more inconsequent in that in the syllogism it is _the_ _references_ among them that make up the essential thing, and inherence and subsumption already contain this, that the singular, having the universal inhere in it, is itself something universal, and the universal, subsuming the singular as it does, is itself something singular, while more precisely the syllogism expressly posits just this _unity_ _as_ _middle_ _term_ and its determination is _mediation_ precisely, that is, that the determinations of the concept no longer have for their basis, as they do in the judgment, their externality towards one another, but rather their unity. – Through the concept of the syllogism, then, the imperfection of the formal syllogism is pronounced, the syllogism in which the middle term, instead of being the unity of the extremes, is to be held fast as an abstract determination of form, qualitatively diverse from them. – The consideration is rendered still emptier of content by the fact that even such references or judgments as those in which the formal determinations themselves become indifferent, as in the negative and the particular judgment, and which therefore come close to being mere propositions, are still accepted as perfect relations. – Since now the qualitative form S–P–U counts quite generally as the last and absolute one, the dialectical consideration of the syllogism drops away entirely, and the remaining syllogisms are accordingly regarded as _species_ rather than as _necessary_ _alterations_ of that form. – Here it makes no difference whether the first formal syllogism is itself regarded merely as one {{{ 12.108 }}} species _alongside_ the rest, or else as _genus_ and species at once; the latter happens when the remaining syllogisms are led back to the first. Where this reduction is not carried out expressly, the same formal relation of external subsumption which the first figure expresses always lies at the basis.

The formal syllogism just described is the contradiction of a middle term that is to be the determinate unity of the extremes and yet is not that unity, but a determination qualitatively diverse from the very terms whose unity it is to be. Being this contradiction, the syllogism is dialectical in its own self. Its dialectical movement exhibits it in the complete moments of the concept, so that not merely the relation of subsumption, that is, particularity, but _just_ _as_ _essentially_ _the_ negative unity, along with universality, are moments of the conjoining. So far as each of them, taken for itself, is again only a one-sided moment of particularity, they too are imperfect middle terms, though they constitute at the same time the developed determinations of that middle term; the whole course through the three figures displays the middle term in one after another of these determinations, and what truly results from this is that the middle term is not a single one of them but their totality.

The formal syllogism's defect lies, therefore, not in the _form_ _of_ _the_ _syllogism_, – this form is rather the very form of rationality, – but in its being there only as an _abstract_ and hence conceptless form. It has been shown of the abstract determination that, by virtue of its abstract reference to itself, it may just as well be regarded as content; to that extent the formal syllogism accomplishes nothing beyond this, that a subject's reference to a predicate does or does not follow _merely_ _from_ _this_ _middle_ term. Nothing is gained by having proved a proposition through a syllogism of that sort; because the middle term has an abstract determinateness, being a conceptless quality, there may equally well be other middle terms yielding the opposite, and indeed from the same middle term opposed predicates can in turn be derived by further syllogisms. – Apart from accomplishing little, the formal syllogism is besides a very simple affair; the many rules people have devised are burdensome already because they stand in such contrast with the simple nature of the fact, but then also because they bear upon those cases in which the formal content of the syllogism is diminished altogether by the external form determination, particularity especially, and chiefly insofar as it must for this purpose be taken in the comprehensive sense, and in which results are brought out that are wholly void of content even as regards form. – The most just and the most important side of the disfavour into which syllogistic has fallen, however, is that it is so long-drawn-out and _conceptless_ a business {{{ 12.109 }}} with an object whose one and only content is the _concept_ itself. – The many syllogistic rules call to mind the procedure of reckoning masters, who likewise give a multitude of rules about the arithmetical operations, all of which presuppose that one does not have the _concept_ of the operation. – But numbers are a conceptless material, the operation of reckoning an external gathering or separating, a mechanical procedure, as witness the calculating machines that have been invented to carry these operations out; the harshest and most glaring thing, by contrast, is that the form determinations of the syllogism, which are concepts, should be handled as a conceptless material.

The uttermost point of this conceptless way of taking the conceptual determinations of the syllogism is surely that Leibniz (Opp. Tom. II. P. I.) subjected the syllogism to the combinatorial calculus and by its means computed how many positions of the syllogism are possible; – with reference, namely, to the differences between positive and negative judgments, and then between universal, particular, indeterminate and singular ones; of such combinations 2048 turn out to be possible, and after exclusion of the unusable ones 24 usable figures are left over. – Leibniz makes a great deal of the usefulness of combinatorial analysis for finding not only the forms of the syllogism but the combinations of other concepts as well. The operation by which this is found is the same as the one by which it is computed what combinations of letters an alphabet permits, how many kinds of throw a game of dice affords, how many games are possible with a hand of L’hombre cards and so forth. What one finds here, then, is that the determinations of the syllogism are set in one class with the pips of the die and of the L’hombre card, that the rational is taken as something dead and conceptless, and that what is peculiar to the concept and its determinations, namely _to_ _refer_ _themselves_ as spiritual essences and through this referring _to_ _sublate_ their _immediate_ determination, is left on one side. – This Leibnizian application of combinatorial calculus to the syllogism, and to how other concepts combine, differed in nothing from the notorious _Lullian_ _art_ save that on the side of _amount_ it was more methodical, matching it for the rest in senselessness. – Bound up with this was a favourite thought of Leibniz's, conceived by him in his youth and, for all its immaturity and shallowness, not given up in later years either, of a _universal_ _characteristic_ of concepts, – a written language in which every concept would be exhibited as being a reference out of others, or as referring itself to others – as though in the rational combination, which is essentially dialectical, a content still kept the same determinations that it has when it is fixed for itself.

{{{ 12.110 }}} _Ploucquet's_ _calculus_ has without doubt seized upon the most consistent procedure by which the relation of the syllogism becomes fit to be subjected to a calculus. This rests on abstracting from the difference of relation, from the difference of singularity, particularity and universality in the judgment, and on holding fast the _abstract_ _identity_ of subject and predicate, whereby these stand in _mathematical_ _equality_; – a reference which turns inferring into a formation of propositions wholly empty of content and tautological. – In the proposition: _the_ _rose_ is _red_, what the predicate signifies is supposed to be not red in its universality but only the determinate _red_ _of_ _the_ _rose_; again, in the proposition: all Christians are human beings, only those human beings who are Christians are what the predicate is supposed to signify; from this together with the proposition: the Jews are not Christians, there then follows the conclusion that did this syllogistic calculus no good service with _Mendelssohn_: _therefore_ _the_ _Jews_ _are_ _not_ _human_ _beings_, (namely not those human beings who are the Christians). – _Ploucquet_ states, as something his invention brings with it, _posse_ _etiam_ _rudes_ _mechanice_ _totam_ _logicam_ _doceri_, uti pueri arithmeticam docentur, ita quidem, ut nulla formidine in ratiociniis suis errandi torqueri, vel fallaciis circumveniri possint, si in calculo non errant. – This recommendation, that the whole of logic could be imparted _mechanically_ to the uneducated by way of the calculus, is assuredly the worst that could be said of an invention regarding the exposition of logical science.
