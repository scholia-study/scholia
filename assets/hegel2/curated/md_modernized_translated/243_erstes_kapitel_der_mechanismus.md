## {{{ 12.133 }}} Chapter 1. Mechanism

Since objectivity is the totality of the concept that has gone back into its unity, there is thereby posited an immediate which in and for itself is that totality and is also _posited_ as such, but in which the negative unity of the concept has not yet parted itself off from the immediacy of this totality; – or objectivity is not yet posited as _judgment_. Insofar as it has the concept immanent within it, the difference of the concept is present at it; but for the sake of the objective totality the differentiated terms are _complete_ and _self-subsistent_ _objects_, which accordingly, even in their reference, comport themselves towards one another only as _self-subsistent_ and in every combination remain _external_ to each other. – This is what makes up the character of _mechanism_, that whatever reference takes place among the combined terms, this reference is one _alien_ to them, one that has nothing to do with their nature, and that even when it is tied up with the shine of a one it remains nothing more than _composition_, _mixture_, _heap_, and so forth. As with _material_ mechanism, so too _spiritual_ mechanism consists in this, that what is referred to one another in spirit remains external to each other and to spirit itself. A _mechanical_ _way_ _of_ _representing_, a _mechanical_ _memory_, _habit_, a _mechanical_ _mode_ _of_ _action_ all mean that spirit's own characteristic pervasion of, and presence in, whatever it takes in or performs is absent. Granted that a theoretical or practical mechanism of spirit is impossible apart from spirit's self-activity, apart from a drive and from consciousness, the freedom of individuality is nonetheless wanting there, and since that freedom does not come to view in it, such doing shows itself as something merely external.
