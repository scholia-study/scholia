## c. The Disjunctive Judgment

The concept in the categorical judgment is as objective universality, and an external singularity. Upon this externality, in the hypothetical, the concept comes forward in its negative identity; through that identity they obtain the determinateness now posited in the disjunctive judgment, the determinateness which in the former they have immediately. The disjunctive judgment is accordingly objective universality posited at once together with the form. What it holds is therefore, _first_, the concrete universality or the genus, under _simple_ _form_, as the subject; _second_, however, _that_ _same_ universality as totality of the determinations differentiated within it. A is either B or C. Herein lies the _necessity_ _of_ _the_ _concept_, wherein, _first_, there is the selfsameness of the two extremes, one and the same extension, content and universality; _second_, they stand differentiated following the form of the determination of the concept, though so that, for the sake of that identity, this form _is_ as _mere_ _form_. Third, the identical objective universality appears on that account as what is reflected into itself over against the unessential form, as _content_, which nonetheless carries the determinateness of form in its own self; the one time as the simple determinateness of the _genus_; the other time just this determinateness as developed into its difference, – in which manner it constitutes the particularity of the _species_, and the _totality_ of these, the genus's universality. – Particularity in its development makes up the _predicate_, because it is to that extent the _more_ _universal_, in that it contains the whole universal sphere of the subject, yet contains it also in the laying-out of particularization.

Looking at this particularization more closely, then _in_ _the_ _first_ _place_ the substantial universality of the species is constituted by the genus; the subject is accordingly _both_ _B_ _and_ {{{ 12.81 }}} C; this _both_ _and_ marks the _positive_ identity of particular with universal; this objective universal maintains itself completely in its particularity. The species, _second_, _shut_ _each_ _other_ _out_; _A_ _is_ _either_ _B_ _or_ _C_; for they constitute the _determinate_ _difference_ of the universal sphere. This _either-or_ _is_ _the_ _negative_ _reference_ of them. In the latter, however, they are just as identical as in the former; the genus is their _unity_ _as_ _determinate_ _particulars_. – Were the genus abstract universality, as in the judgments of existence, then the species too would have to be taken merely as _diverse_ ones, indifferent towards each other; but the genus is not that external universality arising only through _comparison_ _and_ _omission_, but rather _their_ _immanent_ _and_ _concrete_ one. – Necessity is lacking in an empirical disjunctive judgment; A is either B or C or D and so forth, because the species B, C, D and so forth have simply _turned_ _up_; strictly speaking no _either-or_ can be pronounced by such a judgment; for species of that kind make up at best a subjective completeness; the _one_ _species_ does indeed exclude the _other_; but _either-or_ _excludes_ _every_ _further_ _one_, and closes off a total sphere within itself. The _necessity_ of this totality lies in the negative unity of the objectively universal, which has dissolved singularity within itself and carries it immanently within itself as the simple _principle_ of difference, whereby the species stand _determined_ _and_ _referred_ to one another. The empirical species, by contrast, have their differences in some contingency or other, one that is an external principle and hence not _their_ principle, and so also not the immanent determinateness of the genus; for that reason they are not referred to one another according to their determinateness either. – It is through _the_ _reference_ of their determinateness, though, that the species make up the universality of the predicate. – The so-called _contrary_ _and_ _contradictory_ concepts ought strictly to find their place only here; for in the disjunctive judgment the essential difference of the concept is posited; but therein they at the same time also have their truth, namely that the contrary and the contradictory are themselves differentiated just as much contrarily as contradictorily. The species are contrary insofar as they are only _diverse_, namely, through the genus as their objective nature they have a subsistence in and for itself; _contradictory_, insofar as they exclude each other. Each of these determinations taken on its own is, however, one-sided and lacking in truth; within the _either-or_ of the disjunctive judgment there is posited their unity as their truth, according to which that self-subsistent subsistence, _as_ _concrete_ _universality_, is itself likewise the _principle_ of the negative unity through which they shut each other out.

Through the identity of subject and predicate just now exhibited, an identity following {{{ 12.82 }}} negative unity, the disjunctive judgment _determines_ the genus as the _nearest_ one. This expression points at first to a bare quantitative difference of _more_ or _fewer_ determinations that a universal may contain over against a particularity standing beneath it. On such a reckoning it remains contingent what the nearest genus properly is. But insofar as one takes the genus for a universal formed merely by leaving determinations out, it cannot properly form any disjunctive judgment; for it is contingent whether the determinateness making up the principle of the _either-or_ has happened to remain in it; the genus would not be exhibited in the species according to its _determinateness_ at all, and these could have only a contingent completeness. In the categorical judgment the genus stands over against the subject at first only in this abstract form, hence not necessarily as the genus nearest to it, and to that extent externally. But since the genus, as concrete, is essentially _determinate_ universality, as the simple determinateness it is the unity of those _moments_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ which in that simplicity are merely sublated but have their real difference in the species. The genus is therefore the _nearest_ one of a species insofar as that species has its specific differentiation in the essential determinateness of the genus, and insofar as the species in general have their differentiated determination, as principle, in the nature of the genus.

The side just considered makes up the identity of subject and predicate on the side of _being-determined_ in general; a side that has been posited through the hypothetical judgment, whose necessity is an identity of immediates and of diverse things, and is therefore essentially as negative unity. This negative unity is what in general separates subject and predicate, though it is now itself posited as differentiated, in the subject as _simple_ determinateness, and in the predicate as _totality_. That separating of the subject from the predicate is the _difference_ _of_ _the_ _concept_; but the _totality_ _of_ _the_ _species_ in the predicate can just as little be _any_ _other_ difference. – The _determination_ _of_ _the_ _disjunctive_ _members_ over against each other thus issues from this. It reduces itself to the difference of the concept, for the concept alone disjoins itself and manifests its negative unity in its determination. For the rest, the species comes into consideration here only according to its simple determinateness of the concept, not according to the _shape_ in which it has stepped out of the idea into a further self-subsistent _reality_; that shape does indeed _fall_ _away_ where the genus's simple principle is concerned; but the _essential_ differentiation has to be a moment of the concept. In the judgment considered here it is properly through the concept's _own_ further determining that its disjunction is now itself _posited_, that which emerged in the case of the concept as its determination existing in and for itself, as its differentiation into determinate concepts. – Since the concept is now the universal, the positive just as much as the negative {{{ 12.83 }}} totality of the particulars, precisely thereby _it_ _itself_ is also immediately _one_ _of_ _its_ _disjunctive_ _members_; the _other_, though, is this universality resolved into _its_ _particularity_, that is, the concept's determinateness _as_ _determinateness_; and in that determinateness universality just so presents itself as the totality. – If a genus's disjunction into species has not yet attained this form, that is proof that it has not risen to the concept's determinateness and has not issued out of it. – _Colour_ is either violet, indigo, light blue, green, yellow, orange, or red; – in a disjunction of that sort its mixedness and impurity, empirical as well, can be seen at once; regarded on this side on its own, it already deserves to be called barbaric. If colour has been grasped as the _concrete_ _unity_ of bright and dark, then this _genus_ _carries_ _the_ _determinateness_ in it which makes up the _principle_ by which it is particularized into species. Among these, however, one has to be the absolutely simple colour, which holds the opposition in even balance and enclosed and negated within its intensity; over against it the opposition of the relation of the bright and the dark must present itself, to which, since a phenomenon of nature is at issue, the indifferent neutrality of the opposition must further be added. – Taking mixtures like violet and orange, and gradations like indigo and light blue, for species can have its ground only in a wholly unconsidered procedure, one showing too little reflection even for empiricism. – What differentiated and still more closely determined forms the disjunction may have, according as it takes place in the element of nature or of spirit, does not belong here to be worked out.

The disjunctive judgment has the members of the disjunction at first in its predicate; but it is just as much itself disjoined; the members of the disjunction are its subject and its predicate; these are the concept's moments, posited in their determinateness and yet at once as identical, as _identical_ α) in objective universality, this being the simple _genus_ in the subject, and in the predicate the universal sphere together with the _totality_ of the concept's moments, and β) in the _negative_ unity, the developed interconnection of necessity, according to which the _simple_ _determinateness_ in the subject has gone apart into the _difference_ _of_ _the_ _species_, and precisely therein is their essential reference and what is identical with itself.

The copula of this judgment, this unity into which the extremes have gone together through their identity, is thus the concept itself, and indeed _as_ _posited_; with this the mere judgment of necessity has raised itself to the _judgment_ _of_ _the_ _concept_.
