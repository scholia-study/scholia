## B. The World of Appearance and the World-in-itself

1. The concretely existing world lifts itself calmly into a realm of laws; the null content belonging to its manifold existence subsists within something other; that subsistence of its is accordingly its dissolution. But within this other what appears also goes together _with_ _itself_; hence appearance, amid its changing, is likewise an abiding, and its positedness is law. This simple identity of appearance with itself is what law is; law is on that account appearance's foundation, not appearance's ground; for law is not appearance's negative unity, but rather, being appearance's simple identity, the immediate as an abstract unity, _alongside_ which the remaining content of appearance therefore _also_ finds a place. The content is _this_ one, coheres internally, or possesses its negative reflection inside its own self. It stands reflected into something other; and that other is itself one of appearance's concrete existences; appearing things find their grounds and their conditions in other appearing things.

As a matter of fact, though, law is likewise the _other_ _of_ _appearance_ _as_ _such_, and it is appearance's negative reflection, reflection namely into appearance's other. That content of appearance which is diverse from law's content is the concretely existing, which holds its negativity as its ground or stands reflected into its own non-being. Yet this _other_, itself something _concretely_ _existing_, has likewise been reflected in just that way into its non-being; it is thus _the_ _same_, and therein what appears is in fact reflected not into an other at all but _into_ _itself_; law is precisely this reflection of positedness into itself. But qua something appearing it stands essentially _reflected_ _into_ _its_ _non-being_, or else its identity is itself, essentially and in equal measure, its negativity and its other. Appearance's reflection-into-itself, law, is thus not merely appearance's identical foundation either; in law appearance rather possesses its own opposite, and law constitutes appearance's negative unity.

The determination of law has thereby altered in law's own self. To begin with it is only a diverse content, plus the formal reflection of positedness into itself, such that positedness on one of its sides amounts to positedness on the other. But since law is likewise the negative reflection into itself, {{{ 11.348 }}} its two sides stand related not merely as diverse ones but as ones referring negatively to each other. – Or again, law taken barely for itself: then the sides of its content are mutually indifferent; but they are equally sublated through their identity; what is posited in the one is _the_ _other's_ positedness; and so each side's subsisting is also _its_ _own_ _non-subsistence_. That the one is posited within the other constitutes their negative unity, and each of them is _not_ _merely_ _its_ _own_ _positedness_ _but_ _the_ _other's_ _as_ _well_, or rather each is itself this negative unity. Within law as such the positive identity belonging to them is as yet only their _inner_ unity, one requiring _proof_ and _mediation_, since this negative unity has not yet been posited in them. But now that law's diverse sides are determined as being diverse within their negative unity, or as such that each harbours its other in its own self while at once, being self-subsistent, repelling this otherness of its away from itself, law's identity is henceforth a _posited_ and _real_ one too.

Law has therewith likewise acquired the moment it lacked, that of the negative form on its sides; the moment which up to now still belonged to appearance; concrete existence has consequently gone back wholly into itself, and has reflected itself into that absolute otherness of its own which is in and for itself. What was law before is on this account no longer just One side of a whole whose other side had been appearance as such; it is rather the whole itself. It is appearance's essential totality, so that it now embraces the moment of unessentiality as well, which up to now still fell to appearance; embracing it, however, as reflected unessentiality that is in itself, that is, as _essential_ _negativity_. – Being immediate content, law is _determinate_ in general, marked off from other laws, and of these there is an indeterminable multitude. But since essential negativity now belongs to it in its own self, it no longer harbours a determination of content merely indifferent and contingent in that way; all determinateness whatever is rather its content, standing in an essential reference which makes itself into totality. So appearance reflected into itself is henceforth a _world_ that, being _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, _opens_ _up_ above the _world_ _of_ _appearance_.

What the realm of laws holds is only the simple, changeless, yet diverse content of the concretely existing world. Yet now that it is that world's total reflection, it holds the moment of that world's essenceless manifoldness as well. This moment of changeableness and of change, reflected into itself and thereby essential, is absolute negativity or form in general as such, though the moments of that form possess, in the world in and for itself, the reality of concrete existence that is self-subsistent and yet reflected; conversely, this reflected self-subsistence {{{ 11.349 }}} now carries form in its own self, whereby its content is not something merely manifold but something essentially cohering with itself.

– This world in and for itself is also called the _supersensible_ _world_; insofar, that is, as the concretely existing world gets determined as the _sensible_ one, namely as the one that is there for intuition, for consciousness in its immediate comportment. – Immediacy, concrete existence, belongs to the supersensible world too, but reflected, essential concrete existence. _Essence_ has as yet no existence; but it _is_, and in a deeper sense than being; the _thing_ marks the start of reflected concrete existence; it is an immediacy not yet _posited_ as essential or reflected; in truth, however, it is no immediate _that_ _is_. Only as things of another world, a supersensible one, do things get posited: firstly as genuine concrete existences, secondly as the true over against what has being; – in them one acknowledges that a being distinct from immediate being exists, a being which is genuine concrete existence. This determination overcomes, on the one hand, the sensible representation that grants concrete existence solely to the immediate being of feeling and intuition; on the other hand it overcomes unconscious reflection too, which does indeed carry the representation of _things_, of _forces_, of what is _inner_ and so forth, without knowing that determinations of that sort are not sensible immediacies, nor immediacies that have being, but reflected concrete existences.

2. The world in and for itself is concrete existence in its totality; outside it there is nothing else. But since in its own self it is absolute negativity, or form, its reflection-into-itself is a _negative_ _reference_ to itself. Opposition is contained in it, and it repels itself into itself as the essential world and into itself as the world of otherness, the world of appearance. Thus, on the very grounds that it is the totality, it also is merely as _one_ _side_ of that totality, and under this determination it makes up a self-subsistence diverse from appearance's world. In the essential world the world of appearance possesses its negative unity, foundering in that unity and going back into it as into its ground. The essential world is furthermore the ground that posits the world of appearance; for, holding absolute form within its essentiality, its self-identity sublates itself, turns itself into positedness, and, as this posited immediacy, it is the appearing world.

Furthermore, it is not merely the ground of the appearing world in general, but that world's _determinate_ ground. Already qua realm of laws it is manifold _content_, the essential content namely of the world of appearance, and, being a ground filled with content, it is the _determinate_ ground of the _other_ world, though only as regards this content; for the appearing world still possessed manifold content besides what that realm did, {{{ 11.350 }}} since the negative moment still fell to it as its own. But now that the realm of laws likewise carries this moment in itself, it is the content of the world of appearance in its totality and the ground of that world's entire manifoldness. Yet it is at the same time the negative of that world, and so [it] is the world _opposed_ to it. – In the identity of the two worlds, namely, and given that the one is determined as regards form as the essential one and the other as that same world though posited and unessential, the _ground-reference_ has indeed been re-established; but re-established at once as the _ground-reference_ _of_ _appearance_, that is, as a reference neither of an identical content nor of a merely diverse one such as law is, but as total reference, or as negative identity and _essential_ _reference_ _of_ _content_ _as_ _opposed_. – The realm of laws does not amount merely to this, that one content's positedness is another's; this identity is rather essentially, as has come out, negative unity as well; within the negative unity each of law's two sides is _in_ _its_ _own_ _self_ _its_ _other_ content; hence the other is not indeterminately an other in general, but is _its_ other, or carries the first one's determination of content just as much; the two sides are opposed ones accordingly. Since the realm of laws now carries this negative moment and this opposition in itself, and thus, being the totality, repels away from itself both a world in and for itself and an appearing world, the identity of the two is the _essential_ _reference_ _of_ _opposition_. – Ground-reference as such is opposition foundered in its contradiction; and concrete existence is the ground going together _with_ _itself_. But concrete existence turns into appearance; ground stands sublated in concrete existence; ground re-establishes itself as appearance's return into itself, but re-establishes itself at once as something sublated, namely as ground-reference of opposed determinations; the identity of such determinations, though, is essentially becoming and passing over, no longer ground-reference as such.

The world in and for itself is thus itself a world internally differentiated into the totality of manifold content; it is identical with the appearing or posited world, and to that extent its ground, yet its identical connection stands at the same time determined as opposition, since the form of the world of appearance consists in reflection into that world's otherness, so that within the world in and for itself the appearing world has genuinely gone back into its own self in the very measure in which this latter is its opposite. So the reference is determined precisely thus, that the world in and for itself is the _inverted_ world of the appearing one.
