## Remark

The formal ground-reference contains but one single content for ground and grounded, and in this identity lies its necessity, though at the same time its tautology. The real ground contains a differing content, but with that the contingency and outwardness of the ground-reference sets in. On the one side, that which counts as the essential, and for that very reason as the determination of ground, fails to ground the other determinations linked with it. On the other side it also stays undetermined which of a concrete thing's several content-determinations should be assumed to be the essential one, the one that is ground; the choice between them is thus left free. Thus in the first respect the ground of a house, for example, is its foundation; what makes that foundation a ground is the _heaviness_ residing in sensuous matter, the element utterly identical in the ground and in the grounded house alike. That heavy matter should exhibit such a difference as that between a foundation and a modification distinguished from it, whereby the matter makes up a dwelling, is a thing of complete indifference to the heavy itself; its reference to the other content-determinations, to the purpose, to the fitting out of the house and so forth, is outward to it; heaviness is therefore certainly the base of them, but not their ground. Heaviness is just as much the ground of a house's standing as it is the ground of a stone's falling; the stone has this ground, heaviness, within it; but that it carries a further content-determination, whereby it counts not simply as a heavy thing but as stone, lies outside heaviness; it is moreover posited through something else that the stone should have been removed beforehand from the body onto which it falls, just as time and space, and their reference, motion, are a content other than heaviness and admit (as the way of speaking goes) of being represented apart from it, and consequently are not essentially posited through it. – Heaviness is quite as much the ground of a projectile's performing the hurled motion opposed to falling. – From the diversity of the determinations of which heaviness is the ground it becomes clear that something else is required alongside, something that makes heaviness into the ground of this determination or of another. –

When _nature_ is said to be the _ground_ _of_ _the_ _world_, then whatever bears the name of nature is in one respect _one_ with the world, the world being no more than nature itself. They are also distinguished, however, in such a way that nature is rather the indeterminate, or at any rate is the self-identical essence of the world determined only in those universal differences which {{{ 11.310 }}} are laws, and that to nature, if it is to be world, a manifoldness of determinations still accrues outwardly. Their ground, however, does not lie in nature as such; nature is rather what stands indifferent towards them as contingencies. – Just the same relation holds when _God_ receives the determination of being the _ground_ _of_ _nature_. Being ground, he is nature's essence; nature harbours that essence in it and is something identical with him; but nature has besides a further manifoldness distinguished from the ground itself; nature is the _third_ in which these two differing things are linked; that ground is neither the ground of the manifoldness differing from it nor the ground of its own linkage with that manifoldness. Nature is therefore not cognized out of God as its ground, for so taken he would be only its universal essence, an essence that does not contain nature as nature is determinate essence and nature.

The adducing of real grounds accordingly turns into just as much of a formalism as the formal ground itself, and this by reason of the content-difference between the ground — or, properly speaking, the base — and whatever is bound up with it in the grounded. In the formal ground the self-identical content stands indifferent towards form; the same holds good in the real ground. Beyond that there is now the further case that the real ground does not carry within its own self any decision as to which among the manifold determinations is to count as the essential one. _Something_ is a _concrete_ made up of such manifold determinations, all of which show themselves equally constant and abiding upon it. Any one of them may therefore be determined as ground just as well as any other; determined, that is, as the _essential_ one, in comparison with which the other would then be only something posited. Bound up with this is what was mentioned just before, namely that if some determination is at hand which in a given case gets viewed as another's ground, nothing follows to the effect that the second is posited together with the first in a further case, or generally. – _Punishment_, to take an example, carries the manifold determinations of being retribution, of being furthermore a deterring example, of being something the law holds out as a threat for deterrence, and of being something that brings the criminal to reflection and improvement. Every one of these differing determinations has at some time counted as the _ground_ _of_ _punishment_, since each of them is essential as a determination, and thereby the rest, as distinguished from it, get determined over against it as merely contingent. The one taken as ground, however, is not yet the whole of punishment itself; this concrete contains those others as well, and they are merely linked with the one within it without having their ground in it. – Or an _official_ has skill in office, stands as an individual within a kinship, has this and that acquaintance, a particular character, was placed in these and those circumstances and occasions for showing himself, and so forth. Each of these properties may be, or may be looked upon as, the ground of his holding this office; they are a differing content bound together in a third; the {{{ 11.311 }}} form of being determined against one another as the essential and as the posited is outward to that third. Each of these properties is essential to the official, since it is through them that he is this determinate individual and no other; insofar as the office admits of being regarded as an outwardly posited determination, each property may be determined as ground over against it, yet conversely those properties may equally be viewed as posited and the office as what grounds them. How they _actually_ stand, which is to say in the particular case, is a determination lying outside the ground-reference and outside the content as well; some third thing is what bestows on them the form of ground and grounded.

In general, then, every existence can have grounds of many kinds; each of its content-determinations, being self-identical, pervades the concrete whole and therefore admits of being regarded as essential; and given the contingency of the manner of linkage, door and gate stand infinitely open to the many _respects_, that is, to determinations lying _outside_ the fact itself. – Whether this or that _consequence_ attends a ground is on the same account equally contingent. Moral motives, to take an example, count as _essential_ _determinations_ of ethical nature; what issues from them, though, is at once an outwardness distinct from them, which issues from them and equally does not; only by way of a third does it get added to them. Put more precisely, this is to be taken thus: it is _not_ contingent for the moral determination, _if_ it is ground, to have a consequence or something grounded, but it is contingent whether it be made into a ground at all or not. Yet since the content that is its consequence, once the determination has been made into a ground, itself has the nature of outwardness, that content can immediately be sublated by some other outwardness. Out of a moral motive an action may therefore come forth, or may just as well not. Conversely, an action can have grounds of many kinds; being a concrete, it contains manifold essential determinations, any of which may on that account be adduced as its ground. The searching out and adducing of grounds, in which _ratiocination_ chiefly consists, is for this reason an endless driving about that contains no final determination; for anything and everything one good ground and several can be adduced, and just as many for its opposite, and a whole crowd of grounds can be at hand without anything at all resulting from them. What Socrates and Plato name _sophistry_ amounts to nothing other than ratiocinating from grounds; over against it Plato places the consideration of the idea, in other words of the fact in and for its own self, or in its _concept_. Grounds are taken only from _essential_ content-determinations, relations and respects, of which every fact has several, exactly as its contrary does too; in their form of essentiality the one counts as much as the other; because it does not contain the whole compass of the fact, each is a one-sided ground, whose other particular sides in turn have particular {{{ 11.312 }}} ones of their own, and of which none exhausts the fact that makes up their _linkage_ and holds them all within it; not one of them is a _sufficient_ ground, in other words the concept.
