## c. The Syllogism of Analogy

1. The third figure of the immediate syllogism, S–U–P, serves this syllogism for its abstract schema. No longer, though, is its middle term any singular quality whatever; it is a universality constituting _the_ _reflection-into-itself_ _of_ _a_ _concrete_, hence that concrete's _nature_; – and inversely, since the universality it is belongs thus to a concrete, it is in its own self at once this very _concrete_. – What is middle here is therefore a singular, yet a singular taken by its universal nature; and there is besides a further singular, an extreme, in which the very same universal nature is found. For instance.

_The_ _earth_ has inhabitants The moon is _an_ _earth_, Hence the moon has inhabitants.

2. Analogy grows the shallower in proportion as the universal wherein the two singulars are one, and by which the one turns into predicate of the other, amounts to a bare _quality_ or, quality being taken subjectively, to some _mark_ or other, and as the identity of the pair in this respect passes for bare _similarity_. Shallowness of this kind, however, into which a form of understanding or of reason is dragged down when one demotes it to the sphere of bare _representation_, has no business being cited in logic {{{ 12.116 }}} at all. – Improper too is any presentation of this syllogism's major premise as reading: _What_ _resembles_ _an_ _object_ _in_ _certain_ _marks_ _resembles_ _it_ _in_ _others_ _besides_. By such a procedure the _form_ _of_ _the_ _syllogism_ finds expression under the guise of a content, and the empirical content, the one properly so named, is put wholesale into the minor premise. So too the entire form of the first syllogism, for instance, admits of being stated as that syllogism's major premise: _Whatever_ _is_ _subsumed_ _under_ _another_ _in_ _which_ _a_ _third_ _inheres_, _has_ _this_ _third_ _inhering_ _in_ _it_ _too_; _Now_ _however_ _and_ _so_ _forth_. In the syllogism itself, though, empirical content is beside the point, and turning its own form into a major premise's content is as immaterial as it would be to put any other empirical content there instead. But supposing that, with the syllogism of analogy, nothing were to turn on that content, which holds nothing except the syllogism's peculiar form, then with the first syllogism just as little would turn on it, that is, on what makes a syllogism into a syllogism. – What things turn on is invariably the form of the syllogism, no matter whether this form itself or something else supplies its empirical content. Hence the syllogism of analogy is a peculiar form, and quite empty is the ground for declining to see one in it, namely that its form admits of being made the content or matter of a major premise, whereas matter has nothing to do with the logical. – What can tempt one to this thought in the case of analogy, and possibly of induction too, is that in these the middle and the extremes alike carry a further determination than they do in the merely formal syllogism, so that the form-determination, being no longer simple and abstract, is bound to show itself as a _content-determination_ besides. That the form determines itself in this way into content is, though, in the first instance a necessary onward movement of the formal, and so touches the syllogism's nature essentially; on that account, _secondly_, a content-determination of the sort cannot be looked upon as one like any other empirical content, nor abstracted away from.

Should the form of the syllogism of analogy be regarded in that formulation of its major premise, that _when_ _two_ _objects_ _agree_ _in_ _one_ _or_ _indeed_ _in_ _several_ _properties_, then _a_ _further_ _property_ _possessed_ _by_ _the_ _other_ _accrues_ _to_ _the_ _one_ _too_, it may look as though _four_ _determinations_ were contained in this syllogism, the quaternionem terminorum; – a circumstance that carried with it the awkwardness of bringing analogy under the form of a formal syllogism. – _Two_ _singulars_ there are, _thirdly_ a property immediately taken to be shared, and _fourthly_ that other property which one of the singulars possesses immediately whereas the other {{{ 12.117 }}} comes by it only through the syllogism. – The source of this lies, as has come out, in the fact that within the analogical syllogism _the_ _middle_ stands posited as singularity and yet immediately _also_ as the genuine universality thereof. – With _induction_ the middle, over and above the two extremes, is an indeterminable multitude of singulars; in that syllogism one would accordingly have to count an infinite multitude of termini. – Within the syllogism of allness the universality on the middle is as yet nothing but the outward form-determination of allness; within the syllogism of analogy, on the other hand, it is essential universality. In the above example the Medius Terminus: _the_ _earth_, is taken as a concrete which in accordance with its truth is no less a universal nature, a genus, than it is a singular.

From this side the Quaternio terminorum did not render analogy an imperfect syllogism. From another side, though, it does render it such; for even granting that the one subject possesses the universal nature that the other possesses, it stays undetermined whether the determinateness inferred for the other as well accrues to the one subject in virtue of its _nature_ or in virtue of its _particularity_, whether, say, the earth possesses inhabitants qua celestial body _as_ _such_, or only qua _this_ _particular_ _celestial_ _body_. – To this extent analogy remains a syllogism of reflection, singularity and universality being _immediately_ conjoined within its middle. Owing to that immediacy the reflective unity still carries its _externality_ with it; the singular is only _in_ _itself_ _the_ genus, it is not posited in that negativity whereby its own determinateness would count as the determinateness of the genus itself. The predicate accruing to the singular that is middle is on that account not already predicate of the other singular as well, notwithstanding that the two belong to one genus.

3. S–P (the moon has inhabitants) is the conclusion; the one premise, however (the earth has inhabitants), is an S–P of precisely the same sort; if S–P is to count as a conclusion, then the demand is implicit that this premise be a conclusion too. In its own self, then, this syllogism is the demand for itself directed against the immediacy it harbours; or it presupposes its own conclusion. Where a syllogism of existence finds its presupposition in the _other_ syllogisms of existence, in the syllogisms just examined the presupposition has shifted inward, they being syllogisms of reflection. Since the syllogism of analogy is thus the demand for its own mediation over against the immediacy that clings to that mediation, what it calls for is the sublation of the moment of _singularity_. For the middle there is left, then, the objective universal, the _genus_, cleansed of immediacy. – Within the syllogism of analogy the genus counted as a moment of the middle merely by way of _immediate_ _presupposition_; and since the syllogism itself calls for the sublation of the presupposed immediacy, the negation of singularity, and thereby the universal, is no longer immediate but _posited_. – Only the _first_ negation of immediacy was contained in the syllogism of reflection {{{ 12.118 }}}; the second has now supervened, and with it the outward universality of reflection is determined into one that is in and for itself. – Regarded from the positive side, the conclusion turns out identical with the premise, the mediation grown together with its own presupposition, and thus an identity of reflective universality by which that universality has become universality of a higher kind.

If we survey the course taken by the syllogisms of reflection, mediation there is throughout the _posited_, or _concrete_, unity of the extremes' determinations of form; reflection lies in just this positing of the one determination within the other; what mediates is accordingly _allness_. What shows itself as the essential ground of allness, however, is _singularity_, universality figuring on it only as an outward determination, as _completeness_. Universality is nevertheless _essential_ to the singular if the latter is to be a middle that joins in syllogism; the singular is therefore to be taken as a universal that is _in_ _itself_. With universality, though, it is not conjoined in this merely positive manner; it is sublated within universality and is a negative moment; thus the universal, the one that is in and for itself, is posited genus, whereas the singular, being immediate, is rather that genus's externality, or is _extreme_. – Broadly taken, the syllogism of reflection falls under the schema P–S–U, wherein the singular still counts as such for the essential determination of the middle; but with its immediacy sublated and the middle determined as universality in and for itself, the syllogism has moved under the formal schema: S–U–P, and the syllogism of reflection has crossed over into the _syllogism_ _of_ _necessity_.
