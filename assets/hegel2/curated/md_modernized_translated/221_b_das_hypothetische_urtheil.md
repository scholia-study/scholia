## {{{ 12.79 }}} b. The Hypothetical Judgment

_If_ _A_ _is_, _then_ _B_ _is_; _or_, _A's_ _being_ _is_ _not_ _a_ _being_ _of_ _its_ _own_, _but_ _the_ _being_ _of_ _an_ _other_, _of_ _B_. – What this judgment posits is the _necessary_ _interconnection_ of immediate determinatenesses, something the categorical judgment does not yet posit. – Two immediate concrete existences are present here, or externally contingent ones, of which the categorical judgment at first has only the one, the subject; but since the one stands externally over against the other, this other likewise stands, immediately, externally over against the first. – Given such immediacy the _content_ of the two sides remains indifferent, each towards the other; hence this judgment is at first a proposition of empty form. Now immediacy is, _first_, admittedly a self-subsistent, concrete _being_ as such; but, _second_, it is the reference of this being that is essential; hence that being counts equally as mere _possibility_; what the hypothetical judgment contains is not that A _is_, nor that B _is_, but only this: _if_ one of them is, _then_ so is the other; the interconnection of the extremes alone is posited, as being, and not the extremes themselves. On the contrary, within this necessity each is posited as being equally the _being_ _of_ _an_ _other_. – The proposition of identity declares: A is A only, not B; and B is B only, not A; whereas in the hypothetical judgment the concept posits the being of finite things according to their formal truth, namely this: that the finite is a being of its own and yet equally not _its_ _own_, but the being of an other. Within the sphere of being the finite _alters_ itself, it turns into an other; within the sphere of essence it is _appearance_, and it is posited that its being consists in an other's _shining_ in it, while _necessity_ is the _inner_ reference, not yet posited as such. But the concept is precisely this: that such identity is _posited_, and that whatever has being is not abstract identity with itself but _concrete_ identity, and, immediately in its own self, the being of an other.

The hypothetical judgment may be taken in a closer determinateness by way of the relations of reflection, as the relation of _ground_ and _consequence_, of _condition_ and _conditioned_, of _causality_, and so forth. What substantiality is in the categorical judgment, the interconnection of causality is in the hypothetical, in its form as concept. This relation and the rest all fall under it, but here they are no longer relations between _self-subsistent_ _sides_; these sides essentially are only moments of one and the selfsame identity. – Within it, however, they are not yet opposed following the determinations of the concept, as singular or particular and universal, but only as _moments_ _in_ _general_. So far, then, the hypothetical judgment has rather the shape of a proposition; {{{ 12.80 }}} as the particular judgment is indeterminate in content, so the hypothetical is indeterminate in form, since its content does not stand in the determination of subject and predicate. – Yet _in_ _itself_ being, being as it is the being of the other, is precisely thereby _unity_ _of_ _itself_ _and_ _of_ _the_ _other_, and so _universality_; therewith it is at the same time properly only a _particular_, since it is determinate and, in its determinateness, does not refer merely to itself. What is posited, however, is not the _simple_ abstract particularity; through the _immediacy_ which the _determinatenesses_ _have_, the moments of that particularity are as differentiated; through their unity, which constitutes their reference, particularity is at the same time also their totality. – Accordingly, what this judgment in truth posits is universality, the concept's concrete identity, whose determinations possess no subsistence on their own account and are merely particularities posited within it. So it is the _disjunctive_ _judgment_.
