## {{{ 12.173 }}} Third Section. The Idea

The idea is the _adequate_ _concept_, that which is objectively _true_, or the _true_ _as_ _such_. Should anything whatever possess truth, it possesses it through its idea, or _something_ _has_ _truth_ _only_, _insofar_ _as_ _it_ _is_ _idea_. – Elsewhere the word _idea_ has often enough been used, in philosophy as in ordinary life, for _concept_ as well, indeed for a bare _representation_; and when someone says that he has as yet no _idea_ of this lawsuit, this building, this stretch of country, nothing more is intended by it than the _representation_. Kant reclaimed the word _idea_ for the _concept_ _of_ _reason_. – On Kant's account the concept of reason is to be the concept of the _unconditioned_, though _transcendent_ with regard to appearances, that is, incapable of any _empirical_ _use_ _adequate_ _to_ _it_. Concepts of reason are said to serve for _comprehending_, concepts of the understanding for the _understanding_ of perceptions. – In truth, however, if the latter really are _concepts_, then _they_ _are_ _concepts_, – by them something is comprehended, and where perceptions are _understood_ through concepts of the understanding, that will be a _comprehending_. Should understanding, though, amount to nothing more than a determining of perceptions by determinations such as whole and parts, force, cause and so forth, then it signifies no more than a determining through reflection, just as by _understanding_ one can mean only the determinate _representing_ of a thoroughly determinate sensuous content; if a man to whom the route is pointed out, that he must turn left where the wood ends, replies say: I _understand_, then his _understanding_ amounts to nothing beyond a taking of it up into representation and into memory. – The term _concept_ _of_ _reason_, moreover, sits rather awkwardly; for the concept is as such already something rational; and to the extent that reason gets distinguished from the understanding and from the concept as such, reason is the totality of concept together with objectivity. – Taken in that sense, the idea is what is _rational_; – and it counts as unconditioned because conditions belong only to whatever refers itself essentially to an objectivity, and to an objectivity not determined through its own self at that, but to one that still confronts it under the form of indifference and of externality, as external purpose still did.

{{{ 12.174 }}} Now that the word _idea_ is being held back for the objective or real concept, and marked off from the concept itself and still more from mere representation, there is all the further reason to throw out that estimate of the idea by which it passes for something merely unactual, and by which it is said of true thoughts that _they_ _are_ _only_ _ideas_. Where _thoughts_ are something merely _subjective_ and contingent, they have of course no worth beyond that; but on this score they are not outdone by temporal and contingent _actualities_, whose worth likewise reaches no further than that of contingencies and appearances. If, on the other hand, the idea is denied the worth of truth on the ground that it is _transcendent_ with regard to appearances, that no object congruent with it can be furnished in the sensuous world, then here is a strange misunderstanding, since objective validity is refused the idea for want of precisely what makes up appearance, the _untrue_ _being_ of the objective world. With regard to the practical ideas Kant grants that »nothing more harmful, nothing less worthy of a philosopher can be found than the _vulgar_ invoking of an _experience_ supposedly at odds with the idea. That experience would not so much as exist, had institutions of state been set up in good time in accordance with the ideas, and had _crude_ _concepts_ not, in their place and just _because_ _they_ _had_ _been_ _drawn_ _from_ _experience_, brought every good intention to nothing.« Kant looks upon the idea as something necessary, as the goal that has to be erected as the _archetype_ for a maximum, and towards which the endeavour must be to bring the condition of actuality ever closer.

Now that the result has come out that the idea is the unity of concept and objectivity, the true, it is not to be looked upon merely as a _goal_ one should draw near to while it stays for its own part a sort of _beyond_; rather everything actual only _is_ to the extent that it carries the idea within it and gives it expression. The object, the objective world and the subjective world, are not merely _supposed_ to _be_ _congruent_ with the idea; they are themselves the congruence of concept and reality; whatever reality does not answer to the concept is bare _appearance_, the subjective, the contingent, the arbitrary, that which is not the truth. When one says that experience turns up no object perfectly congruent with the _idea_, the idea is thereby placed opposite the actual as a subjective yardstick; but what an actual thing is truly to _be_, granted that its concept is not in it and its objectivity not in the least commensurate with that concept, no one can say; for it would be the nothing. Mechanical and chemical object alike, and the spiritless subject too, and the spirit aware only of the finite and not {{{ 12.175 }}} of its own essence, none of these, each after its differing nature, has its concept existing in it _in_ _its_ _own_ _free_ _form_. Yet true they can be at all only to the extent that they are the uniting of their concept with reality, of their soul with their body. Wholes of the sort of the state or the church stop existing once the unity of concept and reality in them has come undone; the human being, the living being, lies dead when in it soul and body come apart; dead nature, the mechanical and chemical world, if, that is, the dead be taken to stand for the inorganic world, since apart from that it would carry no positive sense whatever, – dead nature, then, once it is sundered into concept on the one side and reality on the other, amounts to no more than a subjective abstraction, a form merely thought and a matter without form. A spirit which were not idea, not the unity of the concept itself with itself, – [were] not the concept having the concept itself for its reality, would be spirit dead and spiritless, an object of matter.

_Being_ has reached the meaning of _truth_ in that the idea is the unity of concept and reality; henceforth, accordingly, that alone _is_ which is idea. Finite things are finite just because they do not carry the reality of their concept complete within their own selves but require others for it; – or, put the other way about, because they are presupposed as objects and so carry the concept in them as an external determination. External purposiveness is the utmost they reach along the side of this finitude. That actual things fail to be congruent with the idea is the side of their _finitude_, of their _untruth_, the side on which they are _objects_, each of them determined within its own several sphere and within the relations of objectivity, mechanically, chemically, or by an external purpose. That the idea has not worked its reality through to the end, has brought it only incompletely under the concept, has its possibility resting on this, that the idea itself carries a _limited_ _content_, that, essentially the unity of concept and reality as it is, it is quite as essentially the difference of the two; for the immediate unity, the unity, that is, which merely _in_ _itself_ is, belongs to the object alone. But suppose an object, the state for instance, were _not_ _at_ _all_ commensurate with its idea, which is to say were rather no idea of the state whatever, suppose the reality of it, namely the self-conscious individuals, were in no way answerable to the concept, then soul and body in it would have parted; the former would take flight into the sequestered regions of thought, the latter would have crumbled into the single individualities; but since the concept of the state so essentially makes up their nature, it works in them as a drive so mighty that they find themselves driven either to set it over into reality, if only under the form of an external purposiveness, or else to acquiesce in it as it stands, or they would have to founder. The {{{ 12.176 }}} poorest state, whose reality answers least to the concept, is, so long as it still exists, idea nonetheless; its individuals still obey a concept that has power over them.

The idea, however, carries not merely the more general sense of _genuine_ _being_, of the unity of _concept_ and _reality_, but the more determinate sense of _subjective_ _concept_ and _objectivity_. For the concept as such is of itself already the identity of itself and of _reality_; the indeterminate expression reality, after all, says nothing else at all than _determinate_ _being_; and the concept has this in its particularity and in its singularity. In the same way, further, _objectivity_ is the total _concept_, gone together out of its determinateness into _identity_ with itself. Within that subjectivity the determinateness, or the difference, of the concept is a _shine_, immediately sublated and gone back into being-for-itself, into the negative unity, an _inhering_ predicate. Within this objectivity, by contrast, the determinateness stands posited as immediate totality, as an external whole. The idea has now shown itself to be the concept set free once more, out of the immediacy in which it lies sunk within the object, into its subjectivity, a concept marking itself off from its objectivity, which objectivity is nonetheless quite as much determined by it and draws its substantiality from that concept alone. Rightly, then, has this identity been determined as the _subject-object_; namely that it is _just_ _as_ _fully_ the formal or subjective concept _as_ it is the object in its own right. This, though, calls for a more determinate grasp. The concept, once it has genuinely reached its reality, is that absolute judgment whose _subject_, being the negative unity that refers itself to itself, marks itself off from its objectivity and is the being-in-and-for-itself of that objectivity, yet refers itself to it essentially through its own self, – and therefore _is_ _self-purpose_ and _drive_; – objectivity, on the other hand, does not for that very reason carry the subject immediately in it, for on that footing the subject would be nothing but the object's own totality, lost within it; objectivity is rather the realization of the purpose, an objectivity _posited_ by the purpose's activity, one that as _positedness_ holds its subsistence and its form solely in being shot through by its subject. Being objectivity, it carries in it the moment of the concept's _externality_, and is on that account the side of finitude, of changeableness and of appearance generally, a side whose going-under lies in this, that it goes back into the negative unity of the concept; the negativity by which its indifferent asunderness shows itself up as unessential and as positedness is the concept itself. For all that objectivity, therefore, the idea is downright _simple_ and _immaterial_, since the externality counts only as determined by the concept and taken up into the concept's negative unity; to the extent that it subsists as indifferent externality it is not merely surrendered to mechanism at large, but is only as the perishable and the untrue. – Though the idea thus holds its reality in a {{{ 12.177 }}} materiature, that reality is no abstract _being_ subsisting for itself against the concept, but is only as _becoming_, by way of the negativity of indifferent being, as simple determinateness of the concept.

There issue from this the following closer determinations of the idea. – It is _first_ simple truth, namely the identity of concept with objectivity taken as a _universal_, one wherein the opposition and the subsistence of the particular have dissolved into the negativity identical with itself, and wherein it is as equality with its own self. _Secondly_, it is the _reference_ between the subjectivity of the simple concept, existing for itself, and the objectivity _distinguished_ from that subjectivity; the first of these is essentially the _drive_ to sublate this separation, the second the indifferent positedness, a subsistence null in and for itself. As this reference the idea is the _process_ of dirempting itself into individuality and into the inorganic nature belonging to that individuality, of drawing this nature back once more beneath the might of the subject, and of returning to the first simple universality. One with the _process_ is the _identity_ of the idea with its own self; the thought that frees actuality from the shine of purposeless changeableness and transfigures it into the _idea_ dare not picture this truth of the actual to itself as a dead stillness, as a bare _image_, lifeless, void of drive and of motion, as a genius, or a number, or an abstract thought; for the sake of the freedom the concept reaches within it, the idea harbours the _harshest_ _opposition_ too; the repose of the idea lies in the assurance and the certainty wherewith it eternally begets that opposition and eternally masters it, going together in it with its own self.

To begin with, though, the idea is once again at first merely _immediate_, or merely within its _concept_; objective reality does answer to the concept, yet it has not been freed into the concept, and the concept does not exist _for_ _itself_ _as_ _the_ _concept_. The concept is thus indeed _soul_, but soul after the manner of something _immediate_, that is, its determinateness is not present as the soul itself, it has not laid hold of itself as soul, does not carry its objective reality within its own self; the concept is as a soul not yet _soulful_.

Thus the idea is FIRST _life_; the concept that, marked off from its objectivity and simple within itself, saturates that objectivity, that as self-purpose finds its means in it and posits it for its means, while staying immanent in this means and being therein the realized purpose identical with itself. – Because of its immediacy this idea has _singularity_ for the form of its existence. The reflection of its absolute process into its own self, however, is the sublating of this immediate singularity; by it the concept, which within the idea is as universality the _inner_, turns the externality into universality, or posits its objectivity as equality with its own self. Thus the idea is

_secondly_ the idea of the _true_ and of the _good_, as _cognition_ and {{{ 12.178 }}} _willing_. To begin with it is finite cognition and finite willing, in which the true and the good still stand apart and both are as yet only as _goal_. The concept has at first set _itself_ free into its own self and has as yet furnished itself with an _abstract_ _objectivity_ alone for reality. The process of this finite cognizing and acting, though, turns the at first abstract universality into a totality, by which it comes to be _complete_ _objectivity_. – Or, looked at from the other side, the finite, that is, the subjective spirit _makes_ for itself the _presupposition_ of an objective world, as life _has_ such a presupposition; its activity, however, consists in sublating that presupposition and turning it into something posited. The objective world is accordingly its reality for it; or, the other way about, that world is the ideality wherein spirit cognizes its own self.

_Thirdly_, spirit cognizes the idea as _absolute_ _truth_ of its own, as truth such as is in and for itself; the infinite idea, wherein cognizing and doing have come into balance with each other, and which is _absolute_ _knowledge_ _of_ _its_ _own_ _self_.
