## 3. Opposition

It is in opposition that _determinate_ _reflection_, difference, has reached completion. Opposition constitutes the unity of identity and of diversity; within one identity its moments are diverse ones; as such they are _opposites_.

_Identity_ and _difference_ are those moments of difference that are kept inside difference itself; they count as _reflected_ moments of its unity. _Likeness_ and _unlikeness_, on the other hand, are reflection that has divested itself; their identity with self is not merely each one's indifference towards whatever stands distinguished from it, but indifference towards being-in-and-for-itself as such; an identity with self held against the one that is reflected into itself; hence what it amounts to is _immediacy_ lacking any reflection into itself. For the sides of external reflection, then, positedness amounts to a _being_, and their non-positedness to a _non-being_.

Considered more closely, the moments of opposition turn out to be positedness reflected into itself, that is, determination as such. Positedness is likeness and unlikeness; the two of them reflected into themselves make up the determinations of opposition. That they are reflected into themselves consists in this, that each of them, upon itself, is likeness and unlikeness in unity. Likeness has its being only in the reflection which compares with respect to unlikeness, and is thus mediated through the other moment indifferent to it; unlikeness likewise has its being only in that very reflecting relation in which likeness has its own. – Each of these moments is therefore, in its determinateness, the whole. It is the whole inasmuch as it also contains its other moment; but {{{ 11.273 }}} this other of it is something that indifferently _is_, so each contains the relation to its non-being and is only the reflection into itself, or the whole as relating itself essentially to its non-being.

_Likeness_ with self, reflected into itself in this way and carrying within it the relation to unlikeness, is the _positive_; and _unlikeness_, carrying within it the relation to likeness, which is its non-being, is the _negative_. – Or both are the _positedness_; now insofar as the differentiated determinateness is taken as the differentiated _determinate_ _relation_ of positedness _to_ _itself_, opposition is on the one hand the _positedness_ reflected into its _likeness_ _with_ _itself_; on the other hand the same positedness reflected into its unlikeness with itself; the _positive_ and the _negative_. – What the _positive_ is, is positedness reflected into likeness with itself; but the thing reflected is positedness, which is to say negation as negation, and hence this reflection into itself has as its determination the relation to the other. What the _negative_ is, is positedness reflected into unlikeness; but positedness is unlikeness itself, and so this reflection turns out to be the identity of unlikeness with its own self and absolute relation to itself. – Both, then: the positedness reflected into likeness with itself has unlikeness upon it, and the positedness reflected into unlikeness with itself has likeness upon it as well.

So the two sides of opposition, the positive and the negative, have become self-subsistent. Their self-subsistence lies in their being the _whole's_ reflection into itself, and their belonging to opposition lies in this, that opposition is the _determinateness_ which, as a whole, stands reflected into itself. It is for the sake of their self-subsistence that they make up the opposition determined _in_ _itself_. Each is itself and its other, and by this each has _its_ _determinateness_ not upon an other but _upon_ _its_ _own_ _self_. – Each relates itself to itself only as relating itself to its other. This has a twofold side; each is a relating to its non-being that sublates this otherness within itself; thus its non-being is only a moment within it. On the other part, though, positedness has here turned into a being, into indifferent subsistence; hence the other of each, the one that each contains, is likewise the non-being of the very term within which it ought to figure only as a moment. Each of them, therefore, is only insofar as its <i>non</i>-being _is_, and this within an identical relation.

The determinations that constitute the positive and the negative therefore amount to this: _first_, that both are absolute _moments_ of opposition; inseparably, their subsistence is one reflection; it is one mediation, and in it each of them is by way of the non-being of its other, thus by way of its other or of its own non-being. – In this way they are _opposed_ terms as such; or _each_ of them is nothing but the opposite of the other; neither is the one positive yet nor the other negative yet, but the two are negative towards one another. {{{ 11.274 }}} Each is thus, as such, _first_ _insofar_ _as_ _the_ _other_ _is_; through the other, through its own non-being, it is what it is; it is only _positedness_; _second_, it is _insofar_ _as_ _the_ _other_ _is_ _not_; through the non-being of the other it is what it is; it is _reflection_ _into_ _itself_. – Yet these two together make up the _one_ mediation of opposition as such, and within it they are, quite simply, only _posited_ _things_.

But _furthermore_ this bare positedness is, generally speaking, reflected into itself; measured by this moment _of_ _external_ _reflection_, positive and negative stand _indifferent_ over against that first identity in which they figure only as moments; or, since that first reflection is the very reflection of positive and negative into their own selves, each having its positedness upon itself, each stands indifferent to this reflection of its into its non-being, to its own positedness. So the two sides are merely diverse ones, and insofar as their determinateness of being positive and negative constitutes their positedness over against each other, neither is determined in that way upon itself but is only determinateness in general; one of the two determinatenesses, positive or negative, does accordingly fall to each side; but the two can be interchanged, and either side is such that it admits of being taken for positive quite as readily as for negative.

But _third_, the positive and the negative are not only something posited, nor merely something indifferent; rather their _positedness_, or the _relation_ _to_ _the_ _other_ _within_ _a_ _unity_ _which_ _they_ _themselves_ _are_ _not_, is _taken_ _back_ into each of them. Each is upon its own self positive and negative; the determination of reflection in and for itself is what positive and negative amount to; only within this reflection of what is opposed into itself is it positive and negative. The positive has upon its own self that relation to the other wherein its determinateness as positive consists; likewise the negative is not negative as against an other, but has the determinateness through which it is negative equally within its own self.

Thus each is a self-subsistent unity with itself, a unity that is for itself. The positive is admittedly a positedness, but in such a way that for it positedness is only positedness as something sublated. It is the _non-opposite_; opposition sublated, yet as a side of opposition itself. – As positive, something is indeed determined in relation to an otherness, but so that its nature is this, not to be something posited; it is the reflection into itself that negates otherness. But the other of it, the negative, is itself no longer positedness or moment, but a self-subsistent _being_; so the negating reflection of the positive into itself is determined to _shut_ _out_ from itself this its _non-being_.

So too the negative, taken as absolute reflection, is not the negative in immediate fashion but that same negative qua positedness sublated; the negative in and for itself, which {{{ 11.275 }}} rests positively upon its own self. As reflection into itself it negates its relation to the other; the other of it is the positive, a being that is self-subsistent; – its negative relation to that other consists, then, in shutting it out from itself. The negative is the opposite subsisting for itself, over against the positive, which is the determination of sublated opposition; the _whole_ _opposition_ that rests upon itself, set against positedness identical with itself.

The positive and the negative are hereby not only _in_ _themselves_ positive and negative but in and for themselves. _In_ _themselves_ they are so insofar as abstraction is made from their excluding relation to the other and they are taken solely with regard to their determination. A thing is _in_ _itself_ positive or negative in that it is not supposed to be so determined merely _against_ _something_ _else_. But the positive or negative not as positedness, and hence not as opposite, is each of them the immediate, _being_ and _non-being_. The positive and the negative are, however, the moments of opposition, and their being-in-itself makes up only the form of their being reflected into themselves. A thing is _in_ _itself_ positive outside the relation to the negative; and a thing is _in_ _itself_ _negative_ outside the relation to the negative; with such a determination what one holds on to is nothing but the abstract moment of this being-reflected. But the positive or negative that _is_ _in_ _itself_ means essentially that being opposed is not a mere moment, nor does it belong to comparison, but is the _own_ determination of the sides of opposition. _In_ _themselves_ positive or negative they are, then, not apart from the relation to the other, but in that _this_ _relation_, and indeed as an excluding one, makes up their determination or their being-in-itself; herein, then, they are so at once in and for themselves.
