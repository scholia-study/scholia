## b. The Absolutely Unconditioned

Each of the two relatively unconditioned shines, to begin with, into the other: the condition, being immediate, shines into the ground's form-reference, and this reference into immediate existence as the positedness of that reference; but apart from this shine of its other within it, each is self-subsistent in its own self and carries its own peculiar content.

First off, the _condition_ is immediate existence; two moments belong to its form — _positedness_, whereby existence, as condition, counts as material and as a moment of the ground; – and _being-in-itself_, whereby it makes up the ground's essentiality, the ground's simple reflection within itself. To immediate existence both sides {{{ 11.317 }}} of the form are outward; for that existence is the ground-reference sublated. – Yet, _first_, existence in its own self amounts to nothing but this: sublating itself in its immediacy and foundering. _Being_ at large is merely the _becoming_ towards essence; making itself into something posited, into the identity which through the negation of itself is what is immediate — that is being's essential nature. So the form-determinations of positedness and of self-identical being-in-itself, the form whereby immediate existence is condition, are not outward to existence at all; existence is rather this very reflection. _Secondly_, as condition, being is now posited also as that which it essentially is — to wit, as a moment, and so as belonging to an other, and equally as being-in-itself, again of an other; _in_ _itself_, though, being is such solely by the negation of itself, that is, by way of the ground and of the ground's reflection, which sublates itself and so presupposes; the being-in-itself of being is accordingly nothing but something posited. Two sides belong to this being-in-itself of the condition: to be, on the one hand, the condition's essentiality as the ground's, and on the other hand the immediacy of the condition's own existence. Or rather the two come to the same. Existence is an immediate, but immediacy is essentially what has been mediated — mediated, that is, by the ground that sublates itself. Being immediacy thus mediated by a mediating that sublates itself, it counts at once as the ground's being-in-itself and as the ground's unconditioned; only this being-in-itself is, once more, quite as much mere moment or positedness, for it stands mediated. – Hence the condition is the entire form of the ground-reference; it is that reference's presupposed being-in-itself, and just thereby a positedness itself, its immediacy consisting in making itself into positedness — in thrusting itself away from itself so that it founders and equally is ground, a ground making itself into positedness and thus into the grounded too; and both come to one and the same.

In the conditioned ground, similarly, being-in-itself is not present merely as the shining of an other within it. This ground is the self-subsistent reflection of positing, the reflection, that is, which refers to itself; it is thereby the self-identical, or has within its own self its being-in-itself and its content. At the same time, however, it is presupposing reflection; it refers negatively to itself, sets its being-in-itself over against itself as something other to it, and the condition — by its moment of being-in-itself quite as much as by that of immediate existence — is a moment belonging to the ground-reference itself; immediate existence is essentially only through its ground, and is a moment of the ground as presupposing. Hence the ground too is quite as much the whole itself.

Only _one_ whole of _form_, then, is at hand at all; and just as much only _one_ whole of _content_. For the condition's peculiar content {{{ 11.318 }}} counts as essential content only insofar as it is reflection's identity with itself within the form, or insofar as this immediate existence is in its own self the ground-reference. This existence, further, is condition solely by virtue of the ground's presupposing reflection; it constitutes the ground's identity with itself, or its content, over against which the ground sets itself. Existence is accordingly no mere formless material for the ground-reference; since it carries this form in its own self, it is formed matter, and, standing at once in identity with the form and indifferent towards it, it is content. It is, lastly, the very same content the ground has, for it is content precisely as the self-identical within the form-reference.

Condition and ground, the whole's two sides, are accordingly one essential unity — in respect of content as much as of form. Through themselves they pass over into each other; or, being reflections, each posits itself as sublated and refers itself to that negation of its own, so that the two _presuppose_ _each_ _other_. Yet this is at once only a single reflection of the two, their presupposing therefore likewise only one; the mutuality of it passes over rather into their presupposing their one identity as their subsistence and their foundation. This, the one content and unity of form belonging to both, is the _genuinely_ _unconditioned_; _the_ _fact_ _in_ _its_ _own_ _self_. – The condition, as emerged above, is the relatively unconditioned only. One is therefore wont to take it as itself a conditioned and to enquire after a fresh condition, and with that the customary _progress_ _into_ _infinity_ from condition to condition is under way. Now why, given a condition, is a fresh condition enquired after — why, that is, is the condition assumed to be a conditioned? Because it is some finite existence or other. But that is a further determination of the condition, not one lying within its concept. The condition as such, rather, is a conditioned because it is the posited being-in-itself; within the absolutely unconditioned it is therefore sublated.

This last now holds the two sides, condition and ground, within itself as its moments; it is the unity into which they have withdrawn. Taken together the two make up its form, its positedness. Condition of both is the unconditioned fact — the absolute condition, however, that is, the condition which is itself ground. – Qua _ground_ the fact is now that negative identity which has thrust itself apart into those two moments; – _firstly_ into the shape of the ground-reference sublated, of an immediate manifold lacking unity and outward to itself, a manifold that refers itself to the ground as to something other than it and at once makes up the ground's being-in-itself; _secondly_ into the shape of an inward and simple form, which is ground, though it refers itself to that immediate, self-identical as it is, as to an other, and determines it as condition — determines, that is, this its in-itself as a moment of its own. – The totality is _presupposed_ by these two sides in such a way that the totality is what posits them. Conversely, since they _presuppose_ the totality {{{ 11.319 }}}, the latter seems in its turn conditioned by them, and the fact seems to arise out of its condition and out of its ground. But these two sides having shown themselves to be the identical, the relation of condition and ground has vanished; both are lowered to a mere _shine_; and the absolutely unconditioned, in its movement of positing and of presupposing, is nothing other than the movement wherein this _shine_ sublates itself. It is the fact's own doing to condition itself and to set itself as ground over against its conditions; the fact's reference as the conditions and as the ground is, however, a shining _within_ _itself_, and its comportment towards them is _its_ _going_ _together_ _with_ _itself_.
