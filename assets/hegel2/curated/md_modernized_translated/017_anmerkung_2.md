## Remark 2

Yet a further ground can be adduced, one that abets the repugnance felt towards the proposition concerning being and nothing; this ground is that the result issuing from the consideration of being and of nothing finds only imperfect expression in the proposition: _being_ _and_ _nothing_ _is_ _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_. The stress falls by preference on the _being_ _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_, as happens in judgment as such, where the predicate is what first states what the subject _is_. So the sense appears to be a denial of the difference, a difference which all the same crops up immediately in the proposition itself; for the proposition gives voice to _both_ determinations, being and nothing, and holds them in it as differentiated. – Nor can it be the intention that one abstract from them and hold fast to the unity alone. Such a sense would betray its own one-sidedness, seeing that what is to be abstracted from stands there in the proposition none the less, and is named. – Now to the extent that the proposition: _being_ _and_ _nothing_ _is_ _the_ _same_, gives voice to the identity of these determinations while in fact no less holding the two of them in it as differentiated, it stands in contradiction with itself and dissolves. Fix this a little more closely and what has been posited here is a proposition which, looked at more closely, carries the movement of vanishing by its own doing. But with that, there takes place in the proposition itself precisely what is supposed to make up its genuine content, namely _becoming_.

So the result is what the proposition _contains_; the proposition is that result _in_ _itself_. The point {{{ 21.78 }}} to be brought to notice here, though, is a deficiency: the proposition does not itself _express_ the result; it takes an external reflection to recognize it there. – On this head a general observation has to be made straight away at the outset: a proposition in the _form_ _of_ _a_ _judgment_ is no fit vehicle for speculative truths; acquaintance with this circumstance would go far towards clearing away many a misunderstanding of speculative truths. A judgment is an _identical_ reference of subject and predicate; one abstracts in it both from the subject's carrying yet more determinatenesses than the predicate's, and from the predicate's reaching further than the subject. Where the content is speculative, however, what is _non-identical_ in subject and predicate counts no less as an essential moment, and just this the judgment leaves unsaid. Much in more recent philosophy wears a paradoxical and bizarre look for anyone unacquainted with speculative thinking, and that look is in great part to be charged to the form of the simple judgment, whenever that form is pressed into service for expressing speculative results.

With a view to expressing the speculative truth, the deficiency is at first made good by appending the counter-proposition, namely: _being_ _and_ _nothing_ _is_ _not_ _the_ _same_, which was equally enunciated above. Only, a fresh deficiency then arises: the two propositions stand unconnected, so that they exhibit the content merely as antinomy, whereas their content does bear on one and the same thing, and the determinations which the pair of them express are meant to be united outright – a uniting which can then be enunciated only as an _unrest_ of factors at once _incompatible_, as _a_ _movement_. To make it one-sided is the most usual wrong inflicted on speculative content, that is, to lift out only the one of the propositions into which such content admits of resolution. That this proposition does get asserted cannot then be denied; yet so _correct_ _as_ _the_ _report_ _is_, _so_ _false_ _is_ _it_, for once a single proposition has been taken from the speculative, the other would have at the very least an equal claim to be heeded and reported. – Here too the, so to speak, unfortunate word: _unity_ calls for particular mention; _unity_ marks a subjective reflection even more than _identity_ does; it is taken above all for the reference that issues from _comparison_, from reflection carried on externally. To the extent that such reflection lights on the same in two _diverse_ _objects_, a unity is at hand in such fashion that {{{ 21.79 }}} the objects being compared are presupposed to stand in complete _indifference_ towards this unity of theirs, so that the comparing and the unity are nothing to the objects themselves, and are a doing and determining external to them. Hence what unity expresses is a wholly _abstract_ selfsameness, and it rings the harsher and the more startling the more outright the difference shown by the terms of which it is enunciated. In place of unity it would to that extent be better to speak only of _unseparatedness_ and _inseparability_; only, the _affirmative_ side of the whole reference goes unexpressed in that.

The entire, true result which has emerged here is accordingly _becoming_, a becoming that is not just the unity, one-sided and abstract, of being with nothing. What it consists in, rather, is a movement of this sort: that pure being is immediate and simple, that on that very account it is quite as much pure nothing, that the difference of the two _is_ and yet quite as much _sublates_ _itself_ and _is_ _not_. Quite as much, then, does the result assert the difference of being and nothing, though only as a difference _supposed_.

One _supposes_ that being is rather the outright other of what nothing is, that nothing is plainer than their absolute difference, and that nothing could look easier than being able to state it. Just as easily, though, may one become convinced that this cannot be done, that the difference is _unsayable_. _Those_ _who_ _would_ _insist_ _on_ _the_ _difference_ _of_ _being_ _and_ _nothing_ _may_ _challenge_ _themselves_ _to_ _state_ _wherein_ _it_ _consists_. Were there any determinateness in being and nothing through which the two were distinguished, then, as has been recalled, what one would have would be a determinate being and a determinate nothing, not being in its purity and nothing in its purity, which is what they still are at this stage. Their difference is therefore quite empty, the indeterminate is what each of the two equally is; the difference accordingly rests not in them themselves but only in a third, in _supposing_. But supposing is a form belonging to the subjective, which has no place in this order of the exposition. The third, though, wherein being and nothing have their subsistence, must make its appearance here as well; and it has appeared, it is _becoming_. Within it they are as differentiated; becoming has being only so far as they are differentiated. This third is something other than they; – their subsisting only in an other says equally that for themselves they have no subsistence. Becoming is the subsistence of being quite as much as of non-being; or rather their subsistence is nothing but their being in _One_; and it is just this subsistence of theirs which quite as much sublates their difference.

The challenge to state the difference of being and nothing carries within it {{{ 21.80 }}} the further challenge to say what _then_ _being_ and _what_ _nothing_ _is_. Those who balk at recognizing each of the two only as a _passing_ _over_ into the other, and who assert this or that of being and of nothing, may indicate _what_ they speak of, in other words, may put forward _a_ _definition_ of being and of nothing, and demonstrate its correctness. Until that first requirement of the older science has been met, a science whose logical rules they otherwise allow to hold and do apply, all those assertions about being and nothing are mere assurances, nullities in point of science. If it has elsewhere been said that concrete existence, taken at first as equivalent in meaning to being, is the _completion_ of _possibility_, then a further determination, possibility, is thereby presupposed, and being is enunciated not in its immediacy but rather as something not self-subsistent, as conditioned. For being that is _mediated_ we shall keep in reserve the expression: _concrete_ _existence_. Being, though, one does no doubt _represent_ to oneself – under the image, say, of light in its purity, as the brightness of a seeing undimmed, and nothing as sheer night, and one hangs their difference on this familiar sensuous diversity. In fact, however, let this seeing be represented rather more precisely and one may easily grow aware that absolute brightness lets one see exactly as much and exactly as little as absolute darkness, that either seeing is as good as the other, a pure seeing, a seeing of nothing. Light in its purity and darkness in its purity are two vacuities, and they are one and the same. Only in light that is determinate – and light gets its determination from darkness, – hence in dimmed light, and likewise only in darkness that is determinate, – and darkness gets its determination from light, – in illumined darkness, can anything be distinguished, since dimmed light and illumined darkness are the first to have the difference in them themselves, and thereby to be determinate being, _existence_.
