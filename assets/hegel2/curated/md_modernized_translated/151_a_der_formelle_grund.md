## a. Formal Ground

A determinate content belongs to ground. That determinateness of content is, as has come to light, the _groundwork_ for form — the simple _immediate_ over against _the_ _mediation_ of form. Ground is identity referring to itself negatively, and by so doing it makes itself into _positedness_; it refers negatively to _itself_ in that within this negativity of its it is identical with itself; this identity is the groundwork, or the content, which in this manner makes up the indifferent, positive unity of the ground-reference and is what _mediates_ that reference.

Within this content the determinateness of ground and of grounded has at first vanished, the one against the other. The mediation, however, is further a _negative_ unity. The negative, as attaching to that indifferent groundwork, is the {{{ 11.303 }}} _immediate_ _determinateness_ of the groundwork, whereby ground comes to have a determinate content. But then the negative is also form's negative reference to its own self. On one side what is posited sublates itself and withdraws into its ground; but ground, the essential self-subsistence, refers negatively to its own self and makes itself into what is posited. This negative mediation of ground and grounded is the mediation peculiar to form as such, _the_ _formal_ _mediation_. Now since the one of form's two sides passes over into the other, the two thereby posit themselves jointly as sublated _in_ _one_ _identity_; and thereby they at the same time _presuppose_ that identity. That identity is the determinate content, and it is upon this, as upon what mediates positively by way of itself, that the formal mediation accordingly bears. It is what the two have identical in them, and since they are distinguished though each in its difference is reference to the other, in it they subsist, _each_ _one_ subsisting as _the_ _whole_ itself.

From this it follows that in determinate ground the following is at hand: _first_, a determinate _content_ comes under consideration from _two_ _sides_, on the one occasion so far as it is posited as _ground_, on the other so far as it is posited as _grounded_. The content itself is indifferent towards this form; in both it is at large only one determination. _Secondly_, ground is itself no less a moment of form than is what ground posits; this is their _identity_ _as_ _regards_ _form_. It is a matter of indifference which of the two determinations gets made into the first: the transition may run from what counts as posited over to the other as its ground, or from what counts as ground over to the other as posited. Grounded, considered on its own, is the sublating of its own self; by that it makes itself on one side into something posited, and is at the same time a positing of ground. Ground as such is this very same movement: making itself posited, it becomes thereby ground of something — that is, therein it is present both as posited and, for the first time, as ground. That a ground is at all — of this the posited is the ground, and conversely ground is thereby something posited. The mediation begins from the one as readily as from the other; each side is ground as much as posited, and each is the whole mediation, the whole form. – This whole form, further, being itself the self-identical, is the _groundwork_ of those determinations which constitute the two sides, ground and grounded; so form and content are themselves one identity, the selfsame one.

On account of this identity of ground and grounded, in respect of content as well as of form, ground is _sufficient_ (sufficiency confined to this relation); _nothing_ _is_ _in_ _the_ _ground_ that is not in the _grounded_, _just_ _as_ _nothing_ _is_ _in_ _the_ _grounded_ _that_ _is_ _not_ _in_ _the_ _ground_. When a ground is asked after, what one wants is to see the _same_ determination that is the _content_ _twice_ over: on the one occasion in the form of what is posited, on the other in the form of existence reflected into itself, of essentiality.

{{{ 11.304 }}} Now inasmuch as in determinate ground both ground and grounded are the entire form, while the content they have, determinate though it be, remains one and the same, ground is not yet really determined on its two sides; no diverse content belongs to them; determinateness is still simple, not yet gone over into the sides; determinate ground is present only in its pure form, that is, as _formal_ _ground_. – Since the content amounts to no more than this simple determinateness, which does not bear upon itself the form of the ground-reference, it is a content identical with itself, indifferent towards form, form being external to it; an other than form is what content is.
