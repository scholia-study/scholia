## {{{ 21.117 }}} α. The Immediacy of Finitude

Such mourning is carried along by the thought of the finitude of things, since finitude is qualitative negation driven to its sharpest point, and within the simplicity of a determination like that no affirmative being _distinct_ from their determination to perish _is_ any longer allowed them. It is because of this qualitative simplicity of the negation – a negation fallen back into the abstract opposition, over against being, of nothing and of ceasing-to-be – that finitude counts as the understanding's most obstinate category; negation as such, constitution, limit, each of these tolerates its other, existence; even abstract nothing, taken on its own, is surrendered as a mere abstraction; finitude, though, is the negation _fixed_ _in_ _itself_, and for that reason it confronts its affirmative with unrelieved harshness. Set flowing the finite may well be, for it is itself just this, to be determined towards its end, though towards its end alone; – what it amounts to is rather a refusal to be carried over affirmatively to its affirmative, to the infinite, a refusal to be bound up with it; posited it is, then, as inseparable from its own nothing, and any reconciliation with its other, the affirmative, is thereby severed. Finite things have no determination further than their _end_. In this mourning of finitude the understanding digs itself in, making non-being the determination of things while making it at once _imperishable_ and _absolute_. Only in their other, in the affirmative, could their perishableness cease to be; their finitude would in that case detach itself from them; but finitude is a quality of theirs that cannot alter, one which never crosses over into its other, never into its affirmative, and _so_ _it_ _is_ _eternal_.

The consideration is a very important one; that the finite be absolute – no philosophy, no view, no understanding will of course consent to have such a standpoint fastened upon it; quite the reverse stands expressly in the assertion made of the finite; limited and perishable is what the finite is; the finite is _only_ finite, never the imperishable; this lies immediately in its determination and in its expression. Everything turns, though, on whether the view holds fast _to_ _the_ _being_ _of_ _finitude_, whether _perishableness_ endures, or whether _perishableness_ and _ceasing-to-be_ do themselves _cease to be_? That precisely this does not happen is the fact of the matter in that very view of the finite for which _ceasing-to-be_ is made the _last_ thing of the finite. Expressly asserted is this, that finite and infinite do not tolerate each other and cannot be reconciled, that the finite stands utterly opposed to the infinite. Being, absolute being, is what gets ascribed to the infinite; facing it, the finite is accordingly held fast as the infinite's negative; unable to be reconciled with the infinite, the finite stays absolutely on its {{{ 21.118 }}} own side; affirmation would come to it from the affirmative, from the infinite, and it would thereby cease to be; only, a unification with the infinite is precisely what gets pronounced impossible. Should the finite not endure over against the infinite but cease to be instead, then, as already remarked, what is last is just this ceasing-to-be of the finite, and not the affirmative, which could only be the ceasing-to-be of ceasing-to-be. Suppose, on the other hand, that the finite were not to cease to be in the affirmative, and that its end were grasped as the _nothing_: then we should find ourselves back at that first abstract nothing which has itself long since ceased to be.

Now with a nothing of this kind, one that is supposed to be _only_ nothing and to which a concrete existence is at the same time granted, in thinking, in representing, or in speaking, there arises the very contradiction just indicated in the case of the finite, except that there it merely _arises_, whereas in finitude it is _express_. In the former case the contradiction looks subjective; in the latter the claim is made that _perennially_ the finite _stands_ over against the infinite, that what is null in itself _is_, and that it is _as_ something null in itself. This is what has to be brought to consciousness; and the development of the finite shows that in it, as this contradiction, the finite collapses inwardly, yet genuinely resolves the contradiction in this direction: not that the finite is merely perishable and ceases to be, but that ceasing-to-be, the nothing, is not what is last but itself ceases to be.
