## {{{ 12.93 }}} a. First Figure of the Syllogism

S–P–U is the universal schema of the determinate syllogism. By way of particularity singularity closes with universality into one; not immediately is the singular universal, but through particularity; and inversely the universal is not immediately singular either, but lets itself down to singularity by way of particularity. – These determinations face one another as _extremes_, and in a _diverse_ third they are one. Determinateness is what both of them are, and in that they are _identical_; _particularity_ is this universal determinateness of theirs. Yet against this particularity, no less than against each other, they are _extremes_, since each holds to a determinateness of its own that is _immediate_.

What this syllogism means in general is that the singular, which as such is infinite reference to itself and would on that account be merely something _inward_, comes forth through particularity into _existence_, as into universality, where it belongs no longer to itself alone but stands in _outer_ _connection_; inversely, once the singular parts itself off into its determinateness as particularity, it is in that severance a concrete, and, as reference of determinateness to itself, a _universal_ that refers to itself, and hence a truly singular as well; within the extreme of universality it has withdrawn out of _externality_ _into_ _itself_. – Only _superficially_ is the objective meaning of the syllogism present in this first one, for its determinations are not yet posited as that unity which makes up the essence of the syllogism. So far it is still something subjective, seeing that the abstract meaning carried by its Termini stands isolated in this way not in and for itself but only within subjective consciousness. – For the rest, how singularity, particularity and universality are related is, as has come out, the _necessary_ _and_ _essential_ _form-relation_ borne by the determinations of the syllogism; the deficiency is not to be sought in this determinateness of form, but in this, that _under_ _this_ _form_ no single determination is at the same time _richer_. – _Aristotle_ attached himself rather to the bare _relation_ _of_ _inherence_, since he states the nature of the syllogism thus: _when_ _three_ _determinations_ _stand_ _so_ _related_ _to_ _one_ _another_ _that_ _the_ _one_ _extreme_ _is_ _in_ _the_ _whole_ _of_ _the_ _middle_ _determination_, _and_ _this_ _middle_ _determination_ _in_ _the_ _whole_ _of_ _the_ _other_ _extreme_, _then_ _these_ _two_ _extremes_ _are_ _of_ _necessity_ _joined_ _together_. What gets expressed here is rather the repeating of one and the _same_ _relation_ of inherence, first of the one extreme in the middle term, then of that middle term in the other extreme, than the determinateness which the three Terminorum have toward one another. – Since the syllogism now rests on the determinateness of these just indicated over against each other, it {{{ 12.94 }}} becomes plain straightaway that other relations of the Terminorum, those which the other figures supply, can count as valid syllogisms of the understanding only so far as they admit of being _traced back_ to that original relation; they are not _diverse_ _species_ of figure ranged _alongside_ _the_ _first_, but, on the one hand, so far as they are meant to be correct syllogisms, what they rest on is nothing but the essential form of the syllogism as such, and that form is the first figure; on the other hand, so far as they depart from it, they are reshapings, and that first abstract form passes over into them of necessity, determining itself thereby further and up to totality. Just how matters stand with this will emerge more closely in a moment.

S–P–U, then, is the syllogism's universal schema in its determinateness. Under the particular the singular is subsumed, and the particular under the universal; hence the universal subsumes the singular too. Or again, in the singular the particular inheres, and in the particular the universal; _hence_ the universal inheres in the singular as well. Looked at from the one side, namely toward the universal, the particular is subject; toward the singular it is predicate; or toward the former it counts as singular, toward the latter as universal. Since both determinatenesses are united within it, the extremes stand joined together by this unity of theirs. The _hence_ shows up as a consequence drawn within the _subject_, one that is supposed to follow from _subjective_ insight into how the two _immediate_ premises are related. When subjective reflection pronounces the middle term's two references to the extremes as particular _judgments_ or _propositions_, and immediate ones at that, then the conclusion too, being the _mediated_ reference, is admittedly a particular _proposition_, and the _hence_ or _therefore_ voices its being the mediated one. This _hence_, however, must not be taken for a determination lying outside this proposition, one whose ground and seat would be nothing but subjective reflection; it is rather grounded in the very nature of the extremes, whose _reference_ only for the purposes of abstracting reflection, and through it, gets pronounced once again as a _mere_ _judgment_ _or_ _proposition_, while their _true_ _reference_ stands posited as the Terminus Medius. – _Therefore_ _S_ _is_ _U_: that this should be a _judgment_ is a merely subjective circumstance; the syllogism consists precisely in its not being a mere _judgment_, that is, not a reference brought about by the _bare_ _copula_ or by the empty _is_, but one brought about by the determinate middle term filled with content.

If the syllogism is on that account looked at as consisting merely _of_ _three_ _judgments_, this is a formal view, one that says nothing of the relation among the determinations, on which alone everything in the syllogism turns. Throughout, it is a merely subjective reflection that tears the reference of the Terminorum apart into separated premises and a conclusion diverse from them: {{{ 12.95 }}} All human beings are mortal,

Cajus is a human being

Therefore he is mortal.

Boredom overtakes one the moment such a syllogism is heard trotted out; – this comes of that useless form, which through the separated propositions lends a shine of diversity, one that dissolves straightaway in the fact itself. Chiefly through this subjective shaping, syllogizing looks like a subjective _makeshift_ to which reason or the understanding takes refuge wherever it cannot cognize _immediately_. – The nature of things, the rational, certainly does not go about its work by first setting up a major premise, the reference of a particularity to a subsisting universal, then finding, secondly, a separated reference of a singularity to that particularity, out of which at last, thirdly, a new proposition would come to light. – Syllogizing that advances by way of separated propositions is a subjective form and nothing besides; what the fact is, is that the distinguished conceptual determinations of the fact stand united in the essential unity. Such rationality is no makeshift; against the _immediacy_ of the reference that still obtains in the _judgment_ it is rather the _objective_, and that immediacy of cognizing is rather the merely subjective, while the syllogism is the truth of the judgment. – All things are the _syllogism_, a universal that through particularity stands joined together with singularity; but wholes consisting of _three_ _propositions_ they are of course not.

2. In the _immediate_ syllogism of the understanding the Termini carry the form of _immediate_ _determinations_; it is from this side, the side on which they are _content_, that it must now be considered. It can to that extent [be] taken as the _qualitative_ syllogism, in the way the judgment of existence exhibits this same side of qualitative determination. Like the Termini of that judgment, the Termini of this syllogism are thereby _singular_ determinatenesses, determinateness being posited, by its reference to itself, as indifferent toward the _form_ and so as content. The _singular_ is some immediate concrete object or other; _particularity_ is a single one among its determinatenesses, properties or relations; _universality_ is once more a determinateness at the particular that is more abstract and more singular still. – Because the subject, as something _immediately_ determined, is not yet posited in its concept, its concretion has not been led back to the essential conceptual determinations; the determinateness that refers to itself is on that account indeterminate, infinite _manifoldness_. Within this immediacy the singular carries an infinite multitude of determinatenesses belonging to its {{{ 12.96 }}} particularity, and any one of them may accordingly serve it as a Medius Terminus in a syllogism. Through _each_ _other_ Medius Terminus, however, it joins itself together _with_ _another_ _universal_; by each of its properties it stands in a different contact and connection of existence. – Then too the Medius Terminus is itself a concrete when compared with the universal; several predicates are contained in it, and by one and the same Medius Terminus the singular can be joined together again with several universals. Which among a thing's many properties gets seized upon, and from which of them it is bound up with a predicate, is on the whole _wholly_ _contingent_ _and_ _arbitrary_; other Medii Termini are the passages to other predicates, and even the same Medius Terminus may of itself be a passage to diverse predicates, containing as it does, qua particular over against the universal, several determinations.

Not merely, however, is an indeterminate multitude of syllogisms equally possible for one subject, with any single syllogism _contingent_ so far as its content goes; these syllogisms, all bearing on the same subject, must also pass over into _contradiction_. Difference in general, after all, which at first is indifferent _diversity_, is quite as essentially _opposition_. The concrete is no longer something merely appearing; within the concept it is concrete through the unity of the opposed terms, which have determined themselves into moments of the concept. Now when, in the formal syllogism, the qualitative nature of the Terminorum leads the concrete to be seized upon by way of a single one of the determinations that belong to it, the syllogism assigns to it the predicate answering to this Medius Terminus; but when from another side the inference runs to the opposed determinateness, that conclusion is thereby shown up as false, however correct its premises taken singly, and its consequence along with them, may be. – Infer from the Medius Terminus that a wall has been given a coat of blue that the wall is thereby blue, and the inference is correct; yet for all this syllogism the wall may be green, should it have been coated with yellow paint besides, a circumstance from which it would follow on its own account that the wall is yellow. – Infer from the Medius Terminus of sensuousness that the human being is neither good nor evil, on the ground that of the sensuous neither may be predicated, and the syllogism is correct while the conclusion is false, since of the human being as the concrete the Medius Terminus of spirituality holds quite as much. – From the Medius Terminus of the gravity of planets, satellites and comets toward the sun it correctly follows that these bodies fall into it; yet fall into it they do not, being just as much, each for itself, a centre of gravity of their own, or, as the phrase goes, driven by centrifugal force. Just so, from the Medius Terminus of sociality one may conclude to community of goods among the citizens, {{{ 12.97 }}} while from the Medius Terminus of individuality, pursued just as abstractly, there follows the dissolution of the state, such as came about for instance in the German Empire, where this latter Medius Terminus was clung to. – Nothing is with better right counted so inadequate as a formal syllogism of this sort, resting as it does on chance, or on the arbitrary choice of which Medius Terminus is used. Let such a deduction have wound its way through syllogisms ever so handsomely, and let its correctness be granted entirely, this still leads to nothing whatever, since it always remains that further Medii Termini are to be found from which the very reverse may be derived with equal correctness. – The Kantian _antinomies_ of reason amount to nothing else than that out of one concept the one determination of it is laid at the base at one time, and at another, with just the same necessity, the other. – This inadequacy and contingency of a syllogism must not, in that connection, be pushed off onto the content alone, as if it were independent of the form and the form alone were logic's business. It lies rather in the form of the formal syllogism that the content should be a quality so one-sided; to this one-sidedness the content is determined by that _abstract_ form. It is, namely, one single quality among the many qualities or determinations of a concrete object or of a concept, since _according_ _to_ _the_ _form_ nothing more is asked of it than to be such an immediate, single determinateness. The extreme of singularity, as _abstract_ _singularity_, is the _immediate_ concrete, hence the infinitely or indeterminably manifold; the middle term is particularity that is _abstract_ in like measure, hence _one_ _single_ quality out of that manifold; and just so the other extreme is the _abstract_ _universal_. For the sake of its form, then, the formal syllogism is essentially something wholly contingent so far as its content goes; not in the sense that it should be contingent for the syllogism whether _this_ or _another_ object is put under it, for logic abstracts from such content, but in the sense that, a subject being laid at the base, what determinations of content the syllogism infers of it is contingent.

3. On this side the determinations of the syllogism are determinations of content, so far as they are immediate, abstract determinations reflected into themselves. What is essential to them, however, is rather that they should not be such self-reflected determinations, indifferent toward one another, but _form-determinations_; to that extent they are essentially _references_. These references are, _first_, those of the extremes to the middle term, – references which are _immediate_: the propositiones praemissae, in part that of the particular to the universal, propositio major, in part that of the singular to the particular, propositio minor. {{{ 12.98 }}} _Secondly_, there is at hand the reference of the extremes to each other, the one that is the _mediated_ reference, conclusio. The premises, those _immediate_ references, are propositions or judgments as such, and they _contradict_ _the_ _syllogism's_ _nature_, by which the distinguished conceptual determinations are not to be referred immediately, their unity being what is to be posited just as much; the syllogism is the truth of the judgment. All the less can the premises remain immediate references, in that their content is made of immediately _distinguished_ determinations, so that they are not immediately identical in and for themselves; unless they be purely identical propositions, that is, empty tautologies that lead nowhere.

The demand laid on the premises accordingly runs, as a rule, that they are to be _proved_, that is, _that_ _they_ _too_ _are_ _to_ _be_ _set_ _out_ _as_ _conclusions_. So the two premises hand us two further syllogisms. But these _two_ new syllogisms again furnish _four_ premises between them, calling for _four_ new syllogisms; those have _eight_ premises, whose _eight_ syllogisms in turn furnish _sixteen_ syllogisms for their _sixteen_ premises, and _so on_ in a geometrical progression _into_ _infinity_.

So here _the_ _progress_ _into_ _infinity_ comes forward once more, which had turned up earlier in the lower _sphere_ _of_ _being_, and which in the field of the concept, of absolute reflection out of the finite into itself, in the region of free infinity and truth, was no longer to be looked for. In the sphere of being it was shown that wherever the bad infinity that runs out into the progress comes forward, there is at hand the contradiction of a _qualitative_ _being_ and of a _powerless_ _ought_ reaching out beyond it; the progress is itself the repeating of the demand for unity raised against the qualitative, and of the constant relapse into a restriction that does not answer to the demand. Now in the formal syllogism the _immediate_ reference, or the qualitative judgment, is the groundwork, and the _mediation_ of the syllogism is what gets set against it as the higher truth. Proving the premises on into infinity does not resolve that contradiction; it only renews it again and again, repeating one and the same original defect. – Rather, the truth of the infinite progress lies in this, that the progress itself, together with the form it has already marked out as defective, gets sublated. – That form is the form of mediation as S–P–U. The two references S–P and P–U are supposed to be mediated ones; if this is done in the same fashion, the defective form S–P–U is merely doubled, and so on into infinity. P carries toward S the form-determination of a _universal_ as well, and toward U that of a _singular_, these references being judgments throughout. Mediation is therefore what they require, yet through that shape of mediation only the relation which is to be sublated comes on the scene again.

{{{ 12.99 }}} The mediation must accordingly come about in another fashion. For mediating P–U, S is at hand; it is therefore the shape

P–S–U

that the mediation must receive. To mediate S–P, U is at hand; this mediation therefore becomes the syllogism:

S–U–P.

Taking this transition more closely in terms of its concept: _firstly_, the mediation of the formal syllogism is, as regards its _content_, _contingent_, as was shown just now. In its determinatenesses the immediate _singular_ has an indeterminable multitude of Mediis Terminis, and these again have just as many determinatenesses of their own; so that with what sort of universal the subject of the syllogism is to be joined together lies wholly in an external _arbitrariness_, or generally in an _external_ _circumstance_ and contingent determination. As to content, then, the mediation is nothing necessary, nor anything universal; it has no ground in the _concept_ _of_ _the_ _fact_; the _ground_ of the syllogism is rather what is external to it, that is, the _immediate_; and among the conceptual determinations the immediate is the _singular_.

In respect _of_ _the_ _form_, likewise, the _mediation_ has for its _presupposition_ _the_ _immediacy_ _of_ _the_ _reference_; the mediation is therefore itself mediated, and mediated by the _immediate_, that is, by the _singular_. – More closely, it is through the _conclusion_ of the first syllogism that the singular has become what mediates. The conclusion is S–U; by it the _singular_ stands posited as _universal_. In the one premise, the minor premise S–P, it already stands as _particular_; it is thus posited as that in which these two determinations are united. – Or the conclusion in and for itself gives voice to the singular as universal, and this not in an immediate way but through the mediation, hence as a necessary reference. The _simple_ particularity was Medius Terminus; in the conclusion this particularity comes _developed_, _posited_ _as_ _the_ _reference_ _of_ _the_ _singular_ _and_ _universality_. But the universal is still a qualitative determinateness, the _singular's_ predicate; determined as universal, the singular gets _posited_ as universality of the extremes, that is, as middle term; taken for itself it is the extreme of singularity, yet since it now stands determined as universal it is at once the unity of both extremes.
