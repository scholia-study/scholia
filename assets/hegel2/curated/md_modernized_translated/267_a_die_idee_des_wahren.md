## A. The Idea of the True

To begin with, the subjective idea is _drive_. For it is that contradiction in the concept whereby the concept has its own self for _subject_ _matter_ and is its own reality, and yet the subject matter is no _other_, nothing self-subsistent as against it, nor does the concept's difference from its own self carry at the same time the essential determination {{{ 12.200 }}} of _diversity_ and of indifferent existence. The drive accordingly has the determinateness of sublating its own subjectivity, of turning its at first abstract reality into a concrete one, and of filling this reality with the _content_ that the world which its subjectivity presupposes affords. – Regarded from the other side, the drive determines itself hereby thus: the concept is indeed the absolute certainty of its own self; confronting its _being-for-itself_, however, is the presupposition it makes of a world that is _in_ _itself_, a world whose indifferent _otherness_ has for that certainty of itself the worth only of something _unessential_; the concept is so far the drive to sublate this otherness and to intuit within the object identity with its own self. Insofar as this reflection-into-itself amounts to the opposition sublated and to the _posited_ _singularity_ that has been effected for the subject, one which shows itself at first as the presupposed _being-in-itself_, it is form's identity with its own self as brought forth out of the opposition, – an identity determined thereby as indifferent towards form in form's differentiatedness, and hence as _content_.

Accordingly this drive is the drive towards _truth_, so far as truth lies in _cognition_, and hence towards _truth_ as _theoretical_ idea in the term's strict sense. – Granted that _objective_ truth is the idea itself, namely the reality that answers to the concept, so that a subject matter may or may not possess truth in it, the more determinate sense of truth is by contrast this: that truth be such _for_ the subjective concept or _in_ it, in _knowledge_. Truth is the relation belonging to the _judgment_ _of_ _the_ _concept_, which showed itself to be the formal judgment of truth; for there the predicate is not the objectivity of the concept merely, but a comparison that holds together the concept of the fact with that fact's actuality. – This realization of the concept is _theoretical_ so far as the concept, being _form_, still carries the determination of something _subjective_, that is, the determination of belonging to the subject as the subject's own. Because cognition is the idea as purpose or as something subjective, the _first_ negation is that of the world which has been presupposed as _being_ _in_ _itself_; hence the conclusion, one that puts the objective into the subjective, likewise carries at first only the meaning that whatever is in itself is only as something subjective, or is _posited_ merely within the determination of the concept, and just for that reason is not so in and for itself. So far the conclusion attains no more than a _neutral_ unity, a _synthesis_, that is to say, the unity of terms which, separated in their origin, are joined only from without. – Since the concept, in the cognition here at issue, posits the object as _its_ _own_, what the idea gives itself at first is merely a content whose groundwork is _given_ and upon which nothing has been sublated except the form of externality. So far, in the purpose it has carried out, this cognition keeps its _finitude_ still; within that very purpose it has at the same time _not_ reached the purpose, and _in_ _its_ _truth_ it has _not_ yet arrived at _truth_. For so far as the content in the result keeps the determination of something _given_, the {{{ 12.201 }}} _being-in-itself_ presupposed as against the concept stands unsublated; and thus the unity of concept and reality, which is truth, is equally not contained in the result. – Oddly enough, recent times have clung to this side of _finitude_, assuming it to be the relation of cognition that is _absolute_; – as though the finite as such ought to count for what is absolute! Such a standpoint ascribes to the object an unknown _thinghood-in-itself_ lying _behind_ cognition, and takes that thinghood, and truth along with it, for an absolute _beyond_ so far as cognition is concerned. Determinations of thought at large, categories, determinations of reflection, and likewise the formal concept with its moments, are therein assigned a place where they rank as finite determinations, not in and for themselves, but only insofar as they are something subjective as against that empty _thinghood-in-itself_; and to take this relation of cognition's untruth for the genuine one is the error that has grown into the common opinion of recent times.

This determination of finite cognition makes it at once evident that finite cognition is a contradiction sublating its own self; – the contradiction of a truth which is at once meant to be no truth; – of a cognition of that which _is_, a cognition that all the same does not cognize the thing-in-itself. As this contradiction falls in upon itself, so does what constitutes its content, namely subjective cognition together with the thing-in-itself, which is to say that this content shows itself to be something untrue. Cognition, however, has to dissolve its finitude, and with that its contradiction, by the course it runs itself; the consideration we bring to bear upon it is an external reflection; cognition is itself the concept that is purpose to itself, and so accomplishes itself by realizing itself, and in just this accomplishment sublates both its subjectivity and the presupposed being-in-itself. – Cognition is therefore to be looked at on its own account, in its positive activity. Because this idea is, as has been shown, the concept's drive to give itself realization _for_ _its_ _own_ _self_, the concept's activity consists in determining the object and, by such determining, in referring itself within the object to itself identically. The object is, quite generally, that which is utterly determinable, and within the idea there belongs to it the essential aspect of lacking any being in and for itself as against the concept. Since the cognition in question is still finite and not speculative, the objectivity it presupposes has not yet taken on for it the shape of being purely and simply the concept in its own self, containing nothing of its own, nothing particular, as against the concept. Yet just because it passes for a beyond that is in itself, that objectivity has the determination of _determinability_ _by_ _the_ _concept_ essentially, and it has it because _the_ _idea_ is the concept existing for itself, the utterly infinite within itself, in which the object stands sublated _in_ _itself_, so that nothing remains as purpose but to sublate it _for_ _itself_; the idea of cognition does indeed presuppose the object as _being_ _in_ _itself_, yet essentially in such a relation that the idea, sure of its own self and of the nothingness of this opposition, arrives within the object at the realization of its concept.

{{{ 12.202 }}} In the syllogism by which the subjective idea now joins itself together with objectivity, what the _first_ _premise_ presents is that same form of immediate mastery over the object, and of the concept's reference to it, which we saw in the reference of purpose. What the concept does in determining the object is an immediate _imparting_ of itself to the object, an unresisted _spreading_ of itself over it. Herein the concept stays in pure identity with its own self; but this its immediate reflection-into-itself equally bears the determination of objective immediacy; whatever is _for_ _it_ its own determination is just as much a _being_, since it constitutes the presupposition's _first_ negation. Hence the determination that has been posited counts equally as a presupposition merely _found_, as an _apprehending_ of something _given_, in which the concept's activity would rather amount to nothing but being negative towards its own self, holding itself back and rendering itself passive before what is at hand, so that this latter, instead of being determined by the subject, may _show_ itself as it is within its own self.

In this premise, then, the cognition at issue does not even look like an _application_ of the logical determinations; it looks rather like a receiving and apprehending of them as determinations already come upon, and its activity looks to be confined to clearing away from the subject matter a merely subjective obstacle, an outer shell. Such cognition is the _analytic_ one.
