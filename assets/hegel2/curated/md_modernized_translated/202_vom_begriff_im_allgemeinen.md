## {{{ 12.11 }}} On the Concept in General

_What_ _the_ _nature_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ may be admits of immediate statement as little as the concept of any other subject matter admits of being immediately set up. It could well appear that, for the concept of a subject matter to be stated, the logical is presupposed, so that the logical in turn could have nothing else ahead of it, nor be a derivative, in the way geometry sends on ahead those logical propositions that show up in the application to magnitude and get used in that science, doing so in the shape of _axioms_, of _underived_ _and_ _underivable_ determinations of cognition. Now though the concept must count not just as some subjective presupposition but as _absolute_ _foundation_, it can be so only insofar as it has _made_ itself the foundation. The abstractly immediate is doubtless a _first_; yet just as this abstract thing it is rather a mediated one, and hence one whose foundation has first to be looked for if it is to be grasped as it truly is. Its foundation must therefore be immediate, to be sure, but immediate in such fashion that it has made itself so out of the sublating of mediation.

Seen from this side, what the _concept_ first of all amounts to is the _third_ term over against _being_ and _essence_, over against the _immediate_ and _reflection_. To that extent being and essence count as the moments of the concept's _becoming_; the concept, however, is their _foundation_ and their _truth_, the identity wherein they have gone under and are held. Being held in it is theirs because it is their _result_, yet they are so held no longer as _being_ and as _essence_; that determination belongs to them only for as long as they have not gone back into this unity of theirs.

It is the _objective_ _logic_, treating of _being_ and _essence_, that properly makes up the _genetic_ _exposition_ of the _concept_. Taken more closely, _substance_ already is the _real_ _essence_, or _essence_ so far as it has joined with _being_ and entered actuality. Substance is thus the concept's immediate presupposition; what the concept is as _manifested_, substance is _in_ _itself_. Hence the _dialectical_ _movement_ of _substance_ on through causality and reciprocity is the immediate _genesis_ of the _concept_, whereby the concept's _becoming_ gets exhibited. But this _becoming_ {{{ 12.12 }}} carries the sense that becoming carries everywhere: that it is the reflection of what passes over into its _ground_, and that the apparently _other_ into which the first has passed is what makes up that first one's _truth_. The concept is therefore the _truth_ of substance; and because substance's determinate mode of relation is _necessity_, _freedom_ comes to light as the _truth_ _of_ _necessity_ and as the _concept's_ _mode_ _of_ _relation_.

Substance's own necessary onward determining is the _positing_ of that which _is_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_; and the _concept_ is precisely this absolute unity of _being_ and _reflection_, namely that _being-in-_ _and_ _for-itself_ obtains only through being no less _reflection_ or _positedness_, and that _positedness_ is _being-in-_ _and_ _for-itself_. – What this abstract result means is made clear by setting out its concrete genesis; there the nature of the concept lies; but that setting-out had to come before the treatment of the concept. The chief moments of that exposition, (fully worked through in the 2nd book of the objective logic) may therefore be gathered here in brief:

Substance is the _absolute_, the actual which is in and for itself; – _in_ _itself_ as the plain identity of possibility and actuality, an absolute essence holding all actuality and possibility _within_ _itself_; _for_ _itself_, this same identity as absolute _power_, or as a _negativity_ that refers itself purely to itself. – What the movement of substantiality posited through these moments consists in is this,

1) That substance, being absolute power or self-referring _negativity_, sunders itself into a relation wherein those hitherto simple moments stand as _substances_ and as original _presuppositions_. – The determinate relation between them is one of a _passive_ substance, that is, of the originariness belonging to plain _being-in-itself_, which, powerless and not self-positing, is merely original _positedness_; – and of an _active_ substance, of the negativity that _refers_ _itself_ _to_ _itself_ and which, as such, has posited itself as an other and refers itself _to_ _that_ other. That other is just the passive substance, which the active one, out of the originariness of its power, has _presupposed_ for itself as its condition. – The presupposing here has to be understood thus: that the movement of substance itself stands at first under the form of one moment of its concept, namely _being-in-itself_, so that the determinateness of one of the two _substances_ in the relation counts also as the determinateness of the _relation_ itself.

2) The second moment is _being-for-itself_: power posits _itself_ _as_ _self-referring_ _negativity_, and by that sublates once more what it had _presupposed_. – Now the active substance counts as the _cause_; it _acts_; which is to say that it has become the _positing_, as before it was the _presupposing_, so that a) {{{ 12.13 }}} power gets the _shine_ of power along with it, and positedness the _shine_ of positedness. What in the presupposition was _original_ turns, within causality, into what it is in itself, and turns so _through_ _the_ _reference_ _to_ _an_ _other_; a cause yields an effect, and yields it upon another substance; it is now power _in_ _reference_ _to_ _an_ _other_; so far it _appears_ as cause, but it _only_ _is_ _one_ _through_ _this_ _appearing_. – The effect strikes the passive substance, which thereby comes to appear as _positedness_ too, and only therein is it passive substance.

3) Yet more than this mere _appearance_ is present here; namely a) the cause acts on the passive substance and _alters_ the latter's determination; but that determination is positedness, and there is nothing else in it to alter; the further determination it takes on is causality; the passive substance accordingly turns into cause, power and activity. b) the cause _posits_ the effect in it; what the cause posits, however, is the cause itself, self-identical in the acting; it is the cause that steps into the passive substance's place. – On the side of the active substance likewise, a) acting is the translating of cause into effect, into the cause's _other_, into positedness, and b) in the effect the cause displays itself as what it is, the effect being identical with the cause and no other; in acting, then, the cause displays positedness as what it essentially is. – From both sides, therefore, from the identical no less than from the negative _referring_ _of_ _the_ _one_ _to_ _the_ _other_, each turns into the _opposite_ of itself; and each turns into that opposite in such wise that the other, and so each as well, stays _identical_ _with_ _itself_. – Yet identical referring and negative referring are one and the same; only in its opposite is substance self-identical, and therein lies the absolute identity of the two substances that were posited as two. Through acting, then, that is, by positing itself as its own opposite, which is at once the sublating of the _otherness_ it had _presupposed_, namely of the passive substance, the active substance shows itself as cause or original substantiality. Inversely, the acting-upon manifests positedness _as_ positedness, the negative _as_ negative, and so the passive substance _as_ _negativity_ _referring_ _to_ _itself_; and in this its own other the cause merely closes with itself. By such positing the _originariness_ that was _presupposed_, the originariness _that_ _is_ _in_ _itself_, comes to be _for_ _itself_; but this being-in-and-for-itself obtains solely because the positing is equally a _sublating_ of what was presupposed, or because absolute substance has returned to itself only _out_ _of_ _and_ _in_ _its_ _positedness_ and is absolute by that route. This reciprocity, then, is the appearance {{{ 12.14 }}} which sublates itself once again; the revealing of the _shine_ of causality, wherein the cause _is_ _as_ _cause_, the revealing _that_ _this_ _shine_ _is_ _shine_. Such infinite reflection into self, that being-in-and-for-itself first obtains by being positedness, is the _completion_ _of_ _substance_. But with this completion we no longer have _substance_ itself; we have something higher, the _concept_, the _subject_. That the relation of substantiality passes over occurs by its own immanent necessity, and amounts to nothing beyond substance's manifesting of itself: that its truth is the concept, and that the truth of necessity is freedom.

It was already brought to mind earlier, in the 2nd book of the objective logic (p. 376 f., Remark), that the philosophy which takes its stand on the _standpoint_ _of_ _substance_ and goes no further is _Spinoza's_ _system_. There too the _defect_ of that system was exhibited, in regard to form as much as to matter. To refute it, however, is quite another thing. Concerning the refutation of a philosophical system, the general remark has likewise been made elsewhere that the skewed notion must be driven out of it, as if the system were to be presented as _false_ throughout, and as if the _true_ system stood to the false merely as its _opposite_. The context in which the Spinozistic system appears here yields of its own accord that system's true standpoint, and the answer to the question whether it is true or false. Out of the nature of _essence_ the relation of substantiality generated itself; that relation, together with its presentation broadened into a whole within a system, is thus a _necessary_ _standpoint_ that the absolute takes up. So a standpoint of that kind may not be treated as an opinion, as some individual's subjective and arbitrary manner of representing and thinking, as a straying of speculation; speculation is on the contrary carried over to it of necessity along its road, and to that extent the system is true through and through. – _But_ _it_ _is_ _not_ _the_ _highest_ _standpoint_. Just to that extent, though, the system cannot pass for _false_, as something standing in need of and open to _refutation_; the only thing in it to be reckoned _false_ is the claim that it should be the highest standpoint. For that reason the _true_ system cannot stand to it in a relation of bare _opposition_ either; for then this opposite would itself be one-sided. Being the higher, it must instead hold the subordinate within itself.

Nor, further, may the refutation come from without, that is, proceed from assumptions lying outside that system, assumptions it fails to answer to. It has only to withhold recognition from them; a _defect_ counts as a defect only for whoever starts from the needs and demands built {{{ 12.15 }}} upon those assumptions. In this sense it has been said that for anyone who does not presuppose, as settled on his own part, the freedom and self-subsistence of the self-conscious subject, no refutation of Spinozism is possible at all. In any case a standpoint so lofty, and in itself already so _rich_, as the relation of substantiality does not pass that assumption by but harbours it as well; among the attributes of Spinozistic substance is _thinking_. Rather it knows how to break down the determinations under which such assumptions run counter to it and to draw them into itself, so that within _it_ they turn up in the modifications that suit it. The whole nerve of refuting from outside then lies in clinging stiffly, on one's own side, to the opposed forms of those assumptions, for instance to the thinking individual's absolute subsistence on its own, over against that form of thinking which in absolute substance gets posited as identical with extension. A genuine refutation has to go into the opponent's force and take its stand within the compass of his strength; assailing him outside himself and carrying the day where he is not does nothing to advance the fact. The one refutation of Spinozism can accordingly lie only in this, that its standpoint be acknowledged first as essential and necessary, and secondly that this standpoint raise itself _out_ _of_ _itself_ to the higher one. Taken purely and wholly _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, the relation of substantiality carries itself over into its opposite, the _concept_. Hence the exposition of substance given in the preceding book, the exposition which carries over to the _concept_, is Spinozism's one true refutation. It is the _uncovering_ of substance, and this uncovering is the _genesis_ _of_ _the_ _concept_, whose principal moments were gathered above. – Substance's unity is its _relation_ _of_ _necessity_; so taken, however, it is merely _inner_ _necessity_; once substance _posits_ _itself_ by way of the moment of absolute _negativity_, it turns into _manifested_ or _posited_ _identity_, and with that into the _freedom_ that constitutes the concept's identity. The concept, the totality that issues from reciprocity, is the unity of reciprocity's _two_ _substances_, though these now belong to freedom, since their identity is no longer a blind one, that is, an _inward_ one; they have essentially the determination of being _shine_ or moments of reflection, so that each has closed just as immediately with its other or with its positedness, each holds its positedness _within_ _itself_, and each is thus posited in its other purely as self-identical.

With the _concept_, then, the realm of _freedom_ stands open. It is the free, since the _identity_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ that makes up substance's necessity is at once there as sublated, or as _positedness_, and {{{ 12.16 }}} since this positedness, in referring itself to itself, is that very identity. Gone is the opacity which the substances standing in the causal relation had for one another, for the originariness of their subsistence has passed over into positedness and so become a _clarity_ transparent to itself; the _original_ _fact_ is such by being nothing but the _cause_ _of_ _itself_, and that is _substance_ _set_ _free_ _into_ _the_ _concept_.

This yields for the concept, straightaway, the closer determination that follows. Since being-in-and-for-itself immediately is as _positedness_, the concept in its simple self-reference is absolute _determinateness_; a determinateness which, however, referring itself to nothing but itself, is just as immediately simple identity. But this _reference_ of determinateness _to_ _itself_, as its _closing_ _with_ _itself_, is equally the _negation_ _of_ _determinateness_, and in this self-sameness the concept is the _universal_. Yet this identity has just as much the determination of negativity; it is negation or determinateness self-referred, and so the concept is _singular_. Each of the two is the totality, each carries the other's determination in itself, and for that reason these totalities are as plainly only _one_ as this unity is the sundering of itself into the free shine of that duality; – a duality which, in the difference of _singular_ and _universal_, looks like complete opposition, yet is so far _shine_ that with the one being grasped and uttered the other is grasped and uttered immediately in it.

What has just been laid out counts as the _concept_ _of_ _the_ _concept_. Should it appear to depart from what one otherwise means by concept, one might ask for a demonstration of how the very thing that has here come out as the concept lies contained in other representations or accounts. For one thing, though, no confirmation resting on the _authority_ of everyday understanding can be at issue; within the science of the concept, the concept's content and determination admit of verification only through that _immanent_ _deduction_ in which its genesis lies, a deduction now behind us. For another, whatever else gets offered as the concept of the concept must surely allow the one deduced here to be recognized in it. But finding out what others have said of the nature of the concept is no easy matter. For the most part they do not take up the search at all, taking it for granted that everybody understands well enough on his own what is meant in speaking of the concept. Of late one might think oneself all the more excused from any labour with the concept, since, just as for a while the fashion ran to charging imagination, and then memory, with every conceivable ill, it has this good while been the custom in philosophy, and partly {{{ 12.17 }}} remains so, to pile every slander on the _concept_, to bring into contempt what is the highest of thinking, and to look instead upon the _incomprehensible_ and upon _non-comprehension_ as the highest summit, in science as in morals.

I confine myself here to one remark which may help in taking in the concepts developed here and may make it easier to find one's way among them. So far as the concept has thriven into a _concrete_ _existence_ that is itself free, it is nothing else than the _I_, or pure self-consciousness. Concepts I certainly _have_, meaning determinate concepts; the I, though, is the pure concept as such, the concept come to _existence_. So when one calls to mind the basic determinations making up the nature of the I, one may take it that what is being called to mind is something well known, that is, something familiar to representation. The I, however, is _first_ this pure self-referring unity, and is so not at once but by way of abstracting from every determinateness and every content and retreating into the freedom of a self-sameness without restriction. So it is _universality_; a unity that is unity with itself only through that _negative_ bearing which shows up as abstracting, and which therefore holds all being-determined dissolved within it. _Secondly_, the I is just as immediately, as self-referring negativity, _singularity_, _absolute_ _being-determined_, setting itself over against an other and shutting it out; _individual_ _personality_. That absolute _universality_ which is just as immediately absolute _singularizing_, and a _being-in-_ _and_ _for-itself_ that is out and out positedness and is this _being-in-_ _and_ _for-itself_ only by its unity with _positedness_, makes up the nature of the _I_ no less than that of the _concept_; of neither is anything to be grasped unless the two moments named are taken up at once in their abstraction and at once in their complete unity.

When one speaks in the usual manner of the _understanding_ that _I_ _have_, one means by it a _faculty_ or _property_ standing to the I as a thing's property stands to the _thing_, – to an indeterminate substrate that is not the true ground and determinant of its property. On such a representation I _have_ _concepts_, and the concept, in the way I also have a coat, a colour, and other outward properties. – _Kant_ pressed past this outward relation borne to the I by the understanding as faculty of concepts, and by the concepts themselves. Among the profoundest and most correct insights to be met with in the Critique of Reason belongs this, that the _unity_ making up the _essence_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ is discerned as {{{ 12.18 }}} the _originally_ _synthetic_ _unity_ of _apperception_, as the unity of the: _I_ _think_, or of self-consciousness. – This proposition makes up the so-called _transcendental_ deduction of the category; yet it has always passed for one of the hardest pieces in Kant's philosophy, – for no other reason, surely, than that it requires one to move past the mere _representation_ of the relation borne to a thing and its properties or accidents by the _I_ _and_ _the_ _understanding_ or the _concepts_, and to press on to the _thought_. – _Object_, Kant says, Kritik der reinen Vernunft S. 137. 2te Ausg. is that in whose _concept_ the _manifold_ of a given _intuition_ stands _united_. All uniting of representations, however, calls for _unity_ _of_ _consciousness_ _in_ _the_ _synthesis_ of those representations. Consequently this _unity_ _of_ _consciousness_ is what alone makes up the referring of representations to a subject matter, and so their _objective_ _validity_, and on it rests the very _possibility_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_. From this Kant marks off the _subjective_ _unity_ _of_ _consciousness_, the unity of representation as to whether I am aware of a manifold _simultaneously_ or _one_ _after_ _another_, which hangs on empirical conditions. The principles of the _objective_ determination of representations, by contrast, are said to be drawn solely from the fundamental proposition of the _transcendental_ _unity_ _of_ _apperception_. By the _categories_, which are these objective determinations, the manifold of given representations is determined so as to be brought to the _unity_ _of_ _consciousness_. – On this presentation, the concept's unity is that whereby something is not a bare _determination_ _of_ _feeling_, not _intuition_ nor even bare _representation_, but _object_; and this objective unity is the _unity_ of the I with itself. – To _comprehend_ a subject matter consists in fact in nothing else than that the I makes it _its_ _own_, penetrates it, and carries it over into _its_ _own_ _form_, namely into the _universality_ which is immediately _determinateness_, or the determinateness which is immediately _universality_. In intuition, or even in representation, the subject matter is still something _outward_, something _alien_. By comprehending, the _being-in-_ _and-for-itself_ it possesses in intuiting and representing gets turned into a _positedness_; the I penetrates it _in_ _thinking_. As it stands in thinking, though, it first is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_; as it stands in intuition or representation it is _appearance_; thinking sublates the _immediacy_ with which it first comes before us and thereby makes a _positedness_ of it; yet this _positedness_ of its own is its _being-in-_ _and_ _for-itself_, its _objectivity_. Such objectivity, then, the subject matter possesses in the _concept_, and the concept is the _unity_ _of_ _self-consciousness_ into which the subject matter has been _taken_ _up_; its objectivity, or the concept, is thus itself nothing else than the nature of {{{ 12.19 }}} self-consciousness; it has no moments or determinations save those of the I itself.

By a principal proposition of Kant's philosophy it is thus warranted that, to know what the _concept_ is, the nature of the _I_ should be called to mind. Conversely, though, this requires that the _concept_ of the I be grasped as it was set out just above. Stop at the bare _representation_ of the I such as floats before our everyday consciousness, and the I is no more than the simple _thing_, given also the name of _soul_, to which the concept _attaches_ as a possession or property. Such a representation, which never engages in comprehending either the I or the concept, can be of no use for easing or bringing nearer the comprehending of the concept.

Two further sides bearing on the concept are contained in the Kantian presentation cited, and they call for some further remarks. In the first place the _stage_ of the _understanding_ has the _stages_ _of_ _feeling_ _and_ _of_ _intuition_ sent ahead of it; and Kant's transcendental philosophy holds it for an essential proposition that _concepts_ _without_ _intuition_ _are_ _empty_, valid only as _references_ of the _manifold_ which intuition supplies. Secondly the concept has been given out as the _objective_ side of cognition, hence as the _truth_. Yet on the other side it gets taken as something _merely_ _subjective_, out of which _reality_, by which, being set over against subjectivity, objectivity is to be meant, cannot be _fished_; and generally the concept and the logical are pronounced something merely _formal_ which, abstracting as it does from content, does not hold the truth.

As to the first, then, _that_ _relation_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_ _or_ _concept_ _to_ _the_ _stages_ _presupposed_ _for_ _it_, everything turns on which science is under treatment, if the _form_ of those stages is to be settled. In our science, as pure _logic_, the stages are _being_ and _essence_. In _psychology_ it is _feeling_ and _intuition_, and then _representation_ at large, that get sent ahead of the understanding. In the _Phenomenology_ of Spirit, as the doctrine of consciousness, the climb to the understanding went by way of the stages of _sensuous_ _consciousness_ and then of _perceiving_. Kant sends ahead of it feeling and intuition alone. How _incomplete_ this ladder is to start with he himself lets one see, in that he attaches to the transcendental logic, or theory of the understanding, one further _treatise_ by way of _appendix_, on the _concepts_ _of_ _reflection_; – a sphere lying between _intuition_ and the _understanding_, or between _being_ and the _concept_. On the fact itself it is to be noted _in_ _the_ _first_ _place_ that those shapes of _intuition_, {{{ 12.20 }}} _representation_ and the like are the property of _self-conscious_ _spirit_, which as such does not come under consideration in logical science. True, the pure determinations of being, essence and concept do make up the foundation and the inner simple scaffolding of spirit's forms; spirit as _intuiting_, and equally as _sensuous_ _consciousness_, stands in the determinateness of immediate being, while spirit as _representing_, and also as _perceiving_ consciousness, has lifted itself from being to the stage of essence or of reflection. But such concrete shapes are as little the business of logical science as are the concrete forms which the logical determinations take on in nature, forms that would be _space_ _and_ _time_, then space and time filling themselves out, as _inorganic_ _nature_, and _organic_ _nature_. Nor, likewise, is the concept here to be looked at as an act performed by the self-conscious understanding, nor as _subjective_ _understanding_, but rather as the concept in and for itself, one that makes up a _stage_ of _nature_ quite as much as one of _spirit_. Life, or organic nature, is that stage of nature at which the concept steps forth; but as a blind concept, one that does not seize itself, that is, one that does not think; as such the concept falls only to spirit. Of that unspiritual shape of the concept, however, as of this spiritual one, its logical form is independent; the necessary advance notice on this was already given in the _Introduction_; it is a point not to be justified first inside _logic_, but one about which one must be clear _before_ logic.

Yet however the forms preceding the concept may be shaped, what matters _secondly_ is the _relation_ in which the _concept_ is _thought_ to stand to _them_. Both the usual psychological representation and Kant's transcendental philosophy take that relation thus: the empirical _material_, the manifold of intuition and representation, is at first _there_ _on_ _its_ _own_, whereupon the understanding _comes_ _up_ to it, brings _unity_ into it, and by _abstraction_ lifts it into the form of _universality_. On this showing the understanding is a _form_ empty on its own, getting reality in part only from that _given_ content, and in part _abstracting_ from it, that is, _dropping_ it as something which is however unusable for the concept alone. In neither operation is the concept what is independent, what is essential and true in that antecedent material; the material is rather the reality in and for itself, which cannot be fished out of the concept.

It must of course be granted that the _concept_ _as_ _such_ is not yet complete, but has to lift _itself_ into the _idea_, which alone is the unity of concept and reality; as must _emerge_ in the sequel from the nature of the concept _itself_. For the reality it gives itself may not be picked up as {{{ 12.21 }}} something external, but has, on scientific demand, to be derived from the concept itself. But the material given through intuition and representation is truly not what may be urged against the concept as the _real_. »_It_ _is_ _only_ _a_ _concept_,« runs the customary saying, whereby not merely the idea but sensuous, spatial and temporal palpable existence gets set over against it as something more excellent than the concept. The _abstract_ then passes for something lesser than the concrete because so much material of that kind has been dropped from it. Abstracting, on this opinion, means that out of the concrete, and merely to serve _our_ _subjective_ _ends_, one _mark_ or another is lifted out in such a way that, with so many other _properties_ and _constitutions_ of the subject matter dropped, nothing is meant to be taken from these in _worth_ or in _dignity_; they are rather left standing as the _real_, only over there on the other side, and still in full force; so that it is merely the _incapacity_ of the understanding that it cannot take up such wealth and must make do with the scanty abstraction. Now if the given material of intuition and the manifold of representation is taken for the real as against the thought and the concept, that is a view whose being laid aside is not merely a condition of philosophizing but is already presupposed by religion; how could there be any need or sense of religion if the fleeting and surface appearance of the sensuous and the singular still passed for the true? Philosophy, however, delivers the _comprehended_ insight into how things stand with the reality of sensuous being, and it sends those stages of feeling and intuition, of sensuous consciousness and so forth, ahead of the understanding just so far as they are its conditions in its becoming, yet only in such fashion that the concept arises _out_ _of_ _their_ _dialectic_ _and_ _nullity_ as their _ground_, and not that the concept should be conditioned by their _reality_. Abstracting thought is therefore not to be viewed as a mere shunting aside of sensuous material, as though that material took no harm in its reality thereby; it is rather the sublating of the material and its reduction, as mere _appearance_, to what is _essential_, and this shows itself only in the _concept_. If, to be sure, what is to be taken up out of the concrete appearance into the concept is only meant to serve as a _mark_ or _sign_, then it may of course be some merely sensuous singular determination of the subject matter, picked out from the rest for the sake of some external interest, and of the same sort and nature as the rest.

A chief misunderstanding at work here is the notion that the _natural_ _principle_, or the _beginning_ from which the _natural_ {{{ 12.22 }}} development, or the _history_ of the individual as it educates itself, sets out, is the _true_ and the _first_ in the _concept_. Intuition or being are indeed, by nature, the first, the condition for the concept, yet they are not on that score the unconditioned in and for itself; in the concept their reality is on the contrary sublated, and with it the shine they carried as the conditioning real. Where it is not _truth_ that is at issue but only the _history_ of how things proceed in representing and in appearing thinking, one may of course rest with the tale that we begin from feelings and intuitions, that the understanding draws a universality or an abstraction out of their manifold, and that it naturally requires for this the foundation which, throughout such abstracting, remains standing for representation in the entire reality with which it first showed itself. Philosophy, however, is meant to be no tale of what occurs, but a cognition of the _true_ within it, and out of the true it is further to comprehend what in the tale looks like mere occurrence.

If, in the superficial representation of what the concept is, all manifoldness falls _outside_ _the_ _concept_, and only the form of abstract universality or of empty reflection-identity accrues to it, one may recall right away that elsewhere too, in stating a concept or giving a definition, over and above the genus, which itself is strictly not pure abstract universality, the _specific_ _determinateness_ is expressly required. Were one to reflect with a little thoughtful attention on what that means, it would come out that _distinguishing_ is thereby held to be a moment of the concept quite as essential. _Kant_ opened this consideration with the exceedingly important thought that there are _synthetic_ _judgments_ _à_ _priori_. That original synthesis of apperception is among the profoundest principles for speculative development; in it lies the beginning of a true grasp of the nature of the concept, and it stands wholly counter to the empty identity or abstract universality which harbours no synthesis in it. – The carrying out, however, answers poorly to such a beginning. The very expression: _synthesis_ readily leads back to the representation of an _external_ _unity_ and a _mere_ _joining_ of things _separate_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _themselves_. Thereupon Kant's philosophy stayed with the psychological reflex of the concept and reverted to the claim that the concept remains conditioned by a manifold of intuition. It gave out the cognitions of understanding and experience as an _appearing_ content, not because the categories are themselves merely finite, but on grounds of a psychological idealism, since they are supposedly _nothing_ _but_ determinations arising from {{{ 12.23 }}} self-consciousness. Here belongs too the claim that the concept is once again to be _void_ _of_ _content_ and _empty_ without the manifold of intuition, for all that it is _à_ _priori_ a _synthesis_; being that, it surely carries determinateness and difference within itself. As it is the determinateness of the concept, and with that the _absolute_ _determinateness_, _singularity_, the concept is ground and source of all finite determinateness and manifoldness.

The formal position it keeps as understanding reaches its consummation in Kant's account of what _reason_ is. In reason, the highest stage of thinking, one would look for the concept to shed the conditionedness that still clings to it at the stage of the understanding, and to reach consummated truth. That expectation, however, is disappointed. Because Kant determines reason's bearing toward the categories as merely _dialectical_, and takes the result of that dialectic to be simply the _infinite_ _nothing_, the infinite unity of reason forfeits the synthesis as well, and with it the beginning of a speculative, truly infinite concept; it sinks into the well-known wholly formal, merely _regulative_ _unity_ _of_ _the_ _systematic_ _use_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_. It is pronounced an abuse for logic, which is meant to be no more than a _canon_ _of_ _appraisal_, to be viewed as an _organon_ for producing _objective_ insights. The concepts of reason, in which one had to sense a higher power and a deeper content, keep nothing _constitutive_ about them any longer, as the categories still did; they are _mere_ ideas; using them is to be _perfectly_ _permissible_, but by these intelligible essences, in which all _truth_ was to unlock itself entirely, nothing more is to be meant than _hypotheses_, and to credit these with a truth in and for itself would be sheer caprice and rashness, seeing that they – _can_ _turn_ _up_ _in_ _no_ _experience_. – Would one ever have thought that philosophy would refuse truth to intelligible essences for want of the spatial and temporal material of sensibility?

Bound up immediately with this is the point of view under which the concept and the vocation of logic generally have to be regarded, a point of view taken in Kant's philosophy just as it is taken commonly; namely the _relation_ borne by the _concept_ and _its_ _science_ _to_ _truth_ itself. From Kant's deduction of the categories it was cited above that on it the _object_, namely that in which intuition's manifold stands _united_, is this unity only _through_ _the_ _unity_ _of_ _self-consciousness_. Here, then, the _objectivity_ _of_ _thinking_ is definitely pronounced, an identity of concept and thing that is _the_ _truth_. It is likewise granted on all hands that when thinking makes a given subject matter its own, the latter thereby {{{ 12.24 }}} undergoes an alteration and is turned from a sensuous one into a thought one; that this alteration, however, so far from changing anything in its essentiality, means rather that only in its concept does the subject matter stand in its _truth_; that in the immediacy wherein it is given it is nothing but _appearance_ _and_ _contingency_; that the cognition of the subject matter which comprehends it is cognition of it as it is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_; and that the concept is its objectivity itself. On the other side, however, it is with equal assurance maintained once more that _we_ _cannot_ _after_ _all_ _cognize_ _things_ as _they_ _are_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _themselves_, that _truth_ lies _beyond_ _the_ _reach_ _of_ _cognizing_ _reason_; that the truth consisting in the unity of object and concept is after all mere appearance; and this once again on the ground that the content is nothing but the manifold of intuition. On this point it has already been recalled that in the concept, on the contrary, just this manifoldness gets sublated so far as it belongs to intuition in opposition to the concept, and that the subject matter is led back by the concept into its non-contingent essentiality; that essentiality enters appearance, and precisely for that reason appearance is not something merely essenceless but the manifestation of essence. The manifestation of essence grown wholly free, however, is the concept. – The propositions recalled here are no dogmatic assertions, for the reason that they are results issuing through themselves from the whole development of _essence_. Where this development has now brought us is the insight that the form of the _absolute_ standing higher than being and essence is the _concept_. Since on this side the concept has _brought_ _under_ _itself_ being and essence, to which, from other points of departure, feeling and intuition and representation also belong, and which appeared beforehand as conditions of it, and since it has shown itself _to_ _be_ _their_ _unconditioned_ _ground_, what is left over now is the _second_ _side_, to whose treatment this third book of the logic is devoted, namely the presentation of how the concept builds in and out of itself the reality that vanished within it. It has therefore been granted that the cognition standing merely at the concept purely as such is still incomplete and has so far reached only _abstract_ _truth_. Its incompleteness lies not in its wanting that supposed reality alleged to be given in feeling and intuition, but in the concept's not yet having given itself its _own_ reality, one generated out of itself. Herein lies the absoluteness of the concept, proved against and upon the empirical material, more precisely upon its categories and determinations of reflection: that this material has no _truth_ as it shows up _outside_ and _before_ the concept, but only in its ideality, or identity with the concept. The _deriving_ of the real from the concept, if one cares to call it deriving, consists at bottom first of all in this, that the concept in its formal abstraction {{{ 12.25 }}} proves itself unfinished and, by the dialectic grounded within it, goes over to reality in such a way as to generate reality from itself, not in such a way as to fall back again upon a finished reality met with over against it, taking refuge in something that has announced itself as the unessential in appearance because, after looking round for something better, it found nothing of the kind. – It will always be marked out as something to wonder at how Kant's philosophy discerned the relation of thinking to sensuous existence at which it halted to be merely a relative relation of bare appearance, and how very well it acknowledged and pronounced a higher unity of the two in the _idea_ at large, and for instance in the idea of an intuitive understanding, and yet halted at that relative relation and at the claim that the concept is and stays cut off from reality outright, – so declaring to be the _truth_ what it gave out as finite cognition, and declaring to be extravagant, illicit, and mere things of thought what it discerned as _truth_ and of which it set up the determinate concept.

Since it is logic here in the first instance, and not science at large, whose relation to truth is in question, it must further be granted that logic, as the _formal_ _science_, neither can nor should contain that reality which forms the content of philosophy's further parts, of the _sciences_ _of_ _nature_ _and_ _of_ _spirit_. To be sure, these concrete sciences step out into a more real form of the idea than logic does, yet not so as to turn back to that reality which consciousness gave up in raising itself above its appearance into science, nor so as to revert to the use of forms like the categories and determinations of reflection, whose finitude and untruth logic has exhibited. Logic shows rather the raising of the _idea_ to that stage from which the idea turns creator of nature and steps across into the form of a _concrete_ _immediacy_, whose concept then breaks this shape apart again so as to come to itself as _concrete_ _spirit_. Set against these concrete sciences, which have and keep the logical, or the concept, as their inner shaper just as they had it as their pre-shaper, logic is of course itself the _formal_ _science_, but the science of _absolute_ _form_, a form that is totality within itself and holds the _pure_ _idea_ _of_ _truth_ _itself_. On itself this absolute form has its content or reality; the concept, not being the trivial, empty identity, has in the moment of its negativity, or of absolute determining, the differentiated determinations; content in general is nothing else than such determinations of absolute form; content posited by that form itself, and therefore also answering to it. – This form is on that account of quite another nature than the logical form {{{ 12.26 }}} is commonly taken to be. It is already _for_ _itself_ _the_ _truth_, since this content answers to its form, or this reality to its concept, and it is the _pure_ _truth_ because the form's determinations do not yet carry the form of an absolute otherness or of absolute immediacy. – Kant, when he comes to speak (Critique of Pure Reason, p. 83) with reference to logic, of the old and famous question: _What_ _truth_ _is_? bestows at the outset, as something trivial, the nominal explanation that truth is the agreeing of cognition with its subject matter; – a definition of great, indeed of the highest, worth. Recall that definition alongside transcendental idealism's basic claim, that _rational_ _cognition_ has no power to seize _things_ _in_ _themselves_, that _reality_ _lies_ _flatly_ outside _the_ _concept_, and it shows at once that such a _reason_, unable _to_ _bring_ itself _into_ _agreement_ with its subject matter, the things in themselves, and such _things_ _in_ _themselves_, out of agreement with the concept of reason, and a concept out of agreement with reality, and a reality out of _agreement_ with the concept, are _untrue_ _representations_. Had Kant measured the idea _of_ _an_ _intuitive_ _understanding_ against that definition of truth, he would have handled this idea, which expresses the agreement demanded, not as a thing of thought but rather as truth.

»What one wants to know, Kant goes on, is a _universal_ _and_ _secure_ _criterion_ _of_ _the_ _truth_ _of_ _any_ _cognition_ _whatever_; such a one would have to hold of all cognitions _without_ _distinction_ _of_ _their_ _objects_; but since with it one _abstracts_ _from_ _all_ _content_ _of_ _cognition_ (_reference_ _to_ _its_ _object_), while _truth_ bears on _just_ _this_ _content_, it would be altogether _impossible_ _and_ _absurd_ _to_ _ask_ _after_ _a_ _mark_ _of_ _the_ _truth_ _of_ _this_ _content_ _of_ _cognitions_.« – The usual representation of logic's formal function finds very definite expression here, and the reasoning quoted looks highly convincing. First off, however, one must note that formal reasoning of this sort commonly fares so as to forget, in its talking, the very fact it laid down as its basis and of which it speaks. It would be absurd, so runs the claim, to inquire after a criterion of the _truth_ _of_ _the_ _content_ _of_ _cognition_; – but by the definition truth is constituted not by the _content_ but by that content's _agreeing_ with the concept. A content of the sort spoken of here, _without_ _the_ _concept_, is conceptless and hence essenceless; after a criterion of the truth of such a thing one indeed cannot inquire, but for the opposite reason; namely not {{{ 12.27 }}} because, by reason of its conceptlessness, it is not the _agreement_ _demanded_, but because it can be nothing beyond something belonging to opinion void of truth. – Let us leave aside the mention of content, which here breeds the confusion that formalism falls into every time and that makes it say the reverse of what it wants to bring forward whenever it takes up explaining, and let us hold to the abstract view that the logical is merely formal and rather abstracts from all content; – then what we have is a one-sided cognition meant to contain no subject matter, an empty form without determination, which is therefore as little an _agreement_, since agreement essentially takes _two_, – as little truth. – In the à priori _synthesis_ of the concept Kant possessed a higher principle wherein duality within unity, and so what truth demands, could be discerned; but the sensuous material, the manifold of intuition, held too much sway over him for a passage away from it to the consideration of concept and categories _in_ _and_ _for_ _themselves_, and to a speculative philosophizing.

Logic being the science of absolute form, this formal element must, _if_ _it_ _is_ _to_ _be_ _something_ _true_, carry on itself a _content_ suited to its form, and all the more so in that the logically formal is pure form, so that the logically true must be _the_ _pure_ _truth_ itself. This formal element has therefore to be thought as far richer in determinations and content within itself, and as of infinitely greater potency upon the concrete, than it is usually taken for. The logical laws by themselves, (setting aside the heterogeneous matter anyway, the applied logic and the remaining psychological and anthropological stuff,) are usually cut down, besides the proposition of contradiction, to a few thin propositions about the conversion of judgments and the forms of syllogisms. Even the forms that occur there, along with their further determinations, are picked up merely in a quasi-historical way and are never put to the test of whether they are anything true in and for themselves. Thus, for instance, the form of the positive judgment passes for something in itself entirely correct, where whether such a judgment is true supposedly hangs on the content alone. Whether this form is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ a form of truth, whether the proposition it utters, _the_ _singular_ _is_ _a_ _universal_, is not dialectical in itself, is an inquiry that never gets a thought. It is flatly assumed that this judgment is by itself able to contain truth, and that the proposition uttered by every positive judgment is a true one; though it is immediately plain that it wants what the definition of truth demands, namely the agreeing of concept and subject matter; take the predicate, here the universal, for the concept, and the subject, which is the singular, for the subject matter, {{{ 12.28 }}} and the one fails to agree with the other. Now if the _abstract_ _universal_ that serves as predicate does not yet make up a concept, to which more certainly belongs; – as such a subject too is not yet much beyond a grammatical one, how should the judgment be capable of containing truth, when its concept and its subject matter do not agree, or when it wants the concept, and probably the subject matter as well? – Rather it is _impossible_ and _absurd_ to want to catch truth in forms of that kind, such as the positive judgment is, and judgment in general. As little as Kant's philosophy considered the categories in and for themselves, declaring them instead, on the skewed ground that they are subjective forms of self-consciousness, to be finite determinations unfit to contain the true, so much the less did it subject to critique the forms of the concept that make up the content of ordinary logic; it rather took over a part of them, namely the functions of judgment, for determining the category, and let them pass as valid presuppositions. Even were nothing more to be seen in the logical forms than formal functions of thinking, they would on that score alone deserve the inquiry how far by themselves they answer to _truth_. A logic that fails at this can lay claim at most to the worth of a natural-history description of the appearances of thinking as they are met with. That _Aristotle_ was the first to undertake this description is an infinite merit of his, one that must fill us with the utmost admiration for the strength of that spirit. But it is needful to go further, and to discern in part the systematic connection, in part however the worth of the forms.
