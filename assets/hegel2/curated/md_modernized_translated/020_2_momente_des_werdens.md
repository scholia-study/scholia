## 2. Moments of Becoming

Becoming – coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be – is being and nothing in their unseparatedness; not that unity which abstracts away from being and from nothing; as unity _of_ _being_ and _nothing_, however, it is that _determinate_ unity in which being no less than nothing _is_. But inasmuch as being and nothing are each of them unseparated from what is other to it, each _is_ _not_. In this unity, then, they _are_, but as vanishing ones, only as _sublated_. From the _self-subsistence_ initially represented of them they sink down into _moments_, _still_ _differentiated_ yet at the same time sublated.

{{{ 21.93 }}} Take them according to this differentiatedness of theirs, and each is, within _the_ _same_, unity with the _other_. Becoming, accordingly, contains being and nothing in the shape of _two_ _such_ _unities_, and each of them is itself a unity of being and nothing; the first of them being as immediate together with reference to nothing; the second, nothing as immediate together with reference to being; unequal value attaches to the determinations within these unities.

Becoming stands in this way under a twofold determination; in the first, the nothing figures as immediate, that is, the determination sets out from the nothing, which refers itself to being, which is to say passes over into it; in the second, being figures as immediate, that is, the determination sets out from being, which passes over into the nothing, – _coming-to-be_ and _ceasing-to-be_.

Becoming is what both are, one and the same, and even as directions thus differentiated they interpenetrate one another and paralyse one another. _Ceasing-to-be_ is the one; being passes over into nothing, and yet nothing is quite as much the opposite of its own self, a passing over into being, coming-to-be. The other direction is that coming-to-be; nothing passes over into being, and yet being quite as much sublates its own self and is rather that passing over into nothing, is ceasing-to-be. – Neither sublates the other reciprocally, nor does one sublate the other from without; rather each sublates its own self and is within its own self its opposite.
