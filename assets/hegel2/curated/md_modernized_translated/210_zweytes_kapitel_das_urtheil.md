## {{{ 12.53 }}} Chapter 2. Judgment

The judgment is the concept's own _determinateness_, _posited_ at the _concept_ itself. Determinations of the concept, or, what has shown itself to be the same thing, the determinate concepts, have already come under consideration for themselves; but that consideration amounted rather to a subjective reflection, a subjective abstraction. The concept, however, is itself this act of abstracting; the placing of its determinations one against another is its own determining. This positing of the determinate concepts by the concept itself is the _judgment_.

Judging is to this extent _a_ _different_ function from comprehending, or rather _the_ _other_ function of the concept, being as it is the concept's _determining_ of itself through itself; and the onward course of the judgment into the diversity of judgments is this continued determining of the concept. Which determinate concepts there _are_, and how these determinations of the concept necessarily result, is what has to show itself in the judgment.

Hence the judgment may be called the concept's nearest _realization_, so far as reality signifies in general an entry into _existence_ _as_ _determinate_ _being_. Looked at more closely, the nature of this realization has turned out as follows: that _firstly_ the moments of the concept, by way of its reflection-into-itself or its singularity, are self-subsistent totalities, while _secondly_ the concept's unity is the _reference_ between them. Determinations reflected into themselves are _determinate_ _totalities_, standing quite as essentially in an indifferent subsistence void of reference as in mutual mediation with one another. The determining is itself the totality only in holding these totalities and their reference within it. This totality is the judgment. – It holds, then, in the first place the two self-subsistent terms, which go by the names of _subject_ and _predicate_. What each of them is cannot yet properly be stated; they remain indeterminate, since it is the judgment that has first to determine them. Being the concept in determinate shape, the judgment exhibits between them only the general difference of holding the _determinate_ concept against the one still _indeterminate_. The subject may thus at first be taken, as against the predicate, for singular against universal, or again for particular against universal, or for singular against particular; so far, that is, as the two merely stand facing one another as the more determinate and the more universal.

{{{ 12.54 }}} It is accordingly serviceable, and a need besides, that the determinations of the judgment should carry these _names_, _subject_ and _predicate_; as names they are something indeterminate, still awaiting the determination they are to receive, and beyond names they are therefore nothing. For the two sides of the judgment, determinations of the concept themselves were unusable, partly on this ground, but partly, and far more, because a determination of the concept shows its nature to lie in not being anything abstract and fixed, but in carrying its opposite within it and positing that opposite at itself; and since the sides of the judgment are concepts themselves, hence the totality of the concept's determinations, they must traverse them all and exhibit them at themselves, whether in abstract or in concrete form. To keep hold of the sides of the judgment in some general fashion through this alteration of their determination, names serve best, remaining as they do the same throughout. – Yet the name stands opposed to the fact or the concept, and this distinction turns up at the judgment itself as such; since it is the subject that in general voices what is determinate, and so rather what immediately _is_, while the predicate voices the _universal_, essence or concept, the subject taken by itself amounts at the outset to no more than a sort of _name_; for _what_ _it_ IS the predicate alone declares, and the predicate is what holds _being_ in the concept's sense. What _is_ this, or what sort of plant _is_ this and so forth? under the _being_ inquired after, often nothing but the _name_ is meant, and once that has been learned one is content and knows now what the fact _is_. Such is _being_ taken in the subject's sense. It is the predicate, though, that first yields the _concept_, or at any rate essence and the universal as such, and the question in the judgment's sense is directed after the predicate. – _God_, _spirit_, _nature_, or whatever it be, is therefore, as the subject of a judgment, no more than the name as yet; what such a subject _is_ by its concept is at hand only in the predicate. Were one to inquire which predicate belongs to such a subject, a _concept_ would have to underlie the appraisal already; but that concept is uttered only by the predicate itself. So it is really mere _representation_ that constitutes the presupposed meaning of the subject and that leads on to an explanation of the name, where what is or is not meant by a name is a matter of contingency and a historical fact. Ever so many disputes over whether some predicate belongs to a given subject or not are on this account no more than disputes about words, since they set out from that form; the underlying term, (subjectum, ὑποκειμενον) is as yet nothing beyond the name.

It has now to be considered more closely how, _secondly_, the _reference_ of subject and predicate stands determined in the judgment, and how they themselves come to be determined at first by just this. For its sides the judgment has totalities generally, and these count at first as {{{ 12.55 }}} essentially self-subsistent. The concept's unity is therefore as yet only a _reference_ between self-subsistent terms; not yet that _concrete_, _fulfilled_ unity which has come back into itself out of this reality, but a unity _outside_ of which they subsist as _extremes_ _not_ _sublated_ _in_ _it_. – The judgment may now be considered starting either from the concept's original unity or from the extremes in their self-subsistence. The judgment is the concept's diremption of itself through itself; _this_ _unity_ is accordingly the ground on which the judgment gets considered in its genuine _objectivity_. To that extent it is the _original_ _division_ of what was originally one; the word: URTHEIL thereby points to what the judgment is in and for itself. That the concept, however, is in the judgment as _appearance_, its moments having there gained self-subsistence, – it is this side of _externality_ that _representation_ keeps rather to.

On this _subjective_ way of considering it, then, subject and predicate are each regarded as finished for itself outside the other; the subject as an object that would be even without having this predicate, the predicate as a universal determination that would be even without belonging to this subject. Judging then carries with it the reflection whether this or that predicate, present in the _head_, can and ought to be _attributed_ to the object that is _outside_ for itself; and judging itself consists in this, that a predicate first gets _connected_ with the subject through it, so that were this connection not to occur, subject and predicate would each for itself still remain what it is, the one a concretely existing object, the other a representation in the head. – But the _predicate_ attributed to the subject is also supposed to _belong_ to it, that is, to be identical with it in and for itself. By this sense of _attributing_, the _subjective_ meaning of judging, along with the indifferent external subsistence of subject and predicate, is sublated once more: this action _is_ _good_; what the _copula_ announces is that the predicate pertains to the subject's _being_, rather than being tied to it in a merely external fashion. Taken in the _grammatical_ sense, that subjective relation which sets out from an indifferent externality of subject and predicate holds good completely; for what get externally connected here are _words_. – It may be adduced on this occasion, too, that a _proposition_ does have a subject and a predicate in the grammatical sense, yet is not therefore already a _judgment_. The latter requires that the predicate stand to the subject in the relation of determinations of the concept, hence as a universal to a particular or a singular. Should what is said of the singular subject itself express only something singular, we have a mere proposition. For instance, Aristotle died in the 73rd year of his age, in the 4th year of the 115th Olympiad, – is a mere proposition, not a judgment. Something of the latter would lie in it only if one of the circumstances, the time of the death {{{ 12.56 }}} or the age of that philosopher, had been thrown into doubt while the figures given were maintained on some ground or other. For in that case they would be taken as something universal, as a time subsisting even apart from that determinate content of Aristotle's death, filled with something else or else empty. Thus the news: my friend N. has died, amounts to a proposition; it would be a judgment only where the question stood whether he is truly dead or merely apparently so.

When the judgment is commonly explained as being the _connection_ _of_ _two_ _concepts_, the indeterminate expression: _connection_ may well be allowed to pass for the external copula, and so may the further point that what is connected is at least _supposed_ to be concepts. Apart from that, however, the explanation is surely of the shallowest; not merely because more than _two_ so-called concepts are connected in the disjunctive judgment, for instance, but rather because the explanation is a good deal better than the thing itself; for what are meant are no concepts at all, hardly concept-, properly only _representation-determinations_; in dealing with the concept as such, and with the determinate concept, it was remarked that what one habitually calls by that name in no way merits the title of concepts; whence, then, should concepts come in the judgment? – Above all, that explanation passes over what is essential to the judgment, namely the difference between its determinations; and it takes even less account of the relation the judgment bears to the concept.

As to the further determination of subject and predicate, it has been recalled that in the judgment they have properly yet to receive their determination. But to the extent that the judgment is the posited determinateness of the concept, that determinateness carries the differences named _immediately_ and _abstractly_, as _singularity_ and _universality_. – And to the extent that the judgment is generally the _existence_ or the _otherness_ of the concept, a concept not yet restored to the unity by which it is _as_ _concept_, there also steps forth the determinateness that is _conceptless_: the opposition of _being_ and reflection, or of _being-in-itself_. Since the concept, however, makes up the essential _ground_ of the judgment, those determinations are at least indifferent enough that, the one falling to the subject and the other to the predicate, this relation obtains quite as well in reverse. The _subject_ as the _singular_ appears at first as what _is_, or as what is _for_ _itself_, in keeping with the determinate determinateness of the singular – as an actual object, even where the object is one merely in representation, – such as courage, right, agreement and so forth – concerning which judgment is passed; – the _predicate_, for its part, as the _universal_, appears as this _reflection_ upon it, or rather as its reflection into-itself, which {{{ 12.57 }}} reaches out past that immediacy and sublates the determinatenesses as merely being, – _as_ _its_ _being-in-itself_. – So far, the starting point is the singular, as first and immediate, and the judgment _raises_ it _into_ _universality_, just as, conversely, the universal that has being merely _in_ _itself_ comes down in the singular into existence, or turns into a _being-for-itself_.

This meaning of the judgment is to be taken for its _objective_ sense, and at the same time for _the_ _true_ meaning of the earlier forms of transition. What is _becomes_ _and_ _alters_ itself, the finite _goes_ _under_ within the infinite; the _concretely_ _existing_ _issues_ _forth_ from its _ground_ into appearance and _founders_; the accident _manifests_ substance's _wealth_ and equally its _power_; within being there is _transition_ into an other, within essence a shining upon an other, through which the _necessary_ reference discloses itself. This passing over and shining has now passed over into the _original_ _dividing_ _of_ _the_ _concept_, which, by leading the singular back into the _being-in-itself_ of its universality, determines the universal no less as something _actual_. One and the same thing is meant by both: that singularity is posited in its reflection-into-itself, and the universal posited as determinate.

To this objective meaning there belongs no less, however, that the differences named, stepping forth again within the determinateness of the concept, are at the same time posited only as appearing, which is to say that they are nothing fixed, but attach to the one determination of the concept quite as well as to the other. Hence the subject too must be taken for the _being-in-itself_, and the predicate, conversely, for the _existence_. The _subject_ _without_ _predicate_ is what, in appearance, the _thing_ _without_ _properties_, the _thing-in-itself_, is, an empty indeterminate ground; it is thus the _concept_ _within_ _itself_, receiving distinction and determinateness only at the predicate, which thereby constitutes the side of the _existence_ _of_ _the_ _subject_. Through this determinate universality the subject stands referred to what is external, lies open to the influence of other things, and thereby enters into activity towards them. _What_ _is_ _there_ steps out of its _being-within-itself_ into the _universal_ element of interconnection and of relations, into the negative references and into actuality's interplay, and this is a _continuation_ of the singular into other singulars, hence universality.

The identity just exhibited, that the subject's determination belongs quite as well to the predicate and conversely, does not fall merely within our own reflection upon it; it is not only _in_ _itself_ the case but stands posited in the judgment as well; for the judgment is precisely the reference of the two; the copula gives voice to this, that the {{{ 12.58 }}} _subject_ _is_ _the_ _predicate_. Determinate determinateness is what the subject is, while the predicate is that determinateness of the subject _posited_; the subject has its determination only in its predicate, or it is subject only there; in the predicate it has returned into itself, and therein it is the universal. – But to the extent that the subject is what is self-subsistent, that identity takes the shape of a relation in which the predicate has no self-subsistent subsistence of its own, subsisting only in the subject; it _inheres_ there. Insofar as the predicate is on this showing distinguished from the subject, it amounts only to an _isolated_ determinateness of it, to but _one_ among its properties, whereas the subject is the _concrete_, the totality of manifold determinatenesses of which the predicate holds one; it is the universal. – On the other side, though, the predicate is likewise universality in self-subsistent form, and the subject conversely no more than one of its determinations. To that extent the predicate _subsumes_ the subject; singularity and particularity have no being for themselves, their essence and their substance lying in the universal. What the predicate expresses is the subject in its concept; singular and particular are contingent determinations at it; the predicate is their absolute possibility. If in _subsuming_ one has in mind an external reference of subject and predicate, representing the subject as something self-subsistent, then subsuming bears on the subjective judging mentioned above, which starts out from the self-subsistence of _both_. Subsumption is on this footing only the _application_ of the universal to a particular or singular, which gets posited _under_ it, on an indeterminate representation, as of lesser quantity.

If the identity of subject and predicate has been so considered that _the_ _one_ _time_ the one determination of the concept falls to the former and the other to the latter, while the _other_ _time_ it holds quite as much in reverse, the identity is by that still only one that _is_ _in_ _itself_; and because the two sides of the judgment are diverse in a self-subsistent way, their _posited_ reference likewise carries these two sides, diverse ones to begin with. But the _differenceless_ _identity_ is what properly constitutes the _true_ reference of subject to predicate. A determination of the concept is essentially itself _reference_, for it is a _universal_; so the very determinations which subject and predicate have, their reference itself has too. It is _universal_, being the positive identity of the two, of subject and predicate; yet it is _determinate_ as well, since the predicate's determinateness is the subject's; and it is _singular_ besides, since in it the self-subsistent extremes stand sublated as in their negative unity. – In the judgment, however, this identity is not yet posited; the copula stands there as the still indeterminate reference of _being_ as such: A _is_ B; for what the concept has as its _reality_ in the judgment is just the self-subsistence of the concept's determinatenesses, of the extremes. Were the _is_ of the copula already _posited_ as that {{{ 12.59 }}} determinate and fulfilled _unity_ of subject and predicate, as their _concept_, it would be _the_ _syllogism_ already.

The _goal_ _of_ _the_ _movement_ of the judgment is to restore this _identity_ of the concept, or rather to _posit_ it. Already _present_ in the judgment there is, on the one hand, the self-subsistence and also the determinateness of subject and predicate as against each other, on the other hand their reference, _abstract_ though it be. _The_ _subject_ _is_ _the_ _predicate_: this, at the outset, is what the judgment asserts; but inasmuch as the _predicate_ is _not_ meant to be that which the subject is, there is a _contradiction_ at hand, one _which_ _must_ _resolve_ _itself_ and _pass_ _over_ into a _result_. Rather, though, _since_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _themselves_ subject and predicate are the totality of the concept, and the judgment is the concept's reality, its onward movement is only _development_; what steps forth in it is already at hand in it, and the _demonstration_ is to that extent only a _monstration_, a reflection as the _positing_ of what is already _present_ in the extremes of the judgment; yet this positing too is already at hand; it is the _reference_ _of_ _the_ _extremes_.

The judgment as it stands _immediately_ is FIRST OF ALL the judgment of _existence_; its subject is immediately an _abstract_ _singular_ _which_ _is_, its predicate an _immediate_ _determinateness_ or property of that subject, an abstractly universal.

As this qualitative character of subject and predicate sublates itself, the determination of the one shines at first at the other; the judgment is now SECONDLY _judgment_ _of_ _reflection_.

This gathering-together, being the more external, passes over into the _essential_ _identity_ of a substantial, _necessary_ _interconnection_; so it is THIRDLY the _judgment_ _of_ _necessity_.

FOURTHLY, once the difference of subject and predicate has within this essential identity become a _form_, the judgment turns _subjective_; what it holds is the _concept_ opposed to its _reality_, together with the _comparison_ _of_ _the_ _two_; it is the _judgment_ _of_ _the_ _concept_.

It is this stepping-forth of the concept that founds the _transition_ _of_ _the_ _judgment_ _into_ _the_ _syllogism_.
