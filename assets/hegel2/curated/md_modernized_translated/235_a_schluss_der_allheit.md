## a. Syllogism of Allness

1. In the syllogism of allness the syllogism of the understanding stands perfected, though it amounts to nothing beyond that. It is indeed an essential requirement of the concept that the middle term here should not be _abstract_ particularity but should be unfolded into its moments and so be a concrete one; yet the form of _allness_ collects the singular into universality only in an outward fashion to begin with, and, conversely, it lets the singular persist within that universality as something still subsisting immediately for itself. Immediacy in the determinations was indeed negated, this being the result yielded by the syllogism of existence, but the negating is merely a _first_ one and does not yet reach the negation of the negation, or absolute reflection into itself. Hence those singular determinations still underlie the reflective universality that gathers them into itself, – or allness is still not the universality proper to the concept, but the outward one belonging to reflection.

Contingency attached to the syllogism of existence because the Medius Terminus there, being some one singular determinateness of the concrete subject, leaves room for an indeterminable multitude of further such Mediorum Terminorum, whereby the subject could be joined by syllogism to indeterminably many others, and to opposed predicates as well. Now, however, that the middle term contains _singularity_ and {{{ 12.112 }}} is thereby concrete on its own account, the only predicate that can be tied to the subject by its means is one accruing to the subject as concrete. – Suppose, for instance, one wanted to infer from the Medius Terminus: _green_, that a painting is agreeable, on the ground that green is agreeable to the eye, or that a poem, a building and so forth is beautiful because _regularity_ belongs to it; the painting, and so forth, might for all that be ugly, by reason of other determinations from which this latter predicate could be drawn. Where, by contrast, the Medius Terminus carries the determination of _allness_, green and regularity are held in it as _one_ _concrete_, which for that very reason is no abstraction of a merely green, merely regular and so forth; and to such a _concrete_ only those predicates can be attached that answer to the _totality_ _of_ _the_ _concrete_. – In the judgment: _The_ _green_, _or_ _regular_, _is_ _agreeable_, the subject is merely the abstraction green, regularity; in the proposition: _Everything_ _green_, _or_ _regular_, _is_ _agreeable_; the subject is instead: all the actual concrete objects that are green or regular, objects taken therefore _as_ _concrete_, with _all_ _their_ _properties_ besides the green or the regularity.

2. But it is exactly this perfection of reflection that turns the syllogism into a mere sham. The determinateness borne by the Medius Terminus is: _all_; and it is to these that the major premise attributes _immediately_ the predicate which gets joined by syllogism to the subject. Yet _all_ means _all_ _the_ _singulars_; so the singular subject possesses that predicate immediately there already, and _does_ _not_ _first_ _receive_ _it_ _by_ _way_ _of_ _the_ _syllogism_. – Or again: through the conclusion a predicate accrues to the subject as a consequence; but the major premise harbours this very conclusion in itself; _the_ _major_ _premise_ _is_ _accordingly_ _not_ _correct_ _by_ _itself_, nor is it an immediate, presupposed judgment, but _itself_ _presupposes_ _already_ _the_ _conclusion_ of which it was to be the ground. – In that much-loved perfect syllogism:

All men are mortal, _Now_ _Cajus_ _is_ _a_ _man_ Ergo Cajus is mortal,

the major premise is correct only for the reason, and to the extent, that the _conclusion_ _is_ _correct_; should Cajus contingently fail to be mortal, the major premise too would fail of correctness. A proposition destined to figure as conclusion has to be correct immediately and by itself beforehand, for otherwise the major premise could not embrace all the singulars; before the major premise may count as correct, the prior question arises whether that conclusion is not itself an _instance_ telling against it.

3. With the syllogism of existence it emerged from the very concept of syllogism that the premises, as _immediate_ ones, stood in contradiction with the conclusion, that is, with the _mediation_ which the concept of syllogism demands, so that the first syllogism {{{ 12.113 }}} had others for its presupposition, while these others in turn presupposed it. With the syllogism of reflection the matter is posited on the syllogism itself: the major premise presupposes its own conclusion, seeing that it contains that linkage of singular with predicate which is precisely what the conclusion was to supply.

What is actually at hand may then be put in this way to begin with: that the syllogism of reflection amounts to no more than an outward, empty _shine_ _of_ _syllogizing_, – that the essence of such syllogizing rests, accordingly, upon subjective _singularity_, which thereby makes up the middle term and has to be posited as this middle; – singularity as it is qua singularity, carrying universality on it only outwardly. – Or, taking the more determinate content of the syllogism of reflection, it appeared that the singular stands to its predicate in an _immediate_ reference and not in one arrived at by inference, and that the major premise, which links something particular with something universal, or more exactly a formal universal with a universal that is in itself, has for its mediation the reference of the singularity contained in it, – singularity as allness. But this is _the_ _syllogism_ _of_ _induction_.
