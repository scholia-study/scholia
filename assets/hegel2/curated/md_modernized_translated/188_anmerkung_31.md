## {{{ 11.376 }}} Remark

To the concept of the absolute, and to the relation which reflection bears towards it as this has here set itself out, there corresponds _the_ _concept_ _of_ _Spinozistic_ _substance_. Wherein _Spinozism_ is a deficient philosophy is this, that _reflection_ and its manifold determining are _an_ _external_ _thinking_. – Substance in this system is _one_ _substance_, a single totality admitting of no separation; determinateness there is none but is held within this absolute and dissolved there; and it counts for a great deal that whatever appears to natural representation, or to the determining understanding, as self-subsistent and hovers before it as such is, within that necessary concept, brought down wholly to a mere _positedness_. – _Determinateness_ _is_ _negation_ – such is the absolute principle of Spinozistic philosophy; this true and simple insight is what grounds substance's absolute unity. But Spinoza halts at _negation_ taken as _determinateness_ or quality; he does not press on to cognize it as absolute, that is, as _self-negating_ _negation_; accordingly _his_ _substance_ _does_ _not_ _itself_ _contain_ _the_ _absolute_ _form_, and the cognizing of that substance is no immanent cognizing. Substance is indeed the absolute unity of _thinking_ and of being or extension; it thus harbours thinking itself, though only in its _unity_ with extension, which means: not as _severing_ itself from extension, hence not at all as determining and forming, nor yet as the movement that comes back and starts out from itself. Partly, thereby, substance is without the principle _of_ _personality_ – the very defect that has chiefly stirred indignation against the Spinozistic system; – partly the cognizing is that external reflection which fails to grasp and to derive out of substance what has the look of the finite, the determinateness of the attribute and the mode, and itself as well, and which is at work instead as an understanding standing outside, taking the determinations up as _given_ and _leading_ _them_ _back_ to the absolute, though never fetching their beginnings from it.

The concepts which _Spinoza_ furnishes of substance are those of the _cause_ _of_ _itself_, – that substance is that whose _essence_ encloses _concrete_ _existence_ within _itself_; – that the concept of the absolute _stands_ _in_ _need_ _of_ _no_ _concept_ _of_ _another_ out of which it would have to be formed; – deep and correct as these concepts are, they are _definitions_, taken up _immediately_ at the entrance of the science. Mathematics, and the other subordinate sciences, have to make their start from something _presupposed_, which constitutes their element and positive foundation. But the absolute cannot be something first and immediate; the absolute is essentially _its_ _result_.

{{{ 11.377 }}} Following on the definition of the absolute, there next _appears_ in Spinoza the _definition_ _of_ _the_ _attribute_; and it is determined as that wherein the _understanding_ _apprehends_ _substance's_ _essence_. Setting aside that the _understanding_ is by its nature assumed to come later than the attribute – Spinoza determines it, after all, as a _mode_ – the attribute, determination as determination of the absolute, is in this way made _dependent_ _on_ _an_ _other_, on the understanding, which enters over against substance as something external and immediate.

Spinoza determines the attributes further as _infinite_, infinite moreover in the sense of an _infinite_ _plurality_. Only _two_ of them, to be sure, actually come up in the sequel – _thinking_ _and_ _extension_ – and no showing is given of how the infinite plurality necessarily narrows itself down to the opposition, and to this determinate opposition, of thinking and extension. – These two attributes are on that account picked up _empirically_. Thinking and being present the absolute in a determination; the absolute itself is the absolute unity of both, so that they amount to unessential forms merely, the order among things running the same as the order among representations or thoughts, and the one absolute being surveyed under those two determinations by nothing but the external reflection, by a mode – now as a totality of representations, now as a totality of things and of their alterations. Just as it is this external reflection that draws the difference, so it is also this reflection that carries the difference back into the absolute identity and submerges it there. This whole movement, however, takes place outside the absolute. Granted, the absolute is itself also _thinking_, and so far the movement is only within the absolute; but, as was remarked, within the absolute it is only as unity with extension, and so not as this movement, which is essentially the moment of opposition as well. – The sublime demand Spinoza makes upon thinking is that it _consider_ all things _under_ _the_ _shape_ _of_ _eternity_, sub specie aeterni, that is, as they are in the absolute. In that absolute, however, which is only the unmoved identity, the attribute, like the mode, is there only as _vanishing_, not as _becoming_, so that this vanishing too takes its positive beginning only from without.

_The_ _third_, _the_ _mode_, is in Spinoza _affection_ of substance, the determinate determinateness, that which _is_ _in_ _another_ _and_ is conceived _through_ _this_ _other_. Strictly, the attributes have nothing but indeterminate diversity for their determination; each _is_ _to_ express substance's totality and to be conceived out of itself; but insofar as an attribute is the absolute as determinate, it harbours otherness and is not to be conceived _out_ _of_ _itself_ alone. It is thus in the mode that the attribute's determination first gets properly posited. This third stays mere mode besides: on the one hand it is something immediately _given_, on the other its nullity goes uncognized as reflection into itself. – The Spinozistic {{{ 11.378 }}} exposition of the absolute is thus far indeed _complete_, in that it starts from the absolute, lets the attribute come next, and ends with the mode; but these three are merely counted off _one_ _after_ _another_, with no inner developmental sequence, and the third is not the negation _as_ negation, not a negation referring itself negatively to itself, through which it would _in_ _it_ _itself_ be the going-back into the first identity, and that identity the genuine one. What is missing, then, is the necessity of the absolute's progress into unessentiality, and equally the dissolving of unessentiality, in and for itself, into identity; or there is wanting the becoming of identity no less than of its determinations.

In like fashion, _in_ _the_ _oriental_ representation of _emanation_ the absolute is the light that lights itself. But it does not merely light itself; it also _streams_ _out_. Its outstreamings are _removals_ from its unclouded brightness; the progeny that follow are less perfect than those before them, out of which they arise. The streaming out is taken only as a _happening_, the becoming only as an ongoing loss. So being grows ever murkier, and night, the negative, is the last term of the line, which does not go back into the first light.

The defect _of_ _reflection_ _into_ _itself_, which attaches to the Spinozistic exposition of the absolute as it does to the doctrine of emanation, is made good in the concept of _Leibniz's_ _monad_. – To the one-sidedness of a philosophical principle the opposite one-sidedness is wont to be set over against it, and, as everywhere, the totality is wont to be present at least as a _scattered_ _completeness_. – The _monad_ is a _one_, a negative reflected within itself; it is the world's content in its totality; the diverse manifold has in it not merely gone under but is _kept_ in a negative fashion; Spinozistic substance unifies all content, yet the world's manifold content _is_ not in that substance as such, but in the reflection standing external to it. The monad is therefore essentially _representing_; finite though it be, however, it has no _passivity_; the alterations and determinations in it are rather manifestations of itself within itself. It is _entelechy_; the revealing is its own act. – With this the monad is also _determinate_, _marked_ _off_ _from_ _others_; the determinateness falls into the particular content and into the way and manner in which the manifestation proceeds. The monad is therefore _in_ _itself_, in respect of its _substance_, the totality, _not_ _in_ _its_ _manifestation_. This _limitation_ of the monad falls of necessity not into the monad as _self-positing_ or _representing_, but into its _being-in-itself_, or it is an absolute limit, a _predestination_ posited by a being other than the monad itself. Further, since bounded things {{{ 11.379 }}} are only in referring themselves to other bounded things, while the monad is at once an absolute shut up within itself, the _harmony_ of these boundings – the reference, that is, of the monads to one another – lies outside them and is, again, pre-established by another being, or _in_ _itself_.

It comes to light that through the _principle_ _of_ _reflection-into-itself_, which makes up the monad's fundamental determination, otherness and outside influence generally are indeed done away with, the monad's alterations being its own positing – yet that, on the other side, passivity through an other has merely been converted into an absolute restriction, a restriction upon _being-in-itself_. _Leibniz_ credits the monads with a _certain_ perfection within themselves, a _sort_ of self-subsistence; they are _created_ beings. – Looking more nearly at their restriction, this exposition yields the result that the self-manifestation which accrues to them is the _totality_ _of_ _form_. A concept of the highest importance is this: that the monad's alterations are represented as actions without passivity, as _manifestations_ of the monad's own self, and that the principle of reflection into itself, or of _individuation_, stands forth as essential. It is further necessary to let finitude consist in this: that content, or _substance_, is held _distinct_ _from_ _form_, and then further that the one is limited whereas the other is infinite. But in the concept of the _absolute_ _monad_ what would have to be found is not merely that absolute unity of content and form, but the nature of reflection besides – namely, as the negativity referring itself to itself, to repel itself from itself, whereby it is positing and creating. In the Leibnizian system there is, to be sure, this further point present as well, that _God_ is the _source_ _of_ _the_ _concrete_ _existence_ and _of_ _the_ _essence_ _of_ _the_ _monads_, that is, that those absolute restrictions in the monads' being-in-itself do not have being in and for themselves but disappear in the absolute. These determinations, however, exhibit nothing but the ordinary representations, left without philosophical development and never raised into speculative concepts. Thus the principle of individuation does not obtain its deeper working-out; the concepts concerning the distinctions among the various finite monads, and concerning their relation to their absolute, do not issue from this essence itself, or in an absolute manner, but pertain to ratiocinative, dogmatic reflection, and have accordingly reached no inner coherence.
