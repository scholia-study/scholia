## 1. Definition

What comes first is this: objectivity, still given as it is, undergoes transformation into the simple form, the form that comes first, and therewith into the form _of_ _the concept_; the moments belonging to this act of apprehension are consequently no others than those belonging to the concept itself, _universality_, _particularity_ and _singularity_. – As _singular_ we have the object itself under the guise of _immediate_ _representation_, the thing awaiting definition. Its universal, the universal of the object, emerged already when the objective judgment, the judgment of necessity, was being determined: it emerged there as the _genus_, and more exactly as the _proximate_ one, that is, as the universal carrying a determinateness that serves at once as principle for the difference of the particular. The subject matter possesses this difference in its _specific_ _difference_, the difference that renders it the determinate species and that furnishes the ground of its disjunction against the species remaining.

By leading the subject matter back in this manner to its _concept_, definition sheds those externalities which concrete existence requires; it leaves aside whatever accrues to the concept when the concept is realized, through which realization the concept emerges, first as idea and then as external concrete existence. _Description_, which addresses itself to _representation_, does take in this further content, the content belonging to reality. Definition, on the contrary, cuts this wealth of determinations — the determinations, manifold as they are, of intuited existence — down to the simplest moments; and what form these simple elements have, and how each is determined against the rest, is something the concept contains. So the subject matter comes to be grasped, as was said, as a universal which at once is essentially something determinate. As for the subject matter itself, it makes the third, the singular, wherein genus and particularizing are set down as one, and it is an _immediate_ that is set down _outside_ the concept, seeing that the concept has not yet reached self-determination.

In those determinations, in definition's difference of form, the concept comes upon itself, and there it possesses the reality answering to it. But since singularity — the reflecting of the concept's moments back into themselves — is not yet included in that reality, and since accordingly the object, so far as it stands within cognition, has not yet been determined as something subjective, it is cognition instead that is subjective and begins from without; or rather, because it begins from without with the {{{ 12.211 }}} singular, it is subjective. Hence what the concept has for content is given and contingent. The concrete concept is thereby itself contingent in a doubled respect: once as regards its content at large, and then again as regards which of the content's determinations — chosen out of the manifold qualities the subject matter carries in external existence — are to be picked out for the concept and to make up its moments.

The latter respect calls for closer scrutiny. For, given that singularity, as being-determined in and for itself, falls outside the conceptual determination proper to synthetic cognition, no principle is available for deciding which sides of a subject matter ought to be reckoned part of its conceptual determination and which belong only to external reality. Here lies a difficulty attaching to definitions, one that this cognition has no way of clearing away. A distinction must nonetheless be drawn. – _First_ _of all_, with products of self-conscious purposiveness the definition is readily found, since the purpose they are meant to serve is a determination born of a subjective resolve, and it is this that makes up the essential particularizing, the form of the thing that concretely exists, which is all that counts here. Whatever else belongs to the nature of its material, or any other outward properties, fall within its determination just so far as they answer to the purpose; the rest are inessential to it.

_Secondly_, the subject matters of geometry are abstract determinations of space; the abstraction underlying them, absolute space so called, has forfeited every further concrete determination, and from here on has only such shapes and figurations as get posited within it; _they_ _are_ accordingly, in essence, nothing but what they _ought_ to be; their conceptual determination at large, and more nearly the specific difference, finds in them a reality simple and unimpeded; to that extent they stand on a par with the products of external purposiveness, agreeing likewise in this respect with the subject matters of arithmetic, where too nothing underlies them but the determination that has been posited in them. – Space, to be sure, carries further determinations still — the threefoldness of its dimensions, its continuity, its divisibility — which are not something first posited in it by outward determination. These, however, count as part of the material taken up, and are immediate presuppositions; only when those subjective determinations get linked and interwoven with this peculiar nature of the soil into which they have been entered do synthetic relations and laws arise. – With determinations of number, since the simple principle of the _one_ underlies them, the linking and the further determining are through and through merely posited, whereas determinations in space — space being for itself a continuous _outside-one-another_ — spread out yet further and possess a reality other than their concept, one that no longer falls under immediate definition.

{{{ 12.212 }}} _Thirdly_, though, the case of definitions of _concrete_ objects, in nature as much as in spirit, looks altogether different. For representation, such subject matters are quite generally _things_ _of_ _many_ _properties_. The task here is first to take in what their proximate genus may be, and next what their specific difference. It has therefore to be settled which among the many properties accrue to the subject matter as genus and which accrue to it as species, and beyond that which of these properties counts as the essential one; and part of the latter task is to recognize how they hang together, whether the one is already posited along with another. Yet no criterion for this is so far available beyond _existence_ itself. – What makes a property essential, for a definition where it is supposed to have the standing of a simple determinateness, still undeveloped, is its universality. In existence, however, this universality is merely the empirical one; – universality in time, that is, whether the property lasts while others show themselves to be perishable within the subsistence of the whole; – or else a universality arising out of comparison with other concrete wholes, and to that extent never getting beyond what is held in common. Should comparison then offer the total habitus, as it empirically presents itself, as the common ground, reflection must gather that habitus into a single determination of thought and take in the simple character of such a totality. But to authenticate that a determination of thought, or some one of the immediate properties by itself, made up the simple and determinate essence of the subject matter, one would need nothing less than a _derivation_ of such a determination out of the concrete constitution. That, however, would call for an analysis converting the immediate constitutions into thoughts and leading their concreteness back to something simple; an analysis of a higher order than the one under consideration, since it would have to be non-abstractive, preserving within the universal the determinate character of the concrete, unifying it, and displaying it as dependent upon the simple determination of thought.

The references borne by immediate existence's manifold determinations to the simple concept would, though, be theorems standing in need of proof. Definition, however, being the first and as yet undeveloped concept, and being meant to take in the subject matter's simple determinateness in an act of apprehension that is itself immediate, has nothing available to it but one of that subject matter's _immediate_ so-called properties, – a determination of sensible existence or of representation; the singling out of such a property by abstraction is then what its simplicity comes to, whereas for its universality and its essentiality the concept is thrown back upon empirical universality, upon persistence through changed circumstances, and upon that reflection which hunts for the concept's determination out in external existence and in representation, that is, hunts for it where it cannot be found. – Defining thus of its own accord gives up any genuine determinations of the concept, determinations that would be, in essence, the {{{ 12.213 }}} principles of the subject matters, and settles instead for _marks_, that is, for determinations whose _essentiality_ is a matter of indifference to the subject matter itself and whose sole purpose is rather to serve an external reflection as _distinguishing signs_. – A single _external_ determinateness of that sort is far too ill-suited both to the concrete totality and to that totality's own concept for it to be picked out on its own and treated as though a concrete whole had in it its true expression and determination. – On _Blumenbach's_ remark, for instance, the earlobe is something wanting in every other animal, so that, going by the customary talk of shared and differentiating marks, it could with full justice be pressed into service as the distinctive character in the definition of the physical human being. But how ill-suited such an entirely external determination at once proves to be when set beside what we represent to ourselves as the total habitus of the physical human being, and beside the demand that a determination of the concept be something essential! Whether the marks taken up into a definition are nothing but such bare makeshifts, or whether instead they come nearer to the nature of a principle, is wholly a matter of chance. Their very externality lets one see, too, that cognition by concepts did not start out from them; rather, an obscure feeling, an indeterminate but deeper sense, an intimation of the essential, went ahead of the discovery of genera in nature and in spirit, and only afterwards was a determinate externality sought out for the understanding. – Having stepped forth into externality within existence, the concept is unfolded into its differences and cannot be tied down without qualification to any one such property. Properties, being the thing's externality, are external to their own selves; it was shown, in the sphere of appearance, when the thing of many properties came up, that for just this reason they turn essentially into self-subsistent matters; spirit likewise, viewed from that same standpoint of appearance, turns into an aggregate of many self-subsistent forces. By this very standpoint, on which the single property or force is posited as indifferent towards the others, that property or force ceases to be a characterizing principle, and with this determinateness as determinateness of the concept disappears altogether.

Besides the diversity of properties from one another, concrete things exhibit in addition the difference between _concept_ and the concept's _actualization_. In nature and in spirit the concept has an external exhibition, and there its determinateness shows up as dependence on what lies outside, as perishability and want of fit. Something actual does accordingly show in itself what it _ought_ to be, but by the negative judgment of the concept it may equally show that its actuality answers only incompletely {{{ 12.214 }}} to that concept, that it is _bad_. Now since definition is meant to state the determinateness of the concept by way of an immediate property, no property exists against which some instance could not be brought forward — an instance whose whole habitus indeed lets the concrete thing to be defined be recognized, while the property taken as its character turns out immature or stunted. In a bad plant, a bad animal genus, a despicable human being, a bad state, there are sides of concrete existence defective or altogether obliterated which in other cases could have been taken, for the definition, as what differentiates and as the essential determinateness in such a concrete thing's concrete existence. A bad plant, animal, and so forth still remains for all that a plant, animal, and so forth. Should the bad too, then, be taken up into the definition, empirical casting about loses every property it wanted to regard as essential, losing them through the instances of monstrous births in which the property is absent: the essentiality of the brain for the physical human being, for instance, through the instance of the acephalous; the essentiality of protection for life and property in the case of the state, through instances of despotic states and tyrannical governments. – When the concept is asserted against the instance, and the instance, measured by the concept, is pronounced a bad specimen, the concept no longer holds its authentication from appearance. But the concept's self-subsistence runs against the very sense of definition, which ought to be the _immediate_ concept, and can therefore draw the determinations of its subject matters out of nothing but the immediacy of existence, vindicating itself only on what it finds already at hand. – Whether the content of a definition holds truth or contingency _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ lies beyond its sphere; formal truth, though — the concurrence of a concept subjectively posited in definition with a subject matter actual outside it — cannot be settled, for the very reason that a single subject matter may also be bad.

Definition draws its content, generally speaking, from immediate existence, and since that content is immediate no justification attaches to it; why it should be necessary is a question its origin has already set aside; in pronouncing the concept as something merely immediate, definition has already given up comprehending the concept itself. What it presents, accordingly, is nothing more than the form-determination of the concept upon a given content, lacking the concept's reflection into itself, that is, _lacking_ _its_ _being-for-itself_.

Immediacy at large, however, comes forth only out of mediation, and so must make its transition into mediation. Or: the determinateness of content that a definition holds is, just because it is determinateness, not merely something immediate but something mediated by its other; definition can therefore lay hold of its subject matter only by way of the opposed determination, and must accordingly pass over into _division_.
