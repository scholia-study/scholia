## Remark

The _determinations_ _of_ _reflection_ were formerly cast in the _form_ _of_ _propositions_, in which it was said of them that they _hold_ _of_ _everything_. These propositions passed for _the_ _universal_ _laws_ _of_ _thought_, lying at the ground of all thinking, absolute and indemonstrable upon their own selves, yet immediately and without contradiction recognized and accepted as true by every thinking that grasps their sense.

Thus the essential determination of _identity_ finds expression in the proposition: _Everything_ _is_ _equal_ _to_ _itself_; A = A. Or, negatively put: A cannot be A and not A at once.

At first it is not apparent why only these simple determinations of reflection should be cast in this particular form, and not the other {{{ 11.259 }}} categories as well, along with all the determinacies of the sphere of being. There would then result propositions such as, e.g. Everything _is_, Everything has an _existence_ _and so forth_, or Everything has a _quality_, _quantity_ _and so on_. For being, existence and so forth are, as logical determinations in general, predicates of _everything_. The category is, by its etymology and by Aristotle's definition, that which is said, asserted, of what is. – But a determinateness of being is essentially a passing over into its opposite; the negative of any determinateness is every bit as necessary as the determinateness is; as immediate determinacies, each stands immediately over against the other. If these categories are therefore cast in such propositions, then the opposed propositions come into view just as much; both offer themselves with equal necessity, and as immediate assertions have at least equal right. The one would thereby require a proof against the other, and these assertions could accordingly no longer have the character of immediately true and incontrovertible propositions of thinking.

The determinations of reflection, by contrast, are not of a qualitative kind. They are determinations that refer themselves to themselves and are thereby at the same time withdrawn from determinateness over against another. Further, since they are determinacies which are _relations_ upon their own selves, they to that extent already contain the form of the proposition within them. For a proposition is distinguished from a judgment chiefly in this, that in the former the _content_ makes up _the_ _relation_ itself, or that the proposition is a _determinate_ _relation_. The judgment, by contrast, displaces the content into the predicate, as a universal determinateness which is on its own account and is distinguished from its relation, the simple copula. If a proposition is to be turned into a judgment, then the determinate content, if it lies for instance in a verb, is turned into a participle, in order thus to sever the determination itself from the relation it bears to a subject. With the determinations of reflection, on the other hand, as positedness reflected into itself, the form of the proposition itself lies close at hand. – Only, in being pronounced as _universal_ _laws_ _of_ _thought_, they still need a subject for their relation, and that subject is: _everything_; or an A, which signifies as much as everything and every being.

For one thing, this form of propositions is something superfluous; the determinations of reflection have to be considered in and for themselves. Then too, these propositions have the lopsided side of taking _being_, _every_ _something_, for their subject. They thereby awaken being once more, and enunciate the determinations of reflection, identity and so forth, of the something as a quality that it would have on it; not in a speculative sense, but so that the something as subject would remain in such a quality as something _that is_, not that it would have passed over into identity and so forth as into its truth and its essence.

{{{ 11.260 }}} Finally, however, although the determinations of reflection do have the form of being self-same and hence unrelated to another and without opposition, still, as will emerge from a closer consideration of them, – or as is immediately clear from them, from identity, from diversity, from opposition – they are _determinate_ _against_ one another, and thus by their form they are not withdrawn from reflection, from passing over and from contradiction. The _several_ _propositions_ put forward as _absolute_ _laws_ _of_ _thought_ are therefore, on closer consideration, _opposed_ _to one another_; they contradict each other and sublate each other reciprocally. – If everything is _identical_ with itself, then it is not _diverse_, not _opposed_, and has no _ground_. Or if one assumes that there are _no_ _two_ _like_ _things_, i.e. that everything is _diverse_ from everything else, then A is not A's equal, and likewise A is not opposed either, and so forth. The assumption of any one of these propositions does not permit the assumption of the others. – Thoughtless consideration of them enumerates them _one_ _after_ _another_, so that they appear in no relation to one another; it has in mind merely their being reflected into themselves, without attending to their other moment, _positedness_ or their _determinateness_ as such, which tears them away into transition and into their negation.
