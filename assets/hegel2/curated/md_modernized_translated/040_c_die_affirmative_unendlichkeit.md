## c. Affirmative Infinity

In the reciprocal determining of finite and infinite just displayed, the passing over hither and thither, the truth of the two is in itself already _at_ _hand_, and nothing more is required than to pick up what lies there at hand. This passing over hither and thither makes up the concept's external realization; in it what the concept contains stands _posited_, though _externally_ and falling asunder; nothing more is required than a {{{ 21.131 }}} comparison of these various moments, from which the _unity_ emerges that gives the concept itself; – as has often been remarked, though here above all the point wants recalling, the _unity_ of infinite and finite is the lopsided expression for the unity as it truly is in itself; but the removal of this lopsided determination must be at hand as well in that expression of the concept which lies before us.

Taken by its nearest, merely immediate determination, the infinite is only as the _going_ _beyond_ the _finite_; by its determination it is the finite's negation; and the finite, correspondingly, is only as that which has to be gone beyond, the negation of itself in it itself, which negation is infinity. The _determinateness_ of the _other_ accordingly _lies_ in _each_ of them, though on the view held by the infinite progress the two are supposed to shut each other out and to succeed one another merely by turns; neither of them admits of being posited or grasped apart from the other: the infinite not apart from the finite, and the finite not apart from the infinite. Once it is _said_ what the infinite is, i.e. the negation of the _finite_, then the finite too has been _pronounced_ along with it; it _cannot_ _be_ _spared_ where the infinite is to be determined. To find the determination of the finite inside the infinite one need only _know_ _what_ _one_ _says_. Of the finite, for its part, it is conceded straight off that it is the null; but its very nullity is infinity, from which it is every bit as inseparable. – Grasped so, the two may seem to have been taken by their _reference_ to their _other_. Suppose, then, they are taken as _without_ _reference_, joined by nothing but an _And_: then they stand facing one another as self-subsistent, each having being only in it itself. What their constitution is on such terms remains to be seen. The infinite placed thus is _one_ _of_ _the_ _two_; but being _only_ one of them, it is itself finite — not the whole, merely the single side; in the term standing over against it, it has its limit; so it is the _finite_ _infinite_. Only _two_ _finites_ are at hand. Its finitude, and hence its unity with the finite, lies in just this, that it is _set_ _apart_ from the finite and so placed as something _one-sided_. – The finite, on its side, placed for itself away from the infinite, is _this_ _reference_ _to_ _itself_, wherein its relativity, its dependence, its transitoriness has been removed; it is that same self-subsistence and self-affirmation which the infinite is supposed to be.

Both ways of considering the matter — which at first appear to start from a differing determinateness, insofar as the first is to hold infinite and finite only in their _reference_ to one another, each to its other, whereas the second holds them in their utter separation from one another — deliver one and the same {{{ 21.132 }}} result; on the _reference_ of the two to one another, a reference that would be external to them and yet is essential, so that without it neither is what it is, each contains its other within its own determination, quite as much as each of them, taken _for_ _itself_ and considered _in_ _it_ itself, has its other lodged in it as a moment of its own.

What this then yields is the – ill-reputed – unity of finite and infinite, – a unity that is itself the infinite, one comprehending within itself both itself and finitude, – hence infinite in a sense other than the one on which the finite is severed from it and set on the other side. Since the two must now be distinguished as well, each of them, as was shown just above, is itself in it the unity of both; two such unities therefore result. Their common element, the unity of both determinatenesses, posits them, being unity, in the first instance as negated, seeing that each is meant to be what it is in their differentiatedness; within their unity, then, they lose their qualitative nature; – an important reflection to set against the representation that will not give up holding infinite and finite fast, inside their unity, by the quality they are supposed to have when taken as outside one another, and that therefore sees in that unity nothing beyond the contradiction, failing to see also how the contradiction is resolved through negating the qualitative determinateness belonging to both; in this way the initially simple, universal unity of infinite and finite is falsified.

Further, however, since the two are now also to be taken as distinguished, the infinite's unity — the unity each of these moments itself is — is determined differently in the one and in the other. What is infinite by its determination has within it the finitude distinguished from it, the former being the _in-itself_ in this unity, while the latter is only determinateness, limit within it — a limit, though, that is the utterly other of the infinite, its contrary; the infinite's determination, which is being-in-itself as such, is spoiled by the admixture of a quality of that kind; and so it is a _finitized_ _infinite_. In the same way, since the finite as such is only non-being-in-itself, and yet by that unity likewise has its contrary within it, it is lifted above its worth, and lifted, so to speak, infinitely; it is posited as the _infinitized_ finite.

Just as the understanding falsified the simple unity a moment ago, so it falsifies the doubled unity of infinite and finite as well. Here too this happens because, in one of the two unities, the infinite gets assumed as not negated but rather as the being-in-itself, upon which, therefore, determinateness and restriction are not to be posited; being-in-itself, so the claim runs, would thereby be brought down and spoiled. Conversely, the finite is likewise held fast as not negated, null though {{{ 21.133 }}} it be in itself, so that in its conjunction with the infinite it would be raised to what it _is_ not, and would thus be infinitized in the teeth of its own determination, which has not vanished but perennially persists.

The falsification the understanding practises upon finite and infinite — holding fast to their reference to one another as a qualitative diversity, maintaining that in their determination they are separate, indeed absolutely separate — rests on forgetting what, for the understanding itself, the concept of these moments is. On that concept, the unity of finite and infinite is no external bringing of the two together, nor an unseemly conjunction running counter to their determination, in which terms separate and opposed in themselves, self-subsistent and having being over against one another, and so incompatible, would be tied together; rather each is in it itself this unity, and is so only as the _sublating_ of itself, wherein neither would enjoy precedence over the other in respect of being-in-itself and affirmative existence. Finitude, as was shown earlier, is only as the going beyond itself; infinity, finitude's own other, is therefore contained within it. Just so, infinity is only as the going beyond the finite; it therefore contains its other essentially, and is thus, in it, its own other. It is not that the infinite, as a power at hand outside the finite, sublates the finite; rather, the finite's infinity consists in sublating itself.

This sublating, accordingly, is not alteration, nor otherness in general, nor the sublating of a _something_. The infinite, as the negating of finitude, is that wherein the finite sublates itself; but finitude has long since been determined as nothing more than existence qualified as a _non-being_. It is therefore only the _negation_ which in the _negation_ _sublates_ itself. Infinity on its side is in this manner determined as finitude's negative, and hence as the negative of determinateness in general, as the empty beyond; its self-sublating in the finite is a coming back out of the empty flight, a _negation_ of the beyond, and this beyond is a _negative_ in it itself.

What is at hand, then, is in both cases one and the same negation of the negation. This, however, is _in_ _itself_ reference to itself, and is affirmation, yet affirmation as return to itself, i.e. by way of the _mediation_ which the negation of the negation is. These {{{ 21.134 }}} determinations are the ones essentially to be kept in view; but the second thing is that in the infinite progress they are also _posited_, and the manner in which they are posited there, – namely not yet in their ultimate truth.

In it, _first_, the two of them are negated, infinite as much as finite, – both alike are gone beyond; _second_, they are also posited as distinguished, each following the other, each positive for itself. We pick these two determinations out comparatively, just as, in the comparison — an external comparing — we kept apart the two ways of considering finite and infinite, namely in their reference and each taken for itself. The infinite progress, however, says more: within it the _connection_ of the two, distinguished though they equally are, is posited too, albeit at first still merely as transition and alternation; a simple reflection on our part suffices to see what is in fact at hand within it.

To begin with, the negation of finite and infinite that the infinite progress posits can be taken as simple, and hence as lying apart, the one merely succeeding the other. If one begins with the finite, then the limit is gone beyond, the finite negated. Now, therefore, its beyond, the infinite, is at hand; but within this infinite the limit _arises_ afresh, and so the going beyond the infinite is at hand. This twofold sublating is nevertheless, in part, merely an external happening and alternating of the moments, and in part not yet posited as _one_ _unity_; each of these goings-beyond is a start of its own, a fresh act, so that they fall apart in this fashion. – But their _reference_ is at hand in the infinite progress too. _First_ comes the _finite;_ _then_ it is gone beyond, and this negative, or the finite's beyond, is the infinite; _third_, this negation is in turn gone beyond, a new limit arises, and once again a _finite._ – Here is the complete movement, closing upon itself, which has come round to that which made the beginning; there arises _the_ _same_ from _which_ _the_ _start_ _had_ _been_ _made,_ i.e. the finite is restored; that same has therefore _gone_ _together_ _with_ _itself,_ has only _found_ _itself_ _again_ _in_ _its_ _beyond._

The case stands the same way with the infinite. Within the infinite, which is the limit's beyond, nothing arises but a new limit, and this new limit meets the same fate: as finite it must be negated. What is again at hand is thus _the_ _same_ infinite that just vanished in the new limit; through its sublating, through the new limit, the infinite has therefore neither been shoved further out nor set at a distance from the {{{ 21.135 }}} finite — for the finite is just this, the passing over into the infinite, – nor set at a distance from itself, since it has _arrived_ _at_ _itself_.

So each of them, finite and infinite alike, is this _movement_ of returning to itself through its own negation; they are only as _mediation_ within themselves, and what is affirmative in both contains the negation of both, and is the negation of the negation. – Being _result_, then, they are not what the determination of their _beginning_ makes them; – on its side the finite is no _existence_, nor is the infinite an _existence_, or a _being-in-itself_ lying beyond existence, i.e. beyond what has been determined as finite. If the understanding bristles so violently at the unity of finite and infinite, that is solely because it presupposes restriction, the finite, and being-in-itself along with them, to be _perennial_; it thereby _overlooks_ the negation of both that is factually at hand in the infinite progress, and overlooks equally that within it they occur only as moments of a whole, and that they emerge only by means of their contrary — but essentially just as much by means of the sublating of their contrary.

When just now the return into itself was considered, both as the finite's return to itself and as the infinite's, an inaccuracy shows itself in this very result, one bound up with the lopsidedness censured a moment ago; the one time the finite, the other time the infinite, was taken as _point_ _of_ _departure_, and only thereby do _two_ results arise. Which of them is taken as beginning is, however, wholly a matter of indifference; with that, the difference which produced the _twoness_ of the results drops away of itself. The same is posited in the line of the infinite progress, unbounded to either side, wherein each of the moments is at hand with an equal alternating occurrence, and wherein it is entirely external at what place one takes hold and makes a beginning. – In that line they are distinguished, yet in the same way the one is only the other's moment. Since finite and infinite are both of them moments of the process themselves, they are _in_ _common_ _the_ _finite_, and since both are equally negated in common within the process and within the result, this result, being the negation of that finitude belonging to both, is with truth called the infinite. Their difference is thus the _double_ _sense_ that each of them carries. The double sense of the finite lies, first, in its being merely the finite _over_ _against_ an infinite that faces it, and second, in its being _at_ _once_ the finite and that infinite facing it. The infinite too carries a double sense: that of being _one_ of those two moments — taken so, it is the bad infinite — and that of being the infinite within which those two, itself and its other, are only moments. How the infinite is in fact at hand, accordingly, is as the process wherein it lowers itself into being merely _one_ of its own determinations — set over against the finite, and hence itself merely one finite among finites — and wherein it sublates this {{{ 21.136 }}} difference of itself from itself into the affirmation of itself, being through such mediation the _truly_ _infinite_.

This determination of the truly infinite will not go into the _formula_, censured already, of a _unity_ of finite and infinite; that _unity_ is abstract, motionless self-equality, and its moments are likewise things having being without motion. The infinite, though, like both its moments, is rather in essence only as _becoming_ — becoming, now, as _further_ _determined_ in its moments. For its determinations becoming has at first abstract being and nothing; as alteration, things existent, something and other; now, as infinite, finite and infinite, and these themselves in becoming.

This infinite, as having returned into itself, as the reference of itself to itself, is _being_ — not, however, being that is abstract and void of determination, for it stands posited as the negating of negation; it is on that account _existence_ too, since it holds negation in general within it, and hence determinateness. It _is_, and it _is_ _there_, present, here and now. The _beyond_ is the bad infinite and nothing else, for that infinite is _merely_ the negation of a finite posited as _real_, – it is thus the abstract, the first negation; being determined _merely_ as negative, it lacks within it the affirmation of _existence_; held fast as nothing but negative, it is even _supposed_ to be _not_ _there_, supposed to be beyond reach. Such unreachability, however, is not its loftiness but its defect, whose ultimate ground lies in this, that the finite as such is held fast _as_ _having_ _being_. What is unreachable is the untrue; and one must see that an infinite of that kind is the untrue. – As image for the progress into infinity there is the straight _line_, at whose two limits alone the infinite lies, and lies always only where that line — and the line is existence — is not, and which runs out towards this its non-existence, i.e. into the indeterminate; taken as genuine infinity, bent back upon itself, the image for it becomes the _circle_, a line that has caught up with itself, closed and altogether present, with no _starting_ _point_ and no _end_.

Genuine infinity, taken in this general way as _existence_ posited as _affirmative_ over against abstract negation, is _reality_ in a loftier sense, – loftier than the reality determined earlier in its _simple_ form; here it has taken on a concrete content. The real is not the finite but the infinite. In this manner reality gets determined further as essence, as concept, as idea and so forth. Yet there is no point in repeating categories as early and as abstract as reality alongside what is more concrete, and in using them for determinations more concrete than they are in themselves. Repetition of that sort — saying, for example, that essence, or that the idea, is the real — has its occasion in this, that to uncultivated thinking it is the most abstract categories, being, existence, reality, finitude, that come most readily to hand.

{{{ 21.137 }}} Recalling the category of reality has a more determinate occasion here, since the negation to which reality stands as the affirmative is in this place the negation of the negation, whereby reality itself comes to stand opposed to the reality that finite existence is. – So determined, negation is ideality; the ideal [^1] is the finite as the finite is within the genuine infinite, – as a determination, a content, differentiated yet not having being _self-subsistently_, having being rather as a _moment_. This more concrete meaning belongs to ideality, and negation of finite existence does not express it fully. – In respect of reality and ideality, though, the opposition of finite and infinite gets grasped so that the finite passes for the real and the infinite for the ideal; just as, further on, the concept is looked upon as something ideal, indeed as something _merely_ ideal, while existence in general is looked upon as the real. On such terms it is of course no help, for the concrete determination of negation set out above, to possess a term of its own for the ideal; within that opposition one slides back into the one-sidedness of the abstract negative that belongs to the bad infinite, and stays put with the affirmative existence of the finite.

[^1]: _Ideale_ carries a further-specified meaning (that of the beautiful and what tends that way) than _Ideelle_ does; the former does not belong here yet; for that reason the expression _ideell_ is the one used here. With reality this distinction does not really obtain in linguistic usage; _Reelle_ and _Reale_ are said with roughly the same meaning; the shading of the two expressions against one another is of no interest.
