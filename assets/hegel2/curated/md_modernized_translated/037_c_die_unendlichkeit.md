## C. Infinity

Taken in its simple concept, the infinite may at first be counted a new definition of the absolute; as the determinationless reference to itself it is posited as _being_ and _becoming_. Out of the series of determinations that can pass for definitions of the absolute, the forms of _existence_ drop away, since the forms belonging to that sphere are for themselves immediately posited only as determinatenesses, as finite ones generally. The infinite, though, counts as absolute without qualification, for it is expressly determined as negation of the finite, so that in it express reference is made to limitedness – of which being and becoming, even if they neither have nor display any limitedness on themselves, might yet perhaps be capable – and limitedness of that sort is negated on it.

By this very fact, however, the infinite has not yet in truth been withdrawn from limitedness and finitude; what matters above all is that infinity in its genuine concept be marked off from bad infinity – reason's infinite from that which belongs to the understanding; this last, however, is the _finitized_ infinite, and it will turn out that precisely in wanting to hold the infinite pure of the finite and remote from it, one only finitizes it.

The infinite is

a. in _simple_ _determination_ the affirmative as negation of the finite;

b. yet in that very way it enters into _reciprocal determination_ with the _finite_; it is the abstract, _one-sided_ _infinite_;

c. the self-sublating of this infinite, and likewise of the finite, as _one_ single process, – is the _genuine_ _infinite_.
