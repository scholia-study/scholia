## c. Form and Content

Form stands over against essence in the first place; there it is ground-reference at large, its determinations being ground and grounded. Next it stands over against matter; there it is determining reflection, its determinations being the determination of reflection itself and the subsistence such determination has. Lastly it stands over against content, and its determinations are once more form itself and matter. Whatever was previously the self-identical term — first ground, then subsistence at large, and at last matter — falls under form's dominion and is once again one of form's determinations.

_First_, content has a form and a matter that belong to it and are essential to it; their unity is what content is. But this unity, being withal _determinate_, or _posited_, sets content over against form; form makes up the _positedness_ and, measured against content, is the unessential. Content is therefore indifferent towards form; form comprehends both form as such and matter; and so content has a form and a matter whose groundwork it constitutes, and which are for it mere positedness.

_Secondly_, what is identical in form and in matter is content, these two counting then as nothing but indifferent, external determinations. Positedness at large is what they are — a positedness that in content has withdrawn into its unity, or its ground. Content's identity with its own self is therefore, on the one occasion, that identity indifferent towards form; on the other, however, it is the identity _of_ _ground_. Ground has at first vanished within content; but content is withal the form-determinations reflected negatively into themselves; hence its unity, at first merely the unity indifferent towards form, is formal unity as well, the _ground-reference_ as such. Content has this reference, then, for its _essential_ form, and conversely _ground_ has a _content_.

{{{ 11.302 }}} The content of ground is thus ground returned into unity with itself; ground is at first essence identical with itself in its positedness; as diverse from its positedness and indifferent towards it, essence is the indeterminate, matter; but as content essence is at the same time formed identity, and this form becomes ground-reference just because the determinations of its opposition are in content posited as negated too. – Content is, further, _determinate_ upon itself: not, like matter, merely indifferent at large, but formed matter, such that form's determinations possess a subsistence that is material and indifferent. Content is, on one side, ground's essential identity with itself in its positedness; on the other, an identity posited over against the ground-reference; this positedness, which attaches to that identity as a form-determination, confronts the free positedness — confronts form, that is, taken as the entire reference of ground and grounded; the latter form is total positedness returning into itself, the former accordingly nothing but positedness as immediate, _determinateness_ as such.

With this, ground has made itself determinate ground at large, and the determinateness is itself doubled: first that of form, secondly that of content. The former is ground's determinateness of being external to content at large, content being indifferent towards this reference. The latter is the determinateness of the content that ground has.
