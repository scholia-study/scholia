## {{{ 21.53 }}} With What Must the Beginning of Science Be Made?

Only in more recent times has the awareness arisen that it is a difficulty to find a _beginning_ in philosophy, and the ground of this difficulty, as well as the possibility of resolving it, has been discussed in manifold ways. Either something _mediated_ or else something _immediate_ has to serve as philosophy's beginning, and neither alternative, as is readily shown, is open to it; whichever of the two ways of beginning one adopts, therefore, runs into its refutation.

A beginning is voiced in the _principle_ of a philosophy too, granted, though what gets voiced is an _objective_ beginning rather than a subjective one, the beginning of _all_ _things_. Some _content_ or other, determined this way or that, is what a principle amounts to – water, the one, nous, idea – substance, monad, and so forth; and should it instead bear on the nature of cognition, counting thereby as a criterion merely rather than as an objective determination, thinking, intuiting, sensing, I, subjectivity itself, then here as well the interest is aimed at the determination of content. The beginning as such, by contrast, remains unnoticed and a matter of indifference, as something subjective in the sense of a contingent manner of introducing the exposition, and hence the need behind the question with what one is to begin remains insignificant beside the need for the principle, since in the principle alone the interest of _the_ _matter_ seems to lie, the interest in what the _true_ is, what the _absolute_ _ground_ of everything is.

What lies behind the modern perplexity over the beginning, though, is a further need, unknown as yet to anyone whose dogmatic business is the demonstration of the principle, or whose sceptical business is the hunt for a subjective criterion against dogmatic philosophizing, and flatly disowned by anyone who sets out as though discharged from a pistol, out of an inner revelation of his own, out of faith, intellectual intuition and the like, wanting himself excused from _method_ and _logic_. Where thinking in its earlier abstract mode cared at first for the principle only as _content_, yet in the course of cultivation was driven to heed the other side as well, the way _cognition_ conducts {{{ 21.54 }}} itself, there the _subjective_ doing likewise gets grasped as an essential moment of objective truth, and the need makes itself felt for method to be at one with content, _form_ with _principle_. Hence the _principle_ is to be the beginning as well, and whatever ranks as prius for thinking is to rank likewise as the _first_ along the _course_ thinking takes.

Our concern at this point goes no further than the manner in which the _logical_ beginning shows itself; the two aspects under which it admits of being taken have been named already, either mediatedly, as a result, or immediately, as a beginning proper. The question that appears so important in the culture of our time, whether the knowledge of truth is an immediate knowledge that begins absolutely, a faith, or rather a mediated knowledge, is not to be discussed in this place. Insofar as such a consideration can be undertaken _provisionally_, this has been done elsewhere (in my Encyclopaedia of the Philosophical Sciences, 3rd ed., Preliminary Notion, § 61 ff.). Here only this much may be adduced from it, that there _is_ nothing, nothing in heaven or in nature or in spirit or wherever it may be, that does not contain immediacy just as much as mediation, so that these two determinations show themselves to be _unseparated_ and _inseparable_ and that opposition shows itself to be a nullity. But as concerns the _scientific_ _discussion_ of the matter, it is every logical proposition in which the determinations of immediacy and of mediation, and hence the discussion of their opposition and their truth, occur. To the extent that this opposition, referred to thinking, knowledge, cognition, assumes the more concrete figure of _knowledge_ either immediate or mediated, cognition's nature at large receives treatment inside the science of logic, while in its further concrete form it falls to the science of spirit and to the phenomenology thereof. Wanting to get clear about cognition already _before_ the science amounts to demanding that the matter be thrashed out _outside_ it; and _outside_ the science that cannot be managed at least in the scientific manner, the only manner in question here.

What makes the beginning _logical_ is that it has to be made within the element of thinking that is freely for itself, _in_ _pure_ _knowledge_. It is thereby _mediated_ in that pure knowledge is the last, absolute truth of _consciousness_. It was remarked in the Introduction that the _Phenomenology_ _of_ _Spirit_, the science of consciousness, is the exposition of how consciousness has as its result the _concept_ of science, i.e. pure knowledge. To that extent logic has for its presupposition the science of appearing spirit, which contains and displays the necessity {{{ 21.55 }}} and hence the proof of the truth of the standpoint that is pure knowledge, as well as its mediation in general. In this science of appearing spirit the starting point is empirical, _sensuous_ consciousness; and this is knowledge that is _immediate_ in the proper sense; there it is discussed what there is to this immediate knowledge. Consciousness of other sorts – belief in divine truths, inner experience, knowledge by way of inner revelation and so forth – proves on the slightest reflection to be very improperly listed as immediate knowledge. In that treatise immediate consciousness is likewise the first and immediate thing in the science, hence the presupposition; in logic, however, the presupposition is what had proved itself to be the result of that consideration – the idea as pure knowledge. _Logic_ is _pure_ _science_, i.e. pure knowledge in the entire compass of its development. But in that result this idea has determined itself to be the certainty that has become truth, the certainty which on the one side no longer stands over against the object but has made it inward, knows it as itself – and which on the other side has given up the knowledge of itself as of something that stands over against the objective and is merely its annihilation, has divested itself of this subjectivity and is unity with its own divestment.

Now, so that from this determination of pure knowledge the beginning of its science may remain immanent, there is nothing to do but to contemplate it, or rather, setting aside all reflections and all opinions one otherwise has, simply to take up _what_ _is_ _present_.

Having _collapsed_ into this _unity_, pure knowledge has sublated every reference to an other and to mediation; nothing in it is differentiated; and with that the undifferentiated itself stops being knowledge; there is present nothing but _simple_ _immediacy_.

An expression of reflection is what simple immediacy is in its own right, since it bears on the difference from what is mediated. In its true expression, therefore, this simple immediacy is _pure_ _being_. If by _pure_ knowledge nothing is meant {{{ 21.56 }}} save knowledge as such, quite abstractly taken, then by pure being likewise nothing is meant save _being_ at large; _being_, and nothing else, stripped of every further determination and filling.

Being figures here as that which begins, exhibited as having arisen by way of mediation – a mediation, indeed, which is at once the sublating of itself – on the presupposition of pure knowledge as the outcome of finite knowledge, of consciousness. Suppose, however, that nothing whatever is to be presupposed and that the beginning is itself to be taken _immediately_: then its only determination lies in its having to be the beginning of logic, of thinking on its own. Nothing is at hand save the resolve, arbitrary as one may well judge it, to consider _thinking_ _as_ _such_. The beginning must accordingly be an _absolute_ one, or, what here amounts to the same, an abstract one; it may thus _presuppose_ _nothing_, may be mediated by nothing and may possess no ground; rather it has itself to serve as the ground on which the whole science rests. What it must be, then, is _an_ immediate pure and simple, or better still, just _the_ _immediate_ itself. Since no determination over against anything else is possible for it, neither can it harbour one within, nor any content, for anything of that sort would be a distinguishing and a relating of distinct terms to one another, and so a mediation. So the beginning is _pure_ _being_.

With that plain account of what belongs, in the first instance, to this simplest of all things, the logical beginning, some further reflections may still be added; their office, however, cannot really be to elucidate or corroborate an account already finished in itself, since they are prompted merely by representations and reflections that may cross our path beforehand and that nevertheless, like every other antecedent prejudice, must find their disposal inside the science itself, so that strictly one would have to refer them to patience for that.

Out of the insight that the absolutely true has to be a result while a result, conversely, has a first truth for its presupposition – a truth that, being first, is on the objective view not necessary and on the subjective side not cognized – recent times have generated the thought that philosophy can begin only with a {{{ 21.57 }}} _hypothetical_ and _problematic_ truth, and that philosophizing can accordingly at first be no more than a searching, a view urged repeatedly by _Reinhold_ in the later stretch of his philosophizing, one to which justice requires granting that a genuine interest underlies it, an interest concerning the speculative character of the philosophical _beginning_. Setting this view out affords at the same time an opening for a provisional grasp of what logical progression means in general, since regard for the forward course is something that view carries within itself right away. And indeed it represents the matter thus, that advancing in philosophy is rather a going backwards and a grounding, through which alone it turns out that what one began with is not merely something arbitrarily assumed but is in fact partly the _true_, partly the _first_ _truth_.

One must grant that it is an essential consideration – one that will emerge more closely within logic itself – that the forward movement is a _return_ into the _ground_, to the _original_ and the _true_, on which what the beginning was made with depends and by which it is in fact brought forth. – Consciousness, for its part, travelling from the immediacy with which it starts, gets brought back to absolute knowledge as the _truth_ innermost to it. And this final term, the ground, is likewise that out of which the first issues, the first that had stepped forward at the outset as an immediate. – Still more is absolute spirit, which turns out to be the concrete and final, highest truth of all being, cognized as freely divesting itself at the _end_ of the development and releasing itself into the shape of an _immediate_ being – as resolving upon the creation of a world that contains everything that fell within the development preceding that result, and that, through this reversed position, together with its beginning, is transformed into something dependent on the result as the principle. What is essential for science is not so much that a purely immediate be the beginning, but rather that the whole of it is a circle in itself, in which the first also becomes the last and the last also the first.

Hence there follows, on the other side, as equally necessary, that what the movement goes back into as into its _ground_ is to be regarded as _result_. Regarded {{{ 21.58 }}} that way, the first is quite as much the ground and the last a derivative; since one sets out from the first and reaches the last, as the ground, through valid inference, that last counts as a result. The _advance_, further, from that which makes the beginning is to be regarded only as a further determination of it, so that what begins remains at the base of everything that follows and does not vanish from it. The advance does not consist in this, that merely an _other_ is derived, or that there is a passing over into a genuine other; – and insofar as this passing over does occur, it just as much sublates itself again. Thus the beginning of philosophy is the foundation present and preserving itself in all subsequent developments, that which remains throughout immanent in its further determinations.

Through this advance, then, the beginning loses the one-sidedness that attaches to it in this determinateness of being an immediate and an abstract in general; it becomes something mediated, and the line of scientific forward movement thereby makes itself _into_ _a_ _circle_. – At the same time it turns out that what makes the beginning, being therein still the undeveloped and contentless, is not yet truly cognized at the beginning, and that only the science, and indeed in its entire development, is the completed, content-filled and first truly grounded cognition of it.

But just because the _result_ only comes forth as the absolute ground, the progress of this cognition is not something provisional, nor something problematic and hypothetical, but must be determined by the nature of the matter and of the content itself. That beginning is neither something arbitrary and merely assumed for the time being, nor something appearing arbitrary and presupposed as a request but of which it nevertheless shows in the sequel that one did right in making it the beginning; it is not as with the constructions one is instructed to make for the purpose of proving a geometrical proposition, where it turns out only afterwards, in the proofs, that one did well to draw just these lines, and then, in the proofs themselves, to begin with the comparison of these lines {{{ 21.59 }}} or angles; taken by itself, in this drawing of lines or comparing, it is not comprehended. Thus the _ground_ why in pure science the start is made from pure being has just been indicated immediately in that science itself. This pure being is the unity into which pure knowledge goes back, or, if this knowledge itself is still to be held as form distinct from its unity, then pure being is also its content. This is the side according to which this _pure_ _being_, this absolutely immediate, is just as much something absolutely mediated. But it must just as essentially be taken only in the one-sidedness of being the purely immediate, _precisely_ _because_ it is here as the beginning. Insofar as it were not this pure indeterminateness, insofar as it were determinate, it would be taken as something mediated, as something already carried further; something determinate contains an _other_ _to_ a first. It lies, then, in the _nature_ _of_ _the_ _beginning_ _itself_ that it be being and nothing else. No further preparations are therefore needed in order to enter into philosophy, nor any other reflections and points of connection.

From the fact that the beginning is the beginning of philosophy, no _closer_ _determination_ or _positive_ content for it can properly be derived either. For philosophy is here at the beginning, where the matter itself is not yet at hand, an empty word or some assumed, unjustified representation. Pure knowledge yields only this negative determination, that the beginning is to be the _abstract_ beginning. Insofar as pure being is taken as the _content_ of pure knowledge, this knowledge has to step back from its content, to let it have its own way and not determine it further. – Or again, since pure being is to be regarded as the unity into which knowledge, at the very summit of its union with the object, has collapsed, knowledge has disappeared into that unity, leaving behind neither a difference from it nor, in consequence, any determination for it. – Beyond that, no something and no content of any kind is at hand with which the more determinate beginning might be made.

But even the determination _of_ _being_ hitherto assumed as the beginning could {{{ 21.60 }}} be left out, so that all that would be required is that a pure beginning be made. Then nothing is at hand but the _beginning_ itself, and it would remain to be seen what it is. – This position could at the same time be offered as an amicable proposal to those who, on the one hand, are not satisfied that the start is made with being, from whatever reflections, and still less with the outcome that being has, namely to pass over into nothing, – and who, on the other hand, know of no other way at all than that a science should begin with the _presupposition_ of a _representation_ – a representation that is thereupon _analysed_, so that the outcome of such analysis then furnishes the first determinate concept in the science. If we too observed this procedure, we would have no particular object, since the beginning, as the beginning of _thinking_, is to be wholly abstract, wholly universal, wholly form without any content; we would thus have nothing at all but the representation of a mere beginning as such. It is therefore only to be seen what we have in this representation.

There is as yet nothing, and something is to become. The beginning is no pure nothing, rather a nothing out of which something is due to issue; being, then, lies contained in the beginning already. Both are held in the beginning, therefore, being and nothing; the beginning is their unity; – or it is non-being that is being at once, and being that is at once non-being.

Being and nothing are moreover at hand in the beginning as _distinguished_, seeing that the beginning gestures at something else; – it is a non-being standing in relation to being as to an other; whatever begins _is_ not yet; it is merely on its way to being. Being is thus held in the beginning as that which withdraws from non-being, or sublates the latter as its opposite.

Yet again, what begins already _is_, while quite as much it also _is_ _not_ yet. The opposites, being and non-being, are therefore in it in immediate union; or the beginning is their _undifferentiated_ _unity_.

Analysing the beginning would accordingly deliver the concept of a unity of being with non-being – in more reflected form, of a unity of being-distinguished with being-not-distinguished – or of the identity of identity and non-identity. One might take that concept for the first, purest, i.e. most abstract definition of the absolute; – and indeed so it would be, were the form of definitions and the name of the absolute at issue at all. On that reckoning, {{{ 21.61 }}} with the abstract concept as the first, every subsequent determination and development would amount to nothing but a more determinate, richer definition of this same absolute. But those who are not satisfied with _being_ as the beginning, on the ground that it passes over into nothing and that from this the unity of being and nothing arises, may see whether they are any better satisfied with this beginning, which begins with the representation of the _beginning_ and with the analysis of it, an analysis that will doubtless be correct but that likewise leads to the unity of being and nothing, than they are with making being the beginning.

But a further consideration is still to be made concerning this procedure. That analysis presupposes the representation of the beginning as familiar; the procedure has thus followed the example of other sciences. These presuppose their subject matter and assume, as a request, that everyone has the same representation of it and may find in it roughly the same determinations that they, through analysis, comparison and other reasoning, adduce and state about it from here and there. But that which makes the absolute beginning must likewise be something otherwise familiar; now if it is something concrete, hence determined within itself in manifold ways, then this _connection_, which it is within _itself_, is presupposed as something familiar; it is thereby given out as something _immediate_, _which_, _however_, _it_ _is_ _not_; for it is a connection only as a connection of distinct terms, and thus contains _mediation_ within itself. Further, with the concrete there enters the contingency and arbitrariness of analysis and of the various ways of determining. Which determinations are brought out depends on what each person _finds_ in his immediate, contingent representation. The connection contained in a concrete, in a synthetic unity, is a _necessary_ one only insofar as it is not found ready-made but is brought forth by the movement of the moments themselves in going back into this unity; – a movement that is the opposite of the {{{ 21.62 }}} analytic procedure, which is a doing external to the matter itself and falling within the subject.

Contained in this is also the more precise point that what the beginning is to be made with cannot be something concrete, cannot be such as contains a connection _within_ _its_ _own_ _self_. For such a thing presupposes a mediating and a crossing over from a first to another within itself, of which the concrete that has become simple would be the result. But the beginning is not itself to be already a first _and_ an other; something that is a first _and_ an other within itself already contains a having-advanced. What makes the beginning, the beginning itself, is therefore to be taken as something unanalysable, in its simple, unfilled immediacy, hence _as_ _being_, as the wholly empty.

If someone, impatient with the consideration of the abstract beginning, were to say that the start should be made not with the beginning but straightaway with the _matter_, then this matter is nothing but that empty being; for what the matter is, this is precisely what is to emerge only in the course of the science, what cannot be presupposed as familiar before it.

Whatever other form be taken in order to have a beginning other than empty being, that beginning suffers from the defects cited. Those who remain dissatisfied with this beginning may set themselves the task of beginning otherwise while avoiding these defects.

But one original beginning of philosophy cannot be left entirely unmentioned, one that has made itself famous in more recent times, the beginning with the _I_ itself. It came partly from the reflection that from the first truth everything that follows must be derived, partly from the need that the _first_ truth be something familiar and, still more, something _immediately_ _certain_. This beginning is in general not a representation of the sort that is contingent and may be constituted one way in one subject and another way in another. For I, this immediate self-consciousness, itself appears at first partly as something immediate, partly as something familiar in a far higher sense than any other representation; something otherwise familiar does indeed belong to the I, but it is still a content distinguished from it and therewith at once contingent; the I, {{{ 21.63 }}} by contrast, is the simple certainty of itself. But the I in general is also _at_ _the_ _same_ _time_ something concrete, or rather the I is what is most concrete – the consciousness of itself as an infinitely manifold world. For the I to be the beginning and ground of philosophy, the separating off of this concrete is required – the absolute act through which the I is purified of itself and, as abstract I, enters into its consciousness. Yet this pure I is now _not_ an immediate, nor the familiar, ordinary I of our consciousness, to which the science could be attached immediately and for everyone. That act would properly be nothing other than the elevation to the standpoint of pure knowledge, on which the difference of the subjective and the objective has vanished. Demanded thus _immediately_, however, such elevation is a subjective postulate; for it to prove itself a genuine demand, the concrete I's advance from immediate consciousness to pure knowledge would have had to be displayed and set out on that I, out of a necessity of its own. Lacking that objective movement, pure knowledge, determined also as _the_ _intellectual_ _intuition_, comes across as a standpoint adopted at will, or indeed as one among the empirical _states_ of consciousness, where everything hangs on this, whether the one person _finds_ it inside himself or is able to bring it about, while another is not. But insofar as this pure I must be essential pure knowledge, while pure knowledge is posited in individual consciousness only through the absolute act of self-elevation and is not immediately at hand in it, precisely that advantage is lost which is supposed to spring from this beginning of philosophy, namely that it is something simply familiar which everyone finds immediately within himself and to which further reflection could be attached; that pure I is rather, in its abstract essentiality, something unknown to ordinary consciousness, something that it does not find within itself. There enters with this rather the disadvantage of a deception, that the talk is supposed to be of something familiar, the I of empirical self-consciousness, whereas in fact the talk is of something remote from this consciousness. The determination of pure knowledge as I carries with it the continuing recollection of the subjective I, whose restrictions are supposed to be forgotten, and keeps present the representation {{{ 21.64 }}} that the propositions and relations that emerge in the further development of the I occur in ordinary consciousness, since it is surely that of which they are asserted, and can be found within it. This confusion, instead of immediate clarity, produces rather only an all the more glaring bewilderment and a complete disorientation; outwardly it has provoked the crudest misunderstandings of all.

As regards, further, the _subjective_ determinateness of the I in general, pure knowledge does indeed strip the I of its restricted meaning of having its insuperable opposite in an object. For this reason, however, it would be at the very least _superfluous_ to retain this subjective posture and the determination of pure essence as I any longer. Yet this determination not only carries with it that disturbing ambiguity, but also, considered more closely, it remains an I that is still subjective. How the science that sets out from the I actually develops makes plain that within it the object holds and keeps the perennial determination of an _other_ for the I, and hence that the I one starts from is not pure knowledge, which has truly surmounted the opposition of consciousness, but is still caught up in appearance.

Here the essential remark is still to be made that, if _in_ _itself_ the _I_ might _well_ be determined as pure knowledge or as intellectual intuition and asserted as the beginning, still in science it is not a matter of what is present _in_ _itself_ or _inwardly_, but of the existence of the inward in _thinking_, and of the _determinateness_ that such an inward has in this existence. But whatever of intellectual intuition – or, should its object be called the eternal, the divine, the absolute, – whatever of the eternal or the absolute _is_ _there_ at the _beginning_ of science, that can be nothing else than a determination first, immediate and simple. Whatever richer name it be given beyond what mere being expresses, the sole thing that can come into consideration is the way such an absolute enters _thinking_ knowledge and the utterance of that knowledge. Intellectual intuition {{{ 21.65 }}} is indeed the forcible rejection of mediating and of demonstrative, external reflection. But what it utters over and above simple immediacy is something concrete, something that contains different determinations within itself. The uttering and the exposition of such a concrete, however, is, as already remarked, a mediating movement that begins from _one_ of the determinations and advances to the other, even if the latter goes back to the first; – it is a movement that at the same time may not be arbitrary or assertoric. What is therefore _begun_ from in such an exposition is not the concrete itself but only the simple immediate from which the movement proceeds. Besides, if a concrete is made the beginning, the proof is lacking which the connection of the determinations contained in the concrete requires.

Suppose, then, that in the expression of the absolute or the eternal or God (and _God_ would hold the most incontestable right to have the beginning made with him), suppose that what _lies_ in the intuition or the thought of these is _more_ than pure being holds: then whatever so _lies_ there has to _emerge_ into knowledge as thinking and not as representing; and let it be as rich as it likes, the determination that emerges into knowledge _first_ is something simple, since in the simple there is nothing beyond the pure beginning; simplicity attaches to the immediate alone, because only there has no advance from one term to another yet taken place. Anything the richer forms of representing the absolute or God are supposed to utter or contain over and above being amounts, at the beginning, to an empty word and to being alone; and this simple thing, carrying no further meaning, this emptiness, is quite simply where philosophy begins.

So simple is this insight that the beginning as such calls for no preparation and no further introduction; and these preliminary ruminations about it could not have aimed at leading up to it, but rather at clearing every preliminary away.
