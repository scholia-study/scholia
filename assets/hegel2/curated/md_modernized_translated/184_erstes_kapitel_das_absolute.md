## {{{ 11.370 }}} Chapter 1. The Absolute

The absolute's simple, solid identity is indeterminate, or rather within it every determinateness of _essence_ and of _concrete_ _existence_, indeed of _being_ at large as well as of _reflection_, has dissolved. To that extent the _determining_ of _what_ _the_ _absolute_ _is_ turns out negative, and the absolute itself shows up as nothing but the negating of every predicate, as the empty. Yet since it has quite as much to be pronounced the position of every predicate, it shows up as the most formal of contradictions. Where that negating and this positing are the business of _external_ _reflection_, what we have is a formal, unsystematic dialectic which with little trouble picks up the sundry determinations here and there, and with just as little trouble displays on the one side their finitude and mere relativity, while on the other, the absolute hovering before it as the totality, it declares of the absolute that all determinations dwell within it – without being able to raise these positions and those negations into a genuine unity. – What ought to be exhibited, however, is what the absolute is; yet this exhibiting can be neither a determining nor an external reflection, whereby determinations of the absolute would arise; it is rather the _exposition_, and indeed the absolute's _own_ exposition, a mere _showing_ _of_ _what_ _it_ _is_.
