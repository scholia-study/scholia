## Remark 1

_The_ _positive_ _and_ _negative_ _are_ _the_ _same_. This expression belongs to _external_ _reflection_, insofar as such reflection sets up a _comparison_ between these two determinations. It is not, however, an external comparison that is to be set up between them, any more than between other categories; rather they are to be considered upon their own selves, that is, what is to be considered is what their own reflection is. And in this reflection it has shown itself that each is essentially the shining of its own self in the other, and is itself the positing of its own self as the other.

Representation, so far as it does not view the positive and negative as they are in and for themselves, may indeed be referred to comparison, so as to become alert [to] the untenability of these distinct terms which it assumes to stand fixed over against each other. Even slight experience in reflective thinking will already notice that when something has been determined as positive, then, as one proceeds further from this basis, the same thing has immediately turned round under one's hands into something negative, and conversely what was determined as negative into something positive, and that reflective thinking gets confused in these determinations and becomes self-contradicting. Unfamiliarity with the nature of these determinations is of the opinion that this confusion is something wrong which ought not to occur, and puts it down to a subjective mistake. In fact this passing over does remain mere confusion, so long as consciousness of the necessity of the transformation is not at hand. – But it is, even for external reflection, a simple consideration that, in the first place, the positive is not something immediately identical but partly something opposed to the negative, and that only in this relation does it have any meaning, so that the negative itself lies _in_ _its_ _concept_, while partly it is upon its own self the self-relating negation of bare positedness or of the negative, and thus itself the _absolute_ _negation_ within it. – Just so, the negative standing over against the positive has sense only in this relation to this its other; it therefore contains that other _in_ _its_ _concept_. But even without relation to the positive the negative has a _subsistence_ _of_ _its_ _own_; it is identical with itself; taken so, however, it is itself what the positive was supposed to be.

{{{ 11.284 }}} Chiefly the opposition of positive and negative is taken in the sense that the former (although by its very name it expresses _being-posed_, _positedness_) is supposed to be something objective, whereas the latter is supposed to be something subjective, belonging only to an external reflection, of no concern to the objective that is in and for itself, and not at all present for it. In fact, if the negative expresses nothing other than the abstraction of a subjective arbitrariness, or a determination made by an external comparison, then of course it is not present for the objective positive, that is, the latter is not upon its own self related to any such empty abstraction; but in that case the determination of being a positive is equally external to it. – Thus, to adduce an instance of how fixedly these determinations of reflection are opposed, _light_ in general passes for the merely positive and _darkness_ for the merely negative. But light, in its infinite expansion and in the power of its disclosing and enlivening efficacy, essentially has the nature of absolute negativity. Darkness, by contrast, as what is without manifoldness, or as the womb of generation that does not differentiate itself within itself, is the simple self-identical, the positive. Darkness is taken as the merely negative in the sense that, being the sheer absence of light, it is not present for light at all, – so that light, in relating itself to darkness, is supposed to relate itself not to an other but purely to its own self, and darkness is supposed merely to vanish before it. But it is well known that darkness dulls light into grey; and over and above this merely quantitative alteration light also undergoes the qualitative one of being determined into colour through its relation to darkness. – So too, for example, _virtue_ does not exist without struggle; rather it is the supreme, the consummate struggle; and so it is not only the positive but absolute negativity; nor is it virtue merely in _comparison_ with vice, but is _upon_ _its_ _own_ _self_ opposition and combat. Or _vice_ is not just _the_ _lack_ of virtue – innocence as well is such a lack – nor is it distinguished from virtue merely for an external reflection; it is opposed to virtue upon its own self, it is _evil_. Evil consists in resting upon itself over against the good; it is positive negativity. Innocence, however, being the lack of the good as much as of evil, is indifferent towards the two determinations, and is neither positive nor negative. Yet at the same time this lack is also to be taken as a determinateness, and on the one side it is to be regarded as the positive nature of something, while on the other side it relates itself to an opposite, and all natures step out of their innocence, out of their indifferent identity with themselves, relate themselves through their own selves to their other and thereby run themselves aground, or, in the positive sense, go back into their {{{ 11.285 }}} ground. – _Truth_ too is the positive, being knowledge in agreement with the object, but it is this self-sameness only insofar as knowledge has borne itself negatively towards the other, has penetrated into the object, and has sublated that negation which the object is. _Error_ is something positive, as an opinion of that which has no being in and for itself, an opinion which knows and asserts itself. Ignorance, however, is either what is indifferent towards truth and error, hence determined neither as positive nor as negative, and the determination of it as a lack belongs to external reflection, or else, taken objectively, as the proper determination of a nature, it is the drive that is directed against itself; a negative that holds a positive direction within it. – It is one of the most important items of cognition to see into and to hold fast this nature of the determinations of reflection considered here, namely that their truth lies solely in their relation to one another, and therewith in this, that each contains the other within its own concept; without this cognition no step at all can properly be taken in philosophy.
