## Remark

Above already (Division I, p. 64), in connection with the moment of existence, namely being-in-itself, the _thing-in-itself_ was mentioned, and the remark was made there that the thing-in-itself as such is nothing but the empty abstraction from all determinateness, of which indeed _nothing_ _can_ _be_ _known_, precisely on the ground that it is meant to be abstraction from all determination. – Once the thing-in-itself is presupposed in this way as the indeterminate, every determination falls outside it, into a reflection foreign to it, and towards that reflection it stays indifferent. This external reflection is, for _transcendental_ _idealism_, _consciousness_. Because this philosophical system transfers all determinateness of things, as regards form no less than content, into consciousness, it falls on that standpoint within _me_, within the subject, that it is I who see the leaves of a tree not black but green, the sun round and not four-cornered, I who taste sugar sweet and not bitter; that it is I who determine the first and the second stroke of a clock as succeeding one another and not as side by side, and the first neither as cause nor yet as effect of the second, and so forth. – The consciousness of freedom immediately contradicts this garish presentation of subjective idealism, a consciousness according to {{{ 11.332 }}} which I rather know myself to be the universal and the indeterminate, sever those manifold and necessary determinations from myself, and cognize them as an externality for me that accrues to things alone. – Within this consciousness of its freedom the I is for itself the very identity, genuine and reflected into itself, that the thing-in-itself was to have been. – Elsewhere I have shown that transcendental idealism of that sort does not get past the limitedness of the I through the object, past the finite world at all, but alters merely the _form_ of the restriction, which for it stays something absolute, in that it does no more than translate the restriction out of objective shape into subjective shape, turning into determinatenesses of the I, and into a wild alternation of them proceeding within the I as within a thing, what ordinary consciousness knows as a manifoldness and alteration belonging to things merely external to that consciousness. – What stands opposed in the consideration now before us is merely the thing-in-itself together with the reflection at first external to it; the latter has not yet determined itself as consciousness, just as the thing-in-itself has not determined itself as the I either. Out of the nature of the thing-in-itself, and out of that of external reflection, it has emerged that this external element determines itself into thing-in-itself, or conversely becomes the very determination of that first thing-in-itself. What is essential in the inadequacy of the standpoint at which that philosophy comes to a halt lies now in this, that it clings to _the_ _abstract_ _thing-in-itself_ as to a _final_ determination and sets reflection, or the determinateness and manifoldness of the properties, over against the thing-in-itself, whereas in fact the thing-in-itself essentially has that external reflection on it and determines itself into something endowed with determinations of _its_ _own_, with properties, whereby the abstraction of the thing, that of being pure thing-in-itself, shows itself to be an untrue determination.
