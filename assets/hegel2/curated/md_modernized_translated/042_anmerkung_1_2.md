## Remark 1

The infinite, – in the customary sense of the bad infinity, – and the _progress_ _to_ _infinity_, like the ought, give expression to a {{{ 21.138 }}} _contradiction_ which passes itself off as the _resolution_ and as the last thing. This infinite marks a first raising of sensuous representing above the finite into thought, though the thought so reached has for its content only nothing, a nothing posited _expressly_ as non-being, – a flight past what is limited that fails to gather itself into itself and cannot bring the negative back round to the positive. Both determinations of the truly infinite stand complete before this _incomplete_ _reflection_: the _opposition_ of finite and infinite, and the _unity_ of finite and infinite, yet these _two_ _thoughts_ it does _not_ _bring_ _together_; each inseparably summons the other, and still reflection lets them do nothing but _alternate_. Everywhere that one persists in the contradiction between the _unity_ of two determinations and their _opposition_, the display of this alternation, the infinite progress, sets in. The finite is the sublating of its own self, it encloses within itself its negation, infinity; – the _unity_ of the two, – one passes _out_ beyond the finite to the infinite as its beyond, – _separation_ of the two; but past the infinite lies a further finite, – the beyond, the infinite, harbours finitude, – _unity_ of the two; but this finite too is something negative of the infinite; – _separation_ of the two, and on it goes. – So too in the causal relation cause and effect cannot be parted; a cause supposed to have no effect is no cause, just as an effect lacking a cause would be effect no longer. Hence this relation yields the infinite progress of _causes_ and _effects_; something gets determined as cause, yet being a finite one (– and finite it properly is just on account of its severance from the effect) it has itself a cause, i.e. it is effect as well; thus _the_ _same_ that was determined as cause stands determined as effect too; – cause and effect in _unity_; – what now counts as effect has once more a cause, i.e. the cause must be _sundered_ from its effect and posited as a distinct something; – yet this new cause is itself again only an effect – cause and effect in _unity_; – something other is its cause; – separation of both determinations, and on into the _infinite_.

In this way the progress can be given a form more peculiar to it; the assertion gets made that finite and infinite are one unity; this false assertion has to be set right by its opposite: they are utterly diverse and mutually opposed; and that in turn calls for correction, through the assertion of their unity, to the effect that they are inseparable, the one determination lying in the other, and so on into infinity. – Light enough is the demand made for insight into the nature {{{ 21.139 }}} of the infinite: to be conscious that the infinite progress, that developed infinite belonging to the understanding, has the constitution of being the _alternation_ of both determinations, that of the _unity_ and that of the _separation_ of the two moments, and then to be conscious further that this unity and this separation are themselves inseparable.

The resolution of this contradiction does not lie in granting the _equal_ _correctness_ and the equal incorrectness of both assertions; – that is only one more shape of the contradiction abiding, – it lies rather in the _ideality_ of the two, wherein, in their difference, as reciprocal negations, they are nothing but _moments_; that monotonous alternation is factually the negation as much of their _unity_ as of their _separation_. Factually present in it, likewise, is what was pointed out above, that the finite falls beyond itself into the infinite, yet just as much finds itself produced anew beyond that infinite, and so within it comes together with none but itself, as does the infinite equally; whereby this very negation of the negation issues in _affirmation_, a result which thereby shows itself to be their truth and their originariness. In this being, then, as the _ideality_ of what is distinguished, the contradiction has not abstractly vanished but stands resolved and reconciled, and the thoughts are not merely complete, they are also _brought_ _together_. On this worked-out example the nature of speculative thinking shows itself in its determinate manner; it consists in nothing but the grasping of opposed moments in their unity. Since each, and factually so, shows on itself that it has its opposite on its own self and in that opposite comes together with itself, the affirmative truth is this unity moving within itself, the taking together of both thoughts, their infinity, – the reference to its own self, not the immediate but the infinite reference.

Those already more at home with thinking have often placed the essence of philosophy in the task of answering _how_ _the_ _infinite_ _goes_ _out_ _of_ _itself_ _and_ _comes_ _to_ _finitude_? – This, it is supposed, cannot be rendered _comprehensible_. The infinite whose concept we have now reached will, as this exposition proceeds, _determine_ itself _further_, and will exhibit on itself, through the whole manifold of forms, what is here demanded, _how_ it, if one likes so to speak, _comes_ _to_ _finitude_. Here we take the question only in {{{ 21.140 }}} its immediacy, and with regard to the sense just examined, which the infinite is wont to have.

Upon the answering of this question everything is supposed to depend, _whether_ _there_ _is_ _a_ _philosophy_, and in pretending still to want the matter to turn on this, one believes at once to possess in the question itself a sort of catch-question, an insuperable talisman, by which one is firm and secured against the answer and thereby against philosophy and against arriving at it. With other objects too, understanding how to _ask_ presupposes a cultivation, and far more so with philosophical objects, if one is to get any answer besides the one that the question is worth nothing. – With questions of this kind an appeal to fairness is customary, that it does not turn on the words, but that in one manner of expression or another what is at stake be intelligible? Expressions of sensuous representation, such as _going_ _out_ and the like, which the question employs, awaken the suspicion that it springs from the soil of ordinary representing, and that for its answering, too, representations current in common life and the shape of a sensuous simile are looked for.

Should being in general be taken in place of the infinite, then the _determining_ of _being_, a negation or finitude on it, seems easier to comprehend. Being, admittedly, is itself the indeterminate, but nothing on it directly expresses that it is the opposite of the determinate. The infinite, by contrast, holds this expressed; it is the _non_-finite. So the unity of finite and infinite seems straightaway ruled out; against this unity, accordingly, incomplete reflection digs in hardest.

But it has been shown, and it is immediately clear even without going further into the determination of finite and infinite, that the infinite in the sense which that reflecting gives it, – namely as standing over against the finite, – has, just because it so stands over against it, its other on it, is therefore already bounded and itself finite, is the bad infinite. And so the answer to the question _how_ _the_ _infinite_ _becomes_ _finite_ runs thus: there _is_ no {{{ 21.141 }}} infinite which is _to_ _begin_ _with_ infinite and only afterwards has need of becoming finite, of passing out into finitude; rather it is on its own account already finite quite as much as infinite. Since the question assumes that the infinite is truly real on one side for itself, and that the finite too, which has gone out of it into separation, or from wherever it may have come, is truly real apart from it, – one would rather have to say that it is this separation which is _incomprehensible_. Neither a finite of that kind nor an infinite of that kind has truth; the untrue, however, is what cannot be comprehended. Yet one must say with equal right that they are comprehensible; to consider them, even as they stand in representation, in such a way that in the one lies the determination of the other, to have the simple insight into this their inseparability, is to comprehend them; _this_ _inseparability_ _is_ _their_ _concept_. – Granted, however, the _self-subsistence_ of that infinite and that finite, the question sets up an untrue content and harbours in itself already an untrue relating of it. It is therefore not to be answered; what has rather to be negated are the false presuppositions it contains, i.e. the question itself. Asking after the truth of that infinite and that finite alters the standpoint, and this alteration will send back upon the first question the embarrassment which the first question was meant to produce; that _question_ of ours is _new_ for the reflection out of which the first question springs, since such reflecting harbours nothing of the speculative interest which goes, for itself and before it relates determinations, to cognizing whether these, as they are presupposed, are anything true. Insofar as the untruth of that abstract infinite has been cognized, however, and likewise of the finite that is supposed to stay standing on its own side, then concerning the going out of the finite from the infinite this is to be said, that the infinite goes _out_ into finitude for the reason that it has no truth, no subsistence on it, grasped as it is as abstract unity; and conversely, on the same ground of its nullity, the finite goes _into_ the infinite. Or rather this is to be said, that the infinite has eternally gone out into finitude, that it simply _is_ not, no more than pure _being_ is, alone for itself, without having its other _on_ _its_ _own_ _self_.

{{{ 21.142 }}} That question, how the infinite goes out to the finite, may contain the further presupposition that the infinite _in_ _itself_ encloses the finite within it, and hence is in itself the unity of its own self and of its other, so that the difficulty bears essentially on the _separating_, as that which stands opposed to the presupposed unity of the two. Under this presupposition the opposition being clung to merely takes another shape; the _unity_ and the _distinguishing_ get severed from one another and isolated. If, however, that unity is understood not as the abstract indeterminate one but, as already in that presupposition, as the determinate unity of the _finite_ and _infinite_, then the distinguishing of the two is present within it already, – a distinguishing which thereby is not a letting go of them into separate self-subsistence, but leaves them within the unity as _ideal_. This _unity_ of infinite and finite and their _distinguishing_ are the same inseparable as are finitude and infinity.
