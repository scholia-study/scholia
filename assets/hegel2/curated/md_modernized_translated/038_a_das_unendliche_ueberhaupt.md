## {{{ 21.125 }}} a. The Infinite in General

Negation of the negation, the affirmative, _being_ that has restored itself out of limitedness – such is the infinite. The infinite _is_, and in a more intensive sense than the first immediate being; it is genuine being; it is the elevation out of restriction. At the name of the infinite, light _dawns_ upon heart and spirit, for therein spirit _is_ not merely abstractly with itself but lifts itself up to its own self, into the light that is its thinking, its universality, its freedom.

What emerged first of all for the concept of the infinite was that existence determines itself as something finite within its being-in-itself, and passes out beyond the restriction. It belongs to the nature of the finite itself to go out beyond itself, to negate its negation, and to become infinite. Accordingly the infinite is not something finished for itself, standing _above_ the finite, such that the finite would have and keep its abode _outside_ it or _beneath_ it. Nor is it _we_, as a merely subjective reason, who pass out over the finite into the infinite. Say that the infinite is reason's concept, and that by reason we lift ourselves above what is temporal – all this one lets happen quite without detriment to the finite, which is not touched at all by an elevation that stays external to it. Insofar, however, as the finite is itself raised into infinity, no alien force whatever does this to it; on the contrary, this is its own nature – to refer itself to itself as restriction, as restriction as such no less than as ought, and to go out beyond that restriction, or rather, being reference to itself, to have negated it and to be out beyond it. Infinity does not come about in a sublating of finitude generally; rather, the finite is nothing but this, to become infinity of itself, through its own nature. Infinity is its _affirmative_ _determination_, that which it genuinely is in itself.

Thus in the infinite the finite has vanished away, and what there _is_, is the _infinite_ alone.
