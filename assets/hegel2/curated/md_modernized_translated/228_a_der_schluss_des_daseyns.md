## A. The Syllogism of Existence

1. In the way it is _immediate_, the syllogism has for its moments the concept's determinations as _immediate_ ones. These are thus form's abstract determinatenesses, not yet worked up through mediation into _concretion_, but only the determinatenesses taken _singly_. _The_ _first_ syllogism is on that account the strictly _formal_ one. Syllogizing's _formalism_ lies in halting at what determines this first syllogism. Sundered into its _abstract_ moments, the concept has for its extremes _singularity_ and _universality_, while the concept itself shows up as the _particularity_ that stands between them. Owing to their immediacy, as determinatenesses referring solely to themselves, they amount taken together to _one_ _single_ _content_. Particularity makes up the middle term, to begin with, only to this extent, that it holds the two moments — singularity and universality — united _immediately_ within itself. Owing to its determinateness, it falls on one side under the universal as subsumed, and on the other side the singular, in relation to which it counts as universality, falls as subsumed under it. Yet this _concretion_ is at first no more than _a_ _two-sidedness_; owing to the immediacy in which the Medius Terminus stands within the immediate syllogism, it is there only as _simple_ determinateness, and the _mediation_ that it constitutes is _not_ _yet_ _posited_. The dialectical movement belonging to the syllogism of existence now consists in this: that the mediation which alone constitutes the syllogism should come to be posited at the syllogism's moments.
