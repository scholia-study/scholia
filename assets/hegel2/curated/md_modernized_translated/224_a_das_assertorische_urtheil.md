## a. The Assertoric Judgment

At first the judgment of the concept is _immediate_; so taken it is the _assertoric_ judgment. Its subject is a concrete singular in general, while the predicate expresses that same singular as the _reference_ of its _actuality_, determinateness or _constitution_, to its own _concept_. (This house is _bad_, this action _is_ _good_.) More closely, then, what it holds is a) that the subject _ought_ _to_ _be_ something or other; its _universal_ _nature_ has posited itself as self-subsistent concept; b) the _particularity_, which is as _constitution_ _and_ _external_ _concrete_ _existence_ not merely on account of its immediacy but because it is expressly differentiated from its self-subsistent universal nature; owing to the self-subsistence of the concept, this particularity is for its part likewise indifferent towards the universal, and may prove adequate to it or fail to. – Such constitution is the _singularity_ that lies outside the universal's necessary _determination_ in the disjunctive judgment, a determination which is only as the particularizing of the _species_ and as the genus's negative _principle_. To that extent the concrete universality issuing from the disjunctive judgment is, in the assertoric judgment, torn in two into the form of _extremes_, and these still lack the concept itself as the _posited_ unity referring them to each other.

For that reason the judgment is as yet only _assertoric_; its _verification_ is a _subjective_ _assurance_. That something is good or bad, correct, fitting or not, and so forth, has its interconnection in an external third. But that this interconnection is _externally_ _posited_ amounts to the same as its being as yet only _in_ _itself_ or _inwardly_. – If something is good or bad and so forth, hardly anyone will therefore suppose {{{ 12.86 }}} that it is good only in _subjective_ _consciousness_ while in itself perhaps bad, or that good and bad, correct, fitting, and so forth are not predicates of the objects themselves. What is _merely_ _subjective_ in the assertion of this judgment thus lies in this, that the interconnection of subject and predicate which is _in_ _itself_ is not yet _posited_, or, what comes to the same, that it is merely _external_; the copula remains an immediate, _abstract_ _being_.

The assurance of the assertoric judgment is therefore met, with just as much right, by the opposite one. Should the assurance run: this action is good; then the opposite, this action is bad, still has warrant just as good. – Or, considered _in_ _itself_: since the subject of this judgment is an _immediate_ _singular_, it has in this abstraction not yet posited _in_ _it_ that _determinateness_ which would hold its reference to the universal concept; it is thus still something contingent, as apt to answer to the concept as to fail of it. The judgment is therefore essentially _problematic_.
