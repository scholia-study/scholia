## β. Restriction and the Ought

In the abstract, to be sure, this contradiction lies right at hand in the finitude of the _something_, or in the fact that the finite _is_. But _something_, being, is posited abstractly no longer; reflected into itself it is, developed as being-within-itself that has determination and constitution in it, and, said more precisely, that has a limit in it – a limit which, immanent to the something and constituting the quality of its being-within-itself, is finitude. Which moments this concept of the finite something contains is what now has to be seen.

For external reflection determination and constitution turned out to be _sides_; determination, though, already held otherness within it as something belonging to the _in-itself_ of the something; on one side the externality of otherness lies within the something's own inwardness, on the other it stays distinct from that inwardness as externality, being externality as such still, yet _in_ the something. Since otherness is now further determined as _limit_, and hence itself {{{ 21.119 }}} as negation of negation, the otherness that is immanent to the something stands posited as the connection between the two sides, and the something's unity with itself – a unity to which determination belongs no less than constitution – is its connection turned back against itself, the connection of its in-itself-existing determination to its immanent limit, a connection negating that limit within it. Being-within-itself, self-identical, refers itself in this way to itself as to its own non-being, yet as negation of negation, negating a non-being which meanwhile keeps existence within it, for that non-being is the quality of its being-within-itself. The something's own limit, posited by the something as a negative that is essential at the same time, is limit as such no longer but _restriction_. Restriction, though, is not the only thing posited as negated; two-edged is the negation, since what it posits as negated is the _limit_; the limit, namely, is quite generally the common element of something and other, and is also a determinateness of the _being-in-itself_ of determination as such. This being-in-itself, accordingly, taken as the negative connection to a limit that is also distinguished from it, as the negative connection to itself qua restriction, is the _ought_.

If the limit that is in something at all is to be a restriction, then in its own self the something must simultaneously _go_ _out_ _beyond_ _it_, must in its own self refer _to_ _it_ _as_ _to_ _something_ _non-existent_. Quietly and indifferently the existence of the something lies, as it were _alongside_ its limit. Something goes out beyond its limit, however, only insofar as it is that limit's sublatedness, the being-in-itself negative towards it. And since within the _determination_ itself the limit is there as restriction, something thereby goes out _beyond_ _its_ _own_ _self_.

So the ought contains a doubled determination: _on the one hand_ determination as in-itself-existing over against negation, _on the other hand_ that same determination as a non-being distinguished from it qua restriction and yet itself in-itself-existing determination at the same time.

The finite has thus determined itself as the connection of its determination to its limit; the determination, in that connection, is the _ought_, the limit is _restriction_. Both are accordingly moments of the finite, and both are therefore finite themselves, ought as well as restriction. Posited as the finite, however, is restriction alone; the ought is limited only in itself, and hence only for us. Limited it is through its connection to the limit already immanent in it, {{{ 21.120 }}} but this limitation of it is wrapped up within being-in-itself, for by its existence, that is, by its determinateness over against restriction, it is posited as the being-in-itself.

What ought to be _is_, and simultaneously _is_ _not_. If it _were_, it would be no mere _ought_ _to_ _be_. Essentially, then, the ought has a restriction. Alien this restriction is not; _that_ _which_ _only_ ought to be is the _determination_, now posited as it in truth is, namely as being at the same time only a determinateness.

The something's being-in-itself in its determination therefore lowers itself to an _ought_, because the very thing which makes up that being-in-itself is, in one and the same respect, present as _non-being_; and this in such fashion that within being-within-itself, the negation of negation, that being-in-itself is, as the one negation (the negating factor), unity with the other negation, which, being qualitatively other at the same time, is limit, so that the unity stands as a _connection_ to it. Not an external thing is the restriction of the finite; rather its own determination is its restriction as well; and this restriction is itself and the ought together; it is what both have in common, or rather that in which both are identical.

As ought, though, the finite now goes further, out _beyond_ its restriction; the very determinateness that constitutes its negation is sublated as well, and is so its being-in-itself; its limit is likewise not its limit.

_Raised_ _above_ _its_ _restriction_, then, something is as _ought_, while conversely only _as_ _ought_ does it have its _restriction_. Inseparable are the two. Something has a restriction to the extent that negation is in its determination, and the determination is likewise the sublatedness of the restriction.
