## 3. The Theorem

1. The third stage of this cognition, as it advances along the determinations of the concept, is particularity's transition into singularity; and singularity is what constitutes the content of the _theorem_. What falls to be considered here, accordingly, is _the_ _determinateness_ _which_ _refers_ _itself_ _to_ _itself_, the subject matter's difference within its own self, and the way the differentiated determinatenesses refer to one another. Only _One_ _determinateness_ is contained in the definition, and in the division the determinateness _against_ _others_; with singularization the subject matter has fallen apart within its own self. Whereas the definition comes to a halt at the universal concept, in the theorems, by contrast, the subject matter is cognized as it is in its reality, within the conditions and the forms which its real existence has. Taken together with the definition it therefore exhibits the _idea_, that is, the unity of concept and reality. The cognition under consideration here, however, still engaged in searching as it is, does not get so far as this exhibition, since with it reality does not issue from the concept, so that reality's dependence upon the concept, and therewith the unity itself, goes uncognized.

By the determination just given, then, the theorem is the properly _synthetic_ element in a subject matter, so far as the relations among its determinatenesses are _necessary_, that is, have their ground in _the_ _inner_ _identity_ that belongs to the concept. What is synthetic in definition and in division is a linkage picked up from outside; what has been come upon gets brought under the form of the concept, yet precisely as come upon the entire content is no more than _monstrated_; the theorem, by contrast, is to be _demonstrated_. Inasmuch as the content of its definitions and of its determinations of division is _not_ _deduced_ by this cognition, one might suppose that it could spare itself the _proving_ of the very relations the theorems enunciate, and here too rest satisfied with perception. Yet what sets cognition apart from bare perception and from representation is precisely the _form_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ as such, which cognition confers upon the content; definition and division accomplish this; but seeing that the theorem draws its content from _singularity_ as a moment of the concept, that content consists in determinations of reality which no longer have for their relation the merely simple and immediate determinations of the concept; with singularity the concept has gone over into _otherness_, into reality, and by that becomes idea. {{{ 12.221 }}} Hence the synthesis which the theorem harbours no longer has the concept's form to justify it; it is a linking of terms taken as _diverse_; the unity not yet posited along with them has accordingly still to be pointed out, and thus proving becomes a necessity for this cognition itself.

The first difficulty to arise here is that of _distinguishing_ with precision which among the _determinations_ _of_ _the_ _subject_ _matter_ admit of being taken up _into_ _the_ _definitions_, and which, on the other hand, are to be assigned to the _theorems_. No principle can be at hand for this; one might appear to lie in the thought that whatever accrues to a subject matter immediately belongs in the definition, while for the remainder, as something mediated, the mediation has first to be pointed out. But a definition's content is a determinate content at all events, and just for that reason itself essentially a mediated one; the immediacy it possesses is merely _subjective_, which is to say that the subject makes an arbitrary start and lets some subject matter pass as presupposition. Now since what is thus taken is a subject matter concrete within itself and one that has besides to be divided, a whole crowd of determinations results, mediated by their very nature, which are accepted as immediate and unproven on no principle whatever, but merely by subjective decision. – Even in _Euclid_, who has always and rightly been recognized as the master of this synthetic mode of cognition, there stands, under the title of an _axiom_, a _presupposition_ regarding the _parallel_ _lines_, one that has been thought to require proof and whose deficiency people have tried in sundry ways to supply. In a good many further theorems presuppositions have been thought to be detectable which ought not to have been taken up immediately but should rather have been proved. As to that axiom about the parallel lines, one may observe that it is just there that Euclid's sound judgement makes itself known, he having weighed exactly both the element and the character of the science he pursued; a proof of the axiom would have had to be drawn out of the _concept_ of parallel lines; but proof of that kind has no more place in his science than has the deduction of his definitions, of his axioms, and indeed of his subject matter altogether, of space itself and of the nearest determinations of space, the dimensions; – seeing that a deduction of this sort can be carried out only from the concept, while the concept lies beyond what is peculiar to the Euclidean sciences, they must for that science be _presuppositions_, relative firsts.

To the same class belong the _axioms_, which this occasion invites us to mention. It is usual, and unjustly so, that they are wont [to] be taken for absolute firsts, as if of themselves, in and for themselves, they called for no proof at all. Were that truly so, they would amount to nothing more than tautologies, for it is only in abstract identity that diversity fails to occur and hence that no {{{ 12.222 }}} mediation is called for. Should the axioms be something more than tautologies, however, then they are _propositions_ belonging to _some_ _other_ _science_, for within whichever science takes them as its axioms they are supposed to count as presuppositions. Properly speaking they are therefore _theorems_, and for the most part they come out of logic. Lemmas of just this kind are the axioms of geometry, logical propositions which do, it is true, approach tautology, in that magnitude alone is their concern and qualitative differences are consequently extinguished within them; the purely quantitative syllogism, which is the chief axiom, was discussed above. – Taken in and for themselves the axioms therefore call for proof no less than definitions and divisions do, and the sole reason they are not turned into theorems is that, as relative firsts, they are accepted as presuppositions from a particular standpoint.

Turning to _the_ _content_ _of_ _the_ _theorems_, a closer distinction has now to be drawn: since that content consists in a _reference_ among _determinatenesses_ of the concept's reality, these references may be relations of the subject matter that are more or less incomplete and singular, or else a relation of the kind that takes in the _whole_ _content_ of the reality and gives expression to its determinate reference. But the _unity_ _of_ _the_ _complete_ _determinatenesses_ _of_ _content_ is one with _the_ _concept_; a proposition holding that unity is therefore itself once more a definition, though a definition expressing the concept not just as immediately taken up but as unfolded into its determinate, real differences, or expressing the concept's complete existence. Both together therefore exhibit the _idea_.

Compare somewhat more closely the theorems of a synthetic science, and _especially_ _of_ _geometry_, and this difference will come out, that a number of its theorems hold nothing but singular relations of the subject matter, while others hold relations of a kind wherein the subject matter's complete determinateness finds expression. To count all the propositions of equal worth, on the ground that every one of them holds a truth and that within the formal course, within the connectedness of the proving, every one is equally essential, is a very superficial view. How the content of the theorems differs is bound up in the closest way with that course itself; a few additional observations upon the course will help to throw further light both on the difference in question and on the nature of synthetic cognition. From of old, praise has been bestowed on Euclidean geometry, which, as the representative of the synthetic method and as supplying that method's most accomplished model, is to serve us here as the example, for the arrangement in the succession of its theorems, by which, for each theorem, the propositions its construction and proof call for are always to be found already proved beforehand. What this circumstance concerns is formal consistency; and important though that is, it concerns rather the external {{{ 12.223 }}} arrangement of expediency, and taken by itself has no bearing upon the essential difference of concept and idea, in which a higher principle for the necessity of the progression is to be sought. – For the definitions with which the start is made take up the sensible subject matter as immediately given and determine it by its nearest genus and its specific difference, and these are likewise the simple, _immediate_ determinatenesses of the concept, universality and particularity, the relation between which is carried no further. The theorems that come first can accordingly cling to nothing but immediate determinations of the sort that the definitions hold; in like manner their reciprocal _dependence_ can at first bear only upon this universal point, that the one is _determined_ at all through the other. Thus what Euclid's first propositions about triangles bear upon is nothing but _congruence_, that is, _how_ _many_ pieces in a triangle _must_ _be_ _determined_ if the _remaining_ pieces of one and the same triangle, or the whole of it, is to be _determined_ _at_ _all_. That _two_ triangles get compared with each other, and that congruence is made to turn upon _coincidence_, is a roundabout way which the method cannot do without, obliged as it is to make use of _sensible_ _coincidence_ in place of the _thought_: _being-determined_. Looked at otherwise, on their own, those theorems hold _two_ parts within them, one of which may be viewed as the _concept_ and the other as the _reality_, as that which brings the former to completion in reality. For that which determines completely, namely, for instance, the pair of sides along with the angle they enclose, counts already _for_ _the_ _understanding_ as the entire triangle; to determine the triangle completely nothing beyond this is wanted; the two angles left over and the third side make up reality's surplus beyond the determinateness of the concept. Accordingly, what those theorems really do is this, that they cut the sensible triangle, which admittedly calls for three sides and three angles, down to the simplest conditions; mention had been made in the definition only of three lines at large which shut in the plane figure and make of it a triangle; it is a theorem that first states expressly the _being-determined_ of the angles by way of the being-determined of the sides, as the theorems that follow state the dependence of three further pieces upon three such pieces. – What holds the magnitude of the triangle wholly determined according to its sides _within_ _itself_, though, is the _Pythagorean_ _theorem_; here for the first time we have the _equation_ of the triangle's sides, whereas the preceding sides carry it, quite generally, no further than a _determinateness_ of its pieces over against each other, and not as far as an _equation_. With this proposition, then, we have the consummate, _real_ _definition_ of the triangle, in the first place of the right-angled triangle, the one whose differences are simplest and which is therefore the most regular. – It is with this proposition that Euclid brings the first book to a close, and rightly, since a consummate determinateness has in fact been reached in it. The second book he brings to a close in the same fashion, having first led the triangles that carry a greater inequality about them, the ones that are not {{{ 12.224 }}} right-angled, back to the uniform kind, namely with the reduction of the rectangle to the square, – an equation between what is self-equal, the square, and what is unequal within itself, the rectangle; in the same way, within the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse answering to the right angle, to what is self-equal, forms the one side of the equation, and the other side is formed by what is unequal to itself, namely the _two_ legs. That equation between square and rectangle underlies the _second_ definition of the circle, – which is once more the Pythagorean theorem, save that the legs are here taken as variable magnitudes; and the first equation of the circle stands in just that same relation of _sensible_ determinateness to _equation_ in which the two differing definitions of conic sections at large stand to each other.

This genuine synthetic progression is a transition out of the _universal_ into _singularity_, that is to say, into _what_ _is_ _determined_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, or into the subject matter's unity _within_ _itself_, so far as the subject matter has fallen apart into its essential real determinatenesses and been differentiated. In other sciences, though, the customary progression, wholly imperfect as it is, tends to run thus: a start is indeed made from something universal, but the _singularization_ and concretion of the universal amounts only to an _application_ of it to material entering from elsewhere; the idea's properly _singular_ element is on this showing an _empirical_ ingredient.

Be the theorem's content the more imperfect or the more perfect sort, it has in either case to be _proved_. What it states is a relation among real determinations which do not stand in the relation of determinations of the concept; where they do stand in it, as can be pointed out in the propositions we have termed the _second_ or real _definitions_, these are on that very account definitions on the one hand, but since their content lies at the same time in relations among real determinations and not merely in the relation of a universal and the simple determinateness, they, unlike a first definition of that sort, both require proof and admit of it. Being real determinatenesses, they carry the form of what _indifferently_ _subsists_ and is _diverse_; they are consequently not one immediately; and so their mediation has to be pointed out. In the first definition the immediate unity is the one whereby the particular lies within the universal.

2. The _mediation_ that now comes up for closer consideration can be a simple one, or it can run through a plurality of mediations. What mediates hangs together with what is to be mediated; but since in this cognition, to which passing over into the opposite is wholly alien, it is not out of the concept that the mediation and the theorem are traced back, the mediating determinations have to be fetched in from somewhere or other, minus the concept of the connectedness, to serve as a {{{ 12.225 }}} provisional material for the proof's scaffolding. Such preparation is the _construction_.

Out of the references belonging to the theorem's content, which may be manifold indeed, only those are now to be cited and brought before representation which are of service to the proof. Not until the proof does this fetching in of the material acquire its sense; on its own showing it looks blind and devoid of concept. Afterwards, in the course of the proof, one certainly grasps that it was to the purpose to draw upon the geometrical figure, for instance, just those additional lines that the construction prescribes; but at the construction itself obedience has to be blind; taken on its own the operation is therefore without understanding, the purpose guiding it not having been declared as yet. – Whether it be a theorem proper or a problem on whose behalf the construction is undertaken makes no difference; in the shape in which it first shows itself _before_ _the_ proof, it is nothing derived from the determination that theorem or problem has given, and hence, for one who does not yet know the purpose, a senseless business, though always a business steered by nothing but a purpose external to it.

What was at first still a secret comes out in the _proof_. The proof holds, as was stated, the mediation of whatever the theorem enunciates as conjoined; only by way of this mediation does the linkage _appear_ as a _necessary_ one. As the construction on its own lacks the concept's subjectivity, so the proof is a subjective business lacking objectivity. For inasmuch as the theorem's determinations of content are posited not simultaneously as determinations of the concept but rather as given _indifferent_ _parts_ that stand to one another in manifold external relations, the necessity yields itself only in the _formal_, _external_ concept. No _genesis_ of the relation that constitutes the theorem's content is what the proof provides; the necessity exists only for insight, and the proof as a whole exists for the _subjective_ _behoof_ _of_ _cognition_. For that reason it is throughout an _external_ reflection, one _proceeding_ _from_ _without_ _inwards_, that is, one that infers the relation's inner constitution from external circumstances. The circumstances which the construction has set out are a _consequence_ flowing from the subject matter's nature; here they get turned round into the _ground_ and into the _mediating_ relations. The Medius Terminus, the third thing in which the terms conjoined within the theorem exhibit themselves in their unity, and which supplies the proof with its nerve, is on that account nothing but something at which the linkage _appears_ and is _external_. Since the _sequence_ this proving pursues runs the other way round from the nature of the fact, whatever within it counts as _ground_ is a subjective ground, from which the fact's nature issues for cognition alone.

The foregoing makes plain the necessary limit of this cognition, a limit which {{{ 12.226 }}} has been misjudged very often. The _geometrical_ science is the dazzling instance of the synthetic method, – but the method has been carried over, unfittingly, to other sciences as well, and even to philosophy. _Magnitude_ being geometry's concern, _formal_ syllogizing is what suits it best of all; and since it is the merely quantitative determination that gets considered there, abstraction being made from the qualitative, geometry can hold itself inside the _formal_ _identity_, inside that conceptless unity which is _equality_ and belongs to external, abstracting reflection. Its subject matter, the determinations of space, are already abstract subject matters of the sort got ready for the purpose of possessing a perfect finite, external determinateness. Owing to this abstract subject matter the science has, on the one side, the sublime feature that within these empty, still spaces colour has been extinguished and the remaining sensible properties have vanished as well, and that furthermore every other interest which speaks more nearly to living individuality is silenced there. On the other side, that abstract subject matter is still _space_, – a _non-sensible_ _sensible_; _intuition_ has been lifted into its own abstraction, space being a _form_ of intuition and yet intuition still, – something sensible, sensibility's very _outside-one-another_, its sheer _conceptlessness_. – One has heard talk enough in recent times of geometry's excellence from this side; – the fact that it has sensible intuition at its base has been proclaimed its supreme merit, and its lofty scientific standing has even been supposed to rest on that, its proofs being said to repose upon intuition. Against such shallowness the shallow reminder must be issued that intuiting brings no science into being, that this comes about solely _through_ _thinking_. The intuitability that belongs to geometry through its still sensible material lends it no more than the side of self-evidence which whatever is _sensible_ possesses generally for spirit devoid of thought. Pitiably, then, this sensuousness of its material has been put down to geometry's credit, though it rather stamps the lowliness of geometry's standpoint. It is to the _abstraction_ of its sensible subject matter alone that geometry owes its aptitude for a higher scientific standing, and its great superiority over those collections of items of knowledge which people are equally pleased to call sciences, which have something concretely sensible and perceptible for their content, and which display but a distant intimation of and allusion to the demands of the concept through the order they attempt to introduce into it.

Because geometrical space is the abstraction and vacancy of being-outside-one-another, it becomes possible for the figurations to be inscribed into its indeterminateness in such a way that their determinations stay outside one another in settled repose and harbour within themselves no transition into the opposite. Geometry's science is thereby the simple science _of_ _the_ _finite_, of what gets compared as to {{{ 12.227 }}} magnitude and whose unity is the external one, _equality_. Yet since in this figuring the start is made at the same time from various sides and principles, and the various figures come about each on its own, comparing them nonetheless brings to light _qualitative_ inequality too, and _incommensurability_. By that, geometry gets driven past the _finitude_ within which its advance was so orderly and secure, out into _infinity_, – into the equating of things that are qualitatively diverse. Its self-evidence gives out at this point, so far as fixed finitude otherwise underlies it and so far as it has nothing to do with the concept and with the concept's appearing, with that transition. Finite science has reached its limit here, since what is synthetic now has its necessity and mediation grounded no longer in _positive_ _identity_ merely, but in the _negative_.

If geometry, like algebra, runs up against its limit soon enough with objects that are abstract and belong merely to the understanding, the synthetic method proves from the outset that much less adequate for _other_ _sciences_, and least adequate of all for philosophy. For definition and division what is fitting has already emerged; here only theorem and proof would remain to be spoken of, but besides the presupposing of definition and division, which already requires the proof and presupposes it, there is a further inadequacy in the _position_ these occupy generally with regard to the theorems. That position is chiefly striking in the sciences of experience, physics for instance, whenever these mean to give themselves the shape of synthetic sciences. The route taken is then this, that the _determinations_ _of_ _reflection_ belonging to particular _forces_, or to other inner and essential forms, which issue from the manner of analysing experience and can vindicate themselves only as _results_, must be _put_ _at_ _the_ _head_, so that in them one may possess the universal _foundation_ afterwards _applied_ to the _singular_ and pointed out within it. Because these universal foundations have nothing to hold them up on their own, one is expected to _put_ _up_ with them meanwhile; but it is only from the _conclusions_ derived that one comes to notice how these constitute the real _ground_ of those _foundations_. The so-called _explanation_, and the proof of the concrete once it has been cast into theorems, turns out partly to be a tautology, partly a muddling of the true relation, and partly, too, to be a muddling that served to hide the self-deception of a cognition which took experiences up one-sidedly, that being the only way it could arrive at its simple definitions and principles, and which parries any refutation drawn from experience by taking up and admitting experiences not in their concrete totality but as an example, and moreover on whichever side is serviceable to hypothesis and theory. With concrete experience thus subordinated to the presupposed {{{ 12.228 }}} determinations, the theory's foundation is darkened and displayed only on the side that squares with the theory; and generally it is thereby made very hard to look at the concrete perceptions without prejudice, for their own sake. Only when the whole procedure is stood upon its head does the whole acquire the right relation, in which the connectedness of ground and consequence, and the correctness of recasting perception into thoughts, can be surveyed. One of the principal difficulties in studying sciences of this sort is therefore _to_ _get_ _inside_ _them_; and that can come about only by _blindly_ _putting_ _up_ with the presuppositions and, without being able to make for oneself any concept of them, often hardly even a determinate representation, at best a muddled picture of the fancy, imprinting on the memory for the present the determinations of the forces and matters assumed, along with their hypothetical shapes, directions, and rotations. Demand the necessity and the concept of the presuppositions before accepting them and letting them pass, and there is no getting past the beginning.

Occasion has been taken above to speak of how unfitting it is to apply the synthetic method to the strictly analytic science. _Wolf_ stretched this application over every possible sort of item of knowledge that he drew into philosophy and mathematics, – items of knowledge partly quite analytic in nature, partly of a contingent and merely workaday sort. The contrast between such readily grasped material, by its nature incapable of any strict and scientific handling, and the stiff scientific circuit and veneer laid over it, has of itself displayed the ineptness of such an application and destroyed its credit.[^1] What that abuse could not do away with, however, was the {{{ 12.229 }}} faith in this method's suitability and indispensability for scientific strictness in _philosophy_; _Spinoza's_ example in presenting his philosophy went on passing for a model long afterwards. In fact, though, it is by _Kant_ and _Jacobi_ that the entire manner of the former metaphysics, and with it its method, has been thrown over. Kant showed, in his own fashion, concerning the content of that metaphysics, that strict demonstration carries it into _antinomies_, whose remaining constitution has been elucidated in the proper places; but upon the nature of this demonstrating itself, tied as it is to a finite content, he did not reflect; the one, however, has to fall along with the other. In his _Anfangsgründe_ _der_ _Naturwissenschaft_ he furnished an example himself, handling as a science of reflection, and by that science's method, a science he meant in this way to claim for philosophy. – While Kant assailed the former metaphysics rather on the score of its matter, _Jacobi_ assailed it above all on the score of its way of demonstrating, and lifted out, with the utmost lucidity and depth, the point that really matters, namely that a method of demonstration of that kind is tied down without qualification inside the circle of the finite's rigid necessity, while _freedom_, that is, _the_ _concept_, and along with it _everything_ _that_ _truly_ _is_, lies on the far side of that circle, beyond its reach. – On the Kantian result, it is the peculiar material of metaphysics that carries metaphysics into contradictions, and the deficiency of cognition consists in cognition's _subjectivity_; on the Jacobian result, it is the method and the entire nature of cognition itself, which lays hold of nothing but an _interconnection_ _of_ _conditionedness_ and _dependence_, and therefore shows itself unequal to what is in and for itself and is the absolutely true. In truth, the principle of philosophy being the _infinite_ _free_ _concept_, upon which alone the whole of philosophy's content rests, the method of conceptless finitude does not fit that concept. The synthesis and mediation belonging to this method, _proving_, gets no further than a {{{ 12.230 }}} _necessity_ set over against freedom, – an _identity_, namely, of what is dependent, an identity that only is _in_ _itself_, whether it be taken as _inner_ or as _outer_, and within which whatever constitutes the reality in it, the differentiated and what has entered concrete existence, stays through and through a _self-subsistently-diverse_ and hence _finite_ thing. In it, accordingly, this _identity_ does not itself come _to_ _concrete_ _existence_ and stays the _merely_ _inner_ one, or else it is the merely _outer_ one, its determinate content being given to it; – on either view it is an abstract thing, does not have the real side upon itself, and is not posited as an _identity_ _determined_ in and for itself; the _concept_, which alone is what matters and which is the infinite in and for itself, is thus shut out of this cognition.

Within synthetic cognition, then, the idea reaches its purpose only so far as this, that the _concept_, in respect of its _moments_ of _identity_ and the _real_ _determinations_, or in respect of _the_ _universality_ and the _particular_ differences, – and further _as_ _identity_ as well, an identity which is _interconnection_ and _dependence_ of what is diverse, – comes to be _for_ _the_ _concept_. This subject matter of its own, however, is not adequate to it; for the concept does not come to be as the _unity_ _of_ _itself_ _with_ _itself_ _in_ _its_ _own_ _subject_ _matter_, _or_ _in_ _its_ _reality_; within necessity the concept's identity is for the concept, yet the _determinateness_ does not itself lie within that identity, standing there instead as a material external to it, a material, that is, undetermined by the concept, in which accordingly the concept does not cognize itself. Generally speaking, then, the concept is not for itself, nor, in respect of its unity, is it at the same time determined in and for itself. That is why the idea does not as yet attain truth in this cognition, the subject matter being unequal to the subjective concept. – But the sphere of necessity is the topmost summit of being and of reflection; of itself, in and for itself, it goes over into the concept's freedom, and the inner identity goes over into the manifestation of itself that the concept as concept is. That this _transition_ out of the sphere of necessity into the concept happens _in_ _itself_ was shown when the former sphere was under consideration, just as it also presented itself as the _genesis_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ at this book's beginning. Here the position held by _necessity_ is that of being the concept's _reality_ or _subject_ _matter_, just as the concept into which necessity goes over now stands as the subject matter of the same. But the transition itself is the same one. Here too it is as yet only _in_ _itself_, lying still outside cognition, in our reflection, that is, being cognition's own inner necessity as yet. Only the result is there for it. So far as the concept is now _for_ _itself_ what is determined in and for itself, the idea is the _practical_ idea, _action_.
