## a. The Singular Judgment

The immediate judgment of reflection now reads once more: _the_ _singular_ _is_ _universal_; only that subject and predicate now bear the sense indicated; put more closely, therefore, it may be worded thus: _this_ _is_ _an_ _essentially_ _universal_.

But a this is _not_ an essentially universal. Positive though that judgment at large is by its universal form, it has to be taken negatively. But inasmuch as a merely positive judgment is not all that the judgment of reflection amounts to, the negation fails to touch the predicate directly, this being no inhering predicate but rather _what_ _is_ _in_ _itself_. Rather it is the subject that is alterable and awaits determining. The negative judgment must accordingly be caught here in this shape: _not_ _a_ _this_ _is_ _a_ _universal_ _of_ {{{ 12.73 }}} reflection; an _in-itself_ such as this has a concrete existence of wider universality than what lies in one single this. The singular judgment thus has its next truth in the _particular_ one.
