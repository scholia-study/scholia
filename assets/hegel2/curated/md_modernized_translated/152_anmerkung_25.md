## Remark

If reflection upon determinate grounds holds to that form of ground which has here come to light, then the citing of a ground stays a mere formalism, an empty tautology: what it puts into the form of reflection-into-self, of essentiality, is a content already at hand in the form of immediate existence taken as posited. Such an adducing of grounds is accordingly attended by the same emptiness as talk conducted according to the proposition of identity. The sciences, above all the physical ones, are stuffed with tautologies of this sort, which pass, as it were, for a prerogative of science. – For instance, the ground given for the planets moving about the sun is _the_ _attractive_ _force_ that earth and sun exert upon one another. As regards content, nothing else is thereby pronounced than what the phenomenon itself contains, namely the reference of these bodies to one another in their motion, only pronounced in the form of a determination reflected into itself, in the form of force. If it is then asked what sort of force the attractive force may be, the answer runs that it is the force which brings it about that the earth moves about the sun; in other words, its content is throughout the same as the existence whose ground it is supposed to be; the reference of earth and sun in respect of motion is the identical groundwork of ground and of grounded. – If a form of crystallization gets explained by having its ground in the particular arrangement that the molecules take up towards one another, then the crystallization that exists is that very arrangement, expressed now as ground. In everyday life such aetiologies, on which the sciences hold a privilege, are taken for what they are, for tautological, empty chatter. If, to the question why this man is travelling to town, the ground given is that in the town there is an attractive force which {{{ 11.305 }}} drives him there, such a manner of answering, sanctioned though it be in the sciences, passes for absurd. – _Leibnitz_ reproached the _Newtonian_ attractive force with being such a hidden quality as the schoolmen employed for the business of explaining. One would sooner have to reproach it with the opposite, with being a quality _too_ _well_ _known_; for it has no content other than the appearance itself. – What precisely recommends this manner of explaining is its great clarity and comprehensibility; for nothing is clearer or more comprehensible than that a plant, say, has its ground in a vegetative force, that is, in a force that brings plants forth. – An _occult_ quality it could be called only in the sense that ground is supposed to have a content _other_ than what is to be explained; such a content is not given; to that extent the force employed for explaining is indeed a hidden ground, seeing that a ground of the kind demanded has _not_ been given. This formalism explains as little as the nature of a plant is cognized by my saying that it is a plant; clear though that proposition be, or the proposition that the plant has its ground in a plant-producing force, one may on that account call such a way of explaining a very _occult_ one.

_Secondly_, _as_ _regards_ _form_, there occur in this manner of explaining the two _opposed_ _directions_ of the _ground-reference_, without their determinate relation being recognized. Ground is on the one part ground, as the content-determination of the existence it grounds reflected into itself; on the other part it is what is posited. It is that out of which existence is to be comprehended; but _conversely_ it is _from_ _existence_ _that_ _ground_ _is_ _inferred_, and ground is comprehended out of existence. For the chief business of this reflection consists precisely in finding the grounds out of existence, that is, in transposing immediate existence into the form of reflectedness; and so ground, far from being in and for itself, far from being self-subsistent, turns out rather to be what is posited and derived. Since by this procedure ground is fitted up to suit the phenomenon, and its determinations rest upon the phenomenon, the latter of course flows out of its ground quite smoothly and with a favourable wind. But cognition has not thereby stirred from the spot; it drives about within a difference of form which this very procedure inverts and sublates. One of the chief difficulties in getting oneself schooled in the sciences where this procedure prevails rests, therefore, on this perversity of position: putting first, as ground, what is in fact derived, and then, in advancing to the consequences, first stating in them the ground of those alleged grounds. The exposition begins with the grounds; they are set up in mid-air as principles {{{ 11.306 }}} and first concepts; they are simple determinations lacking all necessity in and for their own selves; upon them what follows is to be founded. Whoever, then, would penetrate into sciences of this kind must begin by inculcating those grounds in himself; a business that comes hard to reason, since it is asked to let what is groundless pass for a groundwork. He fares best who, without much reflecting, puts up with the principles as _given_ and from then on uses them as ground-rules of his understanding. Without this method no beginning can be gained; just as little can any advance be made without it. This advance, however, now obstructs itself, in that within those principles the recoil of the method comes to light — a method which means to point out in the sequel what is derived, though the sequel in fact contains the grounds of those very presuppositions. Further, because what follows shows itself to be the existence out of which ground was derived, the relation in which the phenomenon is paraded gives rise to mistrust of the exposition of it; for the phenomenon does not show itself expressed in its immediacy but as a documentary proof of the ground. But since ground is in its turn derived from the phenomenon, one rather demands to see the phenomenon in its immediacy, so as to be able to judge the ground by it. In an exposition of this kind, where what properly grounds occurs as derived, one accordingly does not know where one stands, either with ground or with phenomenon. The uncertainty is increased — especially when the delivery is not strictly consequent but rather _honest_ — by traces and circumstances of the phenomenon betraying themselves everywhere which point to more, and often to something quite other, than is merely contained in the principles. The confusion finally grows greater still when reflected and merely hypothetical determinations are mixed in with immediate determinations of the phenomenon itself, the former being pronounced in a manner suggesting that they belong to immediate experience. So many a one who approaches these sciences in honest faith may well be of the opinion that the molecules, the empty interstices, centrifugal force, the ether, the isolated ray of light, electrical and magnetic _matter_, and a host of the like, are things or relations which, from the way they are spoken of as immediate determinations of existence, really are at hand _in_ _perception_. They serve as first grounds for other things, are pronounced as actualities and confidently applied; on good faith one lets them count as such, before one becomes aware that they are rather determinations inferred from the very things they are meant to ground — hypotheses and fabrications that an uncritical reflection has derived. In fact one moves within a sort of witches' circle where existence-determinations and reflection-determinations, ground and grounded, phenomena and phantoms run about among one another in {{{ 11.307 }}} unseparated company and all enjoy equal rank.

Alongside the formal business of explaining in this way out of grounds, and for all the explaining out of forces and matters that are well known, one hears it said again that of these forces and matters _the_ _inner_ _essence_ itself is something we _do_ _not_ _know_. In this there is only the confession to be seen that this grounding is by itself wholly inadequate, that it itself demands something quite other than such grounds. Only then it is not to be seen why this trouble was taken with such explaining at all, why the other was not sought instead, or why that explaining was not at least set aside and a halt made at the simple facts.
