## a. The Positive Judgment

1. Subject and predicate, as was recalled, are at first only names; their actual determination is won solely through the judgment's own course. As sides of the judgment, which is the _posited_ determinate concept, they do have the determination of its moments; but for immediacy's sake that determination is still quite _simple_, on the one hand not yet enriched by mediation, on the other hand taken at first under the abstract opposition, as _abstract_ _singularity_ and _universality_. – Taking the predicate first, it is the _abstract_ universal; yet since the abstract is conditioned by mediation, by the sublating of the singular or the particular, to that extent it is nothing more than a _presupposition_. Within the sphere of the concept no other _immediacy_ can be had than one which _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ holds mediation within it and has arisen only by sublating that mediation, that is, the _universal_ one. Thus _qualitative_ _being_ too, in its own _concept_, is a universal; as _being_, however, immediacy is not yet _posited_ in this way; only as _universality_ is it that concept-determination in which it is _posited_ that negativity belongs to it essentially. This reference is at hand in the judgment, where universality is the predicate of a subject. – Likewise the subject is an _abstract_ singular; that is, the _immediate_ which is supposed to be _as_ _such_; hence it is supposed to be the singular as a _something_ in general. So far the subject makes up the judgment's abstract side, the side by which the concept in it has gone over _into_ _externality_. – As the two {{{ 12.61 }}} concept-determinations are determined, so likewise is their reference, the _is_, the copula; it too can bear no other meaning than that of an immediate, abstract _being_. Because this reference as yet holds neither mediation nor negation in it, the judgment goes by the name of the _positive_ one.

2. Hence the immediate pure expression which the positive judgment receives is the proposition:

+ The singular is universal.

One must not construe this expression as: A is B; for A and B are names wholly devoid of form and hence of meaning, whereas the judgment as such, and thus already the judgment of existence, has concept-determinations for its extremes. A is B can stand for any bare _proposition_ quite as well as for a _judgment_. In every judgment, though, even in one whose form is more richly determined, it is this determinate content that gets asserted: _the_ _singular_ _is_ _universal_; namely insofar as every judgment is also abstract judgment in general. Of the negative judgment, how far it too falls under this expression, there will be talk directly. – If people do not otherwise think of it that with every judgment, at least with every positive one to begin with, the assertion is made that the singular is a universal, this comes about partly because the _determinate_ _form_ by which subject and predicate differ gets overlooked, – the judgment being supposed to be nothing but the reference of _two_ concepts, – and partly perhaps also because the judgment's further _content_: _Cajus_ _is_ _learned_, _or_ _the_ _rose_ _is_ _red_, floats before a consciousness which, busied with the representation of Cajus and so forth, does not reflect on the form, – though content of that sort, such as the _logical_ _Cajus_ who habitually has to serve for the example, is at any rate a very slightly interesting content, and is indeed picked just so uninteresting in order that it may not draw attention off the form and onto itself.

According to the objective meaning, the proposition _that_ _the_ _singular_ _is_ _universal_ marks, as was mentioned in passing before, partly the transitoriness of singular things, partly their positive subsistence in the concept in general. The concept itself is imperishable; but whatever steps out of it in its partition is exposed to alteration and to the going back into its _universal_ nature. Conversely, however, the universal gives itself an _existence_. As essence goes forth into the _shine_ of its determinations, ground into the _appearance_ of concrete existence, substance into manifestation in its accidents, so the universal _resolves_ itself into the singular; the judgment is this _disclosure_ of it, the _development_ of the negativity which the universal already is in itself. – This last the converse proposition states: _the_ _universal_ _is_ _singular_, and it too is pronounced in the positive judgment. The subject, at first the _immediately_ _singular_, stands in the judgment referred to its _other_, namely to the universal; with that it is posited as the _concrete_; on the side of being, as a something {{{ 12.62 }}} _of_ _many_ _qualities_; – or, as reflection's concrete, _a_ _thing_ _with_ _manifold_ _properties_, _an_ _actual_ _with_ _manifold_ _possibilities_, _a_ _substance_ _with_ _accidents_ _of_ _just_ _that_ _sort_. Since these manifold determinations here belong to the subject of the judgment, the something, or the thing and so forth, is reflected into itself in its qualities, properties, or accidents, or is _continuing_ itself right through them; holding itself in them, and them just as much in itself. Positedness, or determinateness, belongs to being-in-and-for-itself. Hence the subject is, on its own part, the _universal_. – The predicate, on the other hand, being not a real or concrete but this _abstract_ _universality_, is over against the subject the _determinateness_, and holds only _one_ _moment_ of the subject's totality, shutting out the rest. Because of this negativity, which as an extreme of the judgment refers itself at the same time to itself, the predicate is an _abstractly-singular_. – In the proposition _the_ _rose_ _is_ _fragrant_, for instance, it brings out only _one_ _of_ _the_ _many_ properties of the rose; it singularizes that property, grown together in the subject with the rest, in the way that, when the thing dissolves, the manifold properties inherent in it are _singularized_ by making themselves self-subsistent as _matters_. Taken from this side, the judgment's proposition therefore reads: _the_ _universal_ _is_ _singular_.

If we put together this _reciprocal_ _determination_ of subject and predicate in the judgment, a twofold result comes out: 1) that the subject is indeed immediately what is, or the singular, while the predicate is the universal. But since the judgment is the _reference_ of the two, and the subject is determined by the predicate as universal, the subject is the universal; 2) the predicate gets its determination in the subject; for what it is is no determination _as_ _such_, but one _of_ _the_ _subject_; the rose is fragrant; this sweet scent is no indeterminate one whatever, but the rose's; the predicate is accordingly _a_ _singular_. – Now since subject and predicate stand in the relation of the judgment, they are supposed to stay opposed as regards the concept-determinations; as also in causality's _reciprocity_, so long as it has not reached its truth, both sides are meant to stay self-subsistent and opposed despite the sameness of their determination. If, then, the subject is determined as universal, the predicate's own determination of universality must not be taken up along with it, for otherwise no judgment would be at hand, but only its determination of singularity; just as, so far as the subject is determined as singular, the predicate has to be taken as universal. – If one reflects on that bare identity, two identical propositions come forward:

The singular is singular,

The universal is universal, wherein the judgment's determinations have fallen quite apart, only their reference to themselves being expressed, while their reference {{{ 12.63 }}} to one another is dissolved and the judgment thereby would be sublated. – Of those two propositions the one, _the_ _universal_ _is_ _singular_, states the judgment by its _content_, which in the predicate is a singularized determination but in the subject the totality of such; the other, _the_ _singular_ _is_ _universal_, states the _form_, which that proposition itself immediately gives. – Within the immediate positive judgment the extremes remain simple, and form and content are accordingly not yet apart. Or, put otherwise, it does not consist of two propositions; the doubled reference that came out in it immediately makes up the _one_ positive judgment. For its extremes are a) the self-subsistent, abstract determinations of the judgment, b) each side gets determined by the other in virtue of the copula that refers them. _In_ _itself_, though, the difference of form and content is present in it on that account, as has come out; and what the first proposition, the singular is universal, holds belongs to the form, for what it states is the judgment's _immediate_ _determinateness_. The relation that the other proposition expresses, by contrast, _the_ _universal_ _is_ _singular_, or the determining of the subject as universal and of the predicate as particular or singular, bears on the _content_, since its determinations first rise through reflection-into-self, whereby the immediate determinatenesses are sublated and the form thereby makes itself into content, into an identity gone into itself which stands over against the difference of form.

3. If the two propositions, that of the form and that of the content:

+ (Subject) (Predicate)
+ The singular is universal
+ The universal is singular

were now to be brought into one on the ground of their both lying in the _one_ positive judgment, so that subject and predicate alike would then count as unity of singularity and universality, then both would be the _particular_; which _in_ _itself_ is to be acknowledged as their inner determination. But in part this combination would have come to pass only by an external reflection, and in part the proposition issuing from it, _the_ _particular_ _is_ _the_ _particular_, would be no judgment any more but an empty identical proposition, just as the propositions already turned up within it were, namely _the_ _singular_ _is_ _singular_ and _the_ _universal_ _is_ _universal_. – Singularity and universality do not yet admit of union in particularity, since within the positive judgment they still stand posited as _immediate_. – Or: a distinction must still be drawn in the judgment between its form and its content, precisely because subject and predicate are still distinguished as immediacy and as mediated, or because the judgment, taken by its reference, is both: {{{ 12.64 }}} self-subsistence of the terms referred and their reciprocal determination, or mediation.

The judgment, then, taken _first_ by its _form_, reads:

_The_ _singular_ _is_ _universal_. Rather, though, a singular of that _immediate_ kind is NOT universal; its predicate has a wider extent and so does not answer to it. The subject is something that _immediately_ _is_ _for_ _itself_, and therefore the _opposite_ of that abstraction, of the universality posited by mediation which was to be asserted of it.

_Secondly_, if the judgment is taken by its _content_, that is, as the proposition _the_ _universal_ _is_ _singular_, then the subject is a universal of qualities, a concrete infinitely determined, and inasmuch as its determinatenesses are so far merely qualities, properties, or accidents, the totality of them is their _badly_ _infinite_ _plurality_. Such a subject is therefore rather not one _singular_ property of that sort, as its predicate declares it to be. Both propositions must accordingly be _negated_, and the positive judgment must rather be posited as _negative_.
