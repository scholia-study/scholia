## b. Syllogism of Induction

1. It is the first figure's schema: S–P–U, under which the syllogism of allness stands; induction stands under the second's, U–S–P, because singularity is once again what serves it for middle term, not the _abstract_ singularity, however, but singularity qua _complete_, posited, namely, along with the determination opposed to it, universality. – _The_ _one_ _extreme_ is any predicate whatever that is common to all these singulars; the reference of that predicate to them makes up the immediate premises, one of which, in the preceding syllogism, was supposed to be a conclusion. – _The_ _other_ _extreme_ can be the genus in its immediacy, in the shape in which it stood in the middle of the foregoing syllogism, or else in the subject of the universal judgment, a genus that finds itself exhausted in the whole set of singulars, or of species too, that constitute the middle. The syllogism accordingly takes the shape:

s s U–    –P. s s into infinity.

2. The formal syllogism's second figure, U–S–P, failed to answer to the schema because in the one premise S, which makes up the middle term, was not {{{ 12.114 }}} subsuming or predicate. In induction this defect is removed; the middle term is here: _all_ _the_ _singulars_; and the proposition: U–S, which contains as its subject the objective universal, or genus, set apart so as to be an extreme, has a predicate of at least like extension with it and hence, for external reflection, identical with it. Lion, elephant and so forth make up the _genus_ of the four-footed animal; the difference that _the_ _same_ content stands posited the one time in singularity, the other time in universality, is thereby a mere _indifferent_ _determination_ _of_ _form_, – an indifference that constitutes the result, posited in the syllogism of reflection, of the formal syllogism, and that here is posited through the likeness of extension.

Induction is therefore not a syllogism of bare _perception_, nor of contingent existence, as the second figure answering to it is, but a syllogism of _experience_; – of the subjective drawing-together of the singulars into the genus, and of the genus's being joined by syllogism to a universal determinateness, because that determinateness is encountered in all the singulars. It bears also the objective meaning that through the totality of singularity the immediate genus determines itself into a universal property, and in a universal relation, or mark, possesses its existence. – Yet the objective meaning of this syllogism, as of the others, is so far only their inner concept, and here is still not posited.

3. Induction is rather still an essentially subjective syllogism. Its middle term consists of the singulars in their immediacy; their being drawn together into the genus by way of allness is a reflection that is _external_. Because of the subsisting _immediacy_ of the singulars, and of the _externality_ flowing from it, universality amounts only to completeness, or rather stays behind as _a_ _task_. – In it, accordingly, the _progress_ into the bad infinity comes to light once again; _singularity_ ought to be posited _identical_ with _universality_, yet inasmuch as the _singulars_ are posited just as much as _immediate_ ones, that unity remains a perennial _ought_; it is a unity of _likeness_; what ought to be identical ought at the same time _not_ to be identical. The a, b, c, d, e make up the genus only as carried on into _infinity_, and give the completed experience. To that extent the _conclusion_ of induction remains _problematic_.

In expressing this, however, that perception, if experience is to arise from it, _ought_ to be prolonged _into_ _infinity_, induction presupposes that the genus is joined by syllogism with its determinateness _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_. Thereby it actually presupposes its own conclusion, and rather as something immediate, in the way that the syllogism of allness had the conclusion for a presupposition of one of its premises. – Experience grounded on induction is taken to hold good _even_ _though_ the perception, by common admission, is _not_ _completed_; yet the assumption {{{ 12.115 }}} that no _instance_ _against_ that experience can turn up is possible only insofar as the latter be _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ true. The inference by way of induction is thus grounded indeed upon an immediacy, but not upon the one on which it should ground itself, namely the immediacy of _singularity_ _which_ _is_, but _upon_ _that_ _which_ _is_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, upon the _universal_. – The fundamental determination of induction is to be a syllogism; if singularity were taken as essential and universality merely as an external determination of the middle, the middle would fall apart into two unconnected pieces and no syllogism would be at hand; rather it is to the extremes that this externality belongs. _Singularity_ can be middle term only _as_ _immediately_ _identical_ with _universality_; a universality of that kind is properly the _objective_ one, _the_ _genus_. – The matter may also be viewed thus: on the determination of singularity that underlies the middle term of induction, universality is _external_, _yet_ _essential_; an _external_ of that sort is just as immediately its own opposite, the _internal_. – What induction's syllogism has for its truth, therefore, is a syllogism whose middle term is a singularity that immediately, _in_ _its_ _own_ _self_, is universality; – _the_ _syllogism_ _of_ _analogy_.
