## 2. Diversity

1. Within its own self identity _breaks_ _up_ into diversity, since as absolute difference inside itself it posits itself as its own negative, while these its moments — itself and its own negative — are reflections into themselves, identical with themselves; or precisely because its negating is straightway sublated by itself, so that _in_ _its_ _determination_ _it_ _is_ _reflected_ _into_ _itself_. What is distinguished _subsists_ as terms diverse and mutually indifferent, for the reason that it is self-identical, that identity makes up its soil and element; or the diverse possesses what it is precisely and only in identity, its own opposite.

It is diversity that constitutes the otherness as such belonging to reflection. For the other of existence, immediate _being_ is the ground on which the negative subsists. Within reflection, on the other hand, it is identity with self, reflected immediacy, that gives the negative its subsistence and its indifference.

Difference has for its moments identity and difference itself. Diverse they are as things reflected into themselves, as _referring_ _themselves_ _to_ _themselves_; thus, standing _in_ _the_ _determination_ _of_ _identity_, they are relations to themselves only; identity bears no relation to difference, nor difference to identity; and since each of these moments is in this way related only to itself, they are _not_ _determined_ over against each other. – Since in this manner they are not distinct within their own selves, _difference_ falls to them as something _external_. The diverse terms therefore bear upon one another neither as identity nor as difference, but only as _diverse_ terms in general, indifferent to each other and to their determinateness alike.

2. In diversity, understood as the indifference of difference, _reflection_ has grown _external_ to itself generally; difference is only a _positedness_, difference in sublated shape, and yet it is itself reflection entire. – Looked at more closely: both of them, identity and difference, as they have just been determined, are reflections; each is the unity of itself with its own other; each is the whole. Therewith, however, the determinateness of being _only_ identity or _only_ difference is something sublated. They are for this reason no qualities, since their {{{ 11.268 }}} determinateness, by way of reflection into itself, exists at the same time merely as negation. What is at hand, then, is this twofold thing: _reflection_ _into_ _itself_ as such, and, as negation, determinateness or _positedness_. Now positedness is reflection grown external to itself; it is negation qua negation; _in_ _itself_, therefore, self-referring negation and reflection into itself, yet only in itself; it is a relating to that as to something external.

The two determinations into which the moments of difference, identity and difference, have posited themselves are accordingly reflection in itself and external reflection. They are those moments themselves, insofar as they have by now determined themselves. – _The_ _reflection_ _in_ _itself_ is identity, but identity determined so as to be indifferent towards difference; not so as to lack difference altogether, but so as to bear itself towards difference as self-identical; it is _diversity_. Identity it is, having reflected itself into itself in such fashion that it is properly the _one_ reflection of both moments into itself; each of the two is a reflection into itself. Identity is this single reflection of both, a reflection that carries difference within it merely as something indifferent, and which is diversity as such. – On the contrary, _external_ _reflection_ is the _determinate_ difference of the two, taken not as absolute reflection into itself but as determination, towards which the reflection that is in itself remains indifferent; its two moments, identity and difference itself, are posited externally in this way, and are not determinations that exist in and for themselves.

This external identity, now, is _likeness_, and the external difference _unlikeness_. – _Likeness_ is indeed identity, but identity as a mere positedness, not identity in and for itself. – And just so _unlikeness_ is difference, but difference as something external, which is not, in and for itself, the very difference of the unlike. Whether some something is like another something or not is the affair neither of the one nor of the other; each is related to itself alone and is, in and for its own self, whatever it may be; identity or non-identity, as likeness and as unlikeness, is the regard of a third party and falls outside them.

3. It is external reflection that relates the diverse to likeness and unlikeness. This relating, _comparison_, moves to and fro, from likeness across to unlikeness and back again. But this to-and-fro relating of likeness and unlikeness is external to those determinations themselves; nor, moreover, are they related to each other, but each on its own only to a third. In this alternation each steps forth immediately on its own. – External reflection as such is external to its own self; the _determinate_ difference is absolute difference negated; it is therefore not simple, not reflection into itself, but has that reflection outside it; its moments accordingly fall asunder, and, external to one another as they are, relate themselves besides to the reflection into itself standing over against them.

{{{ 11.269 }}} In reflection thus estranged from itself, then, likeness and unlikeness emerge as unrelated to one another, and reflection _separates_ them by referring them _to_ _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_ thing, doing so by means of the _insofars_, the _sides_ _and_ _regards_. The diverse terms which are the one and the same thing to which both likeness and unlikeness get related are accordingly, _on_ _the_ _one_ _side_, like each other, but unlike _on_ _the_ _other_ _side_, and _insofar_ as they are like, _insofar_ they are not unlike. _Likeness_ relates itself to itself alone, and _unlikeness_ is likewise nothing but unlikeness.

Yet through this separation of theirs from each other, they do nothing but sublate themselves. Precisely what is supposed to keep contradiction and dissolution at bay — namely, that a something is _like_ another _in_ _one_ _regard_ _but_ _unlike_ _in_ _another_ — this holding asunder of likeness and unlikeness is what destroys them. For the two of them are determinations of difference; each is a relation to the other, each is what its counterpart is not; the like is not unlike, and the unlike is not like; both have this relation essentially and, outside it, no significance at all; being determinations of difference, each one is whatever it is only as _distinguished_ from its other. Through their indifference towards one another, however, likeness is related to itself alone, and unlikeness is likewise a regard and reflection of its own on its own account; each is therefore like its own self; difference has vanished, since they carry no determinateness over against each other; or each is thereby nothing but likeness.

This indifferent regard, or external difference, accordingly sublates its own self and is the negativity of itself within itself. It is that negativity which, in comparing, falls to the one who compares. The comparer moves from likeness to unlikeness and back again from unlikeness to likeness; it thus makes the one vanish in the other, and in fact is _the_ _negative_ _unity_ _of_ _both_. At first this unity lies beyond what is compared, as it lies beyond the moments of comparison too, being a subjective doing that falls outside them. But in truth, as has now come out, that negative unity is the nature of likeness and of unlikeness themselves. Precisely that self-subsistent regard which each of them is turns out rather to be the self-relating that sublates their distinctness and with it themselves.

Taken on this side — as moments of external reflection, and as external to their own selves — likeness and unlikeness vanish together into their likeness. But this _negative_ unity of theirs is moreover also _posited_ in them; for they have the reflection that _is_ _in_ _itself_ outside them, or else they are likeness and unlikeness _of_ _a_ _third_ _thing_, of something other than what they themselves are. So the like is not what is like to its own self, and the unlike — unlike not to its own self but to something unlike it — is itself the like. The like as well as the unlike is therefore the _unlike_ _of_ _its_ _own_ _self_. Each is accordingly this reflection — {{{ 11.270 }}} likeness being both itself and unlikeness, unlikeness being both itself and likeness.

Over against what was compared, or the diverse — which had determined itself over against them as the reflection that _is_ _in_ _itself_ — likeness and unlikeness made up the side of _positedness_. But with this the latter has likewise lost its determinateness over against them. Likeness and unlikeness precisely, the determinations of external reflection, are the merely in-itself reflection that the diverse was meant to be as such, the diverse's merely indeterminate difference. The reflection that _is_ _in_ _itself_ is self-relation without negation, abstract identity with self; and hence precisely positedness itself. – The merely diverse therefore crosses over, by way of positedness, into negative reflection. Difference merely posited is what the diverse is, hence a difference that is none, hence the negation of itself within itself. So too likeness and unlikeness themselves, positedness, return by way of indifference, or of the reflection that is in itself, into negative unity with themselves; into that reflection which is the difference of likeness and unlikeness within itself. Diversity, whose _indifferent_ sides are quite as much simply _moments_ only of one negative unity, is _opposition_.
