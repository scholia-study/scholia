## {{{ 12.231 }}} B. The Idea of the Good

Inasmuch as the concept, being the object of its own self, is determined in and for itself, the subject stands determined for itself as a _singular_. In its subjective character it once again has the presupposition of an otherness that is in itself; it is the _drive_ toward its own realization, the purpose that wills to procure objectivity for itself in the objective world _through_ _its_ _own_ _self_ and so to carry itself out. Within the theoretical idea the subjective concept confronts the objective world as the _universal_, as what is in and for itself _without_ _determination_, and from that world it draws the determinate content and the fulfillment it needs. Within the practical idea, by contrast, the concept confronts the actual as itself something actual; yet the certainty of its own self that belongs to the subject in being determined in and for itself is a certainty both that the subject is actual and that the world is _unactual_; not merely the world's otherness as abstract universality counts for the subject as null, but equally the world's singularity together with the determinations of that singularity. Here the subject has laid claim to _objectivity_ for its own self; the determinateness it has within itself is the objective, since this is a universality no less absolutely determined; whereas the world that formerly counted as objective is now nothing but something posited, something determined _immediately_ in manifold ways, which, precisely because its determination is only immediate, lacks the concept's unity within itself and is null for itself.

This determinateness, held within the concept, congruent with it, and enclosing within itself the demand for a singular external actuality, is the _good_. It steps forward laying claim to the dignity of the absolute, since it harbors the concept's totality and is the objective under the form of free unity and subjectivity alike. This idea ranks higher than the idea of the cognition we have considered, for the dignity belonging to it is not only that of the universal but that of what is actual without qualification. – It is _drive_ to the extent that this actual remains subjective and self-positing and does not yet carry the form of immediate presupposition along with it; its drive toward realizing itself is strictly speaking not a drive to procure objectivity, which it possesses in its own self already, but only to procure this empty form of immediacy. – Hence the purpose's activity is not turned back upon itself in order to receive some given determination and appropriate it, but is directed rather toward positing the determination proper to itself and, by sublating whatever determinations the external world carries, toward giving itself reality under the shape of external actuality. – Being self-determining _for_ _itself_, the idea of will holds the _content_ within its own self. Now this content is admittedly a _determinate_ one, and to that degree something _finite_ and _limited_; self-determination amounts essentially to _particularization_, for the will's reflection {{{ 12.232 }}} into itself, as negative unity in general, is also singularity in the sense of excluding an other and presupposing one. Yet the content's particularity is at first infinite through the form of the concept, whose own determinateness that content is, and which possesses in the content the negative identity of itself with itself, and so not merely something particular but its own infinite singularity. The _finitude_ of the content in the practical idea just spoken of is one and the same thing with the idea's being at first an idea not yet carried out; for the content the concept counts as what is in and for itself; the concept is here the idea under the form of an objectivity that is _for_ _its_ _own_ _self_; on one side, therefore, the subjective is no longer something merely _posited_, arbitrary or contingent, but something absolute; on the other side, however, this _form_ _of_ _concrete_ _existence_, _being-for-itself_, does not yet possess the form of _being-in-itself_ as well. What in respect of form as such shows itself as opposition shows itself, on the concept's form as reflected into _simple_ _identity_, that is, on the content, as the content's simple determinateness; and so the good, though valid in and for itself, is thereby some particular purpose or other, one that is nevertheless not to acquire its truth only by being realized, since for itself it is the true already.

The syllogism of immediate _realization_ calls for no closer treatment here; it is wholly and simply the syllogism of _external_ _purposiveness_ examined earlier, and only the content constitutes the difference. In external purposiveness, that is, in the formal kind, the content was an indeterminate finite content in general; here it is finite too, and yet as such carries absolute validity at once. With respect to the conclusion, however, to the purpose as carried out, a further difference sets in. In its _realization_ the finite purpose likewise gets no further than the _means_; because at its outset it is not yet a purpose determined in and for itself, it likewise remains, even when carried out, something not in and for itself. Should the good too be fixed once more as something _finite_, and be essentially such, then for all its inward infinity it likewise cannot elude the destiny of finitude; – a destiny that shows itself in several shapes. The good as carried out is good by virtue of what it already is within the subjective purpose, within its idea; the carrying out furnishes it with an external existence; but since that existence is determined merely as externality null in and for itself, the good has in it nothing beyond a contingent, destructible existence and has not reached a carrying out that corresponds to its idea. – Moreover, since in respect of its content it is something limited, there are also goods of many kinds; the good that concretely exists lies open to destruction not only through external contingency and through evil, but through the good's own collision and antagonism with itself. On the part of the objective world it presupposes, in the presupposing of which the good's subjectivity and finitude consist, and which, as an other, {{{ 12.233 }}} pursues its own course, even the carrying out of the good is exposed to hindrances, indeed to sheer impossibility. Thus the good remains an _ought_; it is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, while _being_, in the character of that final abstract immediacy, persists over against it as determined _also_ into a _non-being_. The idea of a good brought to completion is indeed an _absolute_ _postulate_, though nothing beyond a postulate, that is, an absolute still saddled with the determinateness of subjectivity. Two worlds still stand opposed: the one a kingdom of subjectivity in the pure expanses of thought that is transparent, the other a kingdom of objectivity in the element of an actuality outwardly manifold, a kingdom of darkness that stands unopened. The complete elaboration of this unresolved contradiction, of that _absolute_ purpose over against which the _restriction_ of such actuality stands _insuperable_, has been treated more closely in the Phenomenology of Spirit p. 323ff. – Since the idea holds within it the moment of perfect determinateness, that other concept to which the concept relates itself within the idea carries along with its subjectivity the moment of an object as well; here, therefore, the idea enters the shape of _self-consciousness_, and on this one side coincides with the exposition given of self-consciousness.

What the practical idea is still short of, however, is the moment of actual consciousness as such, namely that within the concept the moment of actuality should for itself have reached the determination of _external_ _being_. – One may also regard this deficiency thus, that the _practical_ idea is still short of the moment belonging to the _theoretical_ one. For in the latter the side of the subjective concept, the concept as intuited within itself by the concept, carries only the determination of _universality_; cognition knows itself merely as apprehension, as the concept's identity with itself which for its own self is _undetermined_; the fulfillment, that is, objectivity determined in and for itself, is for cognition something _given_, and what _truly_ _is_ is the actuality at hand apart from all subjective positing. For the practical idea, conversely, that same actuality, which likewise stands before it as a restriction it cannot surmount, counts as null in and for itself, as what has yet to obtain its genuine determination and its sole worth from the purposes of the good. It is thus the will alone that blocks its own attainment of its goal, in that it severs itself from cognition and external actuality thereby fails to acquire for it the form of what truly is; hence the idea of the good can find what completes it nowhere but in the idea of the true.

This transition, however, the idea accomplishes through its own self. In the syllogism of action the one premise is the _immediate_ _reference_ _of_ _the_ _good_ _purpose_ _to_ _actuality_, an actuality of which the purpose takes hold and which in the second premise {{{ 12.234 }}} it turns, as an external _means_, against external actuality. For the subjective concept the good is the objective; actuality in its existence stands over against the good as a restriction not to be surmounted only so far as actuality still bears the determination of _immediate_ _existence_ rather than that of an objective in the sense of what is in and for itself; actuality is instead either the evil or the indifferent, something merely determinable that does not carry its worth within its own self. But this abstract being that faces the good in the second premise the practical idea has already sublated on its own; the first premise of its action is the concept's _immediate_ _objectivity_, whereby the purpose imparts itself to actuality without meeting any resistance and stands in simple, identical reference with it. To that extent, then, nothing more is required than to bring together the thoughts of its two premises. To what the objective concept has already immediately brought about in the first, the second at first adds only this, that it comes to be posited by way of mediation and hence _for_ _it_. Now as in the purpose-reference generally the purpose carried out is once again only a means, while conversely the means is likewise the purpose carried out, so too in the syllogism of the good the first premise already harbors the second immediately and _in_ _itself_; only this immediacy does not suffice, and the second is postulated for the first already; – the carrying out of the good against another actuality that stands opposed to it is the mediation essentially required for the immediate reference and for the good's being actualized. For that premise constitutes merely the concept's first negation, its otherness, an objectivity in which the concept would lie sunk in externality; the second is the sublating of this otherness, and by it the immediate carrying out of the purpose first becomes the actuality of the good as the concept that is for itself, since therein the concept is posited as identical with its own self and not with an other, and so posited as free and nothing else. If, then, the purpose of the good should for all that still not be carried out, this is a relapse of the concept into the standpoint it occupies before its activity, – the standpoint of an actuality determined as null and yet presupposed as real; – a relapse that turns into the progress into bad infinity, and that has its ground in nothing but this, that in the sublating of that abstract reality the sublating is just as immediately forgotten, or that it is forgotten how this reality has rather been presupposed already as the actuality that is null in and for itself and not objective. Hence this repetition of the presupposition of the purpose not carried out, coming after the purpose has actually been carried out, determines itself also in this way, that _the_ {{{ 12.235 }}} _subjective_ _stance_ of the objective concept is reproduced and rendered perennial, whereby _the_ _finitude_ of the good, in respect of its content no less than of its form, shows itself as the truth that abides, and its actualization shows itself without qualification as never more than a _singular_ deed and not a _universal_ one. – In fact this determinateness has sublated itself in the actualization of the good; what continues to _bound_ the objective concept is the _view_ it takes of itself, a view that disappears through reflection upon what its actualization is _in_ _itself_; by that view it obstructs its own self alone, and on that score it has to turn not against an external actuality but against its own self.

For the activity in the second premise, which brings forth only a one-sided _being-for-itself_ and lets the product appear as something _subjective_ and _singular_, so that the first presupposition is repeated in it, – is in truth no less the positing of that identity of the objective concept and immediate actuality which is _in_ _itself_. This latter is determined by the presupposition to possess merely the reality of appearance, to be null in and for itself, and to be determinable throughout by the objective concept. Since the objective concept's activity alters the external actuality and thereby sublates the determination it had, that very act strips from it the merely apparent reality, the external determinability and the nullity, and so it comes to be _posited_ as being in and for itself. Therein the presupposition as such is sublated: the good's determination as a purpose merely subjective and limited in its content, the necessity of first having to realize it by subjective activity, and that activity itself. Within the result the mediation sublates its own self; what stands there is an _immediacy_ that does not reinstate the presupposition but is rather the presupposition's sublatedness. So the idea of the concept determined in and for itself is now posited, no longer in the acting subject alone but equally as an immediate actuality; and this actuality, conversely, is posited in the shape it has within cognition, as objectivity that genuinely is. The subject's singularity, which clung to it by reason of its presupposition, has vanished along with that presupposition; the subject now stands as _free_, _universal_ _identity_ _with_ _its_ _own_ _self_, for which the objectivity of the concept is just as much something _given_, immediately _present_ for it, as it knows itself to be the concept determined in and for itself. In this result, then, _cognition_ has been restored and joined with the practical idea; the actuality found before us is at once determined as the absolute purpose carried out, yet not, as in cognition that searches, as a merely objective world devoid of the concept's subjectivity, but as an objective world in which the concept is the inner ground and the actual subsistence. This is the absolute idea.
