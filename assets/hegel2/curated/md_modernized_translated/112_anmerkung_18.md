## Remark

The natural number system already displays such a _nodal line_ of qualitative moments that come to the fore in the merely external going-on. On the one part it is a merely quantitative going forwards and backwards, a perpetual adding-on or taking-away, so that every number stands in the same _arithmetical_ ratio to the number before it and the one after it as these do to the numbers before and after them, and so on. But the numbers arising in this way also have a _specific_ ratio to yet other numbers preceding or following, either that of being such a multiple of one of them as a whole number expresses, or that of being power and root. – In the _musical_ ratios a harmonic ratio comes in, upon the scale of the quantitative going-on, by way of a quantum, without this quantum for itself standing on the scale in any other ratio to its predecessor and successor than these in turn stand in to theirs. While the tones that follow seem to be withdrawing ever further from the keynote, or numbers by the arithmetical going-on seem merely to be growing yet more other, all at once there comes forth instead a _return_, a surprising accord, which was not {{{ 21.367 }}} qualitatively prepared by what immediately preceded but appears as an actio in distans, as a connection to something remote; the going-on among merely indifferent ratios, which do not change the preceding specific reality or which form no such reality at all, interrupts itself all at once, and since in quantitative respect it is carried on in the same manner, a specific ratio thus breaks in by way of a leap.

In _chemical_ _combinations_, with the progressive change of the mixture-ratios, such qualitative nodes and leaps occur that two materials at particular points of the mixture-scale form products displaying particular qualities. These products are not distinguished from one another merely by a more and less, nor are they already at hand along with the ratios lying close to those nodal ratios, in a weaker degree perhaps, but are bound to such points themselves. For example, the combinations of oxygen and nitrogen give the various nitrogen oxides and nitric acids, which come forth only at determinate quantitative ratios of the mixture and have essentially different qualities, so that at mixture-ratios lying in between no combinations of specific concrete existences result. – The _metal oxides_, the lead oxides say, take shape at particular quantitative points along the course of oxidation, and they differ in colour and in other qualities. One does not shade over gradually into another; the ratios that lie between those nodes yield no neutral product, no specific existence. Without having gone through intermediate stages, a specific combination steps forth which rests on a measure-relation and has qualities of its own. – Or _water_, in changing its temperature, does not thereby become merely more or less warm but runs through the states of hardness, of drippable liquidity and of elastic liquidity; these different states do not set in gradually, rather the merely gradual going-on of the temperature-change is at these points interrupted and checked all at once, and the onset of another state is a leap. – All _birth_ and _death_, instead of being a continued gradualness, are rather a breaking-off of it, and the leap out of quantitative alteration into qualitative.

{{{ 21.368 }}} _There_ _is_ _no_ _leap_ _in_ _nature_, so it is said; and ordinary representation, when it is to comprehend a _coming-to-be_ or _ceasing-to-be_, supposes, as was recalled, that it has comprehended it by representing it as a _gradual_ emerging or vanishing. It has shown itself, however, that the alterations of being as such are not only the passing-over of one magnitude into another magnitude but a transition from the qualitative into the quantitative and conversely, a becoming-other which is a breaking-off of the gradual and a qualitatively-other over against the preceding existence. Under chilling, water does not grow hard bit by bit, in such a way that it would turn pulpy and stiffen by degrees up to the firmness of ice, but is hard at a stroke; when it stands at rest it can still possess its full liquidity even at the entire temperature of the freezing point, and a slight shaking brings it into the state of hardness.

Underlying the gradualness of coming-to-be is the representation that what is _coming to be_ is already there sensibly, or in general _actually_ _at hand_, only _not_ _yet_ _perceptible_ on account of its smallness, just as, with the gradualness of vanishing, that the _non-being_, or the _other_ that steps into its place, is likewise _at hand_, only _not_ _yet_ _noticeable_; – at hand, that is, not in the sense that the other is contained _in itself_ within the other that is at hand, but that it is _at hand_ _as_ _existence_, only unnoticeable. Coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be as such are thereby sublated, or the _in-itself_, the inner, in which something is prior to its existence, is converted into a _smallness_ of _external_ _existence_, and the essential difference, or the difference of concept, into an external, mere difference of magnitude. – Making a coming-to-be or ceasing-to-be comprehensible out of the gradualness of the alteration has the tediousness proper to tautology; it has what comes to be or ceases to be all finished up beforehand and turns the alteration into a mere change of an external difference, whereby it is in fact nothing but a tautology. The difficulty for an understanding that would comprehend in this fashion lies in the qualitative transition of something into its other as such {{{ 21.369 }}} and into its opposite; against this it deludes itself with _identity_ and with _alteration_ as the indifferent, external alteration of the _quantitative_.

In the _moral_ sphere, insofar as it is considered within the sphere of being, the same transition from the quantitative into the qualitative takes place; and different qualities appear to be grounded upon a diversity of magnitude. It is a more and less by which the measure of frivolity is overstepped and something wholly other, crime, comes forth, by which right passes over into wrong, virtue into vice. – So too do states, through their difference of magnitude, other things being assumed equal, acquire a different qualitative character. Laws and constitution become something other when the compass of the state and the amount of its citizens expand. The state has a measure of its magnitude, and driven out beyond that measure it collapses within itself without holding, under the very constitution which at a merely different compass made for its good fortune and its strength.
