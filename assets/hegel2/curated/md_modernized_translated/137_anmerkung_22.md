## Remark

Diversity, like identity, is put into words in a principle of its own. For the rest, the two principles remain held apart from each other in indifferent diversity, so that each counts on its own with no regard for the other.

_All_ _things_ _are_ _diverse_, or: _there_ _are_ _no_ _two_ _things_ _that_ _are_ _alike_. – In truth this principle runs counter to that of identity, for what it declares is: A is a diverse something, and so A is also not A; or A is unlike another, and then it is not A as such but rather a determinate A. In the identical principle any other substrate whatever may be put in the place of A, whereas A as unlike can no longer be exchanged for just any other. It is meant, to be sure, to be diverse not _from_ _itself_ but only _from_ _something_ _else_; yet this diversity is its own determination. As the A that is identical with itself it is what is indeterminate; but as determinate it is the reverse of that, it no longer has merely identity with itself but a negation as well, and hence a diversity of itself from itself upon it.

That all things are diverse from one another is a very superfluous principle, for the plural of things carries multiplicity and wholly indeterminate diversity in it at once. – The principle, however, that there are no two things perfectly alike says more, namely _determinate_ diversity {{{ 11.271 }}}. Two things are not merely two; numerical plurality is nothing but sameness, whereas they are diverse _through_ _a_ _determination_. The principle that there are no two things alike strikes representation as remarkable – as it does in the anecdote too, at a court where Leibniz is said to have brought it forward and to have set the ladies searching among tree leaves for whether they might not find two of them alike. – Happy days for metaphysics, when a court occupied itself with it, and when the testing of its principles called for no greater exertion than the comparison of tree leaves! – What makes that principle striking has its ground in what was said above: that _two_, or numerical multiplicity, still carries no _determinate_ diversity within it, and that diversity taken by itself, in the abstraction proper to it, stands at first indifferent towards likeness as towards unlikeness. Representation, in going over to determination as well, picks up these very moments as indifferent towards each other, so that the one without the other, _the_ _bare_ _likeness_ of things _without_ _the_ _unlikeness_, would be enough for determination, or so that things would be diverse even if they were only numerically many, diverse as such and not unlike. The principle of diversity, on the other hand, states that things are diverse from one another by virtue of unlikeness, that unlikeness is a determination accruing to them no less than likeness does, for it takes the two together to make up determinate difference.

Now this principle, that the determination of unlikeness belongs to all things, would stand in need of a proof; it cannot be set up as an immediate principle, for the customary way of cognizing itself demands, for the tying together of diverse determinations in a synthetic proposition, a proof, or the pointing out of a third in which they are mediated. Such a proof would have to lay out the transition of identity into diversity, and then the transition of diversity into determinate diversity, into unlikeness. This, however, is not what tends to be delivered; what came out in the foregoing was that diversity, or external difference, is in truth difference reflected into itself, difference upon its own self, and that the indifferent subsistence of what is diverse is bare positedness, and so is not external, indifferent difference but _one_ relation of the two moments.

In this there also lies the dissolution and nullity of the _principle_ _of_ _diversity_. Two things are not perfectly alike; then they are alike and unlike at once; alike already in this, that they are things, or two as such, since each is a thing and a one just as much as the other, each therefore the same as the other; unlike, though, they are by the assumption. So the determination is at hand that both moments, likeness and unlikeness, are diverse in _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_, or that the difference which falls asunder outside itself is at the same time one and the same relation. Diversity has thereby gone over into _opposition_.

{{{ 11.272 }}} The _at-once_ of the two predicates is indeed kept apart by the _insofar_; that two things, _insofar_ as they are alike, are _insofar_ not unlike, or that they are alike on one _side_ and in one _respect_ but unlike on the other _side_ and in the other _respect_. With this the unity of likeness and unlikeness is taken away from the thing, and what would be its own reflection, the reflection of likeness and unlikeness in itself, is clung to as a reflection external to the thing. But this reflection is then the very one _that_ _in_ _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_ _activity_ tells the two sides of likeness and unlikeness apart, and so holds both within one activity, letting the one shine into the other and reflecting it there. – The usual tenderness for things, though, which cares only that they should not contradict themselves, forgets here as elsewhere that the contradiction is not thereby dissolved but merely pushed somewhere else, into subjective or external reflection as such, and that this reflection does in fact hold the two moments, which by such removal and displacement are pronounced to be bare _positedness_, within one unity, as sublated and as bearing on each other.
