## {{{ 12.32 }}} Chapter 1. The Concept

It is customary for _understanding_ to express the faculty of concepts in general, and to that extent understanding is marked off from the _power_ _of_ _judgment_ and from the faculty of syllogisms, that is, from formal _reason_. Above all, however, it is placed in opposition to _reason_; taken that way it signifies not the faculty of the concept in general but that of _determinate_ concepts, the governing representation being that a concept is ONLY something _determinate_. Where understanding in this sense is marked off from the formal power of judgment and from formal reason, it must be taken as the faculty of the _individual_, _determinate_ concept. For judgment and syllogism, or reason, are themselves, taken formally, no more than something _of_ _the_ _understanding_, inasmuch as they fall under the form of abstract conceptual determinateness. Here, though, the concept does not at all count as something merely abstractly determinate; understanding is therefore to be marked off from reason only in this respect, that the former is merely the faculty of the concept in general.

This universal concept, the one now before us for consideration, holds three moments within it: _universality_, _particularity_, and _singularity_. That difference, along with the determinations the concept confers on itself in differentiating, makes up the side named earlier _positedness_. Because within the concept positedness is identical with being-in-and-for-itself, every one of those moments is as much the _whole_ concept as it is a _determinate_ _concept_ and _one_ _determination_ of the concept.

_First_, the concept is the _pure_ _concept_, that is, the determination of _universality_. Yet the pure, or universal, concept is likewise no more than a _determinate_, or _particular_, concept, one that ranges itself off to one side alongside the rest. Since the concept is the totality, and consequently, in its universality — in its purely identical reference to itself — is essentially the determining and the differentiating, it possesses in itself the yardstick whereby this form of its identity with itself, in permeating all the moments and gathering them into itself, just as immediately determines itself to be _only_ the _universal_ as against the moments' differentiatedness.

_Secondly_, the concept is thereby as this _particular_ concept, or as the _determinate_ concept, which stands posited as differentiated over against others.

_Thirdly_, _singularity_ is the concept as it reflects itself out of difference into absolute negativity. Herein lies at once the moment in which the concept has passed out of its identity over into its _otherness_, thereby becoming the _judgment_.
