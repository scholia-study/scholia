## a. The Relatively Unconditioned

1. The ground counts as immediate, the grounded as mediated. But since ground is positing reflection, it makes of itself a positedness; and since it is presupposing reflection too, it refers to itself as to a sublated thing, an immediate by which it is itself mediated. Such mediation, the advance running from immediate to ground, is not reflection coming from outside but, as has turned out, what the ground does of itself; put otherwise, the ground-reference, reflecting as it does into identity with self, is with equal essentiality a reflection that divests itself. What the ground refers to as to its {{{ 11.315 }}} essential presupposition, this immediate, is the _condition_; hence real ground stands essentially conditioned. Its own otherness is the determinateness which it holds.

So condition is, _first_, an existence that is immediate and manifold. _Secondly_, that existence stands referred to another, to a thing which is ground — not ground of this existence, but under a different respect, seeing that existence is of itself immediate and without ground. Under such reference it counts as something _posited_; being condition, immediate existence ought to be, not for itself, but for another. Yet its standing thus for another is likewise, at the same time, no more than a positedness; its being something posited lies sublated in its immediacy, and an _existence_ _is_ _indifferent_ _to_ _being_ _condition_. _Thirdly_, condition is immediate in such wise as to constitute the ground's _presupposition_. So determined, it is the ground's reference of form returned into identity with self, and therewith the _content_ of the ground. Content as such, though, amounts only to the ground's indifferent unity, unity as it stands within form; where form is lacking content is lacking too. Content does nonetheless win free of form, seeing that in the _complete_ ground the ground-reference becomes a reference outward to its own identity, by which content comes by immediacy. To the extent, therefore, that condition is where the ground-reference holds its _identity_ with itself, condition supplies the ground's content; but since content stands indifferent towards that form, it is the ground's content _in_ _itself_ alone, a thing that has yet to _become_ content and so supplies the _material_ for the ground. Set down as condition, existence carries, under the second moment, the determination of forfeiting its indifferent immediacy and becoming a moment of something else. In virtue of its immediacy it stands indifferent towards this reference; to the extent that it steps into the reference, however, it supplies the _being-in-itself_ of the ground and is for the ground the _unconditioned_. To be condition at all, it has its presupposition in the ground and is conditioned itself; such a determination, though, remains outward to it.

2. A something does not exist through its condition; a thing's condition is not its ground. For the ground, condition is the moment of unconditioned immediacy; it is not, though, itself that movement, that positing, which refers to itself negatively and turns itself into positedness. Over against condition there consequently stands the _ground-reference_. Alongside its condition a something has a ground as well. – The ground is reflection's empty movement, because reflection keeps immediacy outside itself as what it presupposes. Reflection is nevertheless the entire form and the mediating that is self-subsistent; for its ground is not the condition. Since this mediating refers to itself in the manner of positing, from that side it too is an immediate and an _unconditioned_; presuppose itself it does, yet as a positing that has been divested or sublated; {{{ 11.316 }}} while whatever it is by its own determination, that it is in and for its own self. – To the extent, then, that the ground-reference is self-subsistent reference to itself and carries reflection's identity upon its own self, it holds a _content_ _peculiar_ to it, over against the content belonging to condition. The one is the ground's content and for that reason essentially formed; the other, contrariwise, is immediate material and nothing more, material to which reference to the ground is as outward as it is at the same time what supplies the ground's being-in-itself; so the other is a mingling of self-subsistent content, bearing no reference to what the ground-determination has for content, with content that passes into it and, serving as its material, is to become a moment of it.

3. _Condition_ _and_ _ground_, the whole's two sides, are therefore on the one hand _indifferent_ towards each other and _unconditioned_ by each other; the first as the unreferred, to which the reference wherein it is condition remains outward; the second as reference or form, before which the condition's determinate existence figures merely as material, as a passive thing whose own form, the form it has upon itself for itself, is unessential. Both of them, further, are _mediated_ as well. Condition supplies the ground's _being-in-itself_; so essential a moment of the ground-reference is it that it constitutes the ground's simple identity with self. Yet this too lies sublated; such being-in-itself is nothing but a posited thing; existence in its immediacy cares nothing about being condition. Condition's being the _being-in-itself_ for the ground supplies, accordingly, that side of it on which it is a mediated thing. The ground-reference likewise, self-subsistent though it be, has a presupposition of its own too, and keeps its being-in-itself outside itself. – Each of the two sides is therefore the _contradiction_ between indifferent immediacy and essential mediation, the two held within one reference; – or the contradiction between subsisting self-subsistently and being determined as a mere moment.
