## 2. External Reflection

Reflection in the sense of absolute reflection is the essence that shines inside its own self, and it presupposes to itself only shine, positedness; as presupposing it is immediately nothing but positing reflection. The external or real reflection, however, presupposes itself as sublated, as its own negative. In this determination it is doubled; on the one occasion as what is presupposed, or reflection into itself, which is the {{{ 11.253 }}} immediate. On the other occasion it is reflection referring itself to itself as negative; it refers itself to itself as to that non-being of its own.

External reflection thus _presupposes_ a being, _firstly_ not so that its immediacy would be merely positedness or a moment, but rather so that this immediacy is the reference to itself and determinateness is only a moment. It relates to its presupposition in such a way that the presupposition is reflection's negative, though in such a way that this negative _as_ negative stands sublated. – Reflection, in the act of positing, sublates that positing straightaway, and thus what it has is an _immediate_ _presupposition_. It accordingly _finds_ the presupposition already before it, as that from which it starts and out of which it first is the going back into itself, the negating of this negative of its own. But that what is presupposed should be a negative or something posited is no concern of that presupposition itself; the determinateness belongs solely to positing reflection, whereas in presupposing, positedness is present only as sublated. The things that external reflection determines and posits in the immediate are, to that extent, determinations external to it. – In the sphere of being it was the infinite; the finite counts as the first, as the real, one begins from it as from what lies at the ground and stays lying at the ground, while the infinite is the reflection into itself that confronts it.

This external reflection is a syllogism whose two extremes are the immediate and reflection into itself; its middle term is the relation of the two, the determinate immediate, such that of this middle one part, immediacy, falls only to the one extreme, while the other, determinateness or negation, falls only to the other.

But if we look more closely at what external reflection does, it is _secondly_ a positing of the immediate, which to that extent becomes the negative or the determinate; yet just as immediately it also sublates this positing of its own, since the immediate is what it _presupposes_; its negating is the negating of this negating of its own. Immediately therewith, however, it is equally _positing_, a sublating of the immediate that is negative to it, and this immediate, which it appeared to take as an alien starting point, first is in this very beginning of its own. The immediate, in this way, is not merely _in_ _itself_ – which would say: for us, or within external reflection – _the same_ as reflection is; rather it is _posited_ that it is the same. For it is determined by reflection as reflection's negative or as its other, yet it is reflection itself that negates this determining. – With this, reflection's externality with respect to the immediate is sublated; its self-negating positing is reflection's going together with its negative, with the immediate, and this {{{ 11.254 }}} going together is essential immediacy itself. – Hence it is at hand that the external reflection is no external one but quite as much immediacy's own immanent reflection; or that what exists by way of positing reflection is the essence that is in and for itself. Thus it is _determining_ _reflection_.
