## Remark

Reflection is commonly understood in a subjective sense, as that movement of the power of judgment which passes out beyond a given immediate representation and seeks universal determinations for that representation, or compares it with such. Kant opposes the _reflecting_ _power of judgment_ to the _determining_ _power of judgment_. (Critique of the Power of Judgment, Introduction, p. XXIII f.) He defines the power of judgment in general as the capacity _to_ _think_ the _particular_ _as_ _contained_ _under_ _the_ _universal_. _If_ _the_ _universal_ _is_ _given_ (the rule, the principle, the law), then the power of judgment that subsumes the particular under it is _determining_. But if the particular alone is given, _for_ _which_ _it_ _is_ _to_ _find_ _the_ _universal_, then the power of judgment is merely _reflecting_. Here too, then, reflection is the going out beyond an immediate to the universal. In part, the immediate is determined as a particular only through this relating of it to its universal; taken on its own it is only a singular, or an immediate being. In part, however, that to which it is related is its own universal, its rule, principle, law; in general, what is reflected into itself, what refers itself to itself, the essence or the essential.

What is at issue here, however, is neither consciousness's reflection nor the more determinate reflection of the understanding, whose determinations are the particular and the universal, but rather reflection as such. That reflection to which Kant ascribes the search for the universal belonging to a given particular is, as is clear, likewise only the _external_ reflection, which relates itself to the immediate as to a given. – Yet the concept of absolute reflection lies in this too; for that universal, the principle or rule and law, to which reflection progresses in its determining, counts as the essence of the immediate from which the start is made, so that this immediate counts as something null, and the return out of it, reflection's determining, counts as what first posits the immediate according to its genuine being; hence what reflection performs on the immediate, and the determinations issuing from reflection, count not as anything external to that immediate but as its own proper being.

External reflection was also what people meant when, as was for a while the fashion in more recent philosophy, every evil was laid at the door of reflection as such {{{ 11.255 }}} and, together with its determining, it was regarded as the antipode and inveterate foe of the absolute way of considering things. And indeed thinking reflection, insofar as it comports itself as external, does set out simply from a given immediate alien to it, and looks upon itself as a merely formal doing that receives content and material from outside and on its own is only the movement conditioned by that material. – Further, as will presently come out more closely in the case of determining reflection, the _reflected_ _determinations_ are of another kind than the merely immediate determinations of being. These latter are the more easily granted to be transitory, merely relative, standing in relation to another; the reflected determinations, by contrast, have the form of being-in-and-for-itself; they therefore assert themselves as the _essential_ ones, and instead of being such as pass over into their opposed determinations, they appear rather as absolute, free, and mutually indifferent. They accordingly set themselves stubbornly against their own movement; the _being_ of these determinations is their identity with themselves in their determinateness, according to which they keep themselves in this relation utterly apart, even though they do presuppose one another.
