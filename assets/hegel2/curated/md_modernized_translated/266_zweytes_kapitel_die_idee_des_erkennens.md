## {{{ 12.192 }}} Chapter 2. The Idea of Cognition

Life is the immediate idea, or the idea as its _concept_ not yet realized in its own self. In its _judgment_ the idea is _cognition_ at large.

The concept is as concept _for_ _itself_ insofar as it concretely exists _freely_, as abstract universality or as genus. So it is its pure identity with itself, an identity that distinguishes itself within itself in such a way that what is distinguished is not an _objectivity_ but is likewise set free into subjectivity, into the form of simple sameness with itself, so that the object of the concept is the concept itself. Its _reality_ at large is the _form_ _of_ _its_ _existence_; everything turns on how this form gets determined; upon that determination depends how the concept _in_ _itself_, or as _subjective_, differs from the concept immersed in objectivity, and again from the concept within the idea of life. In the last of these the concept does stand apart from its outward reality and is posited _for_ _itself_; yet such being-for-itself accrues to it only as that identity which consists in referring to itself while immersed in an objectivity subjugated to it, or in referring to itself as the substantial form dwelling within. What raises the concept above life is that its reality is now concept-form liberated into universality. By this judgment the idea becomes twofold: a subjective concept having itself for its reality, and an objective one that is as life. – _Thinking_, _spirit_, _self-consciousness_ are determinations of the idea to the extent that it takes itself as object, and its _existence_, that is, the determinateness of the idea's being, is nothing other than its own self-differentiation.

The _metaphysics_ _of_ _spirit_, or, as people used more commonly to say, of the _soul_, revolved about the determinations of substance, simplicity, immateriality; – determinations in whose case the _representation_ of spirit drawn from _empirical_ consciousness was laid at the base as subject, and the question was then raised what sort of predicates agree with the perceptions; – a procedure that could get no further than the procedure of physics does in bringing the world of appearance under universal laws and determinations of reflection, since here too it was only spirit in its _appearance_ that lay at the base; indeed it had to fall short even of the scientific standing of physics, for since spirit is not only infinitely richer than nature but has for its essence the absolute unity of what is opposed within the _concept_, spirit exhibits in its {{{ 12.193 }}} appearance and in its reference to externality the contradiction at its highest determinateness, so that for each of the opposed determinations of reflection an experience can be adduced, or, in the manner of formal inference, the opposed determinations can be arrived at from the experiences. Since the predicates that issue immediately from the appearance belong in the first instance to empirical psychology, what is really left over for metaphysical consideration is only quite meagre determinations of reflection. – _Kant_, in his critique of the _rational_ _doctrine_ _of_ _the_ _soul_, pins this metaphysics down to the point that, insofar as it is to be a rational science, the least thing that one might _add_ from perception to the _universal_ _representation_ of self-consciousness would turn that science into an _empirical_ one and would spoil its rational purity and its independence of all experience. – There remains accordingly nothing but the simple representation, wholly empty of content on its own account: _I_, and of this one cannot so much as say that it is a _concept_; it is rather a _bare_ _consciousness_ that _accompanies_ _all_ _concepts_. By this _I_, or also _It_ (_the_ _thing_) which thinks, nothing further is represented, on Kant's further consequences, than a transcendental subject of the thoughts = x, knowable solely by way of the thoughts which constitute its _predicates_, and of which, held apart from them, we can _never_ have the _least_ _concept_; this I has withal, in Kant's own phrase, the _inconvenience_ that we _must_ _always_ _already_ _be_ _availing_ _ourselves_ _of_ _it_ in order to judge anything about it; for it is not so much _a_ _representation_ by which one particular object gets distinguished, as a _form_ of representation in general, insofar as representation is to be called cognition. – The _paralogism_ that the rational doctrine of the soul commits is then said to consist in this, that _modes_ of self-consciousness in thinking are made into _concepts_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_ as concepts of an _object_, that this: I _think_, is taken as a _thinking_ _being_, a _thing-in-itself_; in which manner the unwarranted inference is drawn, from my occurring in consciousness always as _subject_, and indeed as a _singular_ one, one _identical_ throughout all the manifoldness of representation and marking myself off from that manifoldness as external, that I am a _substance_, further something qualitatively _simple_, and a _one_, and something _existing_ _independently_ of the things in space and time. –

I have excerpted this exposition at some length, because it lets one recognize with precision both the nature of the erstwhile _metaphysics_ _of_ _the_ _soul_ and, above all, the nature of _the_ _critique_ through which that metaphysics foundered. – The former set out to determine the _abstract_ _essence_ of the soul; it began originally from perception and converted the empirical universality of perception, together with the determination of reflection that is _external_ {{{ 12.194 }}} to the singularity of the actual at large, into the form of the cited _determinations_ _of_ _essence_. – Kant, for his part, has before him nothing but the condition of the metaphysics of his own day, which for the most part stopped short at such abstract, one-sided determinations without any dialectic at all; the genuinely _speculative_ ideas of older philosophers concerning the concept of spirit he neither heeded nor examined. In his _critique_ of those determinations he simply followed Hume's manner of scepticism; namely, he holds fast to how the I appears in self-consciousness, but from this, since the _essence_ of the I, – _the_ _thing_ _in_ _itself_, is supposed to be cognized, everything empirical is to be left out; now nothing remains but this appearance of the: _I_ _think_, which accompanies all representations, – of which one has _not_ _the_ _slightest_ _concept_. – It must certainly be granted that one has not the least concept either of the I or of anything whatever, and of the concept itself as well, insofar as one does not _comprehend_ and stays put at the simple, fixed _representation_ and at the _name_. – Odd is the thought, – if it may be called a thought at all, – that I must already _avail_ myself of the I in order to judge about the I; the I that _avails_ itself of self-consciousness as a means in order to judge, this surely is an x, of which, as of the relation of such availing, one can have not the slightest concept. But it is surely laughable to call this nature of self-consciousness, that the I is what thinks itself, that no thinking of the I takes place except where it is the I doing the thinking, – an _inconvenience_ and, as something faulty, a _circle_; – a relation through which, in immediate empirical self-consciousness, the absolute, eternal nature of self-consciousness and of the concept reveals itself, and reveals itself for the reason that self-consciousness is precisely the _existent_, hence _empirically_ _perceivable_, pure _concept_, the absolute reference to itself which, as a sundering judgment, makes itself into its own object and is solely this, to make itself thereby into a circle. – A stone has no such _inconvenience_; when it is to be thought, or judged about, it does not stand in its own way; – it is relieved of the burden of availing itself of itself for this business; it is an other outside it that must take that trouble on.

The defect that these representations, which deserve to be called barbaric, place in this, that in the thinking of the I the I itself cannot be left out as _subject_, then shows itself conversely in this way too, that I come forward _only_ as _subject_ _of_ _consciousness_, or that I can _use_ myself only as _subject_ of a judgment, and that the _intuition_ is missing through which the I would be _given_ as an _object_; – but that the concept of a thing which can exist only as subject carries with it no objective reality whatever as yet. – If external intuition, determined in time and space, is what objectivity calls for, and it is this intuition that is found {{{ 12.195 }}} wanting, then one sees clearly enough that by objectivity nothing is meant but that sensuous reality above which to have raised oneself is the condition of thinking and of truth. But certainly, when the I is taken conceptlessly, as the plain simple representation we utter in the consciousness of every day, what we have is the abstract determination, not that self-referring which makes itself its own object; – so taken it is merely _one_ extreme, a subject one-sidedly lacking its objectivity, or it might just as well be object lacking subjectivity, were it not for the inconvenience already touched upon, that from the I as object the thinking subject refuses to be removed. Yet in fact this same inconvenience attends the first determination as well, the I as subject; for the I thinks _something_, whether itself or an other. That the two forms in which the I sets itself over against itself cannot be parted belongs to what is most proper to the nature of the I's concept, and of the concept generally; and this is just what Kant means to keep at bay, in order to retain nothing but what does not differentiate itself internally, hence nothing but the _conceptless_ _representation_. Something conceptless of that sort may well be set opposite the abstract determinations of reflection or categories of the older metaphysics; – in point of one-sidedness it ranks with them, even though they are for all that a higher achievement of thought; but held up against the profounder ideas of earlier philosophy on the concept of soul or of thinking, for instance the truly speculative ideas of Aristotle, it looks all the poorer and emptier. Had Kantian philosophy scrutinized those determinations of reflection, it had all the more reason to scrutinize the empty I clung to as an abstraction, the alleged idea of a thing-in-itself, which by reason of that very abstractness proves rather to be something thoroughly untrue; the felt inconvenience so lamented is itself the empirical datum wherein such an abstraction's untruth announces itself.

The only proof of the soul's persistence that the Kantian critique of rational psychology mentions is Mendelssohn's, and I cite its refutation of that proof for the sake of the curiousness of what is set against it. That proof is grounded on the _simplicity_ of the soul, by virtue of which the soul is said to be incapable of alteration, _of_ _passing_ _over_ _into_ _an_ _other_ in time. Qualitative simplicity is the form of _abstraction_ at large considered above; as _qualitative_ determinateness it was examined in the sphere of being, and it was proved there that the qualitative, as a determinateness referring to itself in this abstract fashion, is for that very reason rather dialectical and is nothing but the passing over into an other. In the case of the concept, by contrast, it was shown that if the concept is considered with respect to persistence, indestructibility, imperishability, it is what is in and for itself and eternal for the reason that it is not _abstract_ but _concrete_ simplicity, not a being-determined that refers to itself {{{ 12.196 }}} abstractly, but the unity _of_ _itself_ _and_ _of_ _its_ _other_, into which it therefore cannot pass over as though it were altering in it, and this precisely because the _other_, the being-determined, is the concept itself, so that in this passing over it only comes to itself. – The Kantian critique now sets the _quantitative_ determination over against that _qualitative_ determination of the unity of the concept. Although the soul, it is said, is not a manifold outside-one-another and contains no _extensive_ magnitude, consciousness nonetheless has _a_ _degree_, and the soul, like _everything_ _that_ _concretely_ _exists_, an _intensive_ _magnitude_; but with that the possibility of passing over into nothing through _gradual_ _vanishing_ is posited. – What, then, does this refutation amount to other than transferring to spirit one category _of_ _being_, namely _intensive_ _magnitude_? – transferring a determination that holds no truth of its own and stands rather sublated within the concept.

Metaphysics, – even that metaphysics which restricted itself to fixed concepts of the understanding and did not raise itself to the speculative, to the nature of the concept and of the idea, had for its purpose _to_ _cognize_ _the_ _truth_, and examined its objects as to whether they are something _true_ or not, substances or phenomena. But the victory of the Kantian critique over it consists rather in getting rid of the inquiry that has _the_ _true_ for its purpose, and of this purpose itself; it does not so much as raise the question that alone holds any interest, whether a determinate subject, here the _abstract_ _I_ _of_ _representation_, has truth in and for itself. To stop short at the appearance, however, and at whatever issues in everyday consciousness for mere representation, amounts to renouncing the concept and philosophy. Whatever goes beyond that counts, in the Kantian critique, as something overflying, something to which reason is by no means entitled. In fact the concept does overfly the conceptless, and the nearest entitlement to go beyond is, on the one side, the concept itself, and on the other, on the negative side, the untruth of appearance and of representation, as also of such abstractions as things-in-themselves and that I which is supposed to be no object for itself.

Within the connection of this logical exposition it is the _idea_ of _life_ out of which the idea of spirit has emerged, or, what says the same, as whose truth it has proved itself. Being this result, the idea possesses its truth in and for its own self, and with that truth the empirical, the appearance of spirit, may afterwards be compared, to see how far the two agree; the empirical, though, is itself likewise graspable only through the idea and out of it. Of _life_ we have seen that it is the idea, and yet it was shown at the same time that life is not yet the genuine exhibition, the very mode and manner in which the idea exists. For within life the idea's reality has the shape of _singularity_, whereas _universality_, the genus, remains the _inner_; hence the truth of life, as absolute negative unity, {{{ 12.197 }}} lies in sublating singularity in its abstract, that is to say immediate, form, and in being identical with itself _as_ _identical_, self-equal as genus. This idea is now _spirit_. – On this it may be further remarked that spirit is considered here in the form belonging to this idea as logical. For it has yet other shapes, mentionable here only in passing, in which it must be treated by the concrete sciences dealing with spirit, namely as _soul_, _consciousness_ _and_ _spirit_ _as_ _such_.

The name: _soul_ used formerly to be employed of the singular finite spirit at large, and the rational or empirical _doctrine_ _of_ _the_ _soul_ was meant to signify as much as _doctrine_ _of_ _spirit_. With the expression: _soul_ there hovers before one the representation that it is a _thing_ like other things; one asks after its _seat_, the _spatial_ determination from which its _forces_ operate; still more after how this thing is _imperishable_, subjected to the conditions of _temporality_ yet withdrawn from alteration within them. The system of _monads_ raises matter up into soul-likeness; on this representation the soul is an atom just as the atoms of matter are; the atom that rises as vapour from the coffee cup is, given favourable circumstances, capable of developing itself into a soul, and only the _greater_ obscurity of its representing distinguishes it from such a thing as appears as soul. – _The_ _concept_ _that_ _is_ _for_ _its_ _own_ _self_ must of necessity be in _immediate_ _existence_ as well; taken in that substantial identity with life, sunk as it is into its own externality, it belongs to the treatment of _anthropology_. But to anthropology too that metaphysics must remain alien in which this form of _immediacy_ becomes a _soul-thing_, an _atom_, on a par with the atoms of matter. – To anthropology there must be left only the dark region in which spirit stands under what used to be called _sidereal_ and _terrestrial_ influences, lives as a nature-spirit in _sympathy_ with nature and becomes aware of nature's alterations in _dreams_ and _presentiments_, dwells in the brain, the heart, the ganglia, the liver and so forth, to which last, according to Plato, the god gave the gift of _divination_, so that the _unreasoning_ part too should be provided for out of his goodness and have a share in what is higher, a gift above which the self-conscious human being stands raised. To this unreasoning side belongs further the relation of representing and of the higher spiritual activity, insofar as in the singular subject this activity is subjected to the play of wholly contingent bodily constitution, of external influences and of singular circumstances.

The lowest among the concrete shapes, the one where spirit lies immersed in materiature, has for its immediately higher shape _consciousness_. In this form the free concept is as the _I_ _that_ _is_ _for_ _itself_, withdrawn from objectivity yet referring itself to objectivity as to _its_ _other_, as to an object standing over against it. {{{ 12.198 }}} Since spirit here no longer is as soul, but rather, in the _certainty_ of itself, the _immediacy_ of _being_ has for it the significance _of_ _a_ _negative_, the identity in which it is with itself in the objective is at the same time still only a _shining_, inasmuch as the objective still also has the form of something _that_ _is_ _in_ _itself_. This stage is the object of _the_ _phenomenology_ _of_ _spirit_, – a science standing midway between the science of nature-spirit and that of spirit as such, and one which considers spirit that is _for_ _itself_ at the same time in its _reference_ _to_ _its_ _other_, an other that is thereby determined, as was recalled, both as object that is _in_ _itself_ and as negated, – considers spirit, that is, as _appearing_, as exhibiting itself in the opposite of itself.

The truth higher than this form, however, is _spirit_ _for_ _itself_, for which the object that is _in_ _itself_ for consciousness now bears the form of spirit's own determining, of _representation_ at large; such spirit, at work upon the determinations as upon what belongs to it, upon feelings, representations and thoughts, is to that degree infinite in itself and in its form. To examine this stage is the business of the _doctrine_ _of_ _spirit_ proper, which would cover the subject matter of what is ordinarily called _empirical_ _psychology_, though if it is to be a science of spirit its procedure cannot be empirical but has to be conceived scientifically. – Spirit at this stage is _finite_ spirit, insofar as the _content_ of its determinateness is immediately given; the science of it has to set forth the course along which spirit liberates itself from that determinateness of its own and moves on to lay hold of its truth, of infinite spirit.

The _idea_ _of_ _spirit_, by contrast, which is a _logical_ object, stands already within pure science; it has therefore no need to watch spirit run through the course in which spirit is entangled with nature, with immediate determinateness and with material or representation, which is what those three sciences consider; it has this course already behind it, or what is the same, rather ahead of it, – the former insofar as logic is taken as _the_ _last_ science, the latter insofar as it is taken as _the_ _first_, from which the idea makes its first transition into nature. Within the logical idea of spirit, accordingly, the I is at once, just as it emerged from the concept of nature as nature's truth, the free concept which in its judgment has itself for object, _the_ _concept_ _as_ _its_ _own_ _idea_. But in this shape too the idea is not yet completed.

Being the concept which, free though it be, has itself for object, the idea is _immediate_, and just on that account of immediacy it remains the idea under the aspect of its _subjectivity_, and thereby in its finitude at large. It is the _purpose_ that is to realize itself, or it is the _absolute_ _idea_ itself still in its {{{ 12.199 }}} _ap__pearance_. What it _seeks_ is the _true_, this identity of the concept itself and of reality, but as yet it only seeks it; for here, as it _first_ is, it is still something _subjective_. The object that is there for the concept is here, accordingly, also a given one, but it does not enter into the subject as an object acting upon it, nor as an object constituted as such on its own account, nor as representation; rather, the subject converts it into _a_ _determination_ _of_ _the_ _concept_; it is the concept that is at work in the object, that refers itself to itself therein, and that, by giving itself its reality in the object, finds _truth_.

The idea, then, is at first the one extreme of a syllogism, namely the concept which as purpose has at first no reality save its own subjective one; the opposite extreme, the restriction upon this subjectivity, is the objective world. What makes the two extremes identical is that both are the idea; in the first place their unity is the unity of the concept, which in the one is only _for_ _itself_ and in the other only _in_ _itself_; in the second place, reality is abstract in the one and, in the other, present in its concrete externality. – Cognition is what now _posits_ this unity; since it is the subjective idea which as purpose starts out from itself, the unity is at first only as _middle_ _term_. – What cognizes does indeed, by the determinateness of its concept, namely abstract being-for-itself, refer itself to an outer world; but it does so in the absolute certainty of itself, so as to lift the reality it has in its own self, this merely formal truth, into real truth. In its concept it has the _whole_ _essentiality_ of the objective world; its process is to posit the concrete content of that world, for itself, as identical with the _concept_, and conversely to posit the concept as identical with objectivity.

Immediately, the idea of appearance is _theoretical_ idea, _cognition_ as such. For immediately the objective world has for the concept that is for itself the form of _immediacy_ or of _being_, just as this concept is at first only the abstract concept of itself still enclosed within itself; it is therefore only as _form_; the reality it has in its own self is only its simple determinations of _universality_ and _particularity_; singularity, however, or the _determinate_ _determinateness_, the content, this form receives from outside.
