## C. Relation of Outer and Inner

1. Whole and parts stand in the relation that is immediate; hence within it each of the two immediacies, the reflected one and the one that is, possesses a self-subsistence of its own; but because they stand in the essential relation, that self-subsistence of theirs amounts to nothing but their negative unity. This is what has now come to be posited in force's expression: the reflected unity is essentially a becoming-other, a carrying over of itself into externality; but with equal immediacy externality is withdrawn back into that unity; whatever difference self-subsistent forces have sublates itself; and force's expression is nothing but the reflected unity mediating itself with its own self. Nothing is at hand but a difference that is empty and transparent, namely shine; and yet shine of this kind is the very mediating in which self-subsistent subsistence consists. These are not merely opposed determinations that sublate themselves in their own selves, nor is their movement merely a crossing over; for in part the {{{ 11.365 }}} immediacy taken as the starting point, out of which the passage into otherness was made, has no standing save as a posited one, and in part every one of the determinations is on that account already, in its immediacy, at one with its other, so that the crossing over is, quite as much and without qualification, a return into self that posits itself.

As the form of _reflected_ _immediacy_, that is, of essence, the _inner_ receives its determination over against the _outer_, which has the form of _being_; and yet the two make up but a single identity. – _First_, this identity is what the two are as one solid unity, a foundation replete with content, or the _absolute_ _fact_, on which the pair of determinations hang as indifferent, external moments. So considered, it is content, and it is that totality which the inner is — an inner becoming external quite as much, yet without thereby being anything that has come to be or has crossed over, for it remains equal to its own self. Taken with this determination, the outer does not merely stand _equal_ to the inner in point of content; the two are rather _one_ _fact_ and no more. – Being, however, _simple_ _identity_ with itself, this fact stands diverse from _its_ _form-determinations_, or rather these lie outside it; so considered, it is an inner in its own right, diverse from the externality belonging to it. That externality, though, has its whole content in this, that inner and outer, the two determinations themselves, are what make the fact up. And the fact, for its part, is no more than the unity of the two. So in point of content the two sides come to the same thing once again. Within the fact, however, they are present as an identity permeating itself, a foundation replete with content. Out in externality, on the other hand, as forms which the fact has, they stand indifferent to that identity, and so the pair of them stand indifferent to one another.

2. So taken, they are the diverse determinations of form, ones whose identical foundation lies not in their own selves but in an other; they are determinations of reflection existing for themselves — the inner as reflection-into-self in its form, as essentiality; the outer as the immediacy reflected into an other in its form, as unessentiality. Only, what the nature of the relation has shown is that these determinations make up one identity and nothing else whatever. In expressing itself, force consists precisely in this, that determining as presupposing and determining as returning into itself are one and the same. Where inner and outer have accordingly been taken as determinations of form, they are, _in the first place_, nothing but the simple form as such, and, _second_, since therein they are at the same time determined as opposites, what unifies them is a purely _abstract_ _mediation_, one in which each is _immediately_ its other and is that other _for the very reason_ that it is the one. The inner, then, is immediately _only_ the outer, and it carries the _determinateness_ of _externality_ just because it is the inner; and the other way round, an outer is _only_ an inner just because it is _only_ an outer. – Since this unity of form holds its two determinations within itself as opposites, the identity it has is just this crossing over, and within that only the _other_ of the pair, not any identity of theirs that would be _content-laden_. Or, put otherwise, clinging to form in this way is quite generally the side {{{ 11.366 }}} of _determinateness_. Whatever gets posited in keeping with that side is not the whole in its real totality, but only the totality, the fact itself, standing within _that_ _determinateness_ of form; and since form is the unqualified binding together of the two opposed determinations into one, then, taking either of them first – and which one is taken makes no difference – one must say of the foundation, or fact, that _on that very account_ it lies just as essentially within the other determinateness, and again _only_ within the other, in the same way as it was said at the outset to lie _only_ within the first. –

A something, accordingly, that is _only_ _as yet_ an _inner_ is on that very account _only_ an outer. Or the reverse: a something that is _only_ an _outer_ is on that very account _only_ an inner. Or again, with the inner determined as _essence_ and the outer as _being_: a fact that is only in its _essence_ is on that very account nothing but an immediate _being_, while a fact which merely _is_ is on that very account still only in its _essence_. – Outer and inner are determinateness posited in such fashion that neither of the two determinations merely presupposes the other and crosses over into it as into its truth; each, rather, so far as it is that truth of the other, stays there _posited_ _as_ _determinateness_ and points beyond to the totality of them both. – The _inner_ is thus what completes _essence_ so far as form goes. For essence, in being determined as inner, carries in itself the implication that it is defective and only is as a reference to its other, the outer; but the outer, in like manner, is not merely being, nor even concrete existence, but is as something referring itself to essence, to the inner. Yet what is at hand is not merely the reference of the two to one another, but the determinate reference belonging to absolute form, whereby each is immediately its own opposite, along with the reference they have in common _to_ _their_ _third_, or rather _to_ _their_ _unity_. Their mediation, though, is still without that identical foundation which would hold the two of them; their reference is on that account the immediate turning round of the one into the other; and the negative unity that ties them together is a simple point empty of content.
