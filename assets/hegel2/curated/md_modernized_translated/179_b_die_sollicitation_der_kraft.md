## {{{ 11.362 }}} b. The Solicitation of Force

Force is conditioned for this reason, that the moment of immediate concrete existence contained in it is only a _posited_ one – but, being at once an immediate one, is a _presupposed_ one, wherein force negates its own self. The externality on hand for force is accordingly _its_ _own_ _presupposing_ _activity_ itself, which at the outset stands posited as _another_ _force_.

This _presupposing_ is, further, mutual. Either of the two forces harbours the unity reflected into itself as sublated, and is on that account presupposing; it posits its own self as something external; that moment of externality is _its_ _own_ one; but because it is no less unity reflected into itself, it at the same time posits this externality of its _not_ _within_ _its_ _own_ _self_, but as another force.

Yet the external as such is what sublates its own self; and further, the activity that reflects itself into itself stands essentially referred to that external as to what is other to it, but no less as to something _in_ _itself_ _null_ _and_ _identical_ _with_ _it_. Seeing that the presupposing activity is quite as much reflection into self, it is the sublating of that negation of its, and posits the negation as its own self, or as _its_ own external. Thus force in conditioning is mutually an _impulse_ for the other force, an impulse against which the latter is active. Its bearing is not the passivity of getting determined, as though something else came into it thereby; the impulse only _solicits_ it. In its own self it is the negativity of itself; its repelling of itself from itself is its own positing. What it does, then, consists in sublating this, that the impulse is something external; it turns the impulse into a bare impulse and posits it as its own repelling of its own self from itself, as _its_ _own_ _expression_.

Force in expressing itself is thus the very same as what at the outset was only presupposing activity, that is, a making of itself external; but force in expressing itself is at once the activity that negates externality and _posits_ it as belonging to itself. Now so far as this consideration takes its start from force as being the negative unity of its own self and thereby presupposing reflection, it comes to the same thing as taking one's start, within the expression of force, from the soliciting impulse. Force is thus determined, _in_ _its_ _concept_, at first as self-sublating identity, and _in_ _its_ _reality_, the one of the two forces as soliciting, the other as the one solicited. But at large the concept of force is the identity of positing and of presupposing reflection, or of reflected and of immediate unity, and each of these determinations is outright a moment only, within unity, and hence is as mediated through the other. But equally, on the two forces standing in mutual reference {{{ 11.363 }}} there is no determination present of which would be the soliciting one and which the one being solicited; or rather, both form-determinations accrue to each alike. Yet this identity is not merely an external one of comparison; it is an essential unity of the two.

The one force, namely, is at the outset determined as _soliciting_, the other as _being_ _solicited_; in this manner the form-determinations show up as immediate differences of the two forces, differences on hand in themselves. But they are essentially mediated. The one force gets solicited; this impulse is a determination posited into it _from_ _without_. Force, however, is itself what presupposes; essentially it is a reflecting of itself into itself and a sublating of this, that the impulse should be something external. That it gets solicited is therefore its own doing; or it is determined through its own self that the other force is an other at all and is the soliciting one. The soliciting force refers negatively to its other, in that it sublates the other's externality; so far it is positing; but it is this only through the presupposition of having an other over against it; which is to say, soliciting it is itself only so far as it has an externality upon it, and hence only so far as it gets solicited. Or it is soliciting only so far as it gets solicited into being soliciting. Thus conversely the first gets solicited only so far as it itself solicits the other into soliciting it, the first, that is. Each of the two receives the impulse from the other, then; but the impulse which it gives as active consists precisely in this, that it receives an impulse from the other; and the impulse received by it is solicited by its own self. Both, the impulse given and the impulse received, or the active expression and the passive externality, are accordingly not something immediate but mediated; and indeed each of the two forces is thereby itself the determinateness which the other has towards it, is mediated through the other, and this mediating other is once again its own determining positing.

So this, that an impulse befalls force through another force, that force so far bears itself _passively_ but then again crosses over from this passivity into activity – this is force's going back into its own self. It expresses _itself_. The expression is reaction in the sense of positing externality as force's own moment, and thereby sublating this, that force should have been solicited by another force. Both are one thing, therefore: the expression of force, whereby through its negative activity upon its own self it gives itself an existence-for-other, and the infinite return, within this externality, upon its own self, such that therein it refers to itself alone. The presupposing reflection, to which conditionedness and the impulse belong, is on that account immediately the reflection returning {{{ 11.364 }}} into itself as well, and the activity is essentially a reacting, _against_ _itself_. To posit the impulse, or the external, is itself to sublate it, and inversely, to sublate the impulse is to posit externality.
