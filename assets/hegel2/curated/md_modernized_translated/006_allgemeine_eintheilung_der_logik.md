## {{{ 21.44 }}} General Division of Logic

In what has been said about the _concept_ of this science and about where its justification falls, it is implied that the general _division_ can here be only _provisional_, can as it were be given only insofar as the author already knows the science and is therefore in a position to state here in advance, _historically_, into which principal distinctions the concept will determine itself in the course of its development.

Yet the attempt may be made to render intelligible in advance and in a general way what is required for _dividing_, although here too a procedure of the method must be laid claim to which receives its full elucidation and justification only within the science. – To begin with, then, let it be recalled that what is presupposed here is that the _division_ has to be bound up with the _concept_, or better still, has to reside within the concept itself. Far from being indeterminate, the concept is _determinate_ in its own self; and what the division does is to set out this _determinateness_ of the concept in _developed_ shape; the division is the concept's _judgment_, not a judgment passed _upon_ some object picked up externally, but rather the concept's own judging, that is, its _determining_ of itself in its own self. Being right-angled, being acute-angled and so forth, as also being equilateral and so forth, the determinations by which triangles get divided up, have no place in the determinateness of the triangle as such, which is to say no place in what people are accustomed to call the concept of the triangle, no more place than there is, in what [holds] for the concept of animal in general, or of mammal, bird and so on, for those determinations by which animals get divided into mammals, birds and so on, and these classes in turn into further genera. Such determinations are taken up from elsewhere, from empirical intuition; they come to that so-called concept from outside. In the philosophical treatment of dividing, the concept must show itself as containing their origin.

But the concept of logic itself has been given in the introduction as the result of a science lying beyond it, and hence here likewise as a {{{ 21.45 }}} _presupposition_. Logic accordingly determined itself as the science of pure thinking, having for its principle _pure_ _knowledge_, a unity that is not abstract but, for that very reason, concrete and living, in that within it the opposition of consciousness between something subjectively _being_-_for_-_itself_ and a second such _being_, an objective one, is known as overcome, and being is known as the pure concept in its own self, and the pure concept as the true being. These, then, are the two _moments_ contained in the logical. But they are now known as being _inseparable_, not, as in consciousness, each _also_ as _being_ _for_ _itself_; solely because they are at the same time known as _distinguished_, (yet not as being for themselves), is their unity not abstract, dead, motionless, but concrete.

This unity constitutes the logical principle at the same time as _element_, so that the development of that difference which is immediately within it goes forward only _within_ this element. For since the division, as has been said, is the _judgment_ of the concept, the positing of the determination already immanent in it and hence of its difference, this positing must not be grasped as a redissolving of that concrete unity into its determinations as these are supposed to count as being for themselves, which here would be an empty falling back into the earlier standpoint, the opposition of consciousness; that opposition has rather vanished; that unity remains the element, and the differentiating of the division, and of the development generally, no longer steps out of it. With this, the determinations which earlier (on _the_ _way_ _to_ _truth_) counted as _being_ for themselves, such as a subjective and an objective, or thinking and being, or again concept and reality, however they may have been determined in one respect or another, are _now_ _in_ _their_ _truth_, that is, in their unity, brought down to the status of _forms_. Even as they differ, therefore, they each remain _in_ _themselves_ the whole concept, and what the division does is simply to posit this concept under determinations that are its own.

Thus it is the whole concept that is to be considered the one time as the concept _which_ _is_, the other time as _concept_; there it is only concept _in_ _itself_, the concept of reality or of being, here it is concept as such, concept _which_ _is_ _for_ _itself_, (as it is, to name concrete forms, in the thinking human being, but also already, though not as a _conscious_ and still less as a _known_ concept, in the sentient animal and in organic individuality generally; but concept _in_ _itself_ it is only in inorganic nature). – Logic would accordingly be divided in the first instance into the logic of the _concept_ as {{{ 21.46 }}} _being_, and of the concept _as_ _concept_, – or, to make use of the otherwise customary though most indeterminate and therefore most ambiguous expressions, – into _objective_ and _subjective_ logic.

But in accordance with the underlying element of the unity of the concept within itself, and hence of the inseparability of its determinations, these determinations must further, even insofar as they are _distinguished_ and the concept is posited in their _difference_, at least stand in _connection_ with one another. There results from this a sphere of _mediation_, the concept as the system of the _determinations of reflection_, that is, of being as it passes over into the _being-within-itself_ of the concept, a concept which in this way is not yet posited for itself _as_ _such_ but is at the same time burdened with immediate being as with something also external to it. This is _the_ _doctrine_ _of_ _essence_, standing as it does in the middle, between the doctrine of being and the doctrine of the concept. – In the general division of this logical work it has still been placed under _objective_ logic, inasmuch as, although essence is already the inner, the character of the _subject_ is expressly to be reserved for the concept.

_Kant_[^1] in recent times has counterposed to what has usually been called logic {{{ 21.47 }}} yet another, namely a _transcendental_ _logic_. In part, that which has been named _objective_ _logic_ in the present work would answer to what in him goes under the head of _transcendental_ _logic_. His way of marking it off from what he terms general logic runs thus: α) it takes into consideration those concepts which refer a priori to _objects_, and so refrains from abstracting from every _content_ of objective cognition, or else, it holds the rules for the pure thinking of an _object_; and β) it goes at the same time into where our cognition originates, so far as that cognition is not to be credited to the objects. – It is this second side to which Kant's philosophical interest is exclusively directed. His governing idea is that the _categories_ are to be vindicated as belonging to self-consciousness, to the _subjective_ I itself. Because it is determined in this way, the view halts inside consciousness together with its opposition, and it has let something over and above the empirical element of feeling and of intuition remain behind, something not posited or determined by self-consciousness in its thinking, a _thing-in-itself_, foreign to thinking and outside it; and yet one sees readily enough that an abstractum of that sort, the _thing-in-itself_, is nothing but a product of thinking, of thinking indeed in its merely abstracting mode. – When other Kantians have put the determining of the _object_ by the I in these terms, that the I's objectifying counts as an originary and necessary deed of consciousness, such that within this originary deed the representation of the I is not yet on hand, – since such a representation would first be a consciousness of that consciousness, or would itself be an objectifying of that consciousness, – then this objectifying deed, once released from the opposition of consciousness, is more precisely what can be taken quite generally for _thinking_ as such. [^2] But this doing should no longer be called {{{ 21.48 }}} consciousness; consciousness encloses within itself the opposition of the I and its object, an opposition not present in that original doing. The name consciousness casts upon it the shine of subjectivity even more than does the expression _thinking_, which here, however, is to be taken quite generally in an absolute sense, as _infinite_ thinking unburdened with the finitude of consciousness, in short as _thinking_ _as_ _such_.

[^1]: I note that I take frequent account of the Kantian philosophy in this work, (which to some may seem superfluous) because, – however its more precise determinateness as well as the particular parts of its execution may be regarded elsewhere and also in this work, – it constitutes the foundation and the starting point of recent German philosophy, and this merit of it remains undiminished by whatever fault may be found with it. Account is also to be taken of it frequently in the objective logic because it enters more closely into important and _more determinate_ sides of the logical, whereas later presentations of philosophy have paid little attention to the same and in part have often shown only a crude, – though not unavenged, – contempt for it. The philosophizing most widely current among us does _not_ step beyond the Kantian results, that reason can cognize no true content and that with respect to absolute truth one must be referred to faith. But what in Kant is a result is in this philosophizing made the immediate starting point, so that the preceding development from which that result comes, and which is philosophical cognition, is cut away in advance. The Kantian philosophy thus serves as a cushion for the indolence of thinking, which sets itself at ease with the thought that everything has already been proved and settled. For cognition, therefore, and for a determinate content of thinking, which is not to be found in such barren and arid repose, one must turn to that preceding development.

[^2]: Should the phrase _objectifying_ deed of the I put one in mind of other productions of spirit, those of _imagination_ for instance, let it be observed that what is under discussion is a determining of an object in so far as the moments of that object's content do _not_ belong _to_ _feeling_ and _to_ _intuition_. An object of that kind is a _thought_, and determining it means on the one hand bringing it forth in the first place, on the other hand, where it is already something presupposed, entertaining further thoughts concerning it, unfolding it further in thinking.

Now inasmuch as what engaged the Kantian philosophy was the so-called _transcendental_ aspect of the determinations of thought, the treatment of those determinations themselves came off empty-handed; what they are in their own selves, apart from that abstract relation to the I which is one and the same for all of them, how each is determined over against the others and how each stands related to the others, was never made a subject of consideration; and so cognition of their nature has not been advanced in the slightest by this philosophy. The only interesting thing bearing on this occurs in the critique of the ideas. – For philosophy to make actual progress, however, it was necessary that thinking's interest be drawn toward the formal side, toward the I, toward consciousness as such, which is to say toward the abstract relating of a subjective knowledge to an object, and that in this manner cognition of the _infinite_ _form_, of the concept, that is, be set under way. In order to attain this cognition, however, that finite determinateness in which the form is as I, as consciousness, had still to be stripped away. The form thus thought out into its purity then contains within its own self the capacity to _determine_ itself, that is, to give itself content, and to give it in its necessity, – as a system of the determinations of thought.

The objective logic thus takes rather the place of the former _metaphysics_, which was the scientific edifice concerning the world that was to be raised by _thoughts_ alone. – If we take account of the last shape in which this science was developed, then it is first of all and immediately _ontology_ whose place the objective logic takes, – that part of the said metaphysics which was to investigate the nature of the _Ens_ in general; – the Ens comprehends within itself both _being_ and _essence_, {{{ 21.49 }}} a difference for which our language has fortunately preserved distinct expressions. – But then the objective logic also comprehends within itself the rest of metaphysics insofar as this sought to grasp with the pure forms of thought the particular substrates taken in the first instance from representation, the soul, the world, God, and the _determinations_ of _thinking_ made up the _essential_ element of the manner of consideration. But logic considers these forms free from those substrates, the subjects _of_ _representation_, and considers their nature and worth in and for themselves. That metaphysics omitted to do this and thus incurred the just reproach of having employed them _without_ _critique_, without the prior investigation whether and how they are capable of being determinations of the thing-in-itself, in Kant's expression, – or rather of the rational. – Hence it is the objective logic that supplies the genuine critique of these forms, – a critique that examines them, not under the abstract form of apriority set against the aposteriori, but in the particular content each of them has.

_The_ _subjective_ _logic_ is the logic of the _concept_, – the logic of an essence that has sublated its reference to a being or to its shine, and that in its determination is external no longer, but is rather the free and self-subsistent subjective which determines itself within itself, or rather is the _subject_ itself. – Since the _subjective_ carries with it the misunderstanding of something contingent and arbitrary, as also of determinations generally that belong to the form of _consciousness_, no particular weight is here to be placed on the difference between subjective and objective, a difference that will later be developed more closely within logic itself.

Broadly speaking, then, logic does indeed break up into _objective_ and _subjective_ logic; taken more precisely, however, its parts are three:

I. _The_ _logic_ _of_ _being_,

II. _the_ _logic_ _of_ _essence_ as well as

III. _the_ _logic_ _of_ _the_ _concept_.
