## {{{ 12.154 }}} Chapter 3. Teleology

Wherever _purposiveness_ is perceived, an _understanding_ is taken to be its originator, so that for purpose one demands the concept's own free concrete existence. _Teleology_ is set over against _mechanism_ above all, since in mechanism the determinateness posited at the object is essentially an external one, a determinateness at which no _self-determination_ shows itself. The opposition between Causis efficientibus and Causis finalibus, between merely _efficient_ and _final_ _causes_, refers back to that same difference, and to it, taken in a concrete shape, there also reverts the inquiry whether the world's absolute essence should be grasped as a blind mechanism of nature or instead as an understanding determining itself by purposes. The antinomy of _fatalism_ together with _determinism_, and of _freedom_, likewise bears on the opposition between mechanism and teleology; for the free is the concept in its concrete existence.

The metaphysics of former times handled these concepts as it handled its others; partly it presupposed a representation of the world and laboured to show that this or that concept suits it while the opposite one falls short, since the world admits of no _explanation_ from it; partly it left uninvestigated, in doing so, the concept of mechanical cause and of purpose, the question which of them has truth _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_. Suppose that has been settled on its own account: then the objective world may offer up mechanical causes and final causes as it will; its concrete existence is no standard of the _true_, and it is rather the true that yields the criterion for which of these concrete existences is the world's genuine one. Just as the subjective understanding displays errors at itself as well, so the objective world displays those sides and levels of truth too which taken by themselves are still one-sided, incomplete, and no more than relations of appearance. Where mechanism and purposiveness stand opposed, precisely on that account they cannot be treated as _indifferent_ ones, each of them a correct concept on its own and holding just as good as the other, with the whole issue turning merely on _where_ the one or the other admits of application. That the two should count equally rests on nothing more than that they _are_, which is to say, that we _have_ the two of them. Yet their being opposed makes the first, unavoidable question this: which of the two is the true one; while the higher and genuinely pressing question is _whether_ _a_ _third_ _is_ _not_ _their_ _truth_, _or_ _whether_ _the_ _one_ {{{ 12.155 }}} _is_ _the_ _truth_ _of_ _the_ _other_. – The _purpose-reference_, however, has shown itself to be the truth of _mechanism_. – Whatever presented itself as _chemism_ is taken together with _mechanism_ to this extent, that purpose is the concept in free concrete existence and has confronting it, quite generally, the concept's unfreedom, its submergedness in externality; the two, mechanism as well as chemism, are on that account gathered under natural necessity, since in the first the concept has no concrete existence at the object, the object as mechanical harbouring no self-determination, whereas in the second the concept either has a strained, one-sided concrete existence, or else, insofar as it steps forward as the unity that strains the neutral object apart into the extremes, is external to its own self insofar as it sublates this separation.

The more closely the teleological principle was bound up with the concept of an _extramundane_ understanding, and the more it was favoured on that score by piety, the further it seemed to withdraw from genuine inquiry into nature, an inquiry that means to cognize nature's properties not as something alien to it but as _immanent_ _determinatenesses_, and that grants the title of a _comprehending_ to no other cognizing. Purpose being the concept itself in its concrete existence, it may well strike one as strange that cognizing objects out of their concept should look rather like an illegitimate step across into a _heterogeneous_ element, while mechanism, for which an object's determinateness counts as one posited at the object from outside and through an other, ranks as the _more_ _immanent_ outlook by comparison with teleology. Mechanism, at any rate the ordinary unfree sort, and chemism along with it, does of course have to count as an immanent principle to this extent, that the determining _external_ is itself _once_ _more_ _only_ _such_ _an_ _object_, an externally determined one to which such becoming-determined is a matter of indifference, or that in chemism the other object is a chemically determined one in its turn, so that quite generally some essential moment of the totality is always lodged in an outer term. Such principles accordingly come to a halt inside one and the same natural shape of finitude; and yet, unwilling as they are to reach out beyond the finite, and leading as they do for appearances only to finite causes that themselves call for a further step, they nevertheless broaden out at the same time, partly into a formal totality within the concept of force, of cause and of like determinations of reflection, which are meant to designate an _originariness_, and partly through the abstract _universality_ of an _all_ _of_ _the_ _forces_, a _whole_ made up of reciprocal causes. That mechanism is itself a striving after totality shows in this, that it undertakes to grasp nature _for_ _itself_ as a _whole_ needing nothing else for _its_ concept, – a totality not to be met with in {{{ 12.156 }}} purpose, nor in the extramundane understanding bound up with purpose.

What purposiveness now displays at the outset is something _higher_ generally; an _understanding_ which determines the manifoldness of objects _externally_, _by_ _means_ _of_ _a_ _unity_ _that_ _is_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, with the result that the objects' indifferent determinatenesses come to be _essential_ _through_ _this_ _reference_. In mechanism they come to be so through the _bare_ _form_ _of_ _necessity_, and here their _content_ is a matter of indifference, for they are meant to stay external, and only the understanding as such is to be satisfied, by cognizing its own interconnection, the abstract identity. In teleology, by contrast, the content acquires weight, since teleology presupposes a concept, something _determined_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ and therefore self-determining, and has consequently marked off, from the _reference_ of the differences and of their determination through one another, from the _form_, namely the unity _reflected_ _into_ _itself_, something _determined_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ and hence _a_ _content_. Yet if that content is otherwise a _finite_ and trifling one, it stands in contradiction with what it ought to be, since purpose is, according [to] its form, a totality _infinite_ _within_ _itself_; – all the more so when the acting that works by purposes is taken to be _absolute_ will and understanding. Teleology has laid itself so wide open to the charge of silliness because the purposes it exhibited are, as chance would have it, now weightier and now utterly slight, and the purpose-reference among objects was bound to look time and again like mere play, this reference showing itself as so external and hence as contingent. Mechanism for its part leaves the determinatenesses of objects, so far as their tenor goes, with the worth of contingencies, towards which the object is indifferent and which are to hold no higher standing either for themselves or for the subjective understanding. In its interconnection of external necessity this principle therefore affords the consciousness of an infinite freedom, over against teleology, which erects the trivialities, indeed the contemptible items, of its content into something absolute, where the more universal thought can discover for itself nothing but an infinite constriction, together with a downright nauseating affection.

The formal drawback under which this teleology at first labours is that it reaches no further than _outer_ _purposiveness_. With the concept posited in this way as something formal, the content too is for this teleology something given to the concept from outside, within the manifoldness of the objective world, – in the very determinatenesses that also make up mechanism's content, though there as something external and contingent. It is this element held in common that makes the _form_ _of_ {{{ 12.157 }}} _purposiveness_, all by itself, what is essential in the teleological. Looked at in this way, and leaving aside for now the difference between outer and inner purposiveness, the purpose-reference as such has proved itself in and for itself to be the _truth_ _of_ _mechanism_. – What teleology possesses on the whole is the higher principle, namely the concept as it stands in concrete existence, the concept that in and for itself is the infinite and the absolute; – a principle of freedom, one which, wholly assured of its own self-determination, has been torn absolutely free of the _external_ _becoming-determined_ that belongs to mechanism.

Among the great merits _Kant_ has earned in philosophy is the distinction he set up between relative or _outer_ purposiveness and _inner_ purposiveness; in the latter he unlocked the concept of _life_, the _idea_, and with it lifted philosophy _positively_ above the determinations of reflection and above metaphysics' relative world, something the Critique of Reason accomplishes only imperfectly, on a very slanted turn, and only _negatively_. – It has been recalled that the opposition of teleology and mechanism is initially the more universal opposition of _freedom_ and _necessity_. It was in this form that Kant listed the opposition among reason's _antinomies_, specifically as the _third_ _conflict_ _of_ _the_ _transcendental_ _ideas_. – His presentation, already referred to earlier, I cite only very briefly, since what is essential in it is simple enough to require no drawn-out exposition, and since the manner of the Kantian antinomies has been illuminated at greater length elsewhere.

The _thesis_ of the one to be looked at here reads: causality after the laws of nature is not the sole causality from which the appearances of the world can one and all be derived. For the explanation of them it is necessary to assume, in addition, a causality through freedom.

The _antithesis_: there is no freedom; on the contrary, everything in the world takes place solely after the laws of nature.

As with the other antinomies, the proof works in the first place apagogically, the contrary of each thesis being assumed; in the second place, to exhibit what is contradictory in that assumption, the contrary of the assumption is in turn assumed, which is accordingly the proposition to be proved, and it is presupposed as holding good; – the entire roundabout of proving might therefore just as well have been spared; it consists in nothing beyond the assertoric affirmation of the two opposed propositions.

For the proof of the _thesis_ one is first supposed to assume that there is _no_ _other_ _causality_ than one following _laws_ _of_ _nature_, which is to say, following the necessity of mechanism at large, chemism being included in it. This proposition, however, is said to contradict itself, on the ground that the law of nature consists precisely in this, {{{ 12.158 }}} that nothing happens _without_ _a_ _sufficiently_ à priori _determined_ _cause_, one that therefore harbours an absolute spontaneity within itself; – which is to say: what makes the assumption opposed to the thesis contradictory is simply that it contradicts the thesis.

To serve the proof _of_ _the_ _antithesis_ one _is_ _to_ posit that there is a _freedom_, a peculiar sort of causality able to begin absolutely a state, and with it a series of that state's consequences. Now since a beginning of that sort _presupposes_ a state having _no_ _connection_ _of_ _causality_ at all with the state preceding it in the same series, it stands in contradiction with _the_ _law_ _of_ _causality_, apart from which no unity of experience, indeed no experience whatever, is possible; – which is to say: the assumption of freedom, opposed as it is to the antithesis, cannot be made, for the reason that it contradicts the antithesis.

Essentially the same antinomy comes back in the _Critique_ of the _teleological_ _power_ _of_ _judgment_ as the opposition: that _all_ _generation_ _of_ _material_ _things_ happens _by_ _merely_ _mechanical_ _laws_, and that _some_ _generation_ _of_ _such_ _things_ _is_ _not_ _possible_ _by_ _laws_ _of_ _that_ _kind_. – Kant resolves this antinomy just as he resolves the rest in general; reason, that is, is in a position to prove neither the one proposition nor the other, since with respect to the possibility of things under merely empirical laws of nature we _can_ _have_ _no_ _determining_ _principle_ à priori; – hence, further, that the pair of them have to count, not _as_ _objective_ _propositions_, but rather _as_ _subjective_ _maxims_; that _on_ _the_ _one_ _side_ _I_ ought at all times to _reflect_ on every natural event by the principle of bare natural mechanism, though this is no bar to _hunting_ _out_ certain natural forms, _as_ _occasion_ _arises_, by a _different_ _maxim_, namely by the principle of final causes; – as if these _two_ _maxims_, which incidentally are supposed to be required only for _human_ _reason_, did not stand in exactly the opposition in which those _propositions_ stand. – As was noted a moment ago, this entire standpoint leaves uninvestigated the one thing philosophical interest demands, namely which of the two principles holds truth in and for itself; and for that vantage point nothing turns on whether the principles are to be viewed as _objective_ determinations of nature, which here means externally existing ones, or as bare _maxims_ of a _subjective_ cognizing; – such cognizing is in fact subjective, that is, contingent, applying now this maxim and now that _as_ _occasion_ _arises_, according as it deems one or the other suited to the objects given, and otherwise raising no question about the _truth_ of these determinations themselves, be they determinations of the objects or of cognizing.

{{{ 12.159 }}} Inadequate though the Kantian treatment of the teleological principle is, then, with respect to the essential point of view, the place Kant assigns that principle is always worth noting. By attributing it to a _reflecting_ _power_ _of_ _judgment_, he turns it into a _middle_ _member_ that binds _the_ _universal_ of _reason_ to _the_ _singular_ _of_ _intuition_; – he goes on to mark off that _reflecting_ power of judgment from the _determining_ one, the latter merely _subsuming_ the particular under the universal. A universal of that kind, one that only _subsumes_, is an _abstract_ universal, one that first becomes _concrete_ at an _other_, at the particular. Purpose on the other hand is the _concrete_ _universal_, carrying within itself the moment of particularity and of externality, and for that reason active, the drive to repel itself away from itself. Taken as purpose the concept is certainly an _objective_ _judgment_, one in which the one determination is the subject, namely the concrete concept as determined through its own self, while the other is not a predicate merely but external objectivity. Yet the purpose-reference is on that account no _reflecting_ judging that surveys external objects merely under a unity, _as_ _though_ some understanding had provided them _for_ _the_ _behoof_ _of_ _our_ _faculty_ _of_ _cognition_; it is instead the true that is in and for itself, judging _objectively_ and determining external objectivity absolutely. With that, the purpose-reference amounts to more than a _judgment_; it is the _syllogism_ in which the free, self-subsistent concept joins together with itself by way of objectivity.

What has emerged as the _third_ over against mechanism and chemism is purpose; it constitutes their truth. Standing as it still does within the sphere of objectivity, or within the immediacy of the concept in its totality, purpose remains affected by externality as such, and an objective world confronts it, one to which it refers itself. Seen from this side, mechanical causality, under which chemism is on the whole to be included, still turns up alongside this _purpose-reference_, the _external_ one, though as _subordinated_ _to_ _it_, as in and for itself sublated. So far as the closer relation goes, the mechanical object, being an immediate totality, is indifferent towards its being-determined, and with that towards being anything that determines. This external being-determined has by now been developed on into self-determination, whereby the concept within the object that is merely _inner_, or, what comes to the same, the merely _outer_ _concept_, stands _posited_ at last; purpose is in the first instance just this concept itself, external to what is mechanical. For chemism too, then, purpose is what does the self-determining, leading the external becoming-determined by which chemism is conditioned back into the unity of the concept. – From this it follows how the two earlier forms of the objective process come to be subordinated; the other which lies at them in the infinite progress is nothing but the concept posited to begin with as something external to them, and this concept {{{ 12.160 }}} is purpose; the concept is not merely their substance, but externality as well is the moment essential to them, the moment constituting their determinateness. Owing to its character of being externally determined, then, mechanical or chemical technique presents itself of its own accord to the purpose-reference, which is now to be examined more closely.
