## Remark 2

_Idealism_ is constituted by the proposition that the _finite_ _is_ _ideal_. Philosophy's idealism amounts to nothing more than this, that the finite is not acknowledged as a truly existent. Every philosophy is essentially idealism, or has idealism at least for its principle, and the only question then is how far that principle is actually carried through. Philosophy is idealism quite as much as religion is; for religion just as little acknowledges finitude as a genuine being, as something ultimate, absolute, or as something unposited, uncreated, eternal. Hence the opposition of idealist and realist philosophy is without significance. A philosophy which ascribed to finite existence as such a genuine, ultimate, absolute being would forfeit any claim to the name of philosophy; the principles of philosophies older or more recent, water, or matter, or the atoms, are _thoughts_, universals, something ideal, and not things as they are immediately met with, i.e. in sensuous singularity, that Thaletic water no more than the rest; for, empirical water though it also is, it is besides that at the same time the _in-itself_ or _essence_ of everything else; and these other things are not self-subsistent, grounded within themselves, but _posited_ out of an other, out of water, i.e. ideal. Since the principle, the universal, has just been called the _ideal_, as the concept, the idea, spirit are still more to be called _something_ _ideal_, and since in turn the singular sensuous things count as _ideal_ in the principle, in the concept, still more in spirit, count as sublated, attention has to be drawn here provisionally to the same {{{ 21.143 }}} two-sidedness that showed itself in the case of the infinite, namely that at one time the ideal is the concrete, the truly existent, while at another time its moments are just as much the ideal, the sublated within it, whereas in fact only the one concrete whole is, and its moments cannot be severed from it.

By the ideal what is chiefly meant is the form _of_ _representation_, and whatever lies _in_ my representing generally, or _in_ the concept, _in_ the idea, _in_ the imagination and so forth, is called _ideal_, so that the ideal in general holds good for figments too, – representations which are not merely distinguished from the real but are supposed to be essentially _not_ real. Spirit, in fact, is the genuine _idealist_ generally; within it, already as it feels and represents, and still more insofar as it thinks and comprehends, the content is not present as what is called _real_ _existence_; in the I's simplicity an external being of that sort stands merely sublated, it is _for_ _me_, within me it is _ideal_. This subjective idealism, whether it be the unconscious idealism of consciousness as such or be consciously pronounced and erected into a principle, bears merely on the _form_ of representation, the form by which a content is mine; within the systematic idealism of subjectivity that form gets asserted as the only true one, as excluding the form of objectivity or of reality, of that content's _external_ _existence_. Such idealism is formal, in that it pays no heed to the _content_ of representing or thinking, a content which in the representing or thinking may meanwhile remain wholly within its finitude. Nothing is lost with such idealism, as much because the reality of such finite content, existence filled out with finitude, is retained, as because, insofar as abstraction is made from it, nothing is supposed to depend _in_ _itself_ on such content; and nothing is gained with it, precisely because nothing is lost, because I, representation, spirit stays filled with the very same content of finitude. The opposition in point of form between subjectivity and objectivity is admittedly a finitude among others; the _content_, however, as it gets taken up into feeling, into intuition, or even into the more abstract element of representation, of thinking, holds finitudes in abundance, and these, with the exclusion of that single mode of finitude, the form of the subjective and the objective, have by no means yet been cleared away, still less have they dropped off of themselves.
