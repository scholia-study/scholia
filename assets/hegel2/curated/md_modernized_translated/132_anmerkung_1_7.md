## {{{ 11.261 }}} Remark 1

A thinking that confines itself to external reflection, acquainted with no other kind of thinking than this, never arrives at cognizing identity as it has just now been grasped, or essence, these being one and the same. Before such a thinking there stands only ever abstract identity, and, outside it and beside it, difference. It supposes reason to be nothing further than a loom on which it externally binds and interlaces the warp, say identity, and then the weft, difference; or again, analysing, now draws identity out on its own and _then_ _once_ _more_ obtains difference _alongside_ it, is now an equating and _then_ _once_ _more_ an unequating; – an equating in that one abstracts from difference, – an unequating in that one _abstracts_ from the equating. – Such assurances and opinions about what reason supposedly does must be set wholly aside, being in a manner merely _historical_ ones, whereas the consideration of anything whatever that is shows, _of_ _it_ itself, that in its likeness with itself it is unlike itself and self-contradicting, while in its diversity, in its contradiction, it is identical with itself, and that it is, on its own account, just this movement whereby either of these determinations crosses over into the other — and so because either of them is, on its own account, its very opposite. The concept of identity — namely, to be simple self-referring negativity — is no product of external reflection; it emerged from being itself. Whereas by contrast that identity which is supposed to lie outside difference, and that difference which is supposed to lie outside identity, are products of external reflection and of the abstraction that arbitrarily fastens on this point of indifferent diversity.

2. This identity is at first essence itself and as yet no determination of essence; it is reflection entire, and not some one moment marked off within reflection. Being absolute negation, it negates itself at once; it is a non-being, a difference perishing in the very act of arising, or a distinguishing that distinguishes nothing and instead falls back straightway into itself. Distinguishing posits non-being, and posits it as the other's non-being. The other's non-being, however, means the sublating of that other, and with it of distinguishing as such. Yet distinguishing is present here as negativity relating itself to itself, as a non-being that is the non-being of itself; a non-being whose non-being lies not in something else, {{{ 11.262 }}} but in it. There is therefore present the self-referring, the reflected difference, or pure, _absolute_ _difference_.

Or identity is reflection into itself, which is such only as an inward repelling, and this repelling it is qua reflection into itself, a repelling that immediately draws itself back into itself. It is thus identity as difference that is self-identical. Difference, however, is self-identical only to the extent that it is not identity but absolute non-identity. And non-identity is absolute to the extent that it holds nothing other than itself within it, only itself — which is to say, to the extent that it is absolute self-identity.

Identity is therefore, _on_ _its_ _own_ _account_, absolute non-identity. But over against that it is also the _determination_ of identity. For, being reflection into itself, it makes of itself its own non-being; it is the totality, and yet qua reflection it makes of itself a moment of its own, a positedness, from out of which it is the returning-into-itself. Only as its moment in this way is it identity as such, as the _determination_ of simple likeness with itself, over against absolute difference.
