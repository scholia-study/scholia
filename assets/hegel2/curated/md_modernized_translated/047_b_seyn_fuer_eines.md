## b. Being-for-one

Expressed in this moment is how the finite is when in unity with the infinite, that is, as something ideal. Being-for-itself does not have the negation _upon_ _it_ in the shape of a determinateness or limit, and consequently not as a reference to any existence other than itself. Now that this moment has been designated _being-for-one_, nothing is as yet at hand for which it might be, – no one, that is, whose moment it might be. Within being-for-itself nothing of the kind has in fact yet been fixed; that for which a something would be (– and there is no something here –), what the other side is supposed to be in general, is likewise a moment, itself merely being-for-one, and not yet a one. – So an undifferentiatedness still stands between the two sides that may hover before one in being-for-one; only _one_ being-for-other, and because it is only _one_ being-for-other, it too is only being-for-one; there exists only the single ideality belonging both to that for which, or in which, a determination is as moment, and to that which is supposed to be moment within it. So _being-for-one_ and _being-for-itself_ make up no true determinatenesses over against each other. Insofar as the difference is granted for a moment and the talk here is of something _existing-for-itself_, then it is this existing-for-itself, as {{{ 21.147 }}} the sublatedness of otherness, that itself relates to itself as to the other now sublated, and so is _for-one_; within its other it relates to nothing but itself. The ideal is necessarily _for-one_, but for an _other_ it is not; the one for which it is remains nothing but itself. – I, then, spirit in general, or God, are ideal, because they are infinite; but ideal they are not as existing-for-themselves, distinct from what is for-one. For in that case they would be merely immediate ones, or more precisely existence and a being-for-other, since whatever was for them would not be they themselves but an other, if the moment of being for-one were not to belong to them. God is therefore _for_ _himself_ to the extent that he is himself what is _for_ _him_.

So these two, being-for-itself and being-for-one, are not different meanings of ideality; they are essential moments of it and cannot be severed from one another.
