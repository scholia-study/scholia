## Remark

By what has gone before, _universality_, _particularity_ and _singularity_ are the _three_ determinate concepts, namely if one wishes to _count_ them. It has already been shown earlier that number is an unfitting form in which to cast determinations of the concept, and altogether the most unfitting for determinations of the concept itself; number, since it has the one for its principle, makes what is counted into things utterly severed and utterly indifferent to one another. It has emerged in the foregoing that the diverse determinate concepts are, quite simply, rather only _one_ and the same concept than that they fall apart into number.

In the otherwise customary treatment of logic there occur all manner of _divisions_ _and_ _kinds_ _of_ _concepts_. The inconsequence in this strikes the eye at once, that the kinds are introduced thus: _there_ _are_, according to quantity, quality and so forth, the following concepts. _There_ _are_ expresses no other warrant than this, that one _comes upon_ such kinds and that they show themselves in accordance with _experience_. In this way one obtains an _empirical_ _logic_, – an odd science, an _irrational_ cognition of the _rational_. Logic thereby gives a very bad example of compliance with its own teachings; it permits itself, in its own case, to do the opposite of what it prescribes as a rule, {{{ 12.44 }}} that concepts are to be derived and scientific propositions (hence also the proposition that there are so and so many kinds of concept) are to be proved. – The Kantian philosophy commits a further inconsequence here: it _borrows_ _for_ _the_ _transcendental_ _logic_ the categories, as so-called root concepts, from subjective logic, in which they had been picked up empirically. Since it concedes the latter, there is no seeing why the transcendental logic resolves to borrow from such a science instead of reaching out empirically at once on its own.

To adduce something of this, concepts are divided chiefly according to their _clarity_, namely into _clear_ _and_ _obscure_, _distinct_ _and_ _indistinct_, into _adequate_ _and_ _inadequate_. The _complete_, the _overflowing_ and other such superfluities may also be brought in here. – As regards that division according to _clarity_, it soon shows itself that this standpoint, along with the differences that attach to it, derives from _psychological_ rather than _logical_ determinations. The so-called _clear_ concept is said to be enough for telling one object apart from another; a thing of that sort is not yet to be called a concept, it is nothing more than the _subjective_ _representation_. What an _obscure_ concept might be has to be left resting on itself, since otherwise it would not be obscure but would turn into a distinct one. – The _distinct_ concept is said to be one for which the _marks_ can be given. Accordingly it is properly the _determinate_ _concept_. The mark, taking hold of whatever is right in it, is nothing else than the _determinateness_ or simple _content_ of the concept, so far as that content is held apart from the form of universality. At first, though, the _mark_ carries no such exact sense; quite generally it is merely a determination by which some _third party_ makes a note of an object or of the concept for himself; it may accordingly be a quite contingent circumstance. On the whole it voices not so much the immanence and essentiality of the determination as that determination's reference to an _external_ understanding. If this last is truly an understanding, it has the concept before it and notes it by nothing else than by _what_ _is_ _in_ _the_ _concept_. Should the mark be something other than this, however, then it is a _sign_, or else a determination pertaining to the _representation_ of the fact rather than to its concept. – What the _indistinct_ concept may be can be passed by as superfluous.

The _adequate_ concept, however, is something higher; what really hovers before one there is the agreement of the concept with reality, which is not the concept as such but the _idea_.

{{{ 12.45 }}} If the _mark_ of the distinct concept were really supposed to be the determination of the concept itself, logic would get into embarrassment over the _simple_ concepts, which by another division are set over against the _composite_ ones. For if a true, that is, an immanent mark were to be specified for the simple concept, one would not want to regard it as a simple one; but insofar as none were specified for it, it would not be a distinct concept. Here, however, the _clear_ concept comes to the rescue. Unity, reality and the like determinations are supposed to be _simple_ concepts, presumably only on the ground that the logicians could not manage to find out the determination of them and therefore contented themselves with having a merely _clear_ concept of them, that is, none at all. For the _definition_, that is, for the specification of the concept, the specification of the genus and of the specific difference is generally demanded. It therefore gives the concept not as something simple but in _two_ _countable_ _components_. But surely such a concept is not for that reason supposed to be _a_ _composite_. – What seems to hover before one in the case of the simple concept is _abstract_ _simplicity_, a unity that does not contain difference and determinateness within itself and hence is also not the unity that belongs to the concept. So far as an object is in representation, in memory especially, or is also the abstract determination of thought, it can be quite simple. Even the object richest within itself: for instance spirit, nature, world, and God as well, taken up quite without concept into the simple representation belonging to the equally simple word: spirit, nature, world, God, is doubtless something simple, at which consciousness may halt without singling out for itself the peculiar determination or a mark; but the objects of consciousness are not meant to stay these simple things, not representations or abstract determinations of thought; they are to be _comprehended_, that is, their simplicity is to be determined along with their inner difference. – The _composite_ concept, however, is surely nothing better than a wooden iron. Of something composite one may well have a concept; but a composite concept would be something worse than _materialism_, which takes only the _substance_ of the _soul_ to be a composite while still grasping _thinking_ as _simple_. Uneducated reflection hits first upon composition as the entirely _external_ reference, the poorest form under which things can be viewed; even the lowest natures have to be an _inner_ unity. That the form of the untruest existence should on top of everything be transferred to the I, to the concept, goes beyond what was to be expected and must count as unseemly and barbaric.

Concepts are further divided chiefly into _contrary_ and _contradictory_ ones. – If the business in the treatment of the concept were to state {{{ 12.46 }}} what determinate concepts there are, then all possible determinations would have to be adduced – for _all_ determinations are concepts, hence determinate concepts – and all the categories of _being_, like all the determinations of _essence_, would have to be listed among the kinds of concept. So too in the logics, the one at pleasure recounting more and the other fewer, we are told what sorts of concept there are. There are _affirmative_, _negative_, _identical_, _conditioned_, _necessary_ concepts and so forth. Since such determinations already lie behind _the_ _nature_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ _itself_, and therefore, when they are listed in connection with it, do not occur in their own proper place, they admit only of superficial verbal explanations and appear here without any interest at all. – At the basis of the _contrary_ and _contradictory_ concepts – a difference chiefly attended to here – lies the determination of reflection of _diversity_ and _opposition_. They are regarded as two particular _kinds_, that is, each as fixed for itself and indifferent towards the other, without any thought of the dialectic and of the inner nullity of these differences; as though what is _contrary_ did not equally have to be determined as _contradictory_. Their nature, and the essential transition of the forms of reflection which they voice, has been examined at the proper place. Within the concept, identity has grown into universality, difference into particularity, and opposition, in going back into the ground, into singularity. In such forms those determinations of reflection stand as they are in their concept. The universal showed itself as the identical and, at once, as what is diverse from or _contrary_ to the particular and the singular; beyond that, as opposed to them, or _contradictory_; yet within that very opposition it is identical with them, their genuine ground, wherein they stand sublated. The same holds of particularity and of singularity, which are equally the totality of the determinations of reflection.

A further division of concepts is into _subordinate_ and _coordinate_ ones; – a difference that comes nearer to the determination of the concept, namely the relation of universality and particularity, where these expressions were also mentioned in passing. Only these too are usually considered as wholly fixed relations, and on that footing manifold barren propositions are set up about them. The most long-winded transaction about it again concerns the reference of contrariety and contradictoriness to sub- and coordination. Since the _judgment_ _is_ _the_ _reference_ _of_ _determinate_ _concepts_, the true relation has first to emerge in connection with it. That manner of _comparing_ these determinations without any thought of their dialectic and of the ongoing alteration of their determination, or rather of the conjunction of opposed determinations present within them, makes the whole {{{ 12.47 }}} consideration of what in them _is_ _consonant_ or is not, as it were as though this consonance or non-consonance were something separate and abiding, into something merely barren and devoid of content. – The great _Euler_, infinitely fruitful and acute in grasping and combining the deeper relations of algebraic magnitudes, and especially the drily intellectual _Lambert_ and others have attempted a _notation_ by lines, figures and the like for this sort of relation among the determinations of the concept; the intention in general was to _raise_ the logical modes of reference to a _calculus_; – or rather in fact to lower them. The very attempt at a notation presents itself at once as null in and for itself if one compares the nature of the sign with that of what is to be signified. The determinations of the concept, universality, particularity and singularity, are indeed _diverse_, like lines or the letters of algebra; – they are further also _opposed_, and to that extent would even admit of the signs of plus and minus. But they themselves, and altogether their references, – even if one stops short at _subsumption_ and _inherence_, are of a quite different essential nature from letters and lines and their relations, from equality or diversity of magnitude, from plus and minus, or from a placing of lines one above another or their joining into angles and the positions of the spaces they enclose. Objects of that sort have, as against them, the peculiarity of being _external_ to one another and of having a _fixed_ determination. Once concepts are taken in a manner answering to signs of that sort, they have ceased to be concepts. Their determinations are nothing lying dead, as numbers and lines are, to which their reference does not itself belong; they are living movements; the differentiated determinateness of the one side is immediately internal to the other as well; what with numbers and lines would be a perfect contradiction is essential to the nature of the concept. – Higher mathematics, which for its part advances to the infinite and permits itself contradictions, is no longer able to employ its usual signs for exhibiting such determinations; for the notation of the still quite conceptless representation of the _infinite_ _approximation_ of two ordinates, or when it sets an arc equal to an infinite amount of infinitely small straight lines, it does nothing more than draw the two straight lines _outside_ _one_ _another_, and draw straight lines into an arc, but as _diverse_ from it; for the infinite, on which everything there turns, it refers us to _representing_.

What tempted people into that attempt in the first place is chiefly the _quantitative_ relation that _universality_, _particularity_ and _singularity_ are supposed to bear to one another; the universal counts as _wider_ than the {{{ 12.48 }}} particular and the singular, and the particular as _wider_ than the singular. The concept is what is _concrete_ and _richest_, since it is the ground and the _totality_ of the determinations that went before, the categories of being, the determinations of reflection; hence these too do indeed emerge in it. Its nature, however, is utterly mistaken if these are still clung to in it in that abstraction; if the _wider_ _compass_ of the universal is so understood as to make it a _more_, a larger _quantum_, than particular and singular are. Being absolute ground, the concept is the _possibility_ of _quantity_, yet no less of _quality_, that is, its determinations are quite as much qualitatively differentiated; so they are already taken against their truth once they are posited under the form of quantity by itself. Furthermore, the determination of reflection is something _relative_, in which its opposite shines; it does not stand in an external relation, as a quantum does. The concept, though, is more than any of this; its determinations are determinate _concepts_, and essentially the _totality_ of all determinations in their own right. It is therefore entirely unfitting, in order to grasp such an intimate totality, to want to apply relations of number and space in which all determinations fall apart; they are rather the last and worst medium that could be employed. Natural relations, such as magnetism, for instance, or relations of colour, would be infinitely higher and truer symbols for it. Since the human being has language, the means of designation peculiar to reason, it is an idle notion to want to look about for a more imperfect mode of presentation and to torment oneself with it. The concept as such can essentially be grasped only with spirit, whose property it not merely is but whose pure self it is. It is futile to want to hold it fast through spatial figures and algebraic signs for the behoof of the _external_ _eye_ and of a _conceptless_, _mechanical_ _mode_ _of_ _treatment_, a _calculus_. Whatever else might be pressed into service as a symbol can, like the symbols for God's nature, at best awaken presentiments and echoes of the concept; but if it were meant seriously to express and cognize the concept, then the _external_ _nature_ of all symbols is unfit for that, and the relation is rather reversed: what in the symbols echoes a higher determination is cognized first through the concept, and [can] be brought nearer to it only through the _severing_ of that sensuous accompaniment which was to have expressed it.
