## c. Finitude

Existence is determinate; a quality belongs to something, and within its quality something stands not merely determined but limited; that quality constitutes its limit, and, saddled with such a limit, something remains for now an affirmative existence at rest. What makes up its finitude, however, is this negation once developed – developed so far that the opposition of its existence to the negation which is its immanent limit is itself the being-within-itself of the something, and the something is thereby nothing other than becoming in its own self.

When we say of things that _they_ _are_ _finite_, this does not mean merely that some determinateness attaches to them, that quality belongs to them only as reality and as determination existing in itself, nor merely that they are limited while still keeping an existence outside their limit, – it means rather that their very nature, their being, is made up of non-being. Finite things _are_, yet their reference to themselves runs thus: as _negative_ they refer to themselves, and in that very self-reference they despatch themselves past themselves, past their own being. They _are_ indeed, yet what this being holds as its truth is their _end_. Not only does the finite alter, as any something does; it _ceases to be_, and this ceasing-to-be of it is no mere possibility, as if the finite could be and yet not cease to be. Rather, the being of finite things as such consists in carrying the germ of ceasing-to-be as their being-within-itself; their hour of birth is likewise their hour of death.
