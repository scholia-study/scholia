## Chapter 1. Concrete Existence

As the principle of ground expresses it — _everything_ _that_ _is_ _has_ _a_ _ground_, _or_ _is_ _something_ _posited_, _something_ _mediated_ — so too a principle of concrete existence would have to be set up and expressed thus: _everything_ _that_ _is_ _concretely_ _exists_. Not to be a first immediate, but to be essence come forth into immediacy — that is the truth of being.

But if it was further said as well that _what_ _concretely_ _exists_ _has_ _a_ _ground_ _and_ _is_ _conditioned_, then it would have to be said no less: _it_ _has_ _no_ _ground_ _and_ _is_ _unconditioned_. For the sublating of that mediation which refers by ground and condition has brought forth an immediacy, and this immediacy is concrete existence, which in emerging sublates just this emerging itself.

Insofar as the proofs _of_ _the_ _concrete_ _existence_ of God may be mentioned here, it should be recalled beforehand that alongside immediate _being_, first, and _concrete_ _existence_, second — the being emerging out of essence — there is a still further being, one emerging out of the concept: _objectivity_. – Proving as such is _mediated_ _cognition_. Each kind of being demands or contains a kind of mediation of its own; the nature of proving therefore comes out differently in respect of each. The _ontological_ _proof_ wants to set out from the concept; it lays the sum total of all realities down as ground, and thereupon subsumes concrete existence too under reality. It is therefore that mediation which is a syllogism, and it does not yet fall to be considered here. Regard has already been paid above (Part I, Division I, p. 47 f.) to Kant's reminder against this, and it was noted that Kant understands by _concrete_ _existence_ the _determinate_ existence whereby something enters the context of the whole of {{{ 11.325 }}} experience, that is, enters the determination of an _otherness_ and the reference to _other_. As something concretely existing, then, a something stands mediated by an other, and concrete existence at large is the side on which it is mediated. Now in what Kant calls the concept — that is, in a something so far as it is taken as merely and simply _referred_ _to_ _itself_, or in representation as such — its mediation does not lie; within abstract identity with itself the setting-in-opposition has been dropped. What the ontological proof would have to exhibit is that the absolute concept, the concept of God namely, arrives at determinate existence, at mediation, or how the simple essence mediates itself with mediation. This comes about through the subsumption indicated, that of concrete existence under its universal, namely under reality, which is assumed as the middle — on the one side God in his concept, on the other side concrete existence. – Of this mediation, so far as it has the shape of the syllogism, there is, as has been said, no question here. How that mediation of essence with concrete existence is in truth constituted, however, is what the exposition up to now has contained. Proving's own nature falls to be considered in the doctrine of cognition. Only what bears upon the nature of mediation at large is to be indicated here.

In the proofs of the existence of God a _ground_ is given for that existence. This is not supposed to be an objective ground of God's existence; for that existence is in and for its own self. It is therefore merely a _ground_ _for_ _cognition_. With that it declares itself at the same time to be something that _vanishes_ in the object which at first appears through it as grounded. The ground now taken from the world's contingency contains the world's going back into the absolute essence; for the contingent is what in its own self is _groundless_ and sublates itself. In this manner, then, the absolute essence does in fact emerge out of the groundless; the ground sublates itself, and with that the shine also vanishes of that relation which was given to God, of being grounded in an other. Hereby this mediation is the genuine one. Only that proving reflection is unacquainted with this nature of its mediation; on the one part it takes itself to be something merely subjective and thereby removes its mediation from God himself, while on the other part it therefore fails to recognize the mediating movement, and to recognize that and how the movement lies within _essence_ _itself_. Reflection's genuine relation consists in its being both in one: mediation as such, yet at the same time, to be sure, a subjective and outward mediation, that is, the _mediation_ outward to itself, which _sublates_ _itself_ _again_ _in_ _its_ _own_ _self_. In that exposition, though, concrete existence acquires the skewed relation of appearing merely as something _mediated_ or posited.

Nor, on the other side, can concrete existence be regarded merely as something _immediate_. Taken in the determination of an immediacy, the apprehending of God's concrete existence has been given out as something unprovable, and the knowledge of it {{{ 11.326 }}} as a _merely_ immediate consciousness, as a _faith_. Knowledge, it is said, must come to this result: that it knows _nothing_; that is, that it _gives_ _up_ once more its _mediating_ movement together with the determinations that occur within it. This has come out in the foregoing as well; only it is to be added that reflection, in ending with the sublating of its own self, does not on that account have _nothing_ as its result, as if the positive knowledge of essence, taken as _immediate_ reference to essence, were _severed_ from that result and were an emerging of its own, an act starting merely from itself; rather this very ending, this _foundering_ of mediation, is at once the _ground_ from which the immediate emerges. As was remarked above, language brings together the meaning of this _going_ _under_ and of the _ground_; the essence of God, one says, is the _abyss_ for finite reason. And such it is in fact, insofar as reason therein surrenders its finitude and lets its mediating movement sink away; yet this _abyss_, the negative ground, is equally the _positive_ ground from which what is emerges, the essence immediate in its own self; mediation is an _essential_ _moment_. The mediation through the ground sublates itself, yet it does not leave the ground below, so that what emerges from it would be a _posited_ having its essence elsewhere, namely in the ground; as abyss, rather, this ground is mediation vanished; and conversely, only mediation vanished is at once the ground, and only by way of this negation is it the self-same and the immediate.

Concrete existence, then, is not here to be taken as a _predicate_, nor as a _determination_ of essence, in such a way that a proposition about it would run: essence concretely exists, or _has_ concrete existence; – essence has rather passed over into concrete existence; concrete existence is essence's absolute externalization, beyond which essence has not remained behind. The proposition would accordingly run: essence is concrete existence; from its concrete existence it is not distinguished. – Essence has _passed_ _over_ into concrete existence insofar as essence, qua ground, draws no further distinction between itself and itself as the grounded, or insofar as that ground has sublated itself. This negation, however, is just as essentially essence's position, or simply and solely positive continuity with its own self; concrete existence is the reflection _of_ _the_ _ground_ into itself, the ground's identity with its own self achieved in the ground's negation, and hence that mediation which has posited itself as self-identical and is thereby immediacy.

Now because concrete existence is essentially _the_ _mediation_ _identical_ _with_ _itself_, it _has_ _the_ _determinations_ of mediation _about_ _it_, yet in such a way that they are at once reflected into themselves and possess subsistence that is essential and immediate. Being the immediacy that posits itself through sublating, concrete existence is negative unity and being-within-itself; it therefore determines itself immediately as _something_ _concretely_ _existing_ and as a _thing_.
