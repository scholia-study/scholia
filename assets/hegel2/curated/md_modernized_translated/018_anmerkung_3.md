## Remark 3

That unity whose moments, being and nothing, are moments that cannot be parted is itself, at the same time, distinct from them, and so stands over against them as a _third_, whose own most proper shape is _becoming_. _Passing_ _over_ says no more than becoming does; only, in the former the two terms, from one of which the passage is made to the other, are pictured rather as lying at rest apart from one another, with the passing over taking place _between_ them. Whenever {{{ 21.81 }}} and in whatever way being or nothing is brought into discussion, this third has to be there; for the two do not stand on their own — they have their place only within becoming, within this third. Now this third takes on a multitude of empirical shapes, and abstraction pushes these aside or lets them go, in order to secure its own products, being and nothing, each in isolation, and to present them as safe from any passing over. The reply to so simple a manoeuvre on abstraction's part is equally simple: one need only recall empirical concrete existence, wherein this abstraction is itself no more than a something, and possesses an existence. Failing that, what one meets with are forms of reflection meant to pin down the separation of what cannot be separated. In and for itself, any such determination already harbours its own contrary within it, and there is no need to fall back on the nature of the matter and appeal to that: the determination of reflection can be thrown into confusion on its own ground, simply by taking it just as it presents itself and showing up its other within it. It would be wasted effort to try to net, as it were, every twist and every notion of reflection and of its ratiocination, with a view to closing off the exits and the sidesteps whereby it hides its self-contradiction from itself. For that reason I likewise decline to take account of the numerous so-called objections and refutations that have been raised against the position that neither being nor nothing is anything true, and that becoming alone is their truth; the cultivation of thought required for grasping how void such refutations are — or rather for chasing such notions away oneself — comes about solely through the critical cognition of the forms of the understanding; but those who are richest in objections of this kind pounce with their reflections on the very first propositions, without having gained, through further study of the logic, any awareness of the nature of these crude reflections, or having had such awareness given to them.

A few of the phenomena may be examined that come of isolating being and nothing from each other, of positing the one outside the province of the other, so that the passing over is thereby denied.

_Parmenides_ clung to being, and he did so with the greatest consistency, inasmuch as he declared of nothing at the same time that it _is_ _not_ _at_ _all_; being alone is. Being taken so entirely on its own is the indeterminate; it therefore bears no reference to anything else, and it looks accordingly as though no further _advance_ were possible _from_ _such_ _a_ _beginning_ — no advance, that is, out of the beginning itself — and as though any advance could come about only by having something {{{ 21.82 }}} foreign attached to it _from_ _outside_. Hence the advance to the proposition that being is the very same as nothing looks like a second, absolute beginning, – a passing over standing on its own, which would accrue to being from without. Were being to possess a determinateness, it would not be the absolute beginning at all; it would then hang upon something else and would not be immediate, not the beginning. If, on the other hand, it is indeterminate, and to that extent a genuine beginning, then it likewise possesses nothing whereby it might carry itself over into an other – it is at once the _end_ as well. Nothing can burst forth out of it any more than anything can burst into it; in Parmenides no less than in Spinoza, no advance is to be made from being, or from absolute substance, to the negative, to the finite. If an advance is made all the same — which, as noted, can only happen externally, given a being without reference and hence without advance — then that advance amounts to a second, fresh beginning. Thus _Fichte's_ utterly absolute, unconditioned first principle runs: A = A, _positing_; the second principle is _opposing_; and this one is supposed to be _in_ _part_ conditioned and _in_ _part_ unconditioned (and so contradictory within itself). What we have here is an advance made by external reflection, which no less denies again what it began with as an absolute – opposing being the negation of the first identity – than it turns its second unconditioned, in the very same breath and quite explicitly, into a conditioned. Yet if there were any title whatever for advancing, i.e. for sublating the first beginning, the ground for it would have to lie in that first itself, namely that an other could bear reference to it; the first would accordingly have to be something _determinate_. But this is just what neither _being_ nor absolute substance claims to be; quite the reverse. It is the _immediate_, that which is still wholly _indeterminate_.

The most eloquent accounts — perhaps by now forgotten — of how impossible it is to get from an abstraction to anything beyond it, and to a unification of the two, come from _Jacobi_, written in the service of his polemic against the Kantian _synthesis_ of self-consciousness à priori, in his Abh. on the undertaking of criticism to bring reason to understanding (Jacobi's Works, Vol. III). He frames the task (p. 113) in this way: that within something _pure_ — whether of consciousness, of space, or of time — the emergence or the producing of a synthesis be displayed. »Let space be _one_, time be _one_, consciousness be _one_; – now say how any single one of these three ones makes itself _purely_ manifold within its own self; – each is but _one_ {{{ 21.83 }}} and _no_ _other_; – a uniformity, a _Der-_ _Die-_ _Das-_ _selfsameness_! with no Der-ness, no Die-ness, no Das-ness; for these are still dozing, along with the _Der_, _Die_, _Das_, in the infinite = 0 of the indeterminate, out of which each and every _determinate_ thing is first supposed to issue! What is it that brings _finitude_ into those three infinities; what fertilizes space and time à priori with number and with measure, turning them into a _pure_ _manifold_; what sets _pure_ _spontaneity_ (I) oscillating? How does its pure vowel reach a consonant, or rather, how does its _soundless_ unbroken _breathing_ break _off_, interrupting itself, so as to win at least some kind of vowel-sound, an _accent_?« – One can see that _Jacobi_ has quite definitely recognized the _non-essence_ of abstraction, be it so-called absolute, i.e. merely abstract, space, or time of that same kind, or pure consciousness, the I, of that same kind; and he holds to it precisely in order to maintain that no advance is possible to an other, to the condition of a synthesis, nor to the synthesis itself. The synthesis that constitutes the point at issue must not be understood as a joining together of determinations already on hand _externally_, – for one thing, the question is precisely that of generating a second alongside a first, of something determinate alongside the indeterminate initial term; for another, the question concerns the _immanent_ synthesis, synthesis à priori, – the unity of what is differentiated, a unity in and for itself. This immanent synthesis of being and nothing is _becoming_; but since the meaning that first suggests itself for synthesis is that of an external gathering-together of things externally on hand over against one another, the term synthesis, synthetic unity, has rightly fallen out of use. – Jacobi asks, _how_ the pure vowel of the I arrives at a consonant, _what_ it is that brings determinateness into indeterminateness? – the _what_? would admit of an easy answer, and Kant did in fact answer this question in his own manner; but to ask after the _How_? is to ask in what fashion and mode, according to what relation and the like, and thus to demand that a particular category be specified; yet fashion and mode, categories of the understanding, cannot come into play here. The question after the _how_? belongs itself among reflection's bad habits: it inquires after comprehensibility while presupposing its own rigid categories, and thereby knows itself armed in advance against any answer to what it inquires after. In Jacobi it does not even carry the loftier sense of an inquiry after the _necessity_ of the synthesis, since he persists, as has been said, firmly in the abstractions, and does so in order to assert that synthesis is impossible. He gives a particularly graphic description (p. 147) of the procedure by which one arrives at the abstraction of space. »For that stretch of time I must seek purely to forget that I ever saw, heard, stirred, or touched anything at all, myself expressly not excluded. Purely, purely, purely must I forget all motion, and let this very _forgetting_ {{{ 21.84 }}} be my most urgent business, since it is the hardest thing of all. Whatever I have thought away, I must let it be got rid of wholly and utterly as well, retaining nothing at all beyond the intuition, kept standing _by_ _force_, of infinite _unchangeable_ _space_ alone. Neither, then, may I _think_ _myself_ _back_ _into_ _it_ as something distinct from it and yet bound up with it; I may not let it merely _surround_ and _permeate_ me; instead I have to _pass_ _over_ into it entirely, to become one with it, to change myself into it; nothing of myself may I leave over save _this_ _intuition_ _of_ _mine_, so that I may regard it as a truly self-subsistent, independent, single and solitary representation.«

With continuity as purely abstract as this, that is, with representing so indeterminate and so empty, it makes no odds whether one names such an abstraction space, or pure intuiting, or pure thinking; – it comes to the very same as what the Indian calls _Brahma_ when, motionless outwardly and equally unstirring in sensation, representation, imagination, desire and so on, he gazes for years together at nothing but the tip of his nose, uttering inwardly only Om, Om, Om, or else nothing whatever. Taken as consciousness, this dull and empty consciousness is – _being_.

Within this emptiness, Jacobi goes on, what happens to him is the reverse of what, according to Kantian assurances, ought to happen to him; he does not come upon himself as a _many_ and a _manifold_, but rather as one, devoid of all plurality and manifoldness; indeed, »I am _impossibility_ itself, I am the _annihilation_ of all that is manifold and plural, – out of my pure, utterly simple, unalterable essence I _can_ _not_ _reinstate_ even the least thing, nor conjure it back into myself as a ghost; – and so, in this purity, everything outside-and-alongside-one-another, and all manifoldness and plurality that rests upon it, is disclosed as something _purely_ _impossible_.«

This impossibility amounts to nothing beyond the tautology: I cling to the abstract unity and shut out all plurality and manifoldness, I confine myself to what is undifferentiated and indeterminate, and I avert my eyes from everything differentiated and determinate. Kant's synthesis à priori of self-consciousness — that is, the activity by which this unity sunders itself and maintains itself in that sundering — Jacobi dilutes into this very same abstraction. That »synthesis _in_ _itself_«, the »_original_ _judging_,« he converts one-sidedly into »the _copula_ _in_ _itself_; – an _Is_, _Is_, _Is_, lacking beginning, lacking end, and lacking any What, Who and Which; this repeating of the repetition, carried on to infinity, is the sole {{{ 21.85 }}} occupation, function and production of the very purest synthesis; that synthesis is nothing but the bare, pure, absolute repeating itself.« Or rather, in point of fact, since there is no break in it, i.e. no negation, no differentiating, it [is] not a repeating at all but merely undifferentiated simple being. – Yet is this still synthesis, once Jacobi drops precisely that whereby the unity is a synthetic unity?

In the first place, when Jacobi installs himself in this fashion in absolute, i.e. abstract, space, time, and consciousness too, one has to say that he thereby places himself in, and holds fast to, something _empirically_ false; there _is_ no space and time, i.e. none is empirically on hand, that would be an unbounded spatial and temporal something, that would not be filled throughout its continuity with existence and alteration bounded in manifold ways, such that these limits and alterations belong to spatiality and temporality unseparated and inseparably; and consciousness, likewise, is filled with determinate sensing, representing, desiring and so on; it exists in no separation from some particular content. – The empirical _passing_ _over_ goes without saying in any case; consciousness may well make empty space, empty time and empty consciousness itself, or pure being, into its object and content, yet it does not remain there — it not merely moves but positively forces its way out of such emptiness towards a better, i.e. in some manner more concrete, content, and however wretched a content may otherwise be, to that extent it is better and truer; a content of just this kind is a synthetic one in general, synthetic taken in the wider sense. Thus Parmenides finds himself with shine and opinion on his hands, the opposite of being and of truth; thus Spinoza with the attributes, the modes, extension, motion, understanding, will and so on. Synthesis holds within it and exhibits the untruth of those abstractions: within it they stand in unity with their other, and so not as subsisting for themselves, not as absolutes, but as relative through and through.

But displaying the empirical nullity of empty space and the rest is not the point at issue. Consciousness can, to be sure, fill itself abstractively with that indeterminate as well, and the abstractions so held onto are _the_ _thoughts_ of pure space, of time, of pure consciousness, of pure being. What has to be displayed as null is the thought of pure space and the rest, i.e. pure space and the rest _in_ _it_ _itself_ — displayed, that is, as being already, as such, its own contrary, as having its contrary already forced its way into it in it itself, as being already on its own account the having-emerged-from-itself, determinateness.

This, however, follows immediately upon them. They are, as Jacobi describes at length, results of abstraction, and they are expressly determined as _indeterminate_ — which, to fall {{{ 21.86 }}} back on its simplest form, is being. Yet it is precisely this _indeterminateness_ that constitutes its determinateness; for the indeterminate stands over against the determinate; standing so over against it, the indeterminate is itself the determinate, or the negative, and indeed the pure, wholly abstract negative. What external reflection, and internal reflection too, gives voice to when it sets being equal to nothing, pronouncing it an empty figment of thought, a nothing, is just this indeterminateness or abstract negation which being thus has in it itself. – Or one may express it so: since being is what lacks all determination, it is not the (affirmative) determinateness that it is, and hence is not being but nothing.

Within the pure reflection of the beginning — the beginning being made, in the present logic, with _being_ as such — the transition still lies hidden; since _being_ has been posited merely as immediate, the _nothing_ in it likewise breaks out merely immediately. All the determinations that come after, however, beginning at once with _existence_, are more concrete; in existence there is already _posited_ whatever contains and generates the contradiction of those abstractions, and with it their passing over. Where being is that simple, immediate term, the reminder that it is a result of complete abstraction, and hence already on that count abstract negativity, nothing, is left in the rear of the science — a science which, within its own bounds and expressly setting out from _essence_, will present that one-sided _immediacy_ as something mediated, at the point where being as _concrete_ _existence_, together with the mediator of this being, the ground, is _posited_.

Armed with that reminder, one can represent the transition of being into nothing as something easy and trivial in itself, or, as the phrase goes, _explain_ it and _make_ it _comprehensible_, by saying that being, which has been made the beginning of the science, is of course nothing, since one may abstract from everything, and when everything has been abstracted from, nothing is left. But, so the argument may run on, the beginning is thus not anything affirmative, not being, but precisely nothing, and then nothing is the _end_ as well, quite as much as immediate being is, and indeed still {{{ 21.87 }}} more so. The quickest course is to let such ratiocination run on and to observe what sort of results these are that it plumes itself upon. That nothing should on this showing be the result of that ratiocination, and that the beginning should now be made with nothing (as in Chinese philosophy), would not be worth so much as turning one's hand over, for before the hand were turned this nothing would just as surely have converted itself into being, (see above: B. Nothing). But further, granting the presupposition of that abstraction from _everything_ — everything being, after all, something _that_ _has_ _being_ — it needs to be taken more precisely; abstraction from all that has being yields at first abstract being, _being_ in general; it is the same in the cosmological proof of God's existence, drawn from the world's contingent being: in rising above that contingent being the proof still carries _being_ up along with it, determining being as _infinite_ _being_. It _is_ possible, however, to abstract from this pure being too, to throw being in with all the rest that has already been abstracted from; then nothing is left. One _can_ now, provided one is willing to forget the _thinking_ of nothing, i.e. its overturning into being, or knew nothing of it in the first place, keep going in the style of that _can_; for it is possible (thank God!) to abstract from nothing as well (the creation of the world being, after all, likewise an abstraction from nothing), and then what is left is not nothing, since it is just this that is abstracted from, but one has thus come back round to being. – This _can_ gives an external game of abstracting, in which abstracting is itself nothing but the one-sided doing of the negative. To begin with, it lies in this can itself that being is as much a matter of indifference to it as nothing is, and that just as surely as each of the two vanishes, each equally comes to be; but it is a matter of equal indifference whether one starts from the doing of nothing or from nothing; the doing of nothing, i.e. mere abstracting, is neither more nor less something true than the bare nothing is.

The dialectic by which _Plato_ handles the one in the Parmenides is likewise to be counted rather as a dialectic belonging to external reflection. Both being and the one are Eleatic forms, and they are the same. Yet they also admit of being distinguished, and that is how Plato takes them in the dialogue in question. Once he has stripped away from the one all the sundry determinations — whole and parts, being in itself, being in an other and so forth, figure, time and so forth — the result is that being does not accrue to the one, since a something acquires being in no way other than according to one of those modes (p. 141. e. Vol. III. ed. Steph.). Plato then takes up the proposition: _the_ _one_ _is_; and one should look in his text to see how the transition from this proposition to the _non-being_ of the one is effected; it comes about by _comparing_ the two determinations of the proposition presupposed, _the_ _one_ _is_: the proposition holds the one _and_ being; and the one _is_ holds more than saying merely, the one. In their being _diverse_ the moment of negation lodged in the proposition is displayed. Clearly this route rests on a presupposition and is an external reflection.

{{{ 21.88 }}} Just as the one is here brought into connection with being, so being — which is supposed to be held fast abstractly _for_ _itself_ — is most simply displayed, without any need to enter into thinking, within a connection that carries the contrary of what is meant to be maintained. Being, taken as it immediately is, pertains _to_ _a_ _subject_, is a thing said, possesses an empirical _existence_ in general, and thereby has its footing on the ground of restriction and of the negative. Whatever expressions or turns of phrase the understanding may resort to when it balks at the unity of being and nothing and calls upon what is immediately on hand, in that very experience it will come upon no being save _determinate_ being, being together with a restriction or a negation, – the very unity it repudiates. The assertion of immediate being thus shrinks to an empirical concrete existence, whose _display_ the assertion cannot reject, since the immediacy outside of thinking is precisely what it means to hold on to.

It stands the same way with _nothing_, only inversely, and this reflection is a familiar one, made about nothing often enough. Nothing, taken in its immediacy, presents itself as _something_ _having_ _being_; for by its nature it is the very same as being. One thinks nothing, one represents it, one speaks of it; therefore it _is_; nothing has its being in the thinking, the representing, the speaking and so forth. This being, however, is also distinguished from it; hence one says that nothing is indeed present in thinking, in representing, but that on that score _it_ _is_ not, that being does not accrue to it as such, and that only thinking or representing constitutes this being. In drawing this distinction it can as little be denied that nothing stands in _reference_ to a being; but within the reference, even though the reference also carries the difference, a unity with being is on hand. However nothing may be uttered or displayed, it shows itself in connection, or in contact if one prefers, with a being, unseparated from a being, in short within an _existence_.

But when nothing is displayed in this way within an existence, there is still apt to hover before the mind this difference of nothing from being: that the existence of nothing is in no way {{{ 21.89 }}} anything accruing to nothing itself, that nothing does not have being for its own in it, that it _is_ not being _as_ such; nothing is said to be mere absence of being, darkness on this view no more than the _absence_ of light, cold no more than the absence of warmth, and so on. Darkness, so it is said, carries meaning only in relation to the eye, in an external comparison with the positive, with light; cold too is only a something within our sensation, while light and warmth, like being, are on their own account the objective, the real, the efficacious, of an utterly other quality and worth than those negatives, than nothing. One may frequently find it advertised as a most important reflection and a weighty piece of cognition that darkness should be _only_ _absence_ of light and cold _only_ _absence_ of warmth. Of this shrewd reflection it may be remarked empirically, within this field of empirical objects, that darkness does in fact prove efficacious within light, in that it determines light into colour and thereby first confers visibility upon light itself, seeing that, as was said earlier, in pure light one sees as little as in pure darkness. Visibility, however, is an efficacy in the eye, and in it that negative has just as large a share as light does, which passes for the real and the positive; cold, likewise, makes itself sufficiently felt by water, by our sensation and so on, and if we refuse it so-called objective reality, absolutely nothing is thereby won against it. Something further would have to be faulted here, namely that in this case too, as above, what is spoken of is a negative of determinate content, and one does not stay put at nothing itself, over against which being, considered as empty abstraction, has neither anything less nor anything more. – Cold, darkness and determinate negations of that sort are, however, to be taken straight away on their own, and one must see what gets posited with them in view of the general determination under which they have been brought in here. They are supposed to be, not the nothing at large, but light's nothing, warmth's nothing and so forth – the nothing of something determinate, of some content; determinate nothings, then, nothings with a content, if one may speak so. But a determinateness, as will emerge further along, is itself a negation; hence these are negative nothings; and a negative nothing is something affirmative. The overturning of nothing, by way of its determinateness (which appeared just now as an _existence_ in the subject, or in whatever else it might be), into something affirmative strikes the consciousness that stands firm in the abstraction of the understanding as the most paradoxical thing imaginable; simple though the insight is, or indeed just because of its simplicity, the insight {{{ 21.90 }}} that negation of negation is positive strikes one as trivial, something the proud understanding therefore need not attend to, granted the matter is right enough, – and it is not merely right: because determinations of this kind are universal, it has infinite reach and universal application, so that it would be well worth attending to.

One further remark may be made about how the passing over of being and nothing into one another is determined: it too is to be grasped without any additional determination of reflection. It is immediate and altogether abstract, owing to the abstractness of the moments that pass over, that is, inasmuch as the determinateness of the other, by means of which they would pass over, is not yet posited in these moments; nothing is not yet _posited_ in being, notwithstanding that being _essentially_ is nothing, and vice versa. For this reason it is not permissible to bring more determinate mediations to bear here, nor to grasp being and nothing within some relation or other, – that passing over is as yet no relation. One may therefore not say: nothing is the _ground_ from which being comes; or being is the _ground_ from which nothing comes; – nothing the _cause_ of being and so forth; or that the passage into nothing can take place only _on_ _the_ _condition_ that something _is_, and the passage into being only _on_ _the_ _condition_ of non-being. The manner of the reference admits of no further determination unless the _sides_ referred to are determined further at the same time. The nexus of ground and consequent and so forth does not have bare being and nothing any longer as the sides it joins, but expressly a being that is ground, and a something which, though it be only a posited and not self-subsistent, is nevertheless not the abstract nothing.
