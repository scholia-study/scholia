## A. The Chemical Object

What marks off the chemical object from the mechanical one is that the latter is a totality standing indifferent towards determinateness, whereas with the chemical object _determinateness_, and with it the _reference_ _to_ _another_ together with the manner of that reference, belongs to its very nature. – Such determinateness is essentially _particularization_ as well, that is, it is received into universality; so it is _principle_ – the _universal_ _determinateness_, not that of _the_ _one_ _singular_ _object_ merely, but that of the _other_ too. Hence there now becomes distinguishable in it, on the one hand its concept as the inner totality of both determinatenesses, and on the other the determinateness constituting the singular object's nature within its _externality_ and _concrete_ _existence_. Being in this way the whole concept _in_ _itself_, it carries within it the _necessity_ and the _drive_ to sublate its opposed, _one-sided_ _subsistence_ and to make itself in existence into the _real_ _whole_ that it is according to its concept.

As regards the expression _chemism_ for the relation of difference in objectivity as it has come out here, it may be remarked in passing that the word is not to be taken as though this relation presented itself only in that form of elementary nature which goes by the name of chemism proper. The meteorological relation already has to be viewed as a process whose parties have the nature of physical rather than of chemical elements. In living things the relation of the sexes falls under {{{ 12.149 }}} this schema; and for spiritual relations too — love, friendship, and so forth — it supplies the _formal_ groundwork.

Considered more nearly, the chemical object is at the outset, as a _self-subsistent_ totality in general, something reflected inwardly, distinguished thereby from its outward reflectedness – an indifferent _base_, an individual whose determination as differentiated has not yet occurred; a person likewise is such a base, one that so far refers merely to itself. The immanent determinateness constituting its _difference_ is, _first_, inwardly reflected in this manner: the taking back of the outward reference amounts to no more than formal abstract universality, and the outward reference is on that account a determination of the immediacy and concrete existence belonging to the object. Viewed from that side, no return _in_ _its_ _own_ _self_ to individual totality takes place; rather, the negative unity finds the two moments of its opposition in a pair of _particular_ _objects_. One chemical object is thus unintelligible from itself alone, the being of the one amounting to the being of an other. – _Second_, though, determinateness is inwardly reflected in absolute fashion and constitutes the concrete moment belonging to the individual concept of the whole, a concept which for the particular object is its universal essence, its _real_ _genus_. Standing thus as the contradiction between its immediate positedness and the individual concept immanent in it, the chemical object is a _striving_: to sublate the determinateness attaching to its existence and to procure concrete existence for the concept's objective totality. Non-self-subsistent it therefore is too, yet after such a fashion that its very nature puts it in tension against this, so that it starts the _process_ in self-determining fashion.
