## a. The Categorical Syllogism

1. For one, or for both, of its premises the categorical syllogism has the categorical judgment. – Bound up here with this syllogism, as with the judgment, is the more determinate meaning that its middle is _objective_ _universality_. Superficially construed, the categorical syllogism as well passes for no more than a bare syllogism of inherence.

Taken in its content-laden meaning, the categorical syllogism is _necessity's_ _first_ _syllogism_, one in which a subject stands joined by syllogism to a predicate through _its_ _substance_. But substance, once raised into the sphere of the concept, is the universal, posited so as to be in and for itself in such wise that what it has for form, for the mode of its being, is not accidentality, which is what it has in its own peculiar relation, but the determination of the concept. The differences of substance are accordingly the syllogism's extremes, and determinately they are universality and singularity. The former, measured against the _genus_ as this is more closely determined to be the _middle_, is abstract universality, or universal determinateness; – substance's accidentality drawn together into the simple determinateness that is nevertheless substance's essential difference, its _specific_ _difference_. – Singularity, on the other hand, is what is actual, in itself the concrete unity of genus and determinateness, here however, as within an immediate syllogism, at first an immediate singularity, accidentality drawn together into the form of a subsistence that is _for_ _itself_. – This extreme's reference to the middle constitutes a categorical judgment; and inasmuch as the other extreme likewise, on the determination stated, gives expression to the specific difference of the genus, or to its determinate principle, that other premise too is categorical.

{{{ 12.120 }}} 2) Standing as the first, and hence immediate, syllogism of necessity, this syllogism falls at the outset under the schema of the first formal syllogism, S–P–U. – Yet because the middle is the singular's essential _nature_ rather than _any_ _one_ _whatever_ of its determinatenesses or properties, and because the extreme of universality likewise is no abstract universal, no merely singular quality over again, but the universal determinateness, the _specific_ _factor_ _of_ _the_ _difference_ of the genus, there falls away the contingency of the subject's being joined by syllogism only through _some_ _Medius_ _Terminus_ _or_ _other_ with _some_ _quality_ _or_ _other_. – Since in this way the _references_ of the extremes to the middle no longer carry the outward immediacy they carried in the syllogism of existence, the demand for a proof does not come up in the sense it had there, the sense that led on to the infinite progress.

Nor, furthermore, does this syllogism presuppose its conclusion for its premises, as a syllogism of reflection does. Following their substantial content, the termini stand in an identical reference to one another, a reference that is _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_; _one_ essence is at hand traversing the three termini, and on it the determinations of singularity, particularity and universality are merely _formal_ moments.

So far, then, the categorical syllogism is subjective no longer; objectivity takes its start in that identity; the middle is the content-laden identity of its extremes, and these are contained in it in respect of their self-subsistence, since their self-subsistence just is that substantial universality, the genus. What is subjective about the syllogism lies in the extremes' indifferent subsistence over against the concept, or the middle.

3) Still, this remains subjective about the syllogism, that the identity in question is there as yet only as the substantial one, or as _content_, and not at the same time as _identity_ _of_ _form_. The concept's identity is therefore still an _inner_ _bond_, and thus, as reference, still _necessity_; the universality of the middle is a compact, _positive_ identity, and not equally the _negativity_ _of_ _its_ _extremes_.

More closely, the immediacy of this syllogism, an immediacy not yet _posited_ as what _it_ _is_ _in_ _itself_, is at hand thus. The properly immediate item of the syllogism is the _singular_. The singular stands subsumed under its genus as middle; but beneath that genus there stand other singulars too, _indeterminately_ _many_; it is _contingent_, then, that just _this_ singular is what gets posited as subsumed there. – Nor, moreover, does this contingency belong merely to _external_ _reflection_, which by _comparison_ with others finds the singular posited in the syllogism to be contingent; rather it is in the very fact of its being referred to the middle as its objective universality that the singular is posited as _contingent_, as a subjective actuality. On the other side, the subject being an _immediate_ singular, it holds {{{ 12.121 }}} determinations that the middle, as the universal nature, does not hold; consequently it has besides a concrete existence indifferent to the middle, determined on its own account and of peculiar content. Conversely, then, this other terminus too has an indifferent immediacy and a concrete existence diverse from the first's. – Between the middle and the other extreme the same relation obtains; for this extreme likewise carries the determination of immediacy, and thus of a contingent being over against its middle.

Thus what the categorical syllogism has posited is, _on_ _the_ _one_ _hand_, extremes so related to the middle that _in_ _themselves_ they possess objective universality, or self-subsistent nature, and at the same time are immediates, hence _actualities_ _indifferent_ to one another. _On_ _the_ _other_ _hand_, though, they are determined just as much as _contingent_, or their immediacy as _sublated_ within their identity. That identity, however, in view of the self-subsistence and totality of actuality just mentioned, is only the formal, inner one; by this the syllogism of necessity has determined itself into the _hypothetical_ one.
