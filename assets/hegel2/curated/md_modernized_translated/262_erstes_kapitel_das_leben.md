## {{{ 12.179 }}} Chapter 1. Life

The idea of life has to do with an object so concrete and, if one will, so real that with it the province of logic might seem, on logic's usual representation, to have been overstepped. Were logic in fact to hold nothing beyond empty, dead thought-forms, no content of the kind that the idea, or life, is could come up in it at all. But if what logic has for its object is absolute truth, and if _truth_ as such lies essentially _in_ _cognition_, then _cognition_ at any rate would have to be dealt with. – And indeed it is the custom to let an _applied_ logic follow upon what is called pure logic, – a logic occupied with _concrete_ _cognition_; leaving out of the count all that _psychology_ and _anthropology_ whose weaving into logic is often reckoned indispensable. The anthropological and psychological side of cognition, however, has to do with the _appearance_ of cognition, wherein the concept is for its own self not yet this, an objectivity equal to it, that is, to have its own self for object. That part of logic which considers cognition does not fall under _applied_ _logic_ as such; on such terms every science would have to be hauled into logic, since each is an applied logic to the extent that it consists in laying hold of its object in forms of thought and of concept. – Presuppositions belong to the subjective concept, and they exhibit themselves in psychological, anthropological and yet other form. Into logic, though, there fall only such presuppositions of the pure concept as have the form of pure thoughts, of abstract essentialities, the determinations of _being_ and of _essence_. In the same way, of _cognition_, the concept's laying hold of its own self, logic is to treat not the remaining shapes of its presupposition but only the one which is itself idea; and this one it must needs consider. This presupposition, now, is the _immediate_ idea; for since cognizing is the concept, yet insofar as the concept is for its own self as a subjective in reference to an objective, it refers itself to the idea as to a _presupposed_ or _immediate_ one. The immediate idea, however, is _life_.

So far as that goes, the need for taking up the idea of life within logic would rest on the need, elsewhere conceded too, of treating the concrete concept of cognition at this place. This idea, though, has brought itself in through the concept's own {{{ 12.180 }}} necessity; the _idea_, what is _true_ in and for itself, is by its essence the object of logic; and since it falls to be considered first in its immediacy, it has to be grasped and cognized under that determinateness wherein it is _life_, so that its consideration may not turn out something empty and void of determination. There may be occasion at most to remark how far the logical view of life differs from other scientific views of it; what belongs here, however, is not the manner in which unphilosophical sciences deal with life, but only how logical life as pure idea is to be marked off from the life of nature, which the _philosophy_ _of_ _nature_ considers, and from life insofar as it stands in connection with _spirit_. – The first of these, as nature's life, is life to the extent that it has been flung out into the _externality_ _of_ _subsistence_, holds its _condition_ in inorganic nature, and is, like the moments of the idea, a manifoldness of actual configurations. Life within the idea is without presuppositions of that kind, presuppositions that are shapes of actuality; what it presupposes is the _concept_, as this has been considered, on one side as subjective, on the other as objective. Within nature life shows itself as the highest stage, one that nature's externality attains through having gone into itself and sublating itself in subjectivity. Within logic it is simple being-within-itself, which in the idea of life has attained an externality genuinely answering to it; the concept, coming on earlier as subjective, is the very soul of life; it is the drive that mediates its own reality for itself by way of objectivity. Since nature attains this idea from out of its externality, it passes beyond its own self; its end is not as its beginning but as its limit, wherein it sublates its own self. – In the idea of life, likewise, the moments making up its reality take on no shape of external actuality but stay enclosed within the concept's form.

In _spirit_, however, life shows itself partly as facing spirit, partly as posited in one with it, and this unity in turn as purely brought forth by spirit. For life is here to be taken throughout in its proper sense as _natural_ _life_, for whatever gets named the _life_ _of_ _spirit_ as spirit is that peculiarity of spirit which stands opposed to bare life; as one speaks likewise of the _nature_ of spirit, although spirit is nothing natural, being rather nature's opposite. For spirit, then, life as such is in part a _means_, and to that extent spirit places it over against itself; in part spirit is itself a living individual with life for its body; in part this unity of spirit with its living corporeality is brought forth out of spirit itself into the _ideal_. None of these references to spirit touches logical life, which is here to be considered neither as the means of a {{{ 12.181 }}} spirit, nor as spirit's living body, nor as a moment belonging to the ideal and to beauty. – Life in either case, both as _natural_ and as standing in reference _to_ _spirit_, has a _determinateness_ _of_ _its_ _externality_, there through its presuppositions, other configurations of nature that these are, but here through the purposes and the activity of spirit. The idea of life for itself is free of that presupposed and conditioning objectivity, as of the reference to this subjectivity.

Life in its idea, taken now more closely, is in and for itself absolute _universality_; the objectivity it carries in it is utterly saturated by the concept, and has nothing but the concept for substance. Whatever marks itself off as a part, or according to some other external reflection, carries the whole concept within its own self; the concept is the soul _omnipresent_ therein, which stays simple reference to its own self and one throughout the manifoldness accruing to objective being. This manifoldness, being objectivity external to itself, has an indifferent subsistence, one that in space and in time, could these already be brought up here, is a wholly diverse and self-subsistent asunderness. In life, however, the externality is at once as the _simple_ _determinateness_ of life's concept; the soul is thus poured out omnipresently through this manifoldness, and stays at once utterly the simple oneness of the concrete concept with its own self. – Before life, before this unity of its concept in the externality of objectivity, in the absolute plurality of atomistic matter, a thinking that clings to the determinations of the relations of reflection and of the formal concept finds all its thoughts running out on it altogether; for reflection, that the simple should be omnipresent in a manifold externality is an absolute contradiction, and inasmuch as reflection has at the same time to apprehend that omnipresence out of the perception of life, and so to concede the actuality of this idea, the thing becomes for it an _incomprehensible_ _mystery_, since it does not lay hold of the concept, nor of the concept as the substance of life. – Simple life, though, is not omnipresent merely; it is downright the _subsistence_ and the _immanent_ _substance_ of its objectivity, yet as subjective substance it is _drive_, and indeed the _specific_ _drive_ of the _particular_ difference, and quite as essentially the one and universal drive of the specific, which leads this its particularizing back into unity and keeps it there. Life is life referring itself to itself, existing for itself, a soul, only as this _negative_ _unity_ of its own objectivity and particularization. With that it is essentially a _singular_, one that refers itself to objectivity as to an other, to an unliving nature. Herein, then, lies the original _judgment_ of life: that it cuts itself off, as individual subject, from the objective, and that, constituting itself the negative unity of the concept, it makes the _presupposition_ of an immediate objectivity.

{{{ 12.182 }}} Life is therefore to be considered _first_ as _living_ _individual_, which for itself is the subjective totality and stands presupposed as indifferent towards an objectivity that faces it as indifferent in turn.

_Secondly_, it is _the_ _life-process_, the sublating of its presupposition, the positing as negative of the objectivity indifferent towards it, and the actualizing of itself as that objectivity's power and negative unity. With that it makes itself the universal, which is the unity of its own self and of its other. Life is therefore

_Thirdly_ the _process_ _of_ _the_ _genus_, the sublating of its singularization and the relating of itself to its objective existence as to its own self. This process is hereby, on the one side, a return into its concept and a repeating of the first diremption, a new individuality coming to be and the first immediate individuality dying; on the other side, however, the _concept_ of life _that_ _has_ _gone_ _into_ _itself_ is the becoming of the concept that relates itself to its own self, existing as universal and free for itself, the transition into _cognition_.
