## A. Absolute Indifference

Being is the abstract indifferentness for which, since it is to be thought for itself as being, the expression _indifference_ has been used, – an indifferentness at which no sort of determinateness is yet supposed to be; pure quantity is indifference as capable of all determinations, yet in such a way that these are external to it and that out of itself it has no connectedness with them; the indifference, however, which may be called the absolute one is the one that, _through_ _the_ _negation_ of every determinateness belonging to being, of quality and of quantity and of the unity these two first have immediately, that of measure, _mediates_ _itself_ _with_ _itself_ into simple unity. Determinateness is at it now only as a state, that is, as a _qualitative_ _external_ which has indifference for its _substrate_.

But whatever has been determined in this fashion as a qualitative external is only something vanishing; being thus external over against being, the qualitative, as the opposite of its own self, is only what sublates itself. Determinateness is in this way posited at the substrate merely as an empty differentiating. Yet precisely this empty differentiating is indifference itself as result. It is indeed in this way the concrete, that which inside its own self, by the negating of all the determinations of being, stands mediated with itself. Being this mediation, it holds within it negation and relation, and what went under the name of state is a differentiating immanent to it and referring itself to itself; precisely the externality, and the vanishing of that externality, makes the unity of being into indifference, and lies therefore _within_ this indifference, which thereby ceases to be merely substrate and, _at_ _its own_ _self_, merely abstract.
