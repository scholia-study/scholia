## Remark 4

Everything said so far shows what is really at issue in the dialectic that assails the _beginning_ _of_ _the_ _world_, and its downfall along with it, a dialectic meant to establish matter's _eternity_ – at issue, that is, in the dialectic that assails _becoming_, coming-to-be or ceasing-to-be as such. – Kant's antinomy about whether the world is finite or infinite in space and in time receives closer treatment below, where the concept of quantitative infinity comes up. – A firm grip on the opposition between being and nothing is what carries that plain, familiar {{{ 21.91 }}} dialectic. Proof that neither the world nor anything else admits of a beginning runs as follows:

Nothing can begin – not insofar as something is, and not insofar as it is not; for insofar as it is, its beginning is not still to come; and insofar as it is not, it does not begin either. – Suppose the world, or anything else, had begun: then its beginning would have lain in the nothing, but in the nothing – or rather, the nothing itself – there is no beginning; a beginning encloses a being within it, while no being is held in the nothing. Nothing is only nothing. Determine the nothing as a ground, a cause, or the like, and an affirmation, a being, is thereby contained in it. – Ceasing, likewise, is ruled out for the same reason. Being would then have to hold the nothing within it, whereas being is only being, never the opposite of itself.

Clearly, what gets urged here against becoming – against beginning and ceasing, that _unity_ of being and nothing – comes to no more than an assertoric denial of it, coupled with the ascription of truth to being and to nothing severally, each apart from the other. – This dialectic is nevertheless more consistent, at least, than representation carried on by reflection. Reflection holds it for perfect truth that being and nothing stand only apart; on the other hand it grants beginning and ceasing the status of determinations equally true; and in granting them it factually presupposes that being and nothing are not apart.

Presuppose that being is absolutely severed from nothing, and beginning, or becoming, does indeed turn into something _inconceivable_ – as the refrain so often runs; for the presupposition laid down cancels the beginning or becoming that one nonetheless _again_ admits, and just this contradiction, self-posited and by one's own doing left insoluble, receives the name of the _inconceivable_.

That same dialectic is what the understanding brings to bear against the concept of _infinitely_-_small_ _magnitudes_ furnished by higher analysis. Fuller treatment of that concept comes further on. – Such magnitudes, taken as such, received the determination of being ones that _are_ _in_ _their_ _vanishing_ – not _prior_ to that vanishing, since then they would be finite magnitudes; – and not _subsequent_ to their {{{ 21.92 }}} vanishing either, since then they would be nothing. The objection raised against this pure concept, and repeated without end, is that magnitudes of the sort must be _either_ something _or_ nothing; that between being and non-being no _intermediate_ _state_ is to be found (state is here an unfitting, barbarous expression). – Once again the absolute separation of being from nothing has been taken for granted. The demonstration has run the other way, however: being and nothing are in truth one and the same, or, to adopt that idiom, nothing at all _is_ there which fails to be an _intermediate_ _state_ _between_ _being_ and _nothing_. It is precisely the assumption gainsaid by the understanding to which mathematics owes its most brilliant successes.

Reasoning of the kind adduced, which sets up the false presupposition that being and non-being are absolutely apart and then halts there, deserves the name not of _dialectic_ but of _sophistry_. Sophistry, after all, reasons from a groundless presupposition let through uncritically and without reflection; whereas what we call dialectic is the higher rational movement in which terms of that sort, seemingly separate through and through, pass into one another by their own doing, by what they are, and in which the presupposition sublates itself. That being and nothing exhibit their unity, becoming, as their truth belongs to the dialectical, immanent nature of the two themselves.
