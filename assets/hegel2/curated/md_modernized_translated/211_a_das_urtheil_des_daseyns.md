## A. The Judgment of Existence

What the subjective judgment seeks is a _twofold_ view of _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_ object: on the one occasion in its singular actuality, on the other in its essential identity, that is, in its concept; here the singular is elevated to its {{{ 12.60 }}} universality, or, equivalently, the universal is made singular within its actuality. So taken, the judgment is _truth_; for it constitutes the concordance of concept with reality. Yet the judgment is not so constituted _at_ _the_ _outset_; for _at_ _the_ _outset_ it is _immediate_, no reflection and no movement of the determinations having emerged in it as yet. It is this _immediacy_ that makes the first judgment a _judgment_ _of_ _existence_, one that may equally be named the _qualitative_ judgment, though only to the extent that _quality_ pertains not solely to the determinateness of _being_ but takes in besides the abstract universality which, by reason of its simplicity, carries the form of _immediacy_ no less.

The judgment of _inherence_ is a further name for the judgment of existence; since immediacy is what determines it, and since, in the distinction between subject and predicate, it is the subject that counts as the immediate and thereby as what comes first and matters essentially in this judgment, the predicate takes on the shape of something non-self-subsistent, grounded as it is in the subject.
