# peirce1 — curation notes

*Pragmaticism*: sixteen papers Peirce published in the journals between 1867
and 1908, in order of publication. One curated layer (`md_reviewed`), because
his English is already modern and a second layer would duplicate it — see
ADR 0011.

Fourteen papers are generated by `peirce1_wikisource_to_md` from Wikisource's
proofread transcriptions. Two are not, and are recorded in full below. All
sixteen are **hand-maintained from here on**: re-running the converter would
discard any review edits, exactly as with hobbes1's modernized layer.

## Provenance

| Papers | Source |
|---|---|
| 02000, 03000, 05000–16000 | Wikisource ProofreadPage transcriptions (`scripts/peirce1_fetch.sh` → `raw/`, then the converter) |
| 01000 | Wikisource plain transcription; page breaks aligned from the IA scan |
| 04000 | peirce.org transcription, collated against the IA scan |

Peirce died in 1914 and every paper here was published 1867–1908, so all are
public domain. A faithful transcription of a public-domain text carries no new
copyright. The modern critical editions — Collected Papers, the Chronological
Edition, The Essential Peirce — are in copyright and were not used.

## Pagination

The page a passage sits on is the citable fact about these texts, so every
paper carries the original printing's pagination as venue-qualified markers
(`{{{ PSM 12:5 }}}`). The volume spans five periodicals whose volume numbers
collide (JSP 2 and Monist 2; PAAAS 7 and Hibbert 7), hence the venue prefix.

Printed numbers are **derived** from each article's declared contiguous page
range and **checked** against every running head the transcribers recorded. All
fourteen converter-built papers passed with no disagreement, which is what
licenses the derivation for pages whose head was not transcribed. A
disagreement aborts the conversion rather than being silently accepted.

## 01000 — On a New List of Categories

Wikisource holds this as a plain transcription with **no page breaks at all**.
The copy-text is that transcription; the eleven internal boundaries
(PAAAS 7:288–298) were located by aligning the Internet Archive's
page-segmented OCR of *Proceedings* vol. 7 (`proceedingsofame07amer`) against
it. The OCR supplied only positions — not one word of it is in the text.

Each boundary was matched on the tail of the preceding page (a page's foot is
body text; its head is a running head the OCR mangles). All eleven matched at
10/10 tokens, monotonically ordered and evenly spaced. Two constraints were
needed to get there: boundaries must run in order, and a match must fall near
where the page proportionally belongs — Wikisource gathers footnote bodies at
the end of the article, so a page-foot footnote otherwise aligns far past its
true position.

## 04000 — Grounds of Validity of the Laws of Logic

No proofread transcription of this paper exists anywhere; it is a red link on
Wikisource's JSP volume index. Two imperfect witnesses were collated:

- **peirce.org**'s transcription of JSP 2 (1869), 193–208 — clean word
  integrity, but with editorial interventions
- **Internet Archive** microfilm OCR of that issue
  (`sim_journal-of-speculative-philosophy_1869_2_4`) — faithful to the
  printing, but mechanically damaged (lost spaces: `sis`, `itis`, `anda`;
  letter slips: `formule`, `principit`)

Every difference between the two was enumerated by a sequence diff before the
text was written. Beyond OCR damage there were exactly two classes, both
systematic, both **reverted** so the printing's readings stand:

| peirce.org | 1869 printing | Reverted |
|---|---|---|
| `premisses` | `premises` | 18 |
| `premiss` | `premise` | 16 |
| `[Ergo,]` | `∴` | 32 |

The spelling call is not a guess: the OCR has *premise* 33 times and *premises*
17 times, and *premiss* zero times. peirce.org normalised Peirce's 1869 usage
to his later preferred spelling; this edition prints what the journal printed.

After reverting, the text agrees with the printing at **95.9%** of tokens (a
gate the build enforces; the residue is OCR damage). All fifteen page
boundaries were placed, the OCR carrying the page number in three different
layouts across versos and rectos.

## Editorial decisions

**Figure captions.** Nineteen tables and one classification diagram are set as
`<figure>` blocks. The parser requires a non-empty `<figcaption>` — it becomes
the figure's anchor sentence, so an empty one would create an empty sentence
row. Where the printing labels the table ("Fig. 1.", "Table II.", "Deduction.")
that label is the caption. Where it does not, the caption is the bare word
**"Table"** (or "Diagram" for the classification tree). This is the only
editorial wording in the corpus, chosen to describe the object's form without
interpreting its content.

**Plates are not reproduced.** Four papers reference engraved diagrams that the
transcriptions carry as images. There is no image pipeline here, so each is a
figure holding the printing's own caption, with the image filename in an HTML
comment for a later pass. The referring text ("Let A B (Fig. 5) be the path…")
is untouched.

**Mathematics** is set as text: `{{over|81|100}}` → `81/100`, `\sqrt{…}` → `√(…)`,
exponents as Unicode (`2⁵`). Exponents must **never** be written as `<sup>`:
a footnote reference is rendered as `<sup>…</sup>` further down the pipeline, so
a literal `<sup>5</sup>` is read back as a footnote reference and silently
attaches footnote 5 to the exponent.

**Footnote markers** follow the other corpora: one more asterisk per note in
document order. The printings restart their own marks (*, †, ‡) on every page,
which a per-file key cannot express, and the importer renumbers footnotes
book-globally regardless. Each definition needs a blank line before it, or the
parser reads them as one block and keeps only the first marker.

**Journal apparatus is dropped**: running heads, the article masthead (series
title, byline, "FIRST PAPER.—…"), and volume signatures. The paper's own title
is the node label.

**What is not normalised.** Spaced punctuation (`reaction ; as when`, `three
kinds : induction`) and the spaced ellipsis (`. . . .`) are the printings' own
typography and stand as transcribed. This is a diplomatic layer.

## Growth

The edition is expected to gain papers. Two rules, and the second is not
obvious:

1. A paper's `position` number (see `common::peirce1::filenames`) is permanent.
   Numbers are spaced a thousand apart so papers can be inserted without
   renumbering — even many between one adjacent pair.
2. **Curate in chronological order, so every import appends.**
   `paragraph_number` and footnote numbers are book-global counters; inserting
   a paper ahead of one already imported shifts them, which the reconciler
   rejects outright. The only remedy is a full reload, which re-mints sentence
   UUIDs and breaks anchored quotations. This constrains growth after the book
   is live; the initial build imports everything at once.

Known future additions: the 1905–06 pragmaticism papers ("What Pragmatism Is",
"Issues of Pragmaticism") between 15000 and 16000. Neither is transcribed
anywhere, so both need OCR — and under rule 2 they cannot be inserted ahead of
16000 once the book is live.
