## 3. Becoming of the One

Being-for-itself is ideality; and it is, as has just now emerged, the simple unity of its moments, and properly no difference of them at all. It contains otherness as sublated; the sublating of otherness and the reference to itself are the same; there is only one determination at hand, the reference-to-itself of the sublating. The inner ***moments*** of being-for-itself have therefore in fact collapsed into ***differencelessness***.

Being-for-itself is therefore a simple being-one with itself, a being-within-itself that has no limit or determinateness, or whose determinateness is pure negating. Since it is on the whole the ***self-referring*** sublating, this simple equality with itself, it is thus a being-within-itself which has the form of immediacy; a something, but an undeterminable one.

In accordance with this immediacy, this being-within-itself is not a referring but a ***being***. But as ***immediacy*** grounded upon negating, it is at the same time essentially reference, and this constitutes its determination. Its immediacy and this its determination {{{ 100 }}} are therefore distinguished from one another. With its simple immediacy, or as being, it is at the same time pure negating, a reference outwards in general, a purely negating referring; but not to an other; for here there is no other at hand any more, but rather one simply sublated. This reference is also not yet a reference to the immediate; rather, this immediacy is at first nothing other than the simple referring of the negation to itself.

What is posited, then, is the return of ideality into simple being-within-itself, into a self-equality which has the form of immediacy and which is a merely negative referring, a referring to nothing at all. Being-for-itself, as this immediate which is pure negating, is ***that which is for itself, the one***.
