## Remark

***Determinateness in general is negation,*** (Determinatio est negatio) said ***Spinoza;*** — {{{ 76 }}} a proposition of thoroughgoing importance; — one that emerged from the consideration of determinateness. For determinateness is essentially the limit and has otherness for its ground; existence is what it is only through its limit; it does not fall outside this its negation. Hence it was necessary that reality passed over into negation; therewith reality makes its ground and essence manifest.

In connection with reality it was noted that the ***sum total of all realities,*** if they are thought without limit, becomes the empty nothing. But if they are preserved as determinate realities, then the sum total of all realities becomes just as much the ***sum total of all negations***. This may, since negation has just now determined itself into restriction and finitude, also be called the sum total of all restrictions and finitudes. But restriction and finitude are only this, to sublate themselves; that negation, however, as absolute negativity, is the essential determination of the absolute essence, and the determination higher than reality, will be mentioned provisionally in what immediately follows.

From the proposition that determinateness is negation, ***the unity of the Spinozist substance,*** or that there is only One substance, — is a necessary consequence. Thinking and being he had to bring together into one within this unity, since, taken as determinate realities, both are negations, and their infinity or truth lies solely in their unity. He therefore grasped them as attributes, that is, as such as lack any subsistence of their own, any being-in-and-for-itself, and are only as sublated, as moments. — Just as little can the substantiality of individuals hold out against that proposition. For the individual is something limited in all {{{ 77 }}} respects; it is individual reference to itself only through this, that it sets limits to everything other; but these limits are thereby also limits of itself, references to an other, and it does not have its existence within itself. The individual is indeed more than merely what is limited on every side; but insofar as it is taken as ***finite***, determinateness essentially asserts itself as negation against the claim that the finite as such should be motionless, ***something that is,*** in and for itself, and tears it into the negative movement, out of which, however, there proceeds not its empty nothing but rather, for the first time, its infinity and its being-in-and-for-itself.

2. Determinateness is negation in general. But more precisely negation is the doubled moment of ***restriction*** and ***of the ought***.

First: negation is not merely the nothing in general, but negation that is reflected, referred to being-in-itself; the ***lack*** as of something, or the ***restriction;*** determinateness, ***posited*** as what it is in truth, as non-being.

Second: negation as ***the ought*** is the determinateness that is in itself, or conversely, the ought is determinateness or negation as being-in-itself. It is to that extent the ***negation*** of that ***first determinateness,*** which is posited as non-being, as ***restriction***. It is thus ***negation of negation,*** and ***absolute negation***.

Thus negation is the genuinely real and being-in-itself. It is this negativity that is the simple, {{{ 78 }}} which as sublating of otherness returns into itself; the abstract foundation of all philosophical ideas and of speculative thinking in general, of which one must say that only more recent times have begun to grasp it in its truth. — This simplicity has to step into the place of ***being***, or of every determinateness that is taken in ***immediate*** form, as being in and for itself. When negativity or negative nature is spoken of hereafter, what is to be understood by it is not that first negation, the limit, restriction or lack, but essentially the negation of otherness, which, as such, is ***reference to itself***.

Here the negation that is in itself is as yet only the ought, negation of negation indeed, yet in such a way that this ***negating itself*** is still the ***determinateness***. It is, namely, the limit or negation which as ***being-in-itself*** refers itself to itself as ***non-being***. Both negations, which refer themselves to each other, make up the ***reference*** of negation ***to itself***, but they are still ***others*** for one another; they bound each other mutually.

These negations, then, which still refer themselves to each other as others, — the negation ***posited as non-being*** and the negation ***that is in itself*** — the restriction and the ought, make up the (qualitatively) ***finite*** and the (qualitatively) ***infinite***, and their ***reference to each other***.
