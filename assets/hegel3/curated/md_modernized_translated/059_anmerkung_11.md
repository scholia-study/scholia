## Remark

The one in this form of existence is that stage of the category which appeared among the ancients as the ***atomistic principle***, the principle that what things are in essence is ***atoms*** and ***the void***, (το ἀτομον or τα ἀτομα και τοκενον.) Abstraction, ripened to this form, has won a determinateness greater than Parmenides' ***being*** and Heraclitus' ***becoming***. High as it stands in raising this simple determinateness of the one and of the void to the principle of all things, in tracing the world's infinite manifoldness back to this simple opposition, and in making bold to cognize that manifoldness out of it, just as easy is it for representational reflecting to picture atoms ***here*** and the void ***alongside*** them. No wonder, then, that the atomistic principle has held its ground at all times; the relation of ***composition***, just as trivial and external, which has still to be added in order to arrive at the shine of a diversity and manifoldness, is quite as popular as the atoms and the void themselves. The one and the void is being-for-itself, the highest being-within-itself sunk down into complete externality; for in the one there is present the immediacy or the being which, since it negates all otherness, is no longer determinable and alterable, and therefore seems also to be unable to turn back into {{{ 104 }}} itself again, but for which, in its absolute brittleness, all determination, manifoldness, connection remains a simply external reference.

With the first thinkers of the atomistic principle, however, it did not remain in this externality; besides its abstraction it also had the speculative depth that the ***void*** was cognized as the ***source*** of ***motion***; which is a quite other reference of the atoms and the void than the mere side-by-side and the indifference of these two determinations towards one another. That the void is the source of motion does not, however, have the trifling sense that something can move only into a void and not into an already filled space; in which understanding the void would be merely the presupposition or condition, not the ***ground*** of motion, just as motion itself would be presupposed as at hand and the essential thing, the thought of a ground of it, forgotten. The view, by contrast, that the void constitutes the ground of motion contains the profound thought that in the negative as such there lies the ground of becoming, of that unrest which is self-movement. Here, however, the negative is to be taken not as the nothing that lies closest to representation, but as genuine negativity, as the infinite.
