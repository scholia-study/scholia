## Remark 3

***Being is nothing, nothing is being***. It has already been remarked that the expression of speculative truth through the form of simple {{{ 39 }}} propositions is imperfect. Here the propositions would still have to be added: ***Being is not nothing, nothing is not being***; so that the difference too may be ***expressed*** which in those propositions is merely ***present***. — These propositions give completely what is to be said, but not as it is to be gathered together, and is gathered together in becoming.

Into those first propositions ***other relations of thought*** can now be brought. They can be enunciated thus:

What is turns ***therefore*** into nothing, ***because*** being is the nothing.

What is not turns ***therefore*** into something, ***because*** the nothing is being.

Or immediately:

What is therefore comes to naught, because it is.

What is not therefore becomes something that is, because it is not.

The ground that anything whatever becomes something that is because it is not, and that what is vanishes because it is, appears unsatisfying already for this reason, that it is abstract and empty, whereas by the something a concrete, empirical thing is understood. True as those propositions are, still, as soon as such an existence is spoken of, the ground cannot be merely empty being or non-being or an empty reference of the two to each other, but must have the complete determinateness of the content in order to grasp that content out of it. The relation of ground in general is a further, more perfect determination of the reference of being and of non-being to each other; it cannot {{{ 40 }}} be applied to this reference as it stands here, because the reference is rather a unity of pure, determinationless abstractions, and so is essentially not yet a mediation.

If the relation of ***condition*** were brought in, the reference of being and non-being would yield the propositions:

Something can pass over into nothing only ***on the condition*** that it is;

and into being only ***on the condition*** that it is not.

These propositions are empty tautologies; for since a passing over into the opposite is assumed in them, it is certain that, in order for the opposite, being, to be, it is necessary that its opposite, the nothing, be. On the other hand, insofar as the connection of this transition is placed in the relation of condition, their proper unity is sublated; for the condition is indeed something necessary for the conditioned, but is not what posits it; a ***third*** must first come in, one which brings the transition about. Through the intrusion of the condition, being and nothing are thus pushed apart, and a third, falling outside them, is demanded for their reference. But becoming is such a unity of them as lies in the nature of each itself; being is in and for itself the nothing, and the nothing in and for itself being.
