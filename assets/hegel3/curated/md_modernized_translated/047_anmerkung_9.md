## Remark

The infinite, — in the ordinary sense of bad infinity, — and the progress into infinity, like the ought, are the expression of a ***contradiction*** that takes itself for the ***resolution***, or for what is ultimate. This infinite is a first elevation of sensuous representing above the finite into thought, a thought that however has only the content of nothing, — a flight beyond what is limited, a flight that fails to collect itself inward and cannot carry the negative back into the positive. This ***incomplete reflection*** places negativity in the beyond, while the positive or the real stays on this side. Although the elevation of the finite into the infinite {{{ 88 }}} and the calling back of the beyond into this side, or the sublating of these two imperfect determinations, is present, reflection nevertheless does not bring these two thoughts together. The nature of speculative thinking consists solely in grasping the opposed moments in their unity. Since each of them shows in itself that it has its opposite on its own self, its positive truth is this unity, the taking together of both thoughts, their infinity, the reference to itself, not the immediate reference but the infinite one.

The essence of philosophy has often been placed, by those who are already more familiar with thinking, in the task of answering ***how the infinite comes out of itself and to finitude***? — The infinite at whose concept we have arrived will ***determine itself further*** in the course of this exposition, and will thereby show on itself what is demanded, namely how it, if one wishes to express it so, ***comes to finitude***. Here we consider this question only in its immediacy, and with regard to the sense considered just now which the infinite is wont to have.

On the answering of this question it is supposed to depend whether ***there is a philosophy at all***, and while one gives out that one is willing to let the matter turn on this, one believes at the same time to possess in the question itself an invincible talisman by which one is made firm and secure against the answer and thereby against philosophy in general. — With other subject matters too it presupposes a formation to understand how to ask, but still more with philosophical subject matters, if one is to receive an answer other than that the question is worthless.

{{{ 89 }}} With such questions it is customary, in respect of the expression, to lay claim to the fairness that the words do not matter, but that in one or another manner of expression what does matter is intelligible? Expressions of sensuous representation, like ***going out*** and the like, which are readily used in the question, arouse the suspicion that the home from which the question stems is the soil of ordinary representing, and that for the answer, too, representations current in common life and the shape of a sensuous simile are expected.

If instead of the infinite being in general is taken, then the determining of being, a negation on it, seems more easily comprehensible. For being is indeed itself the indeterminate; insofar therefore as it is determined, it is the determinate indeterminate, unity of determinateness and indeterminateness. But it is not immediately expressed on being that it is the opposite of what is determinate. The infinite by contrast contains this expressed; it is the ***not-***finite. The unity of the finite and the infinite thus seems immediately shut out; incomplete, representing reflection is therefore most stubborn against this unity.

But it has been shown, and it is immediately evident, that the infinite, and indeed in the sense in which it is taken by that reflecting, — namely as standing over against the finite, — has its other on the finite for the very reason that it stands over against the finite, and is therefore bounded and itself finite. The answer to the question ***how the infinite becomes finite*** is accordingly this, that there ***is*** no infinite which is ***at first*** infinite and which would need only afterwards to become finite, {{{ 90 }}} to come to finitude; rather it is already for its own self just as much finite as infinite. Or since the question assumes the infinite on the one side for itself, and assumes that the finite, which has gone out of it into separation, is, in its severance from it, genuinely real, or that if not precisely this finite then at least that infinite is the truth, — one could say that this separation is indeed incomprehensible. For neither such a finite nor such an infinite has truth; but the untrue is incomprehensible. One can therefore say that this question sets up an untrue content and contains an untrue reference of that content. It is thus not to be answered; rather the false presuppositions that it contains, or the question itself, are to be negated. But what was already noted above concerning the unity of being and nothing is to be recalled, namely that the expression: unity of the infinite and the finite, or: that finite and infinite are the same, also has a lopsided side, because it expresses as motionless being what is a becoming. Thus the infinite too is the becoming towards the finite, and conversely the finite the becoming towards the infinite. One can say in this way that the infinite goes out to the finite, and indeed for the reason that it has no truth, no subsistence on its own self; so conversely the finite goes into the infinite, on the same ground of its nullity. But the question assumes the infinite that stands over against the finite as something genuine; or else the ***reference-less*** infinite, which however ought then to be called not infinite but being; yet with being it has already shown itself that this pure ***immediate*** unity ***has no truth***.
