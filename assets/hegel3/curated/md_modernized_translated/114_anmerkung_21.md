## Remark

The natural number system is already such a ***nodal line*** of qualitative moments, which come to the fore within a merely external going-on. On the one part it is a merely quantitative going forwards and backwards, a perpetual adding-on or taking-away, so that every number stands in the same ***arithmetical*** ratio to the one preceding it and the one following it as these do to the ones preceding and following them, and so forth. But the numbers arising in this way also stand in a ***specific*** ratio to others preceding or following, either that of being such a multiple of one of them as a whole number expresses, or that of being power or root. — In the ***musical*** ratios a harmonic ratio sets in upon the scale of quantitative going-on by way of a quantum, without this quantum having for itself, upon the scale, any other ratio to its predecessor and its successor than these in turn have to theirs. While the tones that follow seem to withdraw ever further from the keynote, or numbers through the arithmetical going-on seem merely to become still more other, all at once there comes forth instead a return, a surprising accord, which was not qualitatively prepared by what immediately preceded but appears as an actio in distans, as a reference to something remote. The going-on among merely indifferent ratios, which do not change the preceding specific reality, or which form no such reality at all, interrupts itself all at once, and since in quantitative respect it is continued in the same manner, a specific ratio thus {{{ 312 }}} sets in by way of a leap.

In ***chemical combinations*** there occur everywhere, with the progressive change of the mixture-ratios, such qualitative nodes and leaps that two materials at particular points of the mixture-scale form products of particular qualities. These products are not distinguished from one another merely by a more and less, nor are they already at hand along with the ratios lying close to those nodal ratios, perhaps only in a weaker degree; rather they are bound to such points themselves. For instance, the combinations of oxygen and nitrogen give the various nitrogen oxides and nitric acids, which come forth only at determinate quantitative ratios of the mixture and have essentially different qualities, so that at mixture-ratios lying in between no combinations and specific concrete existences result. — The ***metal oxides***, for instance the lead oxides, arise at certain quantitative points in the course of oxidation, and are marked off from one another by colour and by further qualities. They do not shade over into one another by degrees; rather the ratios that lie between those nodes do not occur as a specific existence, they are incapable of yielding a product. Without having passed through intermediate ratios, a specific combination steps forward which rests upon a measure-relation and has qualities of its own. — Or ***water***, in changing its temperature, does not thereby become merely more or less warm, but passes through the states of hardness, of drippable liquidity and of elastic liquidity; these different states do not set in gradually, rather it is precisely the merely gradual going-on of the temperature-change that is at these {{{ 313 }}} points interrupted and checked all at once, and the onset of another state is a leap. — All ***birth*** and ***death***, instead of being a continued gradualness, are rather an absolute breaking-off of it, and the leap out of the quantitative into the qualitative.

***There is no leap in nature***; and ordinary representation, when it is to comprehend a ***coming-to-be*** or ***ceasing-to-be***, supposes, as has already been recalled, that it has comprehended it by representing it as a ***gradual*** emerging or vanishing. It has shown itself, however, that the alterations of being at large are not only the passing-over of one quantum into another quantum, but a transition from the qualitative into the quantitative and conversely, a becoming-other which is a breaking-off of the gradual and a qualitatively-other over against the preceding existence. — Thus water does not become hard bit by bit through chilling, in such a way that it would turn pulpy and stiffen by degrees up to the consistency of ice; rather it is hard all at once; even when it already has the full temperature of the freezing point, yet stands at rest, it still has its full liquidity, and a slight shaking brings it into the state of hardness. — Underlying the gradualness of coming-to-be is the representation that what comes to be is already sensibly, or in general actually, at hand, only not yet perceptible on account of its smallness, just as, with the gradualness of vanishing, that non-being, or the other that steps into its place, is likewise at hand, only not yet noticeable; — at hand, that is, not in the sense that the other is contained ***in itself*** within the other that is at hand, but that it is at hand as existence, only unnoticeably. Thereby {{{ 314 }}} coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be at large are sublated, or the ***in-itself***, the inner, in which something is prior to its existence, is converted into a smallness of external existence, and the essential difference, or the difference of concept, into an external, mere difference of magnitude. — Making a coming-to-be or a ceasing-to-be comprehensible out of the gradualness of the alteration has the tediousness proper to tautology, because it has what comes to be or ceases to be already wholly finished beforehand, and turns the alteration into a mere change of an external difference, whereby it is in fact nothing but a tautology.

In the ***moral*** sphere, insofar as it is to be considered within the sphere of being, the same transition of the quantitative into the qualitative takes place; or different qualities ground themselves upon a diversity of quantum. It is a more or less through which the measure of frivolity is overstepped and something wholly other, crime, comes forth, through which right passes over into wrong, virtue into vice. — So too do states acquire, through their difference of magnitude, when the rest is assumed to be equal, a different qualitative character. Laws and constitution become something other when the compass of the state and the amount of its citizens expand. The state has a measure of its magnitude, driven out beyond which it falls apart within itself without holding, under the very constitution which at a different compass made up its good fortune and its strength.
