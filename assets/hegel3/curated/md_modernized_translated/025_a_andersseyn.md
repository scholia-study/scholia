## {{{ 49 }}} a) Otherness

Existence is therefore ***first*** that unity not merely as being, but just as essentially as non-being. Or that unity is not only existence that is, but also existence that is not; ***non-existence***.

In connection with the transition of being into nothing it was recalled in what respect that transition is immediate. The nothing is not yet ***posited*** on being, although being ***essentially*** is nothing. Existence, by contrast, already contains the nothing posited within its own self, and is thereby the very yardstick of its own incompleteness, and with that carries on its own self the necessity of getting posited as non-existence.

***Second***, non-existence is not pure nothing; for it is a nothing ***as that of existence***. And this negation is taken from existence itself; but in existence it is united with being. Non-existence is therefore itself a being; it is ***non-existence that is***. A non-existence that is, however, is itself existence. This ***second existence*** is nevertheless at the same time not existence in the same manner as at first; for it is just as much non-existence; existence as non-existence; existence as the nothing of its own self, so that this nothing of its own self is likewise existence. — Or existence is essentially ***otherness***.

Or, put shortly by comparing it with its own self, existence is the immediate ***simple*** unity of being and nothing; but because it is unity of ***being*** and ***nothing***, it is rather not a unity equal to itself, but is downright unequal to itself, or is ***otherness***.

{{{ 50 }}} Otherness is at first otherness in and for itself, not the other of something, such that existence would have stayed standing over against the other, and such that we should have ***one*** existence and ***another*** existence. For existence has altogether passed over into otherness. Otherness is itself existence; but existence as such is the immediate; this immediacy, however, has not remained, but rather existence is existence only as non-existence, or it is otherness.

As being passed over into nothing, so existence into otherness; otherness is the nothing, yet as ***reference. An other*** is ***not-this***; but ***this*** is likewise an ***other***, hence also not-this. There is no existence that would not at the same time be determined as an other, or that would not carry a negative reference.

Representation grants this too. If we call one existence A, and the other B, then at first B is determined as the other. Yet A is just as much the other of B. Both are others.

In all this, however, otherness shows up as a determination foreign to the existence so determined, or as the other ***outside*** the one existence; partly in such a way that an existence gets determined as other only through the comparison drawn by a third, while for itself it is not an other, partly in such a way that it gets determined as other only for the sake of the other that lies outside it, and not in and for itself. Yet in fact each existence determines itself, for representation too, just as much as an other existence, so that no existence is left to it that would be determined only as an existence and not as an other; or no existence that would not lie outside an existence and hence not itself be an other. — {{{ 51 }}} Representation does indeed reach the universality of a determination, but not the necessity of that determination in and for its own self. This necessity lies, though, in this, that it has shown itself at the concept of existence that existence as such is in and for itself the other, that it holds its otherness within its own self. — But otherness is the nothing essentially as reference, or is the sundering, the removing of itself from itself, and hence this determination of otherness sets existence over against itself; which is the side that alone hovers before representation.

***Third***: existence itself is essentially otherness; it has passed over into it. The other is thus immediate, not a reference to ***something lying outside it***, but an other in-and-for-itself. But so taken it is the ***other of its own self***. — As the other of its own self it is also existence in general, or immediate. Existence therefore does not vanish in its non-existence, in its other; for this is the other of its own self; and non-existence is itself existence.

Existence ***maintains*** itself in its non-existence; it is essentially one with it, and essentially not one with it. Existence therefore stands ***in reference*** to its otherness; it is not purely its otherness; otherness is at the same time essentially contained within it, and at the same time still ***separated*** from it; it is ***being-for-other***.
