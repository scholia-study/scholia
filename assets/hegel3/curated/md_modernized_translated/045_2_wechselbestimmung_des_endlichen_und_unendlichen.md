## 2. Reciprocal Determination of the Finite and the Infinite

Infinity is the determination of the finite, but this determination is what is determinate itself. Infinity is therefore itself determinate, reference to an other. But the other to which the infinite refers itself is the finite. Yet they are not merely others in general over against each other; both are negations, but the one is the negation that is in itself, the other the negation as not being in itself, negation as non-being, as something sublated.

According to this its determinateness over against the infinite, the finite is negation as the determinateness on ***existence;*** it is not the negation of negation, but the ***first*** negation, or the one that has indeed sublated being within itself yet keeps it stored up within itself, merely the ***immediate negation***. The finite therefore, as ***real existence***, stands over against the infinite as its negation. Both stand only in reference to each other; the finite is not yet truly sublated but remains standing over against the infinite; {{{ 82 }}} immediately the infinite has likewise not truly sublated the finite within itself but has it outside itself.

The infinite posited in this way is the ***bad infinite***, or the infinite of the understanding. It is not the negation of negation but is ***brought down to the simple first negation***. It is the nothing of the finite, which is the real; it is the ***empty***, determinationless ***beyond*** of existence. — In this way it is indeed the ***determination*** of the finite to become infinite, but the finite does not have this its determination on its own self; its ***being-in-itself*** is not in its existence but a ***beyond*** of it.

This infinite is the same empty abstraction that at the beginning stood over against being as nothing. There it was the immediate nothing; here it is the nothing that comes back and emerges out of existence, and that stands in reference to existence as merely immediate negation. Because the finite thus remains over against it as existence, the infinite has its limit on the finite and is thus only a determinate, ***self-finite infinite***.

Thus to representation the finite appears as the actual, and the infinite by contrast as the unactual, which in a dim, unreachable distance is supposed to be the ***in-itself*** of the finite yet at the same time only its ***limit***; for the two are outside and beyond one another.

They are outside one another, yet by their nature absolutely referred to each other; each is the limit of the other and consists only in having this limit. In their separation each therefore has at the same time this its other on its own self, but as the non-being of {{{ 83 }}} itself, repelling it from itself just as immediately. Their unity is thus not the reference posited on them; that reference is rather their reference as absolute others, of finitude as reality, of infinity as negation. — Their unity of concept is the ***determination***, in which the ought and the restriction were the same, and out of which finitude and infinity have sprung. But this unity has hidden itself in the otherness of the two; it is ***the inward one, which merely lies at the ground***; — hence the infinite seems merely to ***come forth*** on the finite, and the finite on the infinite, the one other on the other, that is to say, each seems to be a coming-to-be of its own, an ***immediate*** coming-to-be, and their reference merely an external one.

One therefore goes out beyond the finite into the infinite. This going-beyond appears as an external doing. In this void what comes to be? What is the positive in it? For the sake of the unity of the infinite and the finite, or because this infinite is itself limited, the limit comes to be; the infinite sublates itself again, its other, the finite, has entered. But this entering of the finite appears as a doing external to the infinite, and the new limit as something that does not come to be out of the infinite itself. There is thus present the relapse into the previous, sublated determination. This new limit, however, is itself only something that is to be sublated, or gone out beyond. Thus the void, the nothing, has come to be once more, in which, however, that determination, a new limit, can be posited, ***and so on into infinity***.

There is present the ***reciprocal determination of the finite and the infinite***; the finite is {{{ 84 }}} finite only in the reference to the ought or to the infinite, and the infinite is infinite only in reference to the finite. They are absolute others over against each other, and each has the other of itself on its own self.

It is this reciprocal determination which appears more precisely in the quantitative sphere as the ***progress*** into ***infinity***, which in so many shapes and applications counts as an ***ultimate***, beyond which one goes no further; rather, having arrived at that: ***And so on*** into infinity, thought is wont to have reached its end.

The reason why ***this going-beyond*** is not itself ***gone beyond*** has emerged. Only the bad infinite is present; one does indeed go out beyond it, for a new limit is posited, but by that very act one rather only returns to the finite. Bad infinity is the same as the perennial ***ought***; it is indeed the negation of the finite, but it is not able to free itself from the finite in truth; the finite comes forth again ***on infinity itself***, as its other, because this infinite only is as ***in reference*** to the finite that is other to it. The progress into infinity is therefore only the self-repeating sameness, one and the same tedious alternation of this finite and this infinite.

This infinity of the infinite progress, which remains saddled with the finite, has its other, the finite, on its own self; it is thereby bounded and itself ***finite***; it is the ***bad*** infinity for the reason that it is not in and for itself but only is as reference to its other.

{{{ 85 }}} ***This infinite is itself finite***. — It would thus in fact be the unity of the finite and the infinite. But upon this unity no reflection is made. Yet it alone is what calls forth the infinite within the finite and the finite within the infinite, and is, so to speak, the mainspring of the infinite progress. The progress is the ***outward*** side of that unity, at which representation comes to a halt, at that perennial repetition of one and the same alternating, at the empty restlessness of moving on out beyond the limit, a moving-on that ***finds*** a new limit in this infinite yet can hold itself on that limit as little as it can within the infinite. This infinite has, on the one hand, the fixed determination of a ***beyond***, which therefore cannot be reached, and that because it is not ***supposed*** to be reached, because it has the determination of a beyond. According to this determination it has the finite over against it, as the determination of a ***this-side***; a finite which can just as little raise itself into the infinite, and that because it has this determination of an other for it.
