## 1. Being-for-itself in General

Whatever is for itself is such by virtue of sublating otherness, and the reference to and community with an other. What is other is present in it only ***as*** a sublated one, as one of its moments. Being-for-itself does not pass out beyond itself so as to be for itself a restriction, an other; it consists rather in having already passed beyond the restriction, beyond its otherness, and in being the infinite turning back into itself precisely as this negation.
