## b.) Being for One

The infinite reference of being-for-itself to itself consists in the equality of the negation with itself. {{{ 94 }}} Otherness, however, has not vanished, as though being-for-itself were only the immediate reference of being to itself; rather it is something sublated. Otherness is not distributed between being-for-itself and an other; being-for-itself does not have non-being ***on it*** as limit or determinateness, and therefore not as an existence other than itself either. The other is therefore no existence at all, no something; it is only within being-for-itself, is nothing apart from the latter's infinite reference to itself, and thus has only this existence, to be ***for one***.

This second moment of being-for-itself expresses how the finite is in its unity with the infinite. Being-for-other in existence, or existence in general, likewise has this side of being for one; but besides that it is also in itself, indifferent towards this its limit.
