## 1. Finitude and Infinity

Existence is determinate; and determinateness posits itself as negation and restriction through this, that as a determinateness which is within itself it at the same time goes out beyond itself, and refers itself to itself as to its negation. In this way existence is not merely determinate but limited; ***finite***, and it is not merely finite, but it is ***finitude***.

Insofar as we say of things that they are finite, what is understood by this is not merely that they contain a determinateness, — for quality can be taken as determination or also as reality, — but that it is not being, rather non-being as restriction, that makes up their nature.

But what is determinate is finite only in the ought; that is to say, insofar as it goes out beyond itself as beyond its negation. The finite is negation insofar as it is negation ***to itself***, refers itself to itself as to non-being, insofar therefore as ***the restriction*** is by it just as much ***sublated***. It is, namely, the limit insofar as this makes up being-in-itself, or the determination, that is to say, just as much insofar as it refers itself to itself, and so is equal to itself. But in this reference of negation {{{ 80 }}} to itself consists the sublating of the negation of it, or of its inequality. Determinateness is therefore negation and finitude only insofar as at the same time there is present in it the reference to itself, equality with itself, the sublating of the restriction. The finite is therefore itself this sublating of itself; it is itself this, to be ***infinite***.

As the concept of the ***infinite*** has thus emerged, it is the otherness of otherness, the negation of negation, the reference to itself through the sublating of determinateness. — The infinite in this its simple concept can become the second definition of the absolute; this concept is deeper than becoming; but here it is still saddled with a determinateness; and the main thing is to keep the genuine concept of infinity apart from bad infinity, reason's infinite apart from the understanding's infinite.

It first showed itself in determinate existence that existence, in its being-in-itself, determines itself as finite and goes out beyond itself as beyond the restriction. It is therefore in general the nature of the finite itself to go out beyond itself, to negate the negation and to become infinite. The infinite therefore does not stand ***above*** the finite as something finished on its own account, so that the finite would have and keep its abiding outside of it or beneath it. Nor is it ***we***, merely as a subjective reason, who go out beyond the finite into the infinite. As when it is said that the infinite is the concept of reason, and that through reason we raise ourselves above the temporal and the finite, this happens quite without prejudice to finitude, which that elevation, remaining external to it, in no way concerns. But insofar as the finite is itself raised into {{{ 81 }}} infinity, it is just as little an alien force that does this to it; rather this is its nature, to refer itself to itself as to a restriction, and thus to go out beyond that restriction. For as has been shown, the restriction only is insofar as it is gone out beyond. Infinity in general therefore does not consist in the sublating of finitude in general; rather the finite is only this, to become that itself through its own nature. Infinity is its ***determination,*** or that which it is in itself.
