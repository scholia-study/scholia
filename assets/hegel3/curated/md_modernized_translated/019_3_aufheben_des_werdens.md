## 3. Sublation of Becoming

The equilibrium into which coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be settle themselves is at first becoming itself. But this equally collapses together into a ***tranquil unity***. Within it, being and nothing have their standing only as vanishing ones; becoming as such, however, exists solely through the differentiatedness of the two. Their vanishing is therefore the vanishing of becoming, or a vanishing of vanishing itself. Becoming is thus an unsteady restlessness which sinks together into a tranquil result.

{{{ 45 }}} The point admits of this formulation as well: what becoming amounts to is being's vanishing into nothing together with nothing's vanishing into being, and altogether the vanishing of being and of nothing; yet it rests all the while upon the difference between them. It therefore contradicts itself within itself, because it unites within itself such as is opposed to itself; a union of this kind, however, destroys itself.

This result is having-vanished, not as ***nothing***; taken so, it would be merely a relapse into one of the determinations already sublated. It is instead that unity of being and nothing which has grown into tranquil simplicity.

In becoming itself, being as well as nothing is, each in like manner rather only as the nothing of its own self. ***Becoming*** is the unity as vanishing, or ***the unity in the determination of nothing***. But this nothing is an essential passing over into being, and becoming is therefore a passing over into the unity of being and nothing which is as ***something that is***, or has the shape of the ***immediate*** unity of these moments; ***existence***.
