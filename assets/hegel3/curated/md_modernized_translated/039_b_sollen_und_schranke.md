## b.) The Ought and Restriction

Something maintains itself in the alteration of its constitution; the alteration touches only this unstable surface of otherness, not the determination of the something itself. But it is ***the*** constitution of the something that is alteration: that is to say, the otherness of the something, which is ***in its own self***. The constitution of the something is not merely surface; rather the limit is the being-within-itself of the something; or constitution is its determination itself. Above, the two yielded themselves only as different ***sides*** for external reflection; but in themselves they are united and undivided in quality; the externality of otherness is the something's own inwardness. Something is determined, it is within itself only through its limit; the limit is the negation of {{{ 72 }}} otherness, but thereby otherness is the immanent determination, in itself, of the something itself.

For there is present in the something not merely being-within-itself and its other in general; rather this other of it is its determinateness in itself, namely determination itself. Determination is therefore the being-within-itself that refers to itself, but that, as this being-within-itself, is itself its limit. Being-within-itself, equal to itself, therefore refers to itself as to its own non-being. The limit that thus makes up the determination of the something, but in such a way that it is at once determined as the something's non-being, is ***restriction***.

The being-in-itself of determination, however, in this reference to the limit, namely to itself as restriction, is ***the ought***.

The limit that attaches to existence in general is not restriction. For it to be restriction, existence must at the same time ***pass out beyond it***. Existence must refer ***to it as to something that is not***. The existence of the something lies only quietly, indifferently, as it were ***alongside*** its limit. But something passes out beyond its limit only insofar as it is the sublatedness of that limit. And since the ***limit*** is ***determination*** itself, something thereby passes out ***beyond its own self***.

The ought therefore contains determination doubled, ***once*** as determination that is in itself; the ***other time***, however, that same determination as a non-being, as restriction. The ought is determination and the sublatedness of determination itself, and indeed in such a way that just this sublatedness of itself is ***in it***. The ought is thus the reference of determination to itself as to its non-being, or to the non-being that it itself is.

{{{ 73 }}} That which ought to be ***is***, and at once ***is*** ***not***. Were it, it ***ought*** not merely to ***be***. The ought therefore essentially has a restriction. — But further, this restriction is not something alien. ***That which*** ought to be is the ***determination,*** that is, it is the ***determinateness*** of determination itself, which is not. This is what was just now expressed by saying that the ought is determinateness, but equally the ***sublatedness of this determinateness*** itself.

What has emerged, then, consists in this: something has a determination; that is, a determinateness which, however, is not to be its limit, not its ceasing, but rather its being-within-itself itself. But it ***has*** with this at the same time a limit, or is determined; the sublated limit is preserved. This limit is restriction, and determination is the ought, insofar as determinateness in the simple unity of being-within-itself at once ***is*** and ***is not***.

The something's resting within itself in its determination thus lowers itself to the ***ought***, in that the very determinateness which makes up its being-within-itself is at the same time, and in one and the same respect, sublated as well, is as ***non-being***. The restriction of the something is therefore not something outward; rather its own determination is also its restriction.

As ought, something further passes out beyond its restriction, that is, what ***is not*** in it, what is sublated, ***is*** in it as well; namely the same determinateness, as which it is sublated, is its being-in-itself, and its limit is also not its limit.

As ***ought***, then, something stands ***elevated over its restriction,*** yet conversely it possesses its ***restriction*** only ***as*** {{{ 74 }}} ***ought***. Both are inseparable. It has a restriction insofar as it has a determination, and determination is also the sublatedness of restriction.
