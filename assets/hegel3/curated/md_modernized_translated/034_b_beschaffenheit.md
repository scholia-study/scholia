## b.) Constitution

Determination makes up the ***being-in-itself*** of the something. But determinateness is not only being-in-itself; as limit it is also ***being-for-other,*** or being-within-itself that has passed over into otherness. Determinateness is at first indifference towards an other, and the other falls outside the something. Yet at the same time, since the limit belongs to the something itself, the something has otherness in its own self. In this {{{ 67 }}} way determinateness is an external existence of the something, one that is indeed ***its*** existence, but that does not belong to its being-in-itself.

Determinateness is thus ***constitution***.

Constituted this way or that, something is grasped not as being within itself, but as standing under external influence and external relation. This determinateness, which does indeed belong to the something, is rather its otherness, but insofar as that otherness is in it. The external reference on which constitution depends, and the being-determined through an other, appears as something contingent, because it appears as an other, as something external. But the something ***consists*** in being surrendered to this externality and in having a ***constitution***. — Determination is otherness taken back into itself; precisely thereby otherness, instead of being sublated, has rather been made into the determination of determinateness, into its being-in-itself.
