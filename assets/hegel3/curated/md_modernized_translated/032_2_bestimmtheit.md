## {{{ 65 }}} 2. Determinateness

The limit belongs to the something itself; the something has no existence apart from it; the limit is the being-in-itself of the something itself; it is not external to the something's being-within-itself, but is itself a limit that is within itself. Its truth is ***determinateness*** in general. — This is the result of the foregoing. — When the limit alters, the something seems still to remain as an existence in general, and the alteration seems to go on outside it, only in the limit. But as the limit is in truth, namely as ***determinateness,*** (the qualitative, not yet the quantitative limit) it is that through which something is what it is; when determinateness vanishes, something itself vanishes, or when another determinateness steps into the place of one determinateness, something itself is an other.

Something ***has*** a determinateness. In this expression the something and its determinateness are distinguished from each other. But this distinction belongs to external reflection. Something is ***the determinate***; it stands in simple immediate unity with determinateness. Something therefore vanishes in its determinateness; properly speaking, then, one should no longer speak so much of the something as of determinateness. For something is being-within-itself in an immediacy; according to this immediacy it has negation, the limit, only in it, as being-for-other, and something is in itself over against the limit; but in unity with the limit it is sublated, for its immediacy has vanished, and it has passed over into determinateness.

Simple ***determinateness*** is the unity of being-within-itself and of the limit. It contains both within itself as {{{ 66 }}} sublated, as moments, or ***it is itself determined in this twofold manner***. On the one side it is the limit turned back into itself, on the other side it is also being-within-itself that has passed over into being-for-other, or that is as limit.
