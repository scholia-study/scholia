## 3. Something

Existence as reality is the differentiating of its own self into being-in-itself and being-for-other. Herein being-in-itself is as differentiated from being-for-other; but it thereby is only as referred to it, and in unity with it. Just so being-for-other {{{ 58 }}} is not otherness itself, but holds within itself the reference to its own self, being-in-itself. These two unities accordingly make up, in their very difference, One unity, and are the passing over into one another.

Existence at first, as such, is only the ***immediate*** unity of being and nothing. Reality is this unity in the determinate difference of its moments, which make up distinct ***sides*** on it, determinations of reflection that are indifferent toward one another. But because each one is only as standing in reference to the other, and each encloses the other within itself, reality ceases to be a unity of the sort in which both subsist indifferently. It is a unity that does not let them subsist, their ***sublating*** simple unity. Existence is ***being-within-itself***, and as being-within-itself it is an existent, or ***something***.

The being-within-itself of existence is thus the simple reference of existence to its own self, as being-in-itself is. But being-in-itself is this equality with self rather in an immediate manner; in being-in-itself the moment of being is what lies at the ground, and being-for-other stands over against it. This can be put as follows, that being-in-itself is the reference of existence to its own self, not as the ***existence's own reflection into itself***, but as an external one; or only through this, that being-for-other gets severed from the reference to self. — Being-within-itself, on the other hand, is now existence's own being-in-itself; it is ***its reflection into itself***. Existence is the unity which is reality, insofar as this unity has distinct ***sides***, that is, reality is the immediate unity, yet referred to that external reflection which distinguishes distinct sides. Being-within-itself, {{{ 59 }}} by contrast, is the reference of existence to itself insofar as the sublating of being-for-other is existence's own; being-for-other passes over on its own self into being-in-itself, and this is thereby no longer immediate being-in-itself, but that which has likewise united itself with its other moment, and in which being-for-other is sublated, or being-within-itself.

***Something*** determines itself further, more closely, as being-for-itself, or thing, substance, subject, and so forth. At the ground of all these determinations lies the negative unity; the reference to self through negation of otherness. Something is this negative unity of being-within-itself as yet only quite indeterminately.

Existence passes over into an existent within its own self, by gaining, as the sublating of being-for-other, this point of negative unity. Existence therefore, as something, is not the ***immediate*** unity of being and nothing ***that is***; rather, as being-within-itself it has reference to itself insofar as it is negation. The ***being*** of something therefore does not consist in its immediacy, but in the ***non-being*** of otherness; within something, then, existence has passed over into the negative to this extent, that the negative now lies at the ground. Something is existence only insofar as it has a ***determinateness***.
