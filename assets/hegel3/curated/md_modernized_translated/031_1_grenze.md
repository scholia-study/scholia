## 1. Limit

1. Something is ***first*** an existence enclosed in general; it contains within itself the non-being of otherness; a non-being through which it is, as being-within-itself.

{{{ 61 }}} Secondly, as existence it is indeed being-for-other; but being-for-other is taken back into being-in-itself. This means, on the one side, that otherness has not vanished; but because something, precisely on the ground of its having returned into itself, is simple being-within-itself, otherness falls outside it. This other is another something, towards which the something ***is indifferent***; it ***is***, whether this other be, or not be, or however it be. The something is ***being-in-itself***, and indeed ***over against*** the other; this being-in-itself makes up its ***indifference***. — The first being-in-itself of existence is immediate being-in-itself; being-within-itself, by contrast, is likewise being-in-itself, yet not as immediate, but a being-in-itself that is negative, over against an other, or the being-in-itself. That has stepped out, into being-for-other. — In this, then, that existence is determined as indifferent, the ***other*** first properly steps over against ***an*** existence; into otherness, as it appeared earlier, existence had itself passed over; this unity of the two developed into the moments considered, through whose negative unity, being-within-itself, existence severs itself from otherness and sets the two in indifferent reference to each other.

Thirdly, however, because being-within-itself is the non-being of otherness, something is not something indifferent in general; rather, the non-being of the other is an essential moment of its indifference; it is the ***ceasing of an other in it***.

Something therefore contains the three moments 1) its non-being, the other is outside it; it itself is self-equal reference to itself, 2) the other is not an other in general, or in an external reflection, {{{ 62 }}} but ceases in the something, something is its non-being; 3) something thereby has non-being itself in it, but as the ceasing of its otherness, and thus as the being of its own self.

It has a ***limit***.

Something has a limit at first only in relation to an other; that limit is the other's non-being, not the something's own; through it, therefore, the something ***does not limit itself***, ***but its other***.

2. But the other is itself a something in general, for it is likewise existence. The limit, then, which something holds towards the other, is at the same time the other's own limit as a something, or it is that other's limit, by which the other holds off the first something as ***its*** other, or is a ***non-being of that something***. It is therefore not only the non-being of the other, but of the something as well; it is at the something itself.

Or, immediately, insofar as the something only is as the non-being of the other, it is in its own self non-being, and the limit is just as much that by which it is itself limited.

3. As non-being it is the ceasing of the something, but since it is essentially the ceasing of the other, the something at the same time ***is*** through its limit. — The other is likewise the non-being of the something, but if the limit were only this non-being, then something would simply cease in its limit; yet it is the non-being of the something only in such a way that it is at once the non-being of the other, and hence ***the being of the something***.

Insofar, now, as something in its limit ***is*** and ***is not***, and these moments are at first taken in immediate distinctness, the {{{ 63 }}} non-existence and the existence of the something fall apart from each other. Something has its existence ***outside*** its limit; but just so the other too, because it is a something, lies outside that limit. The limit is the ***middle*** of the two, in which they cease. They have ***their existence over beyond*** one another and ***beyond their limit***; the limit, being what each of them is not, counts for both as the other, and so each has its existence apart from its own non-being.

— According to this diversity of the something from its limit, the ***line*** appears to us as line only outside its limit, the ***point***; the ***surface*** as surface outside the line; the ***body*** as body only outside the surface that bounds it. — This is the side from which limit at first enters representation, — the concept's being-outside-itself, — and in which it is therefore taken up chiefly in spatial objects too.

4. Further, however, the something as it is outside the limit, the unlimited something, is merely existence in general. Outside the limit something is not distinguished from its other; it is only existence, and so it has the same determination as its other; each is only something in general, or each is an other.

But something is something only through being-within-itself; and it is ***within itself*** only through the non-being of an other; without limit it is its other. Its being turned outward against an other, the non-being that is its limit, thus makes up what is essential to the something, or its existence. ***Something is what it is only in its limit***.

Being-within-itself, as ***simple*** reference to itself, at first shuts otherness and with it the {{{ 64 }}} limit itself — as the reference to the other — out of itself and out of the something. But the equality of the something with itself rests on its negative nature; or ***non-being*** is here ***being-in-itself*** itself; hence the limit is being-within-itself. Above, the being-within-itself of the something was determined thus, that it is being-for-other taken up into being-in-itself; being-in-itself over against an other was the indifference of the something towards an other. But conversely otherness, or the non-being of the something, is thereby posited as being-in-itself, which has no other content or subsistence than the limit itself.

— The ***point***, then, is not the limit of the ***line*** merely in the sense that the line ceases in it and is, as existence, outside it; — nor the ***line*** the limit of the ***surface*** merely in the sense that the surface ceases in the line, and likewise the ***surface*** as limit of the ***body***. Rather, the line ***begins*** in the point as well; the point is the line's absolute beginning, it constitutes its ***element***, as the line constitutes the element of the surface; the surface that of the body. These ***limits*** are thus at the same time the ***principle*** of what they limit; as the one, for instance as the hundredth, is a limit, but also an element of the whole hundred.

The limit is therefore not distinguished from the something; this non-being is rather its ground, and makes it what it is; it makes up its being, or its being does not reach out beyond its otherness, beyond its negation. Thus the limit is ***determinateness***.
