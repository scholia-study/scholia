## Remark

The ought has of late played a great role in philosophy, chiefly in reference to morality, and in general also as the ultimate and absolute concept of the identity of ***equality with oneself*** and ***of determinateness***.

***You can, because you ought***, — this expression, which was meant to say a great deal, lies in the concept of the ought. For the ought is the being out beyond the restriction; the limit is sublated in the ought. — But conversely it is just as correct: ***You cannot, precisely because you ought***. For in the ought there lies just as much the restriction as restriction; determinateness makes up determination as being-within-itself; but being-within-itself is essentially as the sublatedness of this determinateness, which is nevertheless being-within-itself itself, and hence determinateness as non-being, as restriction.

In the ought the concept of ***finitude*** begins in general, and with it at the same time the passing out beyond finitude, infinity. The ought contains what in further development presents itself as the progress into infinity, in connection with which the nature of the imperfect identity contained in it will be considered more closely.
