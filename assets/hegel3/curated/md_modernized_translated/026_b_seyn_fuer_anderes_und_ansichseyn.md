## b) Being-for-other and Being-in-itself

1. ***Being-for-other*** makes up the genuine determination of existence. Existence as such is something immediate, something without reference; or it stands in the determination of being. But existence, as enclosing non-being {{{ 52 }}} within itself, is essentially ***determinate*** being, negated being, an other, — but because it at the same time also maintains itself in its negation, only ***being-for-other***.

2. As pure being-for-other, existence is properly only in passage over into otherness. Yet it also maintains itself in its non-existence, and is being. It is, however, not merely being in general, but being set in opposition to its non-existence; a being taken as reference to self against its reference to an other, as self-equality against its own inequality. A being of this sort is ***being-in-itself***.

3. Being-for-other and being-in-itself make up the ***two moments*** of existence. There are ***two pairs*** of determinations that come up here: 1) ***existence*** and ***an other; 2) being-for-other, and being-in-itself***. The first pair holds the determination that is indifferent and without reference; existence and an other drift apart from one another. Their truth, however, is the reference between them; being-for-other, then, and being-in-itself are those same determinations as moments; as determinations that are references, and that abide within their unity, within that unity which existence is; or each of them holds on itself, along with itself, the moment that is distinct from it.

It was recalled above that being and nothing are no longer being and nothing once they stand in the unity that is existence, — for that they only are outside their unity; so too being and nothing, taken in their restless unity, in becoming, amount to coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be. — Being within existence is ***being-in-itself***. For being is the reference to self, the self-equality, which now, however, {{{ 53 }}} is no longer immediate; rather it is reference to self only as the non-being of non-existence; (as reflected existence). — Just so, non-being as a moment of existence, within this unity of being with non-being, is not non-existence in general, but immediately an other, and more determinately, a reference toward non-existence, or being-for-other.

Accordingly ***being-in-itself*** is in the first place a negative reference toward non-existence; otherness lies outside it, and it stands opposed to that otherness; so far as something is ***in itself***, otherness and being-for-other have been taken away from it. But in the second place it carries non-being on its own self as well; for it itself ***is the non-being*** of being-for-other.

***Being-for-other***, though, is in the first place the negation of being, within existence; so far as something has its being in an other or for an other, a being of its own is what it lacks. But in the second place it is not the non-existence that would be pure nothing; it is a non-existence pointing over to being-in-itself, just as, the other way round, being-in-itself does the pointing over to being-for-other.
