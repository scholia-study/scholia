## 3. Alteration

Determinateness is quality, reflected determinateness, insofar as it has the two ***sides,*** that of determination and that of constitution.

The latter is determinateness insofar as it is otherness in its own self. The limit, as the being of external determinations, makes up constitution; but it is determinateness itself that is this limit, and the externality is therefore ***its own*** externality ***of its own self***. Since, then, something in its determinateness is in its own self its non-being, or since its determinateness is just as much its other as it is its own, a becoming is here posited which is ***alteration***.

{{{ 70 }}} Alteration lies necessarily already in existence itself; existence is the unity of being and nothing, it is in itself becoming. But it is becoming that has become an immediate unity. Insofar as it develops itself again into becoming, it is not the abstract moments of being and nothing, into which it falls apart, that make up what passes over, but the moments as proceeding out of existence, out of the unity of being and nothing, as such as are themselves these unities, these moments being the being-within-itself of the something, and the other; — not as moments of external reflection, like being-in-itself and being for other — but as immanent moments of existence itself. In determination, otherness, which at first is as limit, is taken back into simple determinateness, or determination is itself the simple unity of both moments. But constitution is the reference of these moments as being other to one another, or as distinguished, and their reference in one and the same respect; hence their sublating in their own selves.
