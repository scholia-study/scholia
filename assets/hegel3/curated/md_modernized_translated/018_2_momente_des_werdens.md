## 2. Moments of Becoming

Becoming is the unity of being and nothing; not a unity that abstracts away from the two; rather, being the unity of being and nothing, it is this ***determinate*** unity, or the one in which both being and nothing ***is***. But inasmuch as being and nothing, each of them, is in the unity with its other, ***it is not***. They ***are***, then, in this unity, yet as vanishing ones, only as ***sublated***.

They ***are*** as not ***being***; or are ***moments***. — To representation they present themselves at first as such that each of them, for itself, separated from the other, is self-subsistent, and they are being and nothing only in this separation. But since both are the same, they sink down from ***self-subsistence*** to ***moments***, in that they are at first still regarded in general as distinguished but at the same time as sublated.

Inasmuch as being and nothing ***are*** in one, they are distinguished therein; but in such a way that at the same time ***each***, in its differentiatedness, is ***unity with the other***. Becoming therefore contains two such unities; each is unity of being and of nothing; but the one is being as reference to nothing, the other nothing as reference to being: the two determinations are of unequal worth in these unities.

{{{ 44 }}} Becoming is in this way in a doubled determination; as beginning from nothing, which refers itself to being, that is, passes over into it, or from being, which passes over into nothing, — ***coming-to-be*** and ***ceasing-to-be***.

But these directions, differentiated in this way, permeate one another and render one another inert. The first is ***ceasing-to-be***; being makes the transition into nothing, yet nothing is quite as much the reverse of its own self and is rather the transition into being, or coming-to-be. This coming-to-be constitutes the second direction; nothing makes the transition into being, yet being quite as much sublates its own self and is rather the transition into nothing, or ceasing-to-be.

Coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be are therefore not a diverse becoming, but immediately one and the same: nor do they sublate each other reciprocally, the one externally sublating the other; rather each sublates itself in itself, and is in it itself the opposite of itself.
