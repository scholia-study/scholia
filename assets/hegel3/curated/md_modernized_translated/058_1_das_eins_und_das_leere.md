## 1. The One and the Void

The one is the simple reference of being-for-itself to itself which, since its moments have collapsed into one another, has the form of ***immediacy***. It therefore simply ***is***, without having an existence; determinate being or existence has, in being-for-itself, returned to pure being.

Because the one has no existence and no determinateness as reference to an other, it is also capable of no constitution and hence of no otherness; it is ***unalterable***.

It is indeterminate, but not in the way being is; rather its indeterminateness is the determinateness which is reference to itself, ***absolute being-determined***. — Absolute being-determined is determinateness, or negation, as reference not to an other but to itself. This equality of the one with itself it has, then, only insofar as it is negating, a direction away from itself, out towards an other, a direction that is however immediately sublated, turned back, because there is no other for it to go to, and that has returned into itself.

Because, for the sake of the simplicity of this ***being***-returned-into-itself, the one has the shape of something immediate, {{{ 102 }}} of ***something that is***, its sublating, or the negation, appears as an other ***that is outside it***, an other which is not a something but the nothing, which itself has the shape of immediacy over against that which is, yet which ***in itself*** is at the same time not the first nothing, is not immediate, but the ***nothing as sublated something*** — or it is the nothing as the ***void***.

The void is therefore in truth not immediate, not indifferent on its own account over against the one; rather it is the one's referring-itself-to-an-other, or its limit. But the one itself, as absolute being-determined, is the pure limit, the pure negation or void. In comporting itself towards the void, therefore, it is the infinite reference to itself. It is itself, however, the pure negation as immediately equal to itself, as ***something that is***; the void, by contrast, is the same negation as ***non-being***.

Being-for-itself, having in this way determined itself as the one and the void, has again attained an existence. But just as something and an other have, so to speak, being for their soil, upon which their determinateness is posited, so the one and the void have the nothing for their common, or rather simple, soil. Being-for-itself has the difference at first within its own self, and the differentiated terms as its moments, being-for-itself as being-in-itself, and being-for-one, whose unity is ideality. They step out of this unity, or become the mutually external terms, the one and the void, in that through the simple unity of the moments themselves the determination of being comes in, whereby what was previously a moment receives the shape of something that is. — Or {{{ 103 }}} there are two moments, simple being-for-itself and being-for-one; each considered on its own, and each also is on its own in this way, for each is also the whole, collapses in the simple reference to itself into immediacy, and with this into existence over against the other, into a reference between such terms as are not only referred but also ***immediate***.
