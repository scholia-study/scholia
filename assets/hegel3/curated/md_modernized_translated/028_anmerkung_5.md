## Remark

***Reality*** can look like a word of many meanings, because it gets used of very diverse, indeed opposed determinations. Where one says of thoughts, of concepts, of theories, ***they have no reality***, the sense here is that no external existence, no ***actuality***, accrues to them; ***in itself*** or in its concept the idea of a Platonic republic, for instance, may well be true. — Conversely, when for instance only the shine of wealth is present in lavish expenditure, it is likewise said that ***reality is lacking***, and what is understood is that such expenditure is only an external existence with no inner ground. Of certain occupations it is said that they are no ***real*** occupations, namely none that have worth in themselves; — or of grounds, that they are not real, insofar as they are not drawn from the essence of the matter.

The one time, then, what is understood by reality is ***external existence***, the other time ***being-in-itself***. Yet this is not a differing or an opposed meaning of reality, but rather only One meaning, because reality essentially encloses both of those determinations within itself. If therefore ***only*** being-in-itself, or ***only*** being-for-other is present, {{{ 55 }}} then reality is missed for the reason that each of these determinations taken by itself is one-sided, whereas reality is the totality that calls for both.

The ***in-itself*** too carries, in part, this double meaning. Something is in-itself insofar as it has gone back into itself out of being-for-other. But something also ***has*** a determination or circumstance ***in itself*** (here the accent falls on ***in***) or ***on it***, insofar as this circumstance is external on it, a being-for-other.

These two are united in existence, or in reality. Existence is in itself just as much as it has something on it, or is being-for-other. But that existence has ***that, which*** it is in itself, also ***on it***, and, the other way round, that it also is in itself ***what*** it counts for as being-for-other, — this bears on the identity of being-in-itself with being-for-other, chiefly with respect to a ***content***, and it comes out formally in part already within the sphere of existence, insofar as ***determination*** passes over into ***constitution***, but more expressly where essence gets considered, and the relation between ***inwardness*** and ***outwardness***, and then most determinately where the idea gets considered, as the unity of concept and actuality.

Here, though, the sense of the ***thing-in-itself*** already shows itself provisionally as well, which is a very simple abstraction, yet for a while was a very important determination, just as the proposition that what things are in themselves is unknown to us passed for a wisdom of wide currency. — Things are called ***in-themselves*** insofar as abstraction is made from all being-for-other, that is, generally speaking, insofar as they get thought without any determination whatever, as {{{ 56 }}} nothing. Taken in this sense one certainly cannot know ***what*** a thing in-itself may be. For the question: ***what***? calls for determinations to be stated; yet since what is at issue is meant all the while to be ***things-in-themselves***, that is, precisely without determination, the impossibility of answering has been thoughtlessly built into the question, or else one produces a self-contradicting answer. The thing-in-itself amounts to the same as that absolute about which nothing is known save that all is one within it. But what the thing-in-itself is in truth, or rather what is in itself at all, of that the Logic is itself the exposition. If it is asked of a determinate thing what it may be in itself, the simple logical answer runs that it is ***in itself*** what it is in its concept.

The former metaphysical ***concept of God***, which was chiefly laid at the ground of the ontological proof, so called, for God's existence, can be mentioned here. God, that is, received the determination ***the sum total of all realities***, and this sum total was said to harbour no contradiction within itself, no one of the realities sublating another; for reality is supposed to count only as a perfection, as a positive that harbours no negation. Hence the realities are said to stand in no opposition and not to contradict one another.

With this concept of reality it is therefore assumed that reality still remains even when all negation, and with it all determinateness of reality, has been sublated. Yet reality is existence in general; it holds non-being as being-for-other, and more closely the ***limit*** or ***determinateness***. Reality that is meant to be taken in the so-called ***eminent sense***, or as ***infi*** {{{ 57 }}} ***nite***, — in the ordinary meaning of the word, — is widened out into what is devoid of determination, and forfeits its meaning. The goodness of God is said to be goodness not in the ordinary sense but in the eminent one, not distinct from justice but ***tempered*** by it, just as conversely justice by goodness; but so taken goodness is goodness no longer, nor is justice still justice. — Power is to be tempered by wisdom, but so taken it is not absolute power; — wisdom is to be widened out into power, but so taken it vanishes as the wisdom that determines purpose and measure. Later on the true concept of the infinite will come out, just as the ***absolute unity*** will determine itself ever more closely, a unity that does not consist in a ***tempering***, a ***reciprocal limiting***, or a blending, seeing that such is a thoroughly superficial reference, kept in indeterminate fog, one with which only conceptless representation can be satisfied. — Reality, as it is taken in that definition of God as a determinate quality, once carried out beyond its determinateness ceases to be reality; it becomes the one-sided in-itself, which is empty; and God as the purely real in everything real, or as the ***sum total*** of all realities, is that same thing devoid of determination and of content that the empty absolute mentioned earlier is, in which everything is One.
