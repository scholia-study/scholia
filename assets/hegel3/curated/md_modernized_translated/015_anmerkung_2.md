## Remark 2

Parmenides held fast to ***being***, and said of nothing that it is not at all; only being is. That through which this pure being was led onward to becoming was the reflection that it is equal to nothing. Being itself is the indeterminate; it therefore has no reference to anything else; it seems accordingly that ***from this beginning*** no further ***advance*** can be made, namely out of the beginning itself, without something alien being attached to it from outside. The reflection that being is equal to nothing thus appears as a second, absolute beginning. On the other side, being would not be the absolute beginning if it had a determinateness, for then it would depend on an other and would in truth not be a beginning. But if it is indeterminate and hence a true beginning, then it also has nothing by which it might carry itself over into an other; it is thereby at the same time the ***end***.

That reflection, that being is not equal to itself but rather is utterly unequal to itself, is, considered from the latter side, indeed to that extent a second, new beginning, but at the same time an ***other*** beginning, by which the ***first is sublated***. This is, as was already recalled above, the true meaning of advancing in general. In philosophy the advance from that which is a beginning is at the same time the ***return*** to its source, to its genuine beginning. Thus in going out beyond the beginning there at the same time begins {{{ 34 }}} a new beginning, and the first thereby shows itself as not the genuine one. This side, then, that the reflection which sets being equal to nothing is a new beginning, is conceded, and it is, as is clear, itself necessary. But conversely this new beginning is, just as much as the first, not an absolute one; for it refers to the first. For this reason, however, it must lie in the first itself that an other refers to it; it must therefore be something determinate. — But it is the immediate, that which is still utterly indeterminate. Yet precisely this ***indeterminateness*** is what constitutes its determinateness, for indeterminateness is opposed to determinateness; as something opposed it is thus itself the determinate, or the negative, and indeed pure negativity. This indeterminateness or negativity which being has in it itself is what the reflection expresses when it sets being equal to nothing. — Or one may put it thus: because being is that which is without determination, it is not the determination which it is, hence not being but nothing.

***In itself***, then, that is, in essential reflection, the transition is not immediate; but it is still concealed. Here only its immediacy is present; because being is posited only as immediate, nothing breaks out immediately in it. — A more determinate mediation is the one from which science itself, and its beginning, pure being, has its existence. Knowledge has attained the element of pure thinking by having sublated within itself all the manifoldness of the manifoldly determined consciousness. The whole sphere of knowledge therefore contains as its essential moment absolute ***abstraction*** and ***negativity***; being, its beginning, is this pure {{{ 35 }}} abstraction itself, or is essentially only as absolute nothing.

This recollection, however, lies behind science, which within itself, namely from essence onward, will exhibit that one-sided immediacy of being as something mediated.

But insofar as that breaking out of nothing, and the consideration of being as to what it is ***in itself***, are spurned, nothing is present but ***pure being***. One holds fast to it as it is beginning and at the same time end, and in its immediate immediacy refuses the reflection that leads it out beyond itself, namely that it is the indeterminate, the empty. Into this pure immediacy nothing seems able to break in.

Since this assertion of reflectionless being holds fast to the merely immediate, to that as which being is posited or as which it is present, one must likewise keep to it and see how this being then is present. Now because being is nothing, this must exhibit itself in its immediacy.

Let us take the assertion of pure being

α) in the form in which it has stepped forth furthest out of supposing, as the proposition: ***Being is the absolute***; then something is asserted of being that is distinguished from it. What is distinguished from it is an other than it; but the other contains the nothing of that whose other it is. What is therefore present in this proposition is not pure being, but being just as much in reference to its nothing. — The absolute is distinguished from it; {{{ 36 }}} but in saying that it is the absolute, it is also said that the two are not distinguished. Thus what is present is not pure being but the movement which is becoming.

β) If now pure being means just as much as the absolute, or if it means only one side or part of the absolute and only this side is held fast, then their difference is dropped, the difference which just now clouded the purity of being, and the diversity vanishes as one merely of the word or as a connection with a useless part.

The proposition now runs: ***Being is being***. — Of this identity, which will be spoken of below, this much is immediately clear, that like every tautology it says ***nothing***. What is present, then, is a saying that is a not-saying; here, accordingly, the same movement, becoming, is present, only that in place of being it is a saying that runs through it.

γ) With the tautological predicate dropped, there remains the proposition: Being ***is***. Here again ***being*** itself and the ***being*** of it are distinguished; through the ***is*** something further and hence other than being is meant to be said. But if through the ***is*** no otherness, and hence no nothing of pure being, is posited, then this ***is*** is likewise to be dropped as useless, and one is to say only: pure being.

δ) ***Pure being***, or rather only ***being***; propositionless, without assertion or predicate. Or the assertion has gone back into supposing. ***Being*** is by now only an exclamation which has its meaning solely in the subject. The deeper and richer this inner intuition {{{ 37 }}} is, if it is to comprise within itself the holy, the eternal, God and so forth, — the more does this inner thing stand off from that as which it is there, from the enunciated empty being which against that content is nothing; it has, in its meaning and its existence, the difference from itself.

Considered from the other side, this being without reference to meaning, as it immediately is and is to be immediately taken, belongs to a subject; it is something enunciated, has […] empirical existence in general, and thereby belongs to the ground of restrictions and of the negative. — Common sense, when it bristles against the unity of being and nothing and at the same time appeals to what is immediately present, will in this very experience find nothing but determinate being, being with a restriction or negation, — that very unity which it rejects. Thus the assertion of immediate being reduces itself to an empirical concrete existence whose ***exhibiting*** it cannot reject, since it is the reflectionless immediacy to which it means to hold.

The same is the case with ***nothing***, only in the opposite way; taken in its immediacy it shows itself as ***that which is***; for by its own nature it is the very same as being. The nothing is thought, is represented; it is spoken of; therefore it ***is***. The nothing has its being in thinking, in representing and so forth. This being, however, is distinguished from it; it is therefore said that nothing indeed is in thinking, in representing, but that on that account it is not the case that ***it is***, that only thinking or representing is this being. In this distinguishing, however, it is just as little to be denied that nothing stands in reference to a being; but in {{{ 38 }}} the reference, even though it also contains the difference, a unity of nothing with being is present.

Pure nothing is not yet the negative, the determination of reflection over against the positive; nor yet the restriction; in these determinations it immediately has the meaning of a reference to its other. Rather, nothing is here the pure absence of being, the nihil Privativum, as darkness is the absence of light. If it has now turned out that nothing is the same as being, the counter-claim is held fast that nothing has no being of its own, that it is, as was said, only absence of being, as darkness is only absence of light — a meaning which it has only in the reference to the eye, in comparison with the positive, with light. — But all this means nothing other than that the abstraction of nothing is nothing in and for itself, but only in reference to being, or, what is the same, what has turned out, that its truth is only its unity with being, — that darkness is only something in reference to light, just as conversely being is only something in reference to nothing. Even if the reference is taken superficially and externally, and one stops within it chiefly at the differentiatedness, still the unity of what is referred is essentially contained in it as a moment, and in the fact that each is only ***something*** in the reference to its other, precisely the transition of being and of nothing into ***existence*** is expressed.
