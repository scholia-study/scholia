## With What Must the Beginning of Science Be Made?

From the Phenomenology of Spirit, or the science of consciousness as spirit in its appearing, it is presupposed that ***pure knowledge*** issues as consciousness's last, absolute truth. ***Logic*** is ***pure science***, pure knowledge in its whole compass and its spread. Pure knowledge is certainty that has become truth, or the certainty that no longer stands over against the object but has made it inward, knows it as its own self, and that on the other side has likewise surrendered the knowing of itself as of something standing over against the objective and being merely the annihilation thereof, has divested itself, and is unity with its own divestment.

Pure knowledge, having collapsed together into this unity, has sublated every reference to an other and mediation as well, and ***is simple immediacy***.

{{{ 7 }}} Simple immediacy is itself an expression of reflection, and refers to the difference from what is mediated. In its true expression this simple immediacy is ***pure being***, or ***being*** at large; ***being***, nothing else, without any further determination and filling.

This backward glance at the concept of pure knowledge is the ***ground*** from which ***being*** comes forth in order to constitute the ***beginning*** of absolute science.

Or secondly, conversely, the ***beginning of absolute science*** must itself be an ***absolute beginning***; it may ***presuppose nothing***. It must therefore be mediated by nothing, nor have a ground; rather it is itself to be the ground of the whole science. It must accordingly be simply an immediate, or rather the immediate itself. Just as it can have no determination over against anything else, so too it can contain none within itself, no content, for anything of that kind would likewise be a distinguishing, and a reference of diverse terms to one another, hence a mediation. The beginning, then, is ***pure being***.

Chiefly in more recent times has it been regarded as a difficulty to find a beginning in philosophy, and the ground of this difficulty, along with the possibility of resolving it, has been discussed in manifold ways. Philosophy's beginning has to be a mediated one or else an immediate one, and there is little trouble in showing that neither of these is open to it; with that, whichever of the two manners of beginning one adopts runs into its refutation.

{{{ 8 }}} In the first presentation just given of being as the beginning, the concept of knowledge is presupposed. This beginning is thus not absolute, but comes out of the preceding movement of consciousness. The science of this movement, from which knowledge results, would now have to have the absolute beginning. It makes that beginning with ***immediate consciousness***, with the knowledge that something ***is***. — Being thus makes the beginning here as well, but as the determination of a concrete shape, of consciousness; only pure knowledge, the spirit that has freed itself from its appearance as consciousness, has free, pure being for its beginning too. — But that beginning, immediate consciousness, contains the I as referred to a sheer other, and conversely the object as referred to the I; hence a mediation. — Consciousness does indeed contain the two mediating terms — which are in turn the mediated ones — itself, points therefore not beyond itself, and is enclosed within itself. But since the mediation is reciprocal, each mediating term is also mediated, so that no genuine immediacy is at hand. — But conversely, were such an immediacy at hand, then, not being grounded, it is something arbitrary and contingent.

The insight that what is absolutely true has to be a result, and conversely that any result has for its presupposition a first truth which, however, precisely as first, is on the objective view not necessary and on the subjective side not cognized, — has in more recent times brought forth the thought that philosophy can begin only with a hypothetical and problematic truth, and that philosophizing can therefore at first be nothing but a searching.

{{{ 9 }}} On this view, striding forward in philosophy is rather a going backwards and a grounding, through which alone it emerges that what was begun with is not merely something arbitrarily assumed but is in fact partly the true, partly the first truth.

One must concede that it is an essential consideration — one that will emerge more closely within logic itself — that going forward is a ***return*** into the ***ground*** and to the ***original***, upon which what the beginning was made with depends. — Consciousness is in this way led back, on its road from the immediacy with which it starts, to absolute knowledge as its truth. This last term, the ground, is then also that out of which the first proceeds, the first that came on the scene at the outset as an immediate. — In this way, too, spirit will at the ***end*** of the development of pure knowledge divest itself with freedom and release itself into the shape of an ***immediate*** consciousness, as the consciousness of a being that stands over against it as an other. What is essential is properly not that a purely immediate be the beginning, but that the whole is a circuit within itself, wherein the first also becomes the last and the last also the first.

Hence it is on the other side just as necessary to regard that into which the movement goes back, as into its ***ground***, as a ***result***. In this respect the first is quite as much the ground, and the last is something derived. For since one sets out from the first and arrives by correct inferences at the last, as at the ground, this last is in fact a result. The advance {{{ 10 }}} from what makes the beginning is furthermore only a ***further determination*** of it, so that this remains lying at the base of all that follows and does not vanish out of it. The advancing does not consist in an ***other*** being derived, or in a transition being made into a genuine other; — and insofar as such passing over does occur, it just as much sublates itself again. Thus the beginning of philosophy is the foundation present and preserving itself throughout all subsequent developments, the concept altogether immanent in its further determinations.

Through this advance, in which the beginning determines itself further, it loses whatever one-sidedness it has in the determinateness of being an immediate, becomes something mediated, and by that very fact turns the line of scientific forward movement into a circle. — At the same time, what makes the beginning, being therein still the undeveloped and contentless, is not yet truly cognized, for such is it at the beginning, that is, still before the science; only the science, and indeed in its entire development, is the completed, content-filled and first truly grounded cognition of it.

But just because the result also constitutes the absolute ground, the progression of this cognition is not something provisional, nor something problematic and hypothetical; it is determined rather by the nature of the matter and of the content itself. Nor is that beginning something arbitrary and merely assumed for the time being, nor something that appears arbitrary and is presupposed as a request, but of which it nevertheless showed in the sequel that one had done right in making it the beginning; — as with geometrical constructions it admittedly emerges only afterwards in the proofs, {{{ 11 }}} that one did well to draw just these lines, or even in the proofs themselves, that it was good to begin with the comparison of these lines or angles; taken for itself, in this drawing of lines or comparing, it is not comprehended.

Thus the ***ground*** why in pure science the start is made from pure being has been stated above immediately on that science itself. This pure being is the unity into which pure knowledge goes back, or it is also the content of that knowledge. This is the side on which this ***pure being***, this absolutely immediate, is just as much something absolutely mediated. But it is just as essentially the purely immediate; only as such is it therefore to be taken, ***precisely because*** it is the beginning; insofar as it were not this pure indeterminateness, insofar as it were further determined, it would be taken as something mediated. It lies in the ***nature of the beginning itself*** that it be being, and nothing else. No other preparations are therefore needed in order to get into philosophy, nor further reflections and points of attachment.

From the fact that the beginning is the beginning of philosophy, no ***closer determination*** or ***positive content*** can now be drawn for it. For philosophy is here at the beginning, where the matter itself is not yet at hand, an empty word, or some assumed, unjustified representation. Pure knowledge gives only this negative determination, that the beginning is to be the ***abstract***, or ***absolute***, beginning. Insofar as pure being is taken as the ***content*** of pure knowledge, this knowledge has to step back from its content, to let it have its own way and not determine it further. — Or since {{{ 12 }}} pure being must be regarded as the unity into which knowledge, at its highest peak of union with the object, has collapsed, knowledge has vanished into this unity and has left over no difference from it and hence no determination for it.

Nor is there otherwise any something, or any content, at hand that could be used to make the more determinate beginning with. There is nothing at hand but ***pure being as beginning***. In this determination, ***as beginning***, pure immediacy is something ***more concrete***, and what is immediately contained in it can be developed analytically, in order to see where this leads further.

In general the determination ***of being***, hitherto assumed as the beginning, can also be left out entirely; all that is demanded is that a pure beginning be made; nothing is thus at hand but the ***beginning*** itself, and it remains to see what it is.

As yet there is nothing, and something is to become. The beginning is not the pure nothing, but a nothing from which something is to proceed; being too is at the same time already contained in it. Both are held in the beginning, then, being and nothing; the beginning is their unity; — or it is non-being which is at once being, and being which is at once non-being.

Being and nothing are present in the beginning as ***distinguished***; for the beginning gestures beyond itself to something else; — it is a non-being referred to being as to an other; whatever begins ***is*** not yet; it is only making its way toward being. At the same time the {{{ 13 }}} beginning holds being within it, yet as a being that withdraws from non-being, or sublates non-being as its opposite.

Further, however, what begins already ***is***, yet just as much it also ***is*** ***not*** yet. Being and non-being are therefore in it in immediate union; or the beginning is their ***undifferentiated unity***.

Analysing the beginning would accordingly furnish the concept of a unity of being with non-being, — or in more reflected form, of a unity of being-distinguished with being-not-distinguished, — or of the identity of identity and non-identity. One might take this concept for the first and purest definition of the absolute; — as indeed it would be, if the form of definitions and the name of the absolute were at issue at all. On this reckoning, with that abstract concept as the first, every subsequent determination and development would amount to nothing but a richer, more determinate definition of the absolute.

Yet this analysis of the beginning presupposes the beginning as ***familiar***; it has our representation of it for its basis. This is an instance of how other sciences proceed. They presuppose their subject matter as familiar, and assume in doing so, as a request, that everyone may find in his representation of it roughly the same determinations that they, by analysis, comparison and other reasoning, gather up from here and there and state about it. What makes the absolute beginning must indeed be something familiar; but if it is something concrete, hence determined within itself in manifold ways, then, in presupposing these its references as something familiar, I give them out as something immediate {{{ 14 }}} which they are not. With them, accordingly, the contingency and arbitrariness of analysis and of the various ways of determining sets in. Because the reference has once been conceded as something immediately given, everyone has the right to bring in or leave out the determinations according to what he ***finds*** in his immediate, contingent representation.

But insofar as the subject matter, as analysis presupposes it, is something concrete, a synthetic unity, the reference contained in it is a ***necessary*** one only insofar as it is not found ready to hand but is brought forth by the moments' own movement of going back into this unity; — a movement that is the opposite of the one mentioned, which is an analytic procedure, and a doing external to the matter itself and falling within the subject.

From this there emerges more closely what was remarked earlier, that what the beginning is to be made with cannot be something concrete, cannot be such as contains a reference within its own self, for such a thing presupposes a movement, a mediating and a crossing over from one term to another within itself, of which the concrete that has become simple would be the result. But the beginning is not to be a result. What makes the beginning, the beginning itself, is therefore to be taken as something non-analysable, in its simple, unfilled immediacy, hence as being, as the wholly empty.

If someone, impatient perhaps with the consideration of the abstract beginning, wanted to say that one should begin not with the beginning but with the matter, then this matter is nothing but that empty being; {{{ 15 }}} for what the matter may be is precisely what is to emerge only in the course of the science, what cannot be presupposed as familiar before it.

Whatever other form may be adopted so as to have a beginning other than empty being, such a beginning suffers from the defects adduced. Insofar as one reflects that from the first truth everything that follows must be derived, that the first truth must be the ground of the whole, the demand seems necessary to make the beginning with God, with the absolute, and to comprehend everything out of him. If, instead of laying the representation at the base in the usual way and sending ahead a definition of the absolute conforming to it, — which was spoken of earlier, — the closer determination of this absolute is on the contrary taken from immediate self-consciousness, if it is determined as I, then this is indeed partly an immediate, partly something familiar in a far higher sense than any other representation; for something otherwise familiar does belong to the I, but since it is only a representation, it is still a content distinguished from the I; the I, by contrast, is the simple certainty of itself. But this certainty is at the same time something concrete, or rather the I is what is most concrete; it is the consciousness of itself as an infinitely manifold world. For the I to be the beginning and ground of philosophy, however, the setting apart of the concrete is required, — the absolute act by which the I is purified of itself and enters as absolute I into its consciousness. But this pure I is then not the familiar, ordinary I of our consciousness, to which the science was to be attached immediately and for everyone. That act would properly be nothing other than the elevation to the standpoint of pure knowledge, on which {{{ 16 }}} the difference of the subjective and the objective has precisely vanished. But since this elevation is demanded thus ***immediately***, it is a subjective postulate; to prove itself a genuine demand, the advance of the concrete I, or of immediate consciousness, to pure knowledge would have to have been exhibited and set forth on that I, out of a necessity belonging to it. Without such objective movement pure knowledge, ***the intellectual intuition***, shows up as a standpoint adopted at will, or indeed as merely one among the empirical ***states*** of consciousness, where all hangs on whether the one person ***finds*** it in himself or can produce it, while another cannot. But insofar as this pure I must be essential pure knowledge, while pure knowledge is posited in individual consciousness only through the absolute act of self-elevation and is not immediately at hand in it, precisely that advantage is lost which is supposed to spring from this beginning of philosophy; namely that it is something simply familiar, which everyone finds immediately within himself and to which further reflection could attach itself; that pure I is rather, in its absolute essentiality, something unknown to ordinary consciousness, something that consciousness does not find within itself. There enters accordingly the deception rather that the talk is supposed to be of something familiar, of the I of empirical self-consciousness, whereas in fact the talk is of something remote from this consciousness. The determination of pure knowledge as I carries with it the continuing recollection of the subjective I, whose restrictions are supposed to be forgotten, and keeps present the representation that the propositions and relations which emerge in the further development from the I occur in ordinary consciousness as something at hand within it — since it is surely that {{{ 17 }}} of which they are asserted — and can be found within it. This confusion produces, instead of immediate clarity, only an all the more glaring bewilderment and complete disorientation.

Pure knowledge takes from the I its restricted meaning of having its insuperable opposite in an object; for this reason it would be at the very least ***superfluous*** to retain this subjective posture and the determination of pure essence as I any further. But this determination not only carries that disturbing ambiguity with it; considered more closely, it also remains an I that is still subjective. The actual development of the science that sets out from the I shows that within it the object has and keeps the perennial determination of an other for the I, and hence that the I one sets out from is not pure knowledge, which has truly overcome the opposition of consciousness, and is thus still within appearance and not the element of being-in-and-for-itself.

But even if the I were in fact pure knowledge, or if intellectual intuition were in fact the beginning, what matters in science is not what is ***inwardly*** at hand, but the ***existence of the inward within knowledge***. But whatever of intellectual intuition — or, should its object be called the eternal, the divine, the absolute, — whatever of the eternal or the absolute is there at the beginning of the science, this is nothing other than a first, immediate, simple determination. Whatever richer name be set over against it than mere being expresses, the only thing that can come into consideration is how it enters into knowledge and into the uttering of knowledge. Intellectual intuition is itself the forcible rejection of {{{ 18 }}} mediating and of demonstrative, external reflection; but whatever it utters over and above simple immediacy is something concrete, something containing diverse determinations within itself. The uttering and the exposition of such a concrete, however, is a mediating movement that begins from ***one*** of the determinations and advances to the other, even if it goes back again to the first; — it is a movement that may at the same time not be arbitrary or assertoric. Hence what is begun from in this exposition is not the concrete itself, but only a simple immediate out of which the movement proceeds.

If then in the expression of the absolute or the eternal or God, if in the intuition or thought of these there ***lies more*** than in pure being, then what lies therein is to step forth into knowledge; let what lies therein be as rich as it may, still the determination that steps ***first*** into knowledge is something simple; for only in the simple is there no more than the pure beginning; or that determination is only the immediate, for only in the immediate is there not yet a having-advanced from one term to another, hence likewise no more than the beginning. Whatever the richer forms of the absolute or of God are meant to utter or to hold over and above being amounts, at the beginning, to a mere empty word and to being alone; and this simple thing, which carries no meaning beyond that, this emptiness, is thus where philosophy absolutely begins.

So simple is this insight in itself that the beginning, as was recalled, needs neither preparation nor further introduction; and these preliminary ruminations upon it could have had no aim of leading up to it, but rather of clearing preliminariness of every kind out of the way.
