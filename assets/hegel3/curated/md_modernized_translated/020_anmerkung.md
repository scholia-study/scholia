## Remark

***Sublating*** and the ***sublated*** is one of the most important concepts of philosophy, a fundamental determination which recurs absolutely everywhere, whose sense is to be grasped determinately and distinguished above all from nothing. — What sublates itself does not thereby become nothing. Nothing is the ***immediate***; a sublated thing, by contrast, is something ***mediated***, it is that which is not, but as a ***result*** which has proceeded out of a being. It therefore has the ***determination from which it derives still in itself***.

{{{ 46 }}} ***To sublate*** has in the language the doubled sense that it means as much as to store up, to ***preserve***, and as much as to make cease, to ***put an end to***. Storing up already carries the negative in it: a thing gets taken out of its immediacy, and so out of an existence lying open to outward influence, for the sake of preserving it. — What is sublated is accordingly a thing stored up as well, one that has forfeited nothing but its immediacy, and has not on that account vanished.

Take the sublated more precisely: a thing counts here as sublated only to the extent that it has stepped into the unity with what stands opposed to it; under this narrower determination it is something reflected, and the fitting name for it is ***moment***. — As the observation will force itself upon us still more often, that the philosophical technical language uses Latin expressions for reflected determinations.

What narrower sense and expression accrue to being and to nothing, now that they have become ***moments***, must emerge more narrowly when existence is considered, as the unity wherein the two are stored up. Only in being differentiated from one another is being being and nothing nothing; in their truth, though — in their unity — the two have vanished qua these determinations, and are by now something else. Being and nothing are the same; on the very ground of that sameness they no longer count as being and nothing, and carry a diverse determination; within becoming they stood as coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be; within existence, a unity otherwise determined, they are again moments otherwise determined.
