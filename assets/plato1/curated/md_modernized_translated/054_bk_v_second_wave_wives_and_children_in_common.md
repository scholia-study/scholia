## Second Wave: Wives and Children in Common

Indeed, he said, that is no small wave you are escaping.

You will say it is not a big one, I said, once you see what comes next.

Say it, then, and let me see, he said.

Following on this, I said, and on the earlier ones too, there is, I believe, this law.

What law?

These women, all of them, are to belong {{{ 457d }}} in common to all these men, and none is to live privately with any one of them; and the children, in turn, are to be held in common as well, with no parent to know which child is his own, and no child its parent.

This, he said, is far more incredible than the last — both as to whether it is possible and as to whether it is beneficial.

I do not think, I said, that there would be dispute, at least about the benefit — that it is not the greatest good for the women to be held in common and the children in common, if indeed that is possible — but I think the greatest dispute would be about whether it is possible or not. {{{ 457e }}}

About both, he said, there would certainly be plenty of dispute.

You are building up quite a case against me, I said — and I had hoped to escape at least one part of it, thinking that if it seemed beneficial to you, all that would remain for me was the question of whether it is possible or not.

But you did not get away with running off, he said — give an account of both.

I must submit to trial, I said. Grant me this much, though {{{ 458a }}}: let me have my holiday, the way idle people are in the habit of feasting their minds by themselves, when they walk along alone. For people like that, before working out how what they desire will come about, set that question aside, so as not to wear themselves out deliberating over what is possible and what is not, and, positing that what they want already exists, go on at once to arrange the rest, and delight in running through all they will do once it has happened, making an idle soul {{{ 458b }}} idler still. Well, I too am already growing soft, and I want to put off that question — in what way these things are possible — and look into it later; for now, if you will allow me, I shall assume they are possible and consider how the rulers will arrange them once they come about, and how, once carried out, they would be more advantageous than anything else for both the city and the guardians. I shall try to examine these matters with you first, and those others afterward, if indeed you allow it.

Well, I allow it, he said — go ahead and examine it.

I think, then, I said, that if the rulers are to be worthy {{{ 458c }}} of that name, and their auxiliaries likewise, the latter will be willing to do what is ordered, and the former will do the ordering — themselves obeying the laws in some matters, and imitating them in others, wherever we leave things to their own discretion.

Likely enough, he said.

You, then, as their lawgiver, I said, will select the women just as you selected the men, and hand them over matched as closely as possible in nature; and since they share houses and common meals, with no one possessing anything of that kind privately, {{{ 458d }}} they will be together, and being mingled together both in their exercises and in the rest of their upbringing, they will, I think, be driven by an innate necessity toward union with one another. Or do you not think what I am saying is a matter of necessity?

Not geometrical necessities, he said, but erotic ones, which are likely to be sharper than the others at persuading and drawing along the mass of people.

Very much so, I said. But after this, Glaucon, mating with one another in disorder, or doing anything else of that kind, is neither {{{ 458e }}} sanctioned in a city of happy people, nor will the rulers allow it.

No, for it is not just, he said.

Clearly, then, after this we will make the marriages sacred, as far as possible; and the most beneficial marriages would be the sacred ones.

Absolutely. {{{ 459a }}}

How, then, will they be most beneficial? Tell me this, Glaucon: I see in your house both hunting dogs and a good many pedigree gamecocks — have you, in Zeus's name, ever paid attention to their mating and breeding?

Paid attention to what? He asked.

In the first place, among these very animals, pedigreed as they are, are not some of them the best, and do they not turn out to be so?

There are.

Do you, then, breed from all of them alike, or are you eager to breed above all from the best?

From the best. {{{ 459b }}}

What else? From the youngest, or the oldest, or above all from those in their prime?

From those in their prime.

And if breeding is not done that way, do you think the stock of birds and of dogs will be much worse?

I do, he said.

And what do you think about horses, I said, and the other animals? Is it any different with them?

That would be strange, he said.

Good heavens, I said, my dear friend, how badly we need our rulers to be of the highest quality, if the same really holds for the human race too. {{{ 459c }}}

Well, it does hold, he said — but why do you say so?

Because they must, I said, make considerable use of drugs. And even a rather inferior doctor, I suppose, we think good enough for bodies that do not need drugs but are willing to submit to a regimen — but whenever it is necessary to administer drugs as well, we know that a bolder doctor is required.

True — but what are you getting at?

At this, I said: it seems our rulers will need to make considerable use of falsehood and deception for the benefit {{{ 459d }}} of those they rule. And we said, I believe, that all such things are useful in the guise of a drug.

And rightly so, he said.

It is in marriages and childbearing, then, that this right use seems to arise not least of all.

How so?

Well, I said, from what we have agreed, the best men must mate with the best women as often as possible, and the worst with the worst the opposite way; and the offspring of the former {{{ 459e }}} must be reared, and those of the latter not, if the flock is to be of the highest quality — and all this must happen without anyone noticing except the rulers themselves, if the herd of guardians is in turn to be as free from faction as possible.

Quite right, he said.
