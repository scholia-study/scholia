## A Life Ruled by Eros

For it does.

And indeed, the man who is mad and deranged both attempts to rule not only men but gods as well, and expects that he can.

Very much so, he said.

And a man becomes tyrannical in the strict sense, I said, my good sir, whenever, whether by nature or by habits or by both, he becomes given to drink, to erotic love, and to melancholy.

Yes, absolutely.

So it seems that such a man comes to be in this way — but how does he live? {{{ 573d }}}

That is what children say at their games, he said — you will tell me that yourself.

I will tell you, I said. I think that after this, festivals and revels and banquets and courtesans and everything of that kind arise among them — the sort of men in whom erotic love, dwelling within as a tyrant, steers everything in the soul.

It must be so, he said.

So then, do not many terrible desires sprout up beside them, every day and every night, and need a great deal?

Many indeed.

So whatever income there may be is quickly used up.

How could it not be? {{{ 573e }}}

And after this come borrowings, and inroads on his estate.

What else?

So whenever everything runs out, is it not inevitable that his desires — crowded together, violent, nesting within him — cry out, and that he, driven as though by goads, by his other desires and above all by erotic love itself, which leads all the rest like a bodyguard, is goaded into frenzy and looks about for whoever has anything that can be taken from him, either by deceit or {{{ 574a }}} by force?

Very much so, he said.

He must carry off plunder from every quarter, or be gripped by great throes and pains.

He must.

So then, just as the pleasures that arise later within him get the better of the older ones and take away what belongs to them, will he not likewise, being younger, think it right to have more than his father and mother, and to take from them, once he has spent his own share, allotted to him out of the family property?

But what else? So he said. {{{ 574b }}}

And if they do not allow it, will he not first attempt to steal from his parents and deceive them?

Certainly.

And when he is unable to, would he then snatch and use force?

I think so, he said.

So if the old man and the old woman hold their ground and fight, my good sir, will he be careful and hold back from doing anything a tyrant would do?

I am not at all confident, he said, about the parents of such a man.

But, Adeimantus, in the name of Zeus, do you think that such a man — for the sake of a mistress newly become dear to him, who is no kin of his — would strike his mother, {{{ 574c }}} long dear to him and his own by blood, or — for the sake of a boy in his bloom newly become dear to him, who is no kin of his — would strike his father, past his bloom yet still his own by blood and the oldest of his friends, and would enslave them both to his new favorites, if he brought them into the same house?

Yes, by Zeus, he said.

It seems, I said, a supremely blessed thing to have begotten a tyrannical son.

Very much so, he said. {{{ 574d }}}
