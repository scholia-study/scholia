## The Regulation of Births

So then, certain festivals must be established by law, at which we shall bring together the brides and the bridegrooms, and sacrifices, and hymns {{{ 460a }}} to be composed by our poets, fitting to the marriages that take place; and we shall put the number of marriages under the control of the rulers, so that they may preserve, as far as possible, the same number of men, having regard to wars and diseases and all such things, and so that our city may become neither too great nor too small, so far as that lies in our power.

Rightly, he said.

Then, I think, some ingenious lots must be devised, so that the base man may blame chance for each pairing rather than the rulers.

Very much so, he said. {{{ 460b }}}

And to the good among the young men, surely, whether in war or elsewhere, rewards and prizes must be given, and among them a more liberal license to sleep with the women, so that at the same time, under this pretext, as many of the children as possible may be begotten from such men.

Rightly.

Then also the officials appointed over these matters, receiving the offspring as they are continually born — whether those officials be men or women or both, since the offices, I suppose, are common to women and men alike —

Yes. {{{ 460c }}}

Taking the offspring of the good, I think, they will carry them off to the pen, to certain nurses who live apart in a section of the city; but the offspring of the inferior, and any of the others that happens to be born deformed, they will hide away in secrecy and obscurity, as is fitting.

If indeed the race of the guardians is to remain pure, he said.

Then will these same officials also take charge of feeding, bringing the mothers to the pen when their breasts are full, devising every contrivance {{{ 460d }}} so that none of them will recognize her own child, and providing other women who have milk, if the mothers themselves are not sufficient, and will they take care that these women nurse only for a moderate time, handing over the wakeful nights and the rest of the labor to wet-nurses and attendants?

You are describing, he said, a great deal of ease in child-bearing for the women of the guardians.

For that is fitting, I said. Let us go through next what we proposed. For we said that the offspring must be born from those in their prime.

True. {{{ 460e }}}

Then do you agree that twenty years is the moderate span of prime for a woman, and thirty for a man?

Which years do you mean? He asked.

A woman, I said, is to bear children for the city beginning at twenty and continuing until forty; and a man, once he has passed the sharpest point of his racing prime, is to beget children for the city from then on until the age of fifty-five. {{{ 461a }}}

At any rate, he said, for both of them that is the prime of body and of practical wisdom.

So then, whether it is someone older or younger than these ages who takes part in begetting children for the common stock, we shall declare the offense to be neither pious nor just, on the ground that he is fathering for the city a child who, if he escapes notice, will come to be not under the sacrifices nor under the prayers that the priestesses and priests and the whole city will offer at each marriage — that the offspring may always turn out better from the good and more {{{ 461b }}} useful from the useful — but in darkness, amid dreadful lack of self-restraint.

Rightly, he said.

And the same law, I said, applies if any of those still capable of begetting children touches one of the women of the proper age without a ruler having brought them together; for we shall say that he is bringing into the city a child that is bastard, unsanctioned, and unholy.

Quite rightly, he said.

And when, I suppose, the women and the men pass beyond the age for begetting children, we shall no doubt release them to be free to have intercourse {{{ 461c }}} with whomever they wish, except a daughter and a mother and the children of daughters and those above a mother, and the women likewise except a son and a father and their descendants downward and ascendants upward, and in permitting all this we shall charge them to be careful above all that not one conceived child, should any be conceived, is ever brought to light, and that if one does force its way through, they dispose of it on the understanding that there is to be no rearing for such a child.

That, at least, he said, is reasonably stated; but fathers and {{{ 461d }}} daughters, and the others you just mentioned, how will they recognize one another?

Not at all, I said; but from whatever day one of them becomes a bridegroom, then, in the tenth month after that day, or the seventh, whatever offspring are born he will call all of them — the males sons and the females daughters — and they in turn will call him father, and in this way their children's children will be called grandchildren, and those before them in turn will be called grandfathers and grandmothers, and all who are born in that period of time in which their mothers and fathers were begetting children {{{ 461e }}} will be called sisters and brothers, so that, as we were just saying, they will not touch one another. But the law will permit brothers and sisters to live together as spouses, if the lot falls that way and the Pythia further sanctions it.
