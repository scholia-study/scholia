## Filling the Soul: Which Pleasures Are True

Would you be surprised, then, if those with no experience of truth also hold unhealthy opinions about many other things, and are disposed toward pleasure and pain and the state between them in such a way that, whenever they are carried toward {{{ 585a }}} the painful, they believe it is true and really are in pain, but whenever they move from pain to the state between, they firmly believe they are approaching fullness and pleasure — just as those who, from inexperience of white, look at gray against black, so too these, from inexperience of pleasure, look at pain against the painless and are deceived?

By Zeus, he said, I would not be surprised — rather I would be far more surprised if it were not so.

Consider it this way, then, I said: aren't hunger and thirst and {{{ 585b }}} things of that sort a kind of emptying of the body's condition?

What else?

And ignorance and folly — are they not, in turn, a kind of emptiness of the soul's condition?

Very much so.

So wouldn't both the one who partakes of nourishment and the one who acquires understanding be filled?

How could it not be?

And which filling is truer — that with what is less real, or that with what is more real?

Clearly, with what is more real.

Which classes, then, do you think partake more of pure being: those such as grain, drink, relish and food in general, or the kind that includes true opinion, knowledge, intellect and, {{{ 585c }}} in sum, all virtue? Judge it this way: does what clings to what is always the same, to the immortal, and to truth — being itself of that nature and coming to be within it — seem to you to be more real than what never clings to the same thing but to the mortal, and is itself of that nature and comes to be within it?

What holds to what is always the same, he said, is far superior.

Does the reality of what is always the same, then, partake any more of being than of knowledge?

Not at all.

And what then? Of truth?

Not that either.

And if it partakes less of truth, does it not also partake less of being?

Necessarily. {{{ 585d }}}

So, on the whole, do the classes concerned with the care of the body partake less of truth and being than the classes concerned with the care of the soul?

Yes, much less.

And do you not think that the body itself stands the same way to the soul?

I do.

So isn't what is filled with things that are more real, and is itself more real, filled more truly than what is filled with things that are less real and is itself less real?

How could it not be?

So if being filled with what is fitting by nature is pleasant, then what is really filled, and filled more with what is real, {{{ 585e }}} would make one rejoice more truly and more really with true pleasure, whereas what partakes of what is less real would be filled less truly and less securely, and would partake of a less trustworthy and less true pleasure.

Most necessarily, he said. {{{ 586a }}}

Those, then, who have no experience of practical wisdom and virtue, but who are always occupied with feasting and things of that sort, are carried downward, it seems, and back up only as far as the state between, and wander about there their whole life long, but they have never once looked up beyond this toward what is truly above, nor been carried there, nor have they ever been filled with what really is, nor tasted a secure and pure pleasure, but, like cattle, always looking downward and with heads bent to the ground and to their tables, they feed, fattening themselves and breeding, {{{ 586b }}} and for the sake of getting more of these things they kick and butt one another with iron horns and hooves, and kill each other from insatiable greed, since it is not with what really is that they fill either the real part of themselves or the part of themselves that can hold it.

That is altogether right, Glaucon said; you are prophesying the life of the many, Socrates.

Is it not necessary, then, that such people also live amid pleasures mixed with pains, mere images of true pleasure, shadow-painted {{{ 586c }}} and tinged by being set beside one another so that each appears intense, and that they breed frenzied erotic loves for themselves in the foolish, and become things fought over — just as Stesichorus says the image of Helen became a thing fought over by those at Troy, through ignorance of the truth?

It is quite necessary, he said, that it be something of that sort.

And what of this? As for the spirited part, is it not necessary that other such things happen to whoever pursues this same goal — whether through envy because of love of honor, or through violence because of love of victory, or through anger because of a difficult temper, {{{ 586d }}} pursuing a surfeit of honor, victory, and anger, without reasoning or intellect?

It is necessary, he said, that things of that sort happen concerning this too.

What then, I said — may we say with confidence that, of all the desires concerned with love of gain and love of victory, those which follow knowledge and reason, and pursue along with these the pleasures that the practically wise part directs, will obtain the truest pleasures, so far as it is possible for them to obtain {{{ 586e }}} true ones, since they follow truth, and will obtain the pleasures proper to themselves — if indeed what is best for each thing is also most proper to it?

But of course, he said — most proper of all.

So when the whole soul follows the philosophic part and is not in conflict with itself, it becomes possible for each part, besides doing its own work in other respects and being just, also to enjoy its own pleasures — the best pleasures and, so far as possible, {{{ 587a }}} the truest.

Quite so.

But whenever one of the other parts gains mastery, the result for it is that it does not find its own pleasure, and it compels the other parts as well to pursue a pleasure that is alien and not true.

So it is, he said.

So would the things that stand furthest from philosophy and reason be most likely to produce such effects?

Very much so.

And is not what stands furthest from reason the very thing that stands furthest from law and order too?

Clearly so.

And did not the erotic {{{ 587b }}} and tyrannical desires appear to stand furthest removed of all?
