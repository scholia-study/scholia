## The Tyrannical Man in Private and in Public

And what happens, then, when the resources of his father and mother fail such a man, and the swarm of pleasures gathered within him has already grown great — will he not first breach the wall of some house, or strip the cloak from someone walking late at night, and after that plunder some temple? And amid all this, the desires newly freed from slavery, waiting on erotic love like a bodyguard, will overpower — together with him — the opinions he had held from childhood about what is noble and shameful, the ones once counted as just, desires that before were released, as if in a dream {{{ 574e }}}, only during sleep, when he himself was still ruled within himself democratically by law and by his father; but tyrannized now by erotic love, becoming while awake always what he rarely became in a dream, he will hold back from no dreadful bloodshed, no food, no deed, {{{ 575a }}} but erotic love, living within him tyrannically in utter anarchy and lawlessness, since it is itself sole monarch, will drive the man who has it, as though he were a city, to every kind of recklessness, so as to feed both itself and the tumult gathered around it — part of it come in from outside through bad company, part of it from within, set loose and freed by the same habits and by the man himself; or is this not the life of such a man?

That is indeed his life, he said.

And if such men are few in a city {{{ 575b }}}, I said, and the rest of the multitude is moderate, they go off and serve as bodyguard to some other tyrant, or hire themselves out as mercenaries, if a war happens to be on somewhere; but if they find themselves in peace and quiet, they do many small evils right there in the city.

What sort of things do you mean?

Such things as stealing, breaking into houses, cutting purses, stripping clothes off people, robbing temples, kidnapping people into slavery; and sometimes, if they are capable speakers, they bring false accusations, bear false witness, and take bribes. {{{ 575c }}}

You call these small evils, he said, if such men are few.

Small things, I said, are small in comparison with great ones, and all these together, set beside a tyrant for wickedness and the misery of a city, as the saying goes, don't even come close. For whenever many such men arise in a city, along with others who follow their lead, and they become aware of their own numbers, then it is they who, together with the folly of the people, breed the tyrant — that one among them who, more than the rest, has the greatest and fullest {{{ 575d }}} tyrant established in his own soul.

Naturally, he said — for he would be the most tyrannical of all.

So then, if they yield willingly — well and good; but if the city does not give way, then, just as he once punished his mother and father, so again he will punish his fatherland, if he is able, by bringing in new companions, and he will keep and nourish his long-beloved motherland — as the Cretans say — and fatherland, enslaved now under them. And this indeed would be the end point of such a man's desire. {{{ 575e }}}

That, he said, absolutely.

So then, I said, in private life, even before they rule, such men become like this: first, in dealing with whomever they are with, either they consort with their own flatterers, men ready to serve them in anything, or, if they need something from someone, {{{ 576a }}} they grovel themselves, daring to strike every pose as though they were intimates, only to be strangers once they have got what they wanted?

Very much so indeed.

So in their whole life they are never a friend to anyone, but always either mastering someone or a slave to another, and a tyrannical nature never tastes freedom or true friendship.

Certainly.

So would we not be right to call such men faithless?

How could we not?

And indeed as unjust as it is possible to be, if we were right {{{ 576b }}} in what we agreed earlier about justice, what sort of thing it is.
