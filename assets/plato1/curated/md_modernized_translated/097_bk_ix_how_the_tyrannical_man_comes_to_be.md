## How the Tyrannical Man Comes to Be

Entirely so, I think, he said.

So then, we have been led on to say more about this than we needed; but what we want to know is this — that a terrible, wild, and lawless kind of desire is present in each of us, even in some of us who seem entirely moderate, and this becomes evident in dreams. So if you think there is something in what I say, and you agree, then consider this.

Well, I agree.

So recall what sort of man we said the democratic man was. {{{ 572c }}} He was, I suppose, one who had been raised from youth by a thrifty father, who honored only the money-making desires, and dishonored those that are not necessary but arise for the sake of play and adornment. Isn't that so?

Yes.

But having associated with more refined men, full of the desires we just went through, and having rushed into every kind of wanton excess and into their kind of life, out of hatred for his father's thriftiness, yet having a nature better than those who corrupt him, and being drawn in both directions, {{{ 572d }}} he settled into a middle between the two ways, and enjoying each in a moderate way, as he supposed, he lives neither an illiberal nor a lawless life, having become democratic instead of oligarchic.

For that was, and still is, he said, the opinion held about such a man.

Now suppose again, I said, that this man, now grown older, has a young son raised in turn in his ways.

I suppose so.

Now suppose also that the same things happen to him as happened to his father, that he is led into every {{{ 572e }}} lawlessness, which those who lead him into it call complete freedom, that his father and the rest of his household come to the defense of these desires in between, while others in turn bring aid on the other side — and that, when these terrible enchanters and tyrant-makers despair of holding the young man by any other means, they contrive to implant in him a certain erotic love as champion of the idle desires and of those {{{ 573a }}} that divide up what lies ready at hand, a great winged drone — or what else do you suppose the erotic love of such men to be?

Nothing else, he said, but this.

So then, when the other desires, buzzing around him, full of incense and perfumes and garlands and wines, and of the pleasures set loose in such gatherings, swell and feed him to the utmost and implant in the drone the sting of longing, then this champion of the soul is given a bodyguard of madness and {{{ 573b }}} is driven into a frenzy, and if it finds within him any opinions or desires that are still decent and still feel shame, it kills them and thrusts them away from itself, until it has purged him of moderation and filled him with an imported madness.

You are describing, he said, exactly how a tyrannical man comes to be.

Is it not for this reason, then, I said, that erotic love has long been called a tyrant?

It seems so, he said.

And does not a drunken man too, my friend, I said, have a somewhat tyrannical {{{ 573c }}} cast of mind?
