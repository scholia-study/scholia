## The Protector Becomes a Tyrant

{{{ 565a }}} The people would be a third class — all who work with their own hands and keep out of public affairs, not possessing a great deal — and this, whenever it is gathered together, is the largest and most dominant group in a democracy.

It is so, he said; but it is not often willing to do this, unless it gets some share of the honey.

And it does always get a share, I said, so far as its protectors are able, by taking the property away from those who have it, distributing it to the people, and keeping the greatest part for themselves. {{{ 565b }}}

It does get a share that way, he said.

Then those it is taken from are compelled, I suppose, to defend themselves, speaking before the people and doing whatever they can.

How could they not?

Then they are accused by the others, even if they have no wish to overturn anything, of plotting against the people and being oligarchic.

What else?

And then in the end, whenever they see the people, not willingly but through ignorance and being deceived by {{{ 565c }}} slanderers, attempting to do them wrong, then at last, whether they wish it or not, they truly become oligarchic — not willingly, but because that drone breeds this evil in them too by stinging them.

Quite so.

Then impeachments, trials, and contests against one another follow.

Very much so.

And is it not always the people's habit to set some one man above itself especially, and to nourish him and make him great?

It is indeed their habit. {{{ 565d }}}

This, then, I said, is clear — that whenever a tyrant springs up, he sprouts from the root of a protector and from nowhere else.

Very clear indeed.

What, then, is the beginning of the change from protector to tyrant? Or is it clear that it happens once the protector begins to do the same thing as the man in the story told about the shrine of Lycaean Zeus in Arcadia?

What story? He asked.

That the one who tastes the human entrail — one piece cut up and mixed in among the entrails of the other sacrificial victims — {{{ 565e }}} must inevitably become a wolf. Or have you not heard the story?

I have.

Is it not the same, then, with one who, standing as protector of the people and getting hold of a mob entirely obedient to him, does not hold back from the blood of his own kin, but, bringing unjust charges, as such men are apt to do, and hauling a man before the courts, commits murder, blotting out a man's life, tasting kindred blood with unholy tongue and mouth, and drives men into exile and {{{ 566a }}} kills them, and hints at the cancellation of debts and the redistribution of land — is it not necessary, then, and fated for such a man, after this, either to be destroyed by his enemies or to become a tyrant and turn from a man into a wolf?
