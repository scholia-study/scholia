## Courage as the Preservation of a Right Belief

What you say is entirely true, he said.

This then is one of our four, and I hardly know in what way we have found it — both the thing itself and where in the city it is situated.

At any rate it seems to me, he said, that it has been found well enough.

But surely it is not at all hard to see courage itself, and the part of the city in which it resides, on account of which the city is to be called courageous.

How so? {{{ 429b }}}

Who, I said, would call a city cowardly or courageous by looking to anything other than that part of it which fights in its defense and takes the field on its behalf?

Not a single person, he said, would look to anything else.

For I do not think, I said, that the rest of the people in it, whether cowardly or courageous, have the power to make it one way or the other.

No, they do not.

So a city is courageous by some part of itself, because it has in that part such a power as will preserve, through everything, the {{{ 429c }}} belief about what things are to be feared — that they are the same things, and of the same kind, that the lawgiver prescribed in education. Or is this not what you call courage?

I did not quite grasp what you said, he said, so say it again.

I mean, I said, that courage is a kind of preservation.

What kind of preservation?

The preservation of the belief that has come to be, by law and through education, about what things are to be feared and of what kind they are — and by preserving it through everything, I meant that a person keeps it safe in the midst of pains and {{{ 429d }}} pleasures and desires and fears, and does not cast it out. And I am willing to compare it, if you like, to something it seems to me to resemble.

Yes, I would like that.

Well then, do you know, I said, that dyers, when they wish to dye wool so that it will be purple, first select out of so many colors the one nature, that of white; then they prepare it beforehand, treating it with no little preparation so that it will take up the bloom as much as possible, and only then do they dye it. {{{ 429e }}} And whatever is dyed in this way becomes fast-dyed, and no washing, whether with soap or without, can strip the bloom from it — but whatever is not dyed this way, you know what happens, whether someone dyes other colors, or dyes these same ones without the prior treatment.

I know, he said, that they turn out both washed-out and ridiculous.

Suppose then, I said, that we too were doing something of this kind, so far as we were able, when we selected our soldiers {{{ 430a }}} and educated them in music and gymnastic — suppose we were contriving nothing else than that they should be won over and take in our laws as finely as possible, like a dye, so that their belief about what is to be feared, and about everything else, would become fast-dyed, because the nature and the nurture they had were suited to it, and so that these soaps, terribly effective at washing things out, should not wash their dye away — pleasure, which is more potent at doing this than any bleaching-soda {{{ 430b }}} and lye, and pain and fear and desire, more potent than any other soap. This power then, and this preservation through everything of right and lawful belief about what is and is not to be feared, is what I call and take to be courage, unless you have something different to say.

I have nothing different to say, he said — for it seems to me that you regard the right belief about these same matters, when it comes about without education, the sort found in beasts and in slaves, as not fully lawful, and that you call it something other than courage. {{{ 430c }}}

What you say is entirely true, I said.

I accept, then, that this is courage.

Yes, do accept it, I said — as civic courage, at any rate — and you will be right to accept it; another time, if you like, we will go through it again in still finer detail. For it is not this we are seeking now, but justice — so for the purposes of that inquiry, I think, this is enough.

Well said, he said.

Two things, then, I said, still remain to be discerned in the {{{ 430d }}} city: moderation, and that for the sake of which we are seeking everything, justice.
