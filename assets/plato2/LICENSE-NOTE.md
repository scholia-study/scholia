# plato2 — licence

Two editions with different positions. Neither is covered by a blanket claim.

## The Greek edition (`gorgias-grc`)

The text is **public domain**. It is the Greek constituted by John Burnet for
*Platonis Opera*, Tomus III (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1903) — the words, the
punctuation, Burnet's editorial brackets, the speaker attributions he prints
before each turn, and the Stephanus pagination are all his, and their copyright
has long expired. Nothing here places them back under licence, and nothing in
this repository should be read as trying to.

What is ours in this edition, and licensed **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** with the rest of
`assets/`:

- the division of the dialogue into titled sections, and those English titles
- the paragraphing within long speeches
- the footnotes identifying the poets Plato quotes
- the arrangement of the curated markdown

The NFC normalisation and the unification of elision marks on U+2019 are
mechanical transformations and carry no copyright. So is the expansion of
Burnet's printed speaker abbreviations (`ΣΩ.`, `ΚΑΛ.`, `ΠΩΛ.`) to the full names
of the five characters the dialogue itself names.

## The English edition (`gorgias`)

Scholia's own translation, prepared from Burnet's Greek. **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**
throughout.

It is worth stating plainly what it is not. W. R. M. Lamb's Loeb *Gorgias*
(1925) is public domain in the United States and sits in the same Perseus
corpus the Greek was taken from, so shipping it would have been the cheap path.
Lamb died in 1961, which leaves him in copyright across the EU until 2032, and
this is a commercial site hosted in Germany. Lamb is held locally as one of
three controls the translation is scored against for accidental overlap, and is
never a source for it.

## Transcription provenance

The Greek was taken from the Perseus Digital Library's `canonical-greekLit`
corpus (Tufts University), whose TEI encoding is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. Only
Burnet's public-domain text, his speaker attributions and his Stephanus
milestones were used; Perseus's own editorial contributions — their section
divisions, paragraph milestones, bibliographic identifications and URNs — were
discarded and the structure re-derived. Perseus is credited by name in the
edition's about text as a courtesy.
