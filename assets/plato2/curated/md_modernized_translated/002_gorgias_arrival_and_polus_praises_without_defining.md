## Arrival, and Polus Praises without Defining

@ Callicles

{{{ 447a }}} They say this is the way to join a war or a battle, Socrates.

@ Socrates

But have we really come, as the saying goes, after the feast, and are we too late?

@ Callicles

And a very elegant feast indeed; Gorgias just now gave us a display of many fine things.

@ Socrates

Well, Callicles, Chaerephon here is to blame for that — he made us waste time in the marketplace.

@ Chaerephon

{{{ 447b }}} No trouble, Socrates; I'll set it right myself. Gorgias is a friend of mine, so he'll perform for us — now, if he likes, or later, if you'd rather.

@ Callicles

What is it, Chaerephon? Does Socrates want to hear Gorgias?

@ Chaerephon

That's exactly what we're here for.

@ Callicles

Well then, come to my house whenever you like — Gorgias is staying with me and will give you all a display.

@ Socrates

Good, Callicles. But would he be willing {{{ 447c }}} to talk with us? I want to find out from him what the power of the man's craft is, and what it is that he professes and teaches; the rest of his display he can give some other time, as you say.

@ Callicles

There's nothing like asking him yourself, Socrates. In fact this was itself part of his display — he was just now urging anyone inside to ask whatever they wanted, and said he would answer everything.

@ Socrates

Well said. Chaerephon, ask him.

@ Chaerephon

Ask him what?

@ Socrates

{{{ 447d }}} Who he is.

@ Chaerephon

What do you mean?

@ Socrates

Say he happened to be a maker of sandals — he'd surely answer you that he's a cobbler; or don't you follow what I mean?

@ Chaerephon

I understand, and I'll ask. Tell me, Gorgias, is Callicles here telling the truth, that you profess to answer whatever anyone asks you?

@ Gorgias

{{{ 448a }}} It's true, Chaerephon — I was just now professing this very thing, and I say that no one has asked me anything new in many years.

@ Chaerephon

Then you must find it easy to answer, Gorgias.

@ Gorgias

You're welcome to put that to the test, Chaerephon.

@ Polus

By Zeus — or if you'd rather, Chaerephon, test me. Gorgias seems to me worn out, in fact — he's just been through a great deal.

@ Chaerephon

What is it, Polus? Do you think you'd answer better than Gorgias?

@ Polus

{{{ 448b }}} What does that matter, if it's enough for you?

@ Chaerephon

Nothing — but since you want to, go ahead and answer.

@ Polus

Ask.

@ Chaerephon

All right, I'm asking. If Gorgias happened to know the same craft as his brother Herodicus, what would we rightly call him? Wouldn't it be what we call him?

@ Polus

Certainly.

@ Chaerephon

Then we'd be right to call him a doctor.

@ Polus

Yes.

@ Chaerephon

And if he had the skill of Aristophon son of Aglaophon, or of his brother, what would we rightly call him?

@ Polus

{{{ 448c }}} Clearly a painter.

@ Chaerephon

But as it is, since he's knowledgeable in some craft, what would we call him, to name him rightly?

@ Polus

Chaerephon, there are many crafts among men, worked out from experience, in an experienced way — for knack makes our lifetime proceed by craft, while lack of experience makes it proceed by chance. Different people share in each of these in different ways, and the best people share in the best of them; and Gorgias here is one of these, and has a share in the finest of the crafts.

@ Socrates

{{{ 448d }}} Polus certainly seems well prepared for speeches, Gorgias — but he isn't doing what he promised Chaerephon.

@ Gorgias

What do you mean exactly, Socrates?

@ Socrates

He doesn't seem to me to be answering the question he's been asked at all.

@ Gorgias

Well, ask him yourself, if you like.

@ Socrates

No — not if you're willing to answer yourself; I'd much rather it were you. Because it's clear to me from what Polus has said that he's practiced what's called rhetoric more than he's practiced conversation.

@ Polus

{{{ 448e }}} Why do you say that, Socrates?

@ Socrates

Because, Polus, when Chaerephon asked what craft Gorgias has knowledge of, you praised his craft as though someone were finding fault with it, but you didn't answer what it actually is.

@ Polus

Didn't I answer that it's the finest one?

@ Socrates

You certainly did. But no one is asking what kind of craft Gorgias's craft is, but what it is, and what we ought to call Gorgias — just as Chaerephon put it to you before, and you answered him well {{{ 449a }}} and briefly, so now tell me the same way: what is the craft, and what must we call Gorgias? Or rather, Gorgias, tell us yourself what we should call you, as someone with knowledge of what craft.

@ Gorgias

Of rhetoric, Socrates.

@ Socrates

Then we should call you an orator?

@ Gorgias

A good one, Socrates, if you want to call me what I pray to be, as Homer put it.

@ Socrates

Well, I do want to.

@ Gorgias

Then call me that.
