## a. The Struggle of the Enlightenment with Superstition

The various modes in which consciousness relates negatively, skepticism in part, in part theoretical and practical idealism, are subordinate shapes when set against this shape of _pure insight_ and of its dissemination, the _Enlightenment_; for insight has substance for its birthplace, it takes the pure _self_ of consciousness for something absolute, and it takes up the contest with the pure consciousness whose object is the absolute essence of all actuality. — Faith and {{{ 489 }}} insight are one and the same pure consciousness, yet opposed as regards form, since for faith the essence counts as _thought_ and not as _concept_, hence as something flatly opposed to _self_-consciousness — whereas for pure insight it is the _self_ that is the essence — and thus, for one another, the one is the utter negative of the other. {{ 294 }} — In the manner in which the two step forth against each other, the whole of the _content_ accrues to faith, since within faith's tranquil element of thinking each moment acquires subsistence; — pure insight, by contrast, has at the outset no content at all, being rather the sheer disappearance of content; but by moving negatively against that which is negative to it, pure insight will bring itself to realization and furnish itself with a content.

It knows faith to be the very opposite of itself, of reason and of truth. Since faith counts for it, quite generally, as a tissue of superstition, prejudices, and errors, the consciousness of this content organizes itself for it, further, into a realm of error, one in which false insight exists at first as the _universal mass_ of consciousness, immediate, ingenuous, and without any reflection into itself, but in which it also carries, cut off from that ingenuousness, the moment of reflection into self, or of self-consciousness, in the shape of an insight and an evil intention that keep to themselves in the background and by which the mass is befooled. That mass falls prey to the fraud of a _priesthood_ that pursues its envious vanity of keeping insight in its own sole possession, {{{ 490 }}} together with whatever else serves its advantage, and that conspires at the same time with _despotism_, which, being the synthetic, conceptless unity of the real realm and this ideal one, — a strangely inconsistent essence, — towers over the multitude's poor insight and the priests' poor intention, unites the two in itself as well, and, holding both in contempt, extracts from the people's stupidity and confusion, using the deceiving priesthood as its instrument, the advantage of undisturbed dominion and of the gratification of its lusts and arbitrary will, while being all the while that identical dullness of insight, that identical superstition and error.

With these three sides of its enemy the Enlightenment does not engage indiscriminately; for its essence being pure insight, which is the _universal_ in and for itself, the relation it truly bears to the other extreme is the one in which it makes for what is _common_ and _equal_ in both. The side on which _singularity_ isolates itself out of the universal ingenuous consciousness is what stands opposed to it, and that it cannot touch immediately. Hence what its activity has for its immediate object is not the will of the priesthood that deceives nor of the despot that oppresses; the object is rather that will-less insight which does not singularize itself into being-for-itself, the _concept_ of rational self-consciousness, a concept whose existence lies in the mass while not yet being present there as concept. But in {{{ 491 }}} tearing this honest insight and its ingenuous essence away from prejudices and errors, pure insight twists the reality and the power of the bad intention's deception out of that intention's hands, since the realm of that deception has its _soil_ and _material_ in the universal mass's conceptless consciousness — as _being-for-itself_ has its _substance_ in _simple_ consciousness generally.

{{ 295 }} Pure insight's relation to the ingenuous consciousness of the absolute essence now presents two sides: for one part, pure insight is _in itself_ identical with that consciousness; for the other part, that consciousness, inside the simple element belonging to its thought, allows the absolute essence along with the parts of that essence to stand and to acquire subsistence for themselves, granting them validity solely as its own _in-itself_ and therefore in an objective fashion, while denying its _being-for-itself_ inside this in-itself. — To the extent that, on the first side, this faith is, for pure insight, _in itself_ pure _self_-consciousness and has merely to become so _for itself_, that concept of faith affords pure insight the element wherein it realizes itself in the stead of false insight.

Seen from this side, namely that the two are essentially the same and that pure insight's relation runs through and within one and the same element, its communicating is _immediate_, and its giving and receiving an undisturbed flowing together. Whatever other stakes may have been hammered into consciousness, consciousness is _in itself_ that simplicity {{{ 492 }}} wherein all things are dissolved, forgotten, and ingenuous, and which is on that account utterly susceptible to the concept. For this reason one may liken the communicating of pure insight to a quiet expansion, or to the _spreading_ of a scent through an atmosphere that offers no resistance. It is an infection that permeates everything, one that gives no prior sign of being opposed to the indifferent element it slips into, and that therefore admits of no defense against it. Only after the infection has spread abroad is _it there for the consciousness_ that had unconcernedly given itself over to it. For what consciousness received into itself was indeed the simple essence, equal to itself and equal to consciousness, yet it was simultaneously the simplicity of a _negativity_ reflected into itself, a negativity that afterwards unfolds, as its nature requires, as something opposed, thereby recalling to consciousness the mode it formerly had; this negativity is the concept, that simple knowledge which knows itself and at once its own contrary, but knows the contrary within itself as sublated. Thus the moment pure insight is there for consciousness, it has already spread; the struggle waged against it gives away that the infection has taken place; the struggle comes too late, and every remedy only aggravates the disease, for what it has gripped is the pith of spiritual life, consciousness in its own concept, that is, its pure essence itself; and so within consciousness there is no force that would stand higher than the infection. Because it resides in the essence itself, its still {{{ 493 }}} scattered expressions can be forced back and its surface symptoms muffled. And this is of the greatest benefit to it, for it now wastes no strength to no purpose, neither does it prove itself unworthy of its essence, as it does whenever it erupts in symptoms and singular outbreaks against the content of faith and against the connectedness of faith's outer actuality. Rather, an invisible and unnoticed spirit now, it steals through the noble parts through and through, and has soon made itself thorough master of every entrail and limb of the unconscious idol, and "on _one fine morning_, with its elbow, it deals its comrade a push, and Bautz! {{ 296 }} Baradautz! the idol lies on the ground." — On _one fine morning_ whose noon runs no blood, once the infection has worked its way through every organ of spiritual life; nothing but memory then still holds fast the dead mode of spirit's earlier shape, as a history gone by one knows not how; and the serpent of wisdom, newly exalted to be adored, has thereby done no more than shed a shriveled skin without pain.

But this silent onward weaving of spirit within the simple interior of its substance, spirit keeping its own activity hidden from itself, is but a single side of how pure insight becomes real. Its dissemination does not consist solely in like joining with like; nor is its actualization merely an expansion free of opposition. The doing of the negative {{{ 494 }}} essence is, rather, just as essentially a movement that has developed and that differentiates itself within itself, a movement which, being conscious doing, has to set its moments out in a determinate, manifest existence, and has to be on hand as a loud uproar and a violent struggle with what is opposed as such.

So we have to see in what way _pure insight_ and _intention_ relate _negatively_ to the other that stands opposed to them and that they find already there. — Pure insight and intention, in relating negatively, can be nothing but their own negative, seeing that their concept is the whole of essentiality with nothing lying outside it. Qua insight, then, it turns into pure insight's negative, into untruth and unreason; and qua intention, into the negative of pure intention, into the lie and the impurity of purpose.

It gets tangled in this contradiction by letting itself into a quarrel and supposing that what it combats is something _other_. — It only supposes this, for its essence, being absolute negativity, lies in having otherness present in it. The absolute concept is the category; the category consists in this, that knowledge, and likewise the _object_ of knowledge, are one and the same. Consequently what pure insight pronounces to be its other, what it declares to be error or lie, can be nothing except itself; it is able to damn only what it itself is. What lacks rationality is without _truth_; or, what is not comprehended _is_ not; so when reason talks about an _other_ {{{ 495 }}} than itself, its talk is in fact about itself alone; in this it never steps out beyond itself. — This struggle with what is opposed therefore carries within it the sense of being reason's _actualization_. For that actualization lies precisely in the movement whereby the moments are developed and then taken back into insight; and one part of that movement is the differentiating in which comprehending insight places itself over against itself as _object_; for as long as it tarries in this moment it stands estranged from itself. {{ 297 }} Taken as pure insight it has no _content_ whatever; the movement by which it is realized lies in its becoming, as _it itself_, its own content, since no other content could accrue to it, it being the self-consciousness of the category. Since, however, in setting the content over against itself, it at first knows the content merely as _content_ and not yet as itself, it mistakes itself in it. Its completion accordingly means this: to cognize as its own the content that was objective for it to begin with. Its result, however, will thereby be neither a restoration of the errors it fought nor merely its own first concept, but an insight that cognizes its own absolute negation as its own actuality, as itself; in other words, its concept cognizing itself. — That the Enlightenment's struggle with errors has this nature, of fighting itself in them and damning in them what it itself maintains, is something _for us_, or is what the Enlightenment and its struggle are _in themselves_. The first side {{{ 496 }}} of that struggle, however, its befoulment through the admission of negative relating into its self-equal _purity_, is how it stands as an _object for faith_, which accordingly undergoes it as lie, unreason, and bad intention, just as faith is for it error and prejudice. — As regards its content, it is at the outset the empty insight, one for which its content shows up as another; it therefore _finds_ this content standing _before_ it in the shape in which the content does not yet belong to it, as an existence wholly independent of it, in faith.

The Enlightenment, then, apprehends its object in the first place and quite generally by taking it for _pure insight_ and, in doing so, failing to cognize itself, pronouncing it error. Within _insight_ as such, the way consciousness apprehends an object is such that the object turns for it into the essence of consciousness, into an object which consciousness saturates, in which consciousness sustains itself, remaining at home with itself and present to itself, and which consciousness, being thereby the object's own movement, brings forth. It is exactly as this that the Enlightenment states faith correctly when it says of faith that what faith holds to be the absolute essence is a being of faith's own consciousness, faith's own thought, a product brought forth by consciousness. With this it pronounces faith error and invention about the very same as what it itself is. — Wanting as it does to school faith in the new wisdom, it tells faith nothing new by this; for faith's object is for faith just exactly {{{ 497 }}} that, the pure essence of faith's own consciousness, such that this consciousness does not posit itself in that object as lost and negated but places its trust in it, which means precisely that _within_ it consciousness finds itself _as this_ consciousness, or as _self_-consciousness. Whomever I trust, his _certainty of his own_ self counts for me as the _certainty of my own_ self; I cognize my being-for-myself in him, in that he acknowledges it and it counts for him as purpose and essence. Trust, however, is faith, because the consciousness of faith stands in an _immediate_ _relation_ to its object and hence also intuits this, its being _one_ with the object and in the object. {{ 298 }} — Furthermore, when what is an object for me is that in which I cognize my own self, I am in it, at the same time and quite generally, an _other_ self-consciousness for myself, that is, one that has in it become estranged from its particular singularity, from its naturalness and contingency, yet that in part stays self-consciousness in it and is in part, precisely in it, _essential_ consciousness, as pure insight is. — The concept of insight holds not merely this, that consciousness cognizes its own self in the object it sees into and, without abandoning what is thought and only thereafter returning out of it into itself, possesses itself _immediately_ in that object; consciousness is aware of itself as the _mediating_ movement as well, aware of itself as the _doing_ or the bringing-forth; and through this there is present in thought, _for consciousness_, this unity of itself as the _self_ with the object. — This very consciousness is faith too; _obedience_ {{{ 498 }}} _and doing_ make up a necessary moment by way of which the certainty of one's being within the absolute essence is brought about. To be sure, this doing of faith does not have the look of the absolute essence itself being brought forth by it. But faith's absolute essence is essentially not that _abstract_ essence lying beyond the believing consciousness; it is the spirit of the community, the unity of abstract essence and self-consciousness. That the essence should be this spirit of the community: in this the community's own doing is an essential moment; the essence is that spirit _only through the bringing-forth_ carried out by consciousness; — or rather it is so _not without_ having been brought forth by consciousness; for however essential the bringing-forth may be, it is just as essentially not the sole ground of the essence, but only one moment. The essence is, simultaneously, in and for its own self.

Looked at from the other side, pure insight's concept is to be for itself an _other_ as its object, since it is just this negative determination that makes up the object. Accordingly it also states, from this other side, the essence of faith to be something _alien_ to self-consciousness, not _its_ essence but slipped under it like a changeling. Only here the Enlightenment is being wholly foolish; faith undergoes it as a talk which has no idea what it is uttering and grasps nothing of the matter when it holds forth about priestly fraud and the duping of the people. {{{ 499 }}} Its talk runs as if some hocus-pocus worked by juggling priests had slipped under consciousness, in place of the essence, something absolutely _alien_ and _other_; and it says in the same breath that this is an essence belonging to consciousness, one that consciousness believes in, entrusts itself to, and seeks to render favorable to itself; — which is to say that in it consciousness intuits _its own pure_ essence quite as much as _its_ singular and universal _individuality_, and that by its own doing consciousness brings forth this unity between itself and its essence. {{ 299 }} What it declares to be _alien_ to consciousness it states immediately as consciousness's _very own_. — How, then, can it possibly talk of fraud and duping? By stating _immediately_, and about faith itself, the reverse of what it maintains of faith, it rather exhibits itself to faith as being the conscious _lie_ that it is. In what way are duping and fraud to occur where consciousness has, in its truth, the immediate _certainty of its own self_; where in its object it is in possession of _its own self_, in that it finds itself there quite as much as it brings itself forth there. The difference is not so much as present in the words any longer. — Where the question has been posed in general terms, _whether one may be permitted to deceive a people_, one would in fact have to reply that the question is worth nothing, since on this head a people cannot be deceived at all. — Brass in the place of gold, counterfeit bills of exchange in the place of real ones, may well be sold in single cases; a battle that was lost may be fastened upon {{{ 500 }}} a good many as a battle won; and further lies about sensuous things and singular occurrences may be given credence for a while; but within the knowledge of the essence, where consciousness possesses the immediate _certainty of its own self_, any thought of duping drops away completely.

Let us go on to observe the way faith undergoes the Enlightenment within the _distinct_ moments of its consciousness, at which the account set out so far was directed only in general terms. These moments are pure thinking, or, as object, the _absolute essence_ in and for itself; then faith's _relating_ to it as a _knowledge_, the _ground of its faith_; and finally its relating to it in its doing, or _its service_. Just as pure insight has in general failed to recognize itself in faith and has disowned itself there, so in these moments too it will relate in an equally inverted manner.

Pure insight relates negatively to _the absolute essence_ of the believing consciousness. That essence is pure _thinking_, thinking that has been posited inside itself as object, that is, as the _essence_; within the believing consciousness this _in-itself_ of thinking takes on, for the consciousness that is for itself, the form of objectivity, though only its empty form; it stands in the determination of something _represented_. To pure insight, on the other hand, being pure consciousness taken on the side of the _self existing for itself_, the _other_ shows up as a _negative_ of {{{ 501 }}} _self-consciousness_. One might still take this either for thinking's pure _in-itself_ or else for the _being_ belonging to sense-certainty. Yet since it is at once for the _self_, and since this _self_, as one that has an object, is actual consciousness, the object proper to pure insight is, as such, an _existing common thing_ of _sense-certainty_. {{ 300 }} This object of its own shows up for it in faith's _representation_. It damns that representation, and in it its own object. But already in this it wrongs faith, apprehending faith's object in such a way that the object is its own. It says of faith accordingly that faith's absolute essence is a lump of stone, a wooden block possessed of eyes that do not see, or else some bread dough that grew in the field, was transformed by human beings, and was sent back there; — or in whatever further ways faith may anthropomorphize the essence and render it objective and representable for itself.

The Enlightenment, which gives itself out as the pure, here makes an actual _perishable thing_ out of what for spirit is eternal life and holy spirit, and it fouls that with the intrinsically null view held by sense-certainty, — a view not present to worshipping faith in the least, so that the Enlightenment simply tells faith this lie about it. What faith reveres is for faith in no sense stone, or wood, or bread dough, nor any other temporal sensuous thing. Should the Enlightenment take it into its head to say that faith's object {{{ 502 }}} is after all this _too_, or even that it is this in itself and in truth, then faith for its part is quite as well acquainted with _that too_, only that for faith it falls outside its worship; and for its part nothing of the sort, no stone and so forth, is _in itself_ for faith at all, but in itself for faith is the essence of pure thinking alone.

The _second moment_ is faith's relation, as a _knowing_ consciousness, to this essence. For faith as thinking pure consciousness this essence is immediate; but pure consciousness is no less a _mediated_ relating of certainty to truth, a relating that makes up the _ground_ of _faith_. This ground becomes for the Enlightenment, in the same way, a contingent _knowledge of contingent_ occurrences. The ground of knowledge, however, is that universal which _knows_, and this in its truth is absolute _spirit_, which, within abstract pure consciousness, or within thinking as such, is merely absolute _essence_, whereas as self-consciousness it is _knowledge_ of itself. Pure insight sets down this knowing universal, the _simple spirit that knows itself_, as being likewise a negative of self-consciousness. Itself, to be sure, it is the _purely mediated_ thinking, that is, thinking that mediates itself with itself, and it is pure knowledge; yet inasmuch as it is _pure insight, pure knowledge_ that does not yet know itself, that is, for which it is not yet the case that it is this pure mediating movement, that movement appears to it, like everything it is itself, as another. {{ 301 }} Engaged, then, in its own actualization, it develops this essential moment of its own, {{{ 503 }}} yet the moment shows up for it as something faith owns, and, in the determinateness of being external to it, as a contingent knowledge of just such commonly actual histories. So it fathers upon religious faith the fabrication that faith's certainty rests upon a few _singular historical testimonies_ which, regarded as historical testimonies, would certainly not yield that degree of certainty about their content which newspaper reports give us about some occurrence or other; — that faith's certainty rests, further, on the accident of the _preservation_ of these testimonies, — preservation by paper on the one hand, and on the other by the dexterity and honesty with which they were carried over from one paper to another, and finally on the correct apprehension of what dead words and letters mean. In point of fact, though, it never enters faith's head to hang its certainty on testimonies and contingencies of that kind; in its certainty faith is an ingenuous relationship to its absolute object, a pure knowledge of that object, one that mixes no letters, no paper, and no copyists into its consciousness of the absolute essence and does not mediate itself with that essence by means of such things; this consciousness is instead the ground of its own knowledge, mediating itself with itself; it is spirit as such, which is its own testimony, as much in the _interior_ of the _singular_ consciousness as through the _universal presence_ of the faith that all have in it. Should faith want to procure for itself, out of the historical, that manner of grounding {{{ 504 }}} or at any rate of confirming its content which the Enlightenment talks about, and should it be in earnest in supposing and acting as if that were what mattered, then it has already allowed the Enlightenment to lead it astray; and its exertions to ground or fortify itself in such a fashion are merely testimonies it gives of its own infection.

There remains the third side still, _the relating of consciousness to the absolute essence_ as a _doing_. This doing consists in sublating the individual's particularity, or the natural way in which the individual is for itself, and out of it there arises for the individual the certainty that it is pure self-consciousness, one with the essence in accordance with its doing, that is, as a singular consciousness that _is for itself_. — Since _purposiveness_ and _purpose_ come apart within this doing, and since pure insight _relates negatively_ to this doing as well and disowns itself here as in the other moments, it must, where _purposiveness_ is concerned, present itself as want of understanding, insight being bound up with intention, so that the accord of purpose with means appears to it as something other, indeed as the contrary; — while where _purpose_ is concerned it must make what is bad, enjoyment and possession, into the purpose, thereby proving itself the most impure intention, since pure intention, as another, is likewise impure intention.

{{ 302 }} In view of _purposiveness_, accordingly, we see the Enlightenment find it foolish when the believing individual procures for itself the higher consciousness of not being {{{ 505 }}} in bondage to natural enjoyment and pleasure, and procures it by _actually_ refusing itself both, showing _by the deed_ that it does not _lie_ when it despises them, but that its contempt is _true_. — In the same way it finds it foolish that the individual should absolve itself of its determinateness of being an absolute singular that shuts out all others and holds property, by letting go of its property itself; whereby the individual displays _in truth_ that it was not in earnest with its isolating, but that it stands above the natural necessity of singularizing itself and of denying, within this absolute singularization of being-for-itself, that the others are one and the same _with it_. — Pure insight finds the two of them unpurposive as well as wrong, — _unpurposive_, that in order to show oneself free of pleasure and possession one should refuse oneself pleasure and part with a possession; conversely, then, it will pronounce him a _fool_ who, so as to eat, lays hold of the means of actually eating. — It finds it _wrong_ as well that one should refuse oneself a meal, and should part with butter and eggs neither in exchange for money nor money in exchange for butter and eggs, but outright, receiving nothing of the sort back for them; such a meal, or possession of items like these, it pronounces an end in itself, and pronounces itself thereby, in fact, a very impure intention, one for which such enjoyment and possession are quite essentially the point. Being pure intention, it once more maintains that a rising {{{ 506 }}} above natural existence and above the greed for its means is necessary; it merely finds it foolish and wrong that such a rising should be proved _by the deed_; or rather, in truth this pure intention is a fraud, one that professes and requires an _inward_ rising while giving out that taking it in earnest, _actually setting it to work_, and _proving its truth_ are superfluous, foolish, and even wrong. — It therefore denies itself both as pure insight, since it denies the immediately purposive doing, and as pure intention, since it denies the intention of showing oneself liberated from the purposes of singularity.

This, then, is how the Enlightenment gives itself to faith to be undergone. It steps forward with this poor look about it because it is precisely by way of its relationship to another that it gives itself a _negative reality_, or exhibits itself as its own contrary; yet pure insight and intention have to give themselves this relationship, for the relationship is their actualization. — This showed itself at first as a negative reality. Maybe its _positive reality_ has a better make to it; let us see how the latter behaves. — Once every prejudice and all superstition have been driven out, the question comes up: _what comes next? {{ 303 }} What truth has the Enlightenment spread abroad in place of them?_ — It has already stated this positive content in the course of rooting out error, since that estrangement of itself is quite as much its positive reality. — In that which is absolute {{{ 507 }}} spirit for faith, it apprehends whatever it discovers there by way of _determination_ as wood, stone, and so on, as singular actual things; since it thus grasps _all determinateness_ in general, that is, all content and fulfillment of that spirit, in this manner as a _finitude_, as a _human essence and representation_, the _absolute essence_ becomes for it a _vacuum_ upon which no determinations, no predicates can be laid. Such a laying-together would in itself be culpable, and it is precisely the one in which the monsters of superstition have been begotten. Reason, _pure insight_, is admittedly not itself empty, seeing that its own negative is present _for it_ and forms its content; it is rich, rather, though rich only in singularity and limit; to let nothing of that kind attach to the absolute essence or be laid upon it is its insightful way of living, a way that understands how to assign itself and its riches of finitude their place, and how to treat the absolute worthily.

Facing this empty essence, the _second moment_ of the Enlightenment's positive truth is _singularity_ as such, the singularity of consciousness and of all being, shut out from an absolute essence, taken as _absolute being in and for itself_. In its very first actuality consciousness is _sense-certainty_ and _opinion_, and here, out of the whole path of its experience, it comes back to that point, being once more a knowledge of that which is _purely negative of its own self_, or of _sensuous things_, that is, of _existing_ things which stand indifferently over against its _being-for-itself_. Here, though, it is not {{{ 508 }}} _immediate_ natural consciousness; rather, it has _become_ such for itself. Having first been given over to all the entanglement into which its own unfolding hurls it, and now returned by pure insight to the shape it first had, that shape it has _experienced as the result_. _Grounded_ upon insight into the nothingness of every other shape of consciousness, and thus of everything that lies past sense-certainty, sense-certainty of this kind is opinion no longer; it is rather the absolute truth. This nothingness of all that reaches out past sense-certainty is admittedly no more than a negative proof of that truth; but of any other it is incapable, for the positive truth belonging to sense-certainty as such is just this, the concept's own _unmediated_ being-for-itself as object, and indeed in the form of otherness, — namely that every consciousness is _utterly certain_ that _it is_, and that there are _other actual things_ outside it, and that in its _natural_ being it is, like those things, _in and for itself_ or _absolute_.

{{ 304 }} _The third moment of the Enlightenment's truth_, finally, is the relationship of the singular essences to the absolute essence, the relating of the first two moments. Insight, as pure insight of what is _equal_ or _unlimited_, also _goes beyond_ what is _unequal_, namely beyond finite actuality, or beyond itself as mere otherness. It has as the beyond of that actuality _the empty_, to which it therefore relates sensuous {{{ 509 }}} actuality. Into the determination of this _relationship_ the two sides do not enter as _content_, for the one of them is the empty, so that content is on hand solely by way of the other, sensuous actuality. The _form_ of the relating, however, in whose determination the side of the _in-itself_ lends a hand, admits of being made at will; for the form is what is _in itself negative_, and therefore what opposes itself: being no less than nothing, the _in-itself_ no less than its _opposite_; or, amounting to the same, the relating of _actuality_ to the _in-itself_ as the _beyond_ is quite as much a _negating_ of that actuality as a _positing_ of it. Finite actuality can therefore, strictly speaking, be taken just as one happens to need it. So the sensuous is now referred to the absolute _positively, as to what is in itself_, and sensuous actuality is itself _in itself_; the absolute makes it, fosters it, and tends it. Then again it is also referred to the absolute as to its contrary, as to its _non-being_; on that relationship it is not in itself, but merely _for an other_. Whereas in the shape of consciousness that went before, the _concepts_ of the opposition determined themselves as _good_ and _bad_, for pure insight they turn instead into abstractions of a purer kind, that of being _in itself_ and that of being _for an other_.

Both ways of regarding the matter, the finite's positive relating to the in-itself as well as its negative relating, are in fact necessary alike, so that everything is _in itself_ in exactly the measure in which it is _for an other_; or: all things are _useful_. {{{ 510 }}} — Each thing surrenders itself to others, at one moment letting others put it to use and being _for them_; at the next it props itself up again, so to speak, on its hind legs, plays hard to get with what is other, is for itself, and puts the other to use on its own account. — For the human being, the thing that is _conscious_ of this relation, his essence and his position follow from that. Immediately as he is, as natural consciousness, he is _in himself, good_, as a singular he is _absolute_, and other things are _for him_; and since for him, being the animal that is conscious of itself, the moments carry the significance of universality, _everything_ exists for his pleasure and diversion, and, just as he issued from God's hand, he strolls about the world as though it were a garden laid out on his behalf. {{ 305 }} — He must have picked from the tree of the cognition of good and evil as well; therein he owns a usefulness that sets him apart from everything else, for as chance would have it his nature, good in itself, is _also_ so made that an overdose of diversion injures it, or rather his singularity has _its beyond in it too_, can pass out beyond itself and destroy itself. As a safeguard here, reason serves him as a useful means for keeping this passing-beyond within due bounds, or rather for preserving himself in passing beyond what is determinate; for that is the force of consciousness. What the conscious essence, _universal_ in itself, enjoys must itself, in variety and in duration, be nothing determinate but universal; measure accordingly has the determination of preventing {{{ 511 }}} pleasure from being broken off in its variety and duration; which is to say that measure is determined as immoderation. — As everything is useful to the human being, so is he useful in his turn, and his determination is just as much to make of himself a publicly useful and universally serviceable member of the troop. However much he looks after himself, exactly that much must he also make himself available to the others, and however much he makes himself available, that much does he look after himself: one hand washes the other. Yet wherever he may find himself, he is well placed: he is of use to others and is put to use.

One thing is useful to another in one manner, another in another; but what gives all things this useful mutuality is their essence, namely their being related to the absolute in the doubled manner: the positive one, — whereby each is _in and for itself_, and the negative one, whereby each is _for others_. The _relating_ to the absolute essence, that is, religion, is therefore of all usefulness the most useful; for it is _pure usefulness itself_: it is that subsistence which all things have, the _being in and for themselves_ that is theirs, and it is the falling of all things, the _being for an other_ that is theirs.

For faith, to be sure, this positive result of the Enlightenment is no less a horror than is the Enlightenment's negative bearing toward it. This _insight_ into the absolute essence, which sees nothing in it save just the _absolute_ essence, _the être supreme, or the void_, — this _outlook_ on which everything, just as it immediately exists, is _in itself_ or {{{ 512 }}} good, and on which, finally, the concept of utility exhaustively expresses the _relation_ of singular conscious being to the absolute essence, namely _religion_, is for faith flatly _detestable_. This peculiar _wisdom_ of the Enlightenment necessarily appears to faith at once as _flatness_ itself and as the _confession_ of flatness; for it consists in knowing nothing of the absolute essence, or, what comes to the same, in knowing about it this wholly level truth, that it is just the _absolute essence_, whereas it knows only of finitude, and indeed knows finitude as the true, and this knowledge of finitude as the true, to be the highest thing of all.

{{ 306 }} Against the Enlightenment faith possesses divine right, the right belonging to absolute _self-equality_, that is, to pure thinking, and at the Enlightenment's hands it meets with nothing but wrong; for the Enlightenment contorts faith in every one of its moments and makes of them something other than they are within it. The Enlightenment, for its part, has merely human right against faith and in support of its truth; for the wrong it does is itself the right of _inequality_, and consists in inverting and altering, a right proper to the very nature of _self-consciousness_ over against the simple essence, that is, over against _thinking_; but because the right it has is that of self-consciousness, the Enlightenment will not stop at keeping its right _too_, as though spirit were left with two equal rights confronting each other, neither of which could satisfy the other; it will instead lay claim to absolute right, since self-consciousness is that negativity {{{ 513 }}} of the concept which not only is _for itself_ but also reaches out over its opposite; and faith, itself being consciousness, will find itself unable to refuse the Enlightenment that right.

For the Enlightenment takes up against the believing consciousness no principles peculiar to itself, but only such as that consciousness already carries within it. It merely gathers together for faith faith's _own thoughts_, which within it drift apart unnoticed; while faith dwells on _one_ of its modes, the Enlightenment merely recalls to it the _others_ that it _also_ has, but of which it always forgets the one whenever it is occupied with the other. It proves itself to be pure insight in the face of faith precisely by seeing the whole while a _determinate_ moment is before it, and so by fetching up the _opposite_ bearing on that moment, and, by inverting the one into the other, driving forth the negative essence of both thoughts, the _concept_. It appears to faith as twisting and lie because it exhibits the _otherness_ of faith's moments; it thereby seems to faith to be making of them straightaway something different from what they are in their singularity; but this _other_ is just as essential, and in truth it is on hand in the believing consciousness itself, only that this consciousness does not think of it there but keeps it somewhere else; hence it is neither foreign to faith nor can faith disown it.

Yet the Enlightenment, for all that it reminds faith of the opposite of its separated-off moments, {{{ 514 }}} remains every bit as unenlightened about itself. Its relating to faith is purely _negative_, in that it excludes faith's content from its own purity and takes that content to be the _negative_ of itself. In this negative, then, in faith's content, it fails to cognize itself, and on that same account it also fails to put together the two thoughts, the one it fetches up and the one against which it fetches that one up. Since it fails to cognize that what it condemns in faith is straightaway its own thought, it is itself caught in the opposition of the two moments, of which it acknowledges only one, namely in each case the one opposed to faith, while severing the other from it exactly as faith does. {{ 307 }} It therefore does not bring forth the unity of the two as their unity, that is, the concept; the concept nevertheless _arises_ for it of its own accord, or it simply finds it there as something _on hand_. For pure insight realizes itself, in itself, in precisely this way: insight, having the concept for its essence, at first turns into an absolute _other_ to itself and denies itself, for the concept's opposition is an absolute one, and then out of that otherness arrives at itself, or at its own concept. — But the Enlightenment _is_ nothing more than this movement, the pure concept's activity still unconscious of itself, which does indeed reach itself as an object but takes that object for an _other_, and which likewise does not know the nature of the concept, namely that it is the undifferentiated that separates itself absolutely. — Against faith, therefore, insight {{{ 515 }}} is the _power_ of the concept just to the extent that it constitutes the movement, the relating, of those moments that lie apart within faith's consciousness, a relating through which their contradiction is brought to light. Herein lies the absolute _right_ of the violence that insight exercises over faith; the _actuality_, however, to which it brings this violence lies in just this, that the believing consciousness is itself the concept and therefore itself acknowledges the opposite that insight fetches up before it. The Enlightenment is therefore in the right as against faith, since what it makes valid in faith is just what faith itself cannot do without and what faith already has in its own self.

The Enlightenment asserts, first of all, the moment of the concept, namely that of being a _doing_ of _consciousness_; it asserts this _against_ faith — that faith's absolute essence is the essence of _its own_ consciousness as a self, or that it is _brought forth_ through consciousness. For the believing consciousness its absolute essence, exactly as it is for it the _in-itself_, is at the same time not like some foreign thing that would _stand_ there, one knows not how or from where; rather, its trust consists precisely in _finding_ itself therein as _this_ personal consciousness, and its obedience and service consist in bringing that essence forth as _its own_ absolute essence through its _doing_. It is really only of this that the Enlightenment reminds faith, whenever faith baldly pronounces the _in-itself_ of the absolute essence to lie _beyond_ the _doing_ of consciousness. — But while it does bring up against faith's one-sidedness the {{{ 516 }}} opposed moment, that of consciousness's _doing_ as against the _being_ on which faith alone dwells here, and while it likewise fails to bring together the thoughts that are its own, it takes the pure moment of _doing_ in isolation and pronounces about faith's _in-itself_ that it is _only_ something consciousness has _brought forth_. Doing that is isolated and set over against the _in-itself_ is, however, a contingent doing, and, as a representing, a manufacture of fictions — of representations that are not _in themselves_; and this is how the Enlightenment views the content of faith. {{ 308 }} — Conversely, though, pure insight says the very opposite as well. In asserting the moment of _otherness_ that the concept has in it, it pronounces the essence of faith to be one that _in no way concerns consciousness, lying beyond_ it, foreign to it and uncognized. For faith, its essence is, on the one side, that which faith trusts and in which it has the _certainty of itself_, and on the other side something inscrutable in its ways and unreachable in its being.

Further, when the Enlightenment views the object of faith's veneration as stone and wood, or otherwise as a finite anthropomorphic determinateness, it asserts against the believing consciousness a right that this consciousness itself concedes. For since faith is this consciousness split in two, having a _beyond_ of _actuality_ and a pure _here below_ over against that beyond, there is in fact present in it _also_ this view of the sensuous thing, {{{ 517 }}} according to which the thing _is valid in_ and _for itself_; yet the two thoughts _of what is in and for itself_, which for faith is at one time the _pure essence_ and at another a common _sensuous thing_, never come together in it. — Even faith's pure consciousness is infected by the latter view, for, lacking the concept, the distinctions of its supersensible realm are a row of self-subsistent _shapes_ and their movement a _happening_; that is to say, they are only in _representation_ and carry about them the mode of sensuous being. — The Enlightenment on its side likewise isolates _actuality_, treating it as an essence forsaken by spirit, and isolates determinateness as an unbudging finitude which would be no _moment_ within the spiritual movement of the essence itself, neither nothing, nor yet a something _that is_ in and for itself, but a vanishing something.

It is plain that matters stand the same way with the _ground_ of _knowledge_. The believing consciousness itself acknowledges a contingent _knowledge_, since contingencies are what it stands in a relationship to, and since the absolute essence itself has for it the form of a represented common actuality; with this the believing consciousness is _also_ a certainty that lacks truth in its own self, and it owns up to being just such an unessential consciousness, on this side of the spirit that makes itself certain and corroborates itself. — This moment, however, it forgets in its spiritual, immediate knowledge of the absolute {{{ 518 }}} essence. — The Enlightenment, though, which recalls this to faith, has in mind once again _only_ the contingent knowledge and lets the other slip, — has in mind only that mediation which takes place by way of an _alien_ third, and not the mediation in which what is immediate serves as its own third, through which it brings about its own mediation with the other, that is, with _itself_.

{{ 309 }} Finally, in its view of the _doing_ of faith, the Enlightenment finds the casting away of enjoyment and of possessions to be wrong and lacking in purposiveness. — As regards the wrong, it secures the agreement of the believing consciousness in this, that the latter itself acknowledges the actuality of owning property, holding fast to it, and enjoying it; and it conducts itself all the more stubbornly and in isolation in asserting its property, just as it surrenders itself all the more crudely in its enjoyment, since beyond this actuality falls its religious doing — the doing that _gives up_ possession and enjoyment — which purchases for it its freedom on that side. Through this opposition, such service of sacrificing natural impulse and enjoyment has in fact no truth; retention takes place _alongside_ sacrifice; the sacrifice is merely a _sign_ that accomplishes the actual sacrifice in only a small part and hence in fact merely _represents_ it.

As for _purposiveness_, the Enlightenment judges it clumsy that _a single_ possession should be thrown away so as to know and show oneself free of _possession as such_, that _a single_ enjoyment should be denied oneself so as to know and show oneself free of {{{ 519 }}} _enjoyment as such_. The believing consciousness itself takes absolute doing to be a _universal_ doing; for it, universality belongs not merely to the acting of that absolute essence which is its object, but the singular consciousness as well is to show itself wholly and universally freed from its sensuous essence. But to throw away a _singular_ possession, or to renounce a _singular_ enjoyment, is not the _universal_ action in question; and since, within action, the _purpose_, which is a universal, and the _execution_, which is a singular, would essentially have to stand before consciousness in their mismatch, such acting proves to be an acting in which consciousness has no share, and thus proves, strictly speaking, too _naive_ to count as an action; fasting is too naive a way to show oneself free of the pleasure of the meal, — too naive, as with Origen, to rid oneself of another pleasure _from the body_ in order to show it done away with. The action itself proves to be an _external_ and _singular_ doing; desire, however, has its roots _within_, and it is a _universal_; the pleasure it takes vanishes neither along with the instrument nor through any singular privation.

Here, however, it is the Enlightenment that takes _the inward, the unactual_ in isolation over against actuality, just as earlier it clung to the externality of {{{ 520 }}} thinghood over against the inwardness of faith in its intuition and devotion. It locates what is essential in the _intent_, in the _thought_, and so spares itself the actual accomplishment of any liberation from natural purposes; in truth this inwardness is itself what is merely formal, finding its fulfillment in the natural drives, which are justified precisely by being inward, by having their place in _universal_ being, in nature.

{{ 310 }} The Enlightenment therefore has an irresistible power over faith for the reason that the very moments it makes valid are to be found in faith's own consciousness. Considered more closely as to its effect, this force in its relating to faith seems to rend the _beautiful_ unity that _trust_ forms with immediate _certainty_, to sully faith's _spiritual_ consciousness with base thoughts taken from _sensuous_ actuality, and, through the _vanity_ of the understanding and of one's own willing and accomplishing, to wreck a disposition that submission had left _quieted_ and _assured_. In fact, though, what it rather sets in motion is the sublation of the _thoughtless_, or rather _conceptless separation_ that is on hand in faith. The believing consciousness employs a double measure and weight; it has two kinds of eyes, two kinds of ears, two kinds of tongue and speech; it has doubled all its representations without ever comparing this doubleness of sense. Or faith lives in two kinds of perception: the one, the perception of the _sleeping_ consciousness that lives purely in {{{ 521 }}} conceptless thoughts, the other that of the waking consciousness that lives purely in sensuous actuality; and in each it keeps a household of its own. — The Enlightenment throws the light of the representations of the sensuous world upon that heavenly one, and pointed out in the latter this finitude, which faith cannot disavow, since faith is self-consciousness and hence the unity to which both modes of representation belong and in which they do not drift apart, for they belong to one and the same inseparable _simple_ self, and it is into this self that faith has passed over.

Faith has thereby lost the content that filled its element, and sinks together into a dull stirring of spirit within itself. It is driven out of its kingdom, or this kingdom has been plundered, since the waking consciousness seized to itself every distinction and every extension of that kingdom, and claimed and gave back all its parts to the earth as the earth's property. Faith is not for that reason satisfied, for this illumination has everywhere brought forth nothing but singular essence, so that what speaks to spirit is an actuality void of essence and a finitude that spirit has abandoned. — Having no content, and unable to remain in this emptiness, or rather, going out past the finite, which is its one content, and coming upon the void alone, faith is _pure longing_; its truth is a _beyond_ that stands empty, for which no adequate content can any longer be found, {{{ 522 }}} since everything has been put to other uses. — With this, faith has in truth turned into the very thing that the Enlightenment is: consciousness of the relation borne by the finite that is in itself to the predicateless, uncognized and uncognizable absolute; the sole difference being _that the Enlightenment_ is the _satisfied one, and faith_ the _unsatisfied_ Enlightenment. Whether the Enlightenment can remain within its satisfaction is something that will show itself in the Enlightenment; that longing of the clouded spirit, mourning as it does the loss of the spiritual world that was its own, lies waiting in ambush. {{ 311 }} The Enlightenment itself bears this blemish of unsatisfied longing about it, — in the guise of _pure object_, upon its _empty_ absolute essence, — in the guise of _doing_ and _movement_, upon the _going out beyond_ its own singular essence toward the unfulfilled beyond, — in the guise of _fulfilled object_, upon the _selflessness_ of the useful. It will sublate this blemish; from a closer consideration of the positive result that is truth for it, it will emerge that the blemish is in itself already sublated therein.
