## c. Law-Testing Reason

For the simple ethical substance, a difference within it is a contingency, and in the case of the determinate commandment we saw it step forward as a contingency belonging to knowledge, to actuality, and to doing. The _comparison_ of that simple being with the determinateness which does not correspond to it was something that occurred in us; and in the course of it the simple substance revealed itself as formal universality, that is, as pure _consciousness_, which confronts the content in freedom from it and constitutes a _knowledge_ of the content qua determinate. In this way this universality stays what _the matter at hand_ had been. In consciousness, however, it is something different; it is, namely, no longer the inert genus devoid of thought, {{{ 366 }}} but is related to the particular and counts as that particular's power and truth. — To begin with, this consciousness seems to be the very testing that we were just now, and its doing seems able to be nothing but what has gone before, namely a matching of what is universal against what is determinate, from which, as before, the two would prove unsuited to each other. Here, though, the content stands in a different relationship to the universal, because the universal has acquired another meaning; it is _formal_ universality, and of this the determinate content is capable, since within such universality the content comes under consideration only as it bears on itself. In the testing that we ourselves performed, the universal and solid substance stood opposed to the determinateness, and that determinateness developed into contingency on the part of the consciousness that the substance had entered. Here one member of the comparison has disappeared; the universal is no longer the substance that _has being_ and _is valid_, nor what is right in and for itself, but is simple knowledge, or a form that holds a content up against nothing but itself and looks to see whether it is tautologous. {{ 233 }} Laws are no longer handed down but _tested;_ and for the consciousness that tests, the laws _already_ lie there as given; it takes their _content_ up just as it simply stands, without going into any consideration of the singularity and contingency that cling to that content's actuality, as we did, but instead halts at the commandment qua commandment and relates itself to it just as simply as the commandment is its standard.

{{{ 367 }}} This testing, however, does not reach far, and for just this reason: since the standard is tautology and is indifferent toward the content, it admits the one content into itself quite as readily as the opposite. — The question runs: is there to be, in and for itself, a law that _property_ exist; _in and for itself_, that is, and not on grounds of usefulness for other purposes; for ethical essentiality consists precisely in this, that the law be equal to itself alone and, through this equality with itself, be grounded in its own essence rather than be something conditioned. Taken in and for itself, property involves no self-contradiction; it is a determinateness that is _isolated_, or posited as equal to nothing but itself. Non-property, the ownerlessness of things, or a community of goods, is exactly as little self-contradictory. That a thing should belong to no one, or to whoever first seizes possession of it, or to everyone together, with each receiving what his need requires or an equal portion, is a _simple determinateness_, a _formal thought_, exactly like its opposite, property. — Of course, if the ownerless thing is regarded as a _necessary object_ of _need_, then it must necessarily pass into some singular individual's possession; and making the thing's freedom into the law instead would be contradictory. Nor, however, does the ownerlessness of the thing mean an absolute ownerlessness; the thing is rather supposed to _come into possession_ in keeping with the _need_ of the singular individual, and this not so that it may be stored up but so that it may be immediately {{{ 368 }}} used. But providing for need in a manner left wholly to contingency contradicts the nature of the conscious being, which is all that is at issue here; for such a being has to represent its need to itself under the form of _universality_, has to take care of its existence as a whole, and has to gain for itself a lasting good. Thus the thought that a thing falls by chance to the nearest self-conscious life in accordance with that life's need turned out not to agree with itself. — In a community of goods, where provision would be made in a universal and lasting way, either each receives as large a share _as he requires_, and then this inequality stands in contradiction with the essence of consciousness, for which the _equality_ of singular individuals is the principle. Or, following the latter principle, distribution is made _equally_, and then the share lacks the relation to need which alone is its concept.

Yet if non-property comes to look contradictory in this way, that is solely because it was not allowed to stand as a _simple_ determinateness. The same happens to property once it is resolved into moments. The singular thing which belongs to me as property therewith counts as something _universal, made fast, enduring;_ that, however, runs counter to its nature, since its nature lies in being used up and in _vanishing_. {{ 234 }} It counts at the same time as what is _mine_, which all the others recognize and {{{ 369 }}} shut themselves out from. But my being recognized contains, rather, my equality with everyone, which is exclusion's opposite. — What I possess is a _thing_, that is to say, a being for others in general, since to be only for me is something entirely universal and indeterminate; that _I_ possess it stands in contradiction with its universal thinghood. Property, then, is in contradiction with itself on all sides quite as much as non-property is; each of the two bears within it these opposed and mutually contradicting moments of singularity and universality. — But each of these determinatenesses, when represented _simply_, as property or as non-property, with no further development, is as _simple_ as the other, which is to say that it does not contradict itself. — The standard for law that reason possesses within itself thus suits anything whatever equally, and with that it is really no standard at all. — It would be an odd state of affairs, too, were tautology, the proposition of contradiction, conceded for the cognition of theoretical truth to be nothing but a formal criterion, that is, something wholly indifferent as between truth and untruth, and yet supposed for the cognition of practical _truth to be something more_.

In the two moments considered just now, in which the previously empty spiritual essence gets filled out, both the positing of immediate determinatenesses on the ethical substance and then the knowledge that asks of them whether they are laws have sublated themselves. The upshot {{{ 370 }}} accordingly seems to be that there can be no determinate laws and no knowledge of them either. Yet substance is _consciousness_ of itself as the absolute _essentiality_, and as such it can give up neither the _difference_ within it nor the _knowledge_ of that difference. The nullity that law-giving and law-testing turned out to have means this, that both of them, taken singly and in isolation, are merely _moments_ of the ethical consciousness with nothing to hold them up; and the movement in which the two make their appearance carries the formal sense that in it the ethical substance exhibits itself as consciousness.

Insofar as the two moments spell out more closely what the consciousness of _the matter at hand_ is, they may be taken as forms of _honesty_, which, having once busied itself with its formal moments, now busies itself with a content of the good and the right that is supposed to be, and with a testing of such fixed truth, and which takes itself to possess in healthy reason and judicious insight the force and validity of the commandments.

Without this honesty, however, it is not as the _essence_ of _consciousness_ that the laws count, and no more does testing count as a doing that goes on _within_ consciousness; instead, these moments, in stepping forward each on its own _immediately_ as an _actuality_, express, the first an invalid setting-up and being of actual laws, the second an equally invalid release from those laws. Qua determinate law, the law carries {{{ 371 }}} a contingent content, — and what that means here is that it is the law of a singular consciousness and has an arbitrary content. {{ 235 }} So that immediate law-giving is the tyrannical sacrilege which turns caprice into law and ethical life into obedience toward caprice, — toward laws which are _merely_ laws without being _commandments_ as well. And likewise the second moment, so far as it stands isolated, the testing of laws, signifies the setting in motion of what cannot be moved and the sacrilege of a knowledge which argues itself loose from the absolute laws and takes them for a caprice foreign to it.

Under both forms these moments stand in a negative relationship to the substance, that is, to the real spiritual essence; or, put otherwise, within them the substance has not yet attained its reality, consciousness still keeping it in the form of consciousness's own immediacy, so that it is so far only a _willing_ and _knowing_ on the part of this individual, or the _ought_ of an unactual commandment, together with a knowledge of formal universality. Now that these modes have sublated themselves, however, consciousness has withdrawn into the universal, and with that the oppositions have disappeared. The spiritual essence is actual substance precisely because these modes are not valid in their singleness but only as sublated; and the unity within which they count for no more than moments is consciousness's self, which, now that it is posited in the spiritual essence, {{{ 372 }}} renders that essence actual, filled out, and self-conscious.

For self-consciousness, then, the spiritual essence is at the outset law which has being _in itself_; the universality that belonged to testing, being the formal universality that did not have being _in itself_, has been sublated. It is likewise an eternal law that finds its ground not in the _will of this individual_; rather it is in and for itself, the absolute _pure will of all_, and this will bears the form of immediate _being_. Neither is it a _commandment_ that merely _ought_ to be; on the contrary, it _is_ and _is valid_; the universal I belonging to the category is what it amounts to, an I which is actuality immediately, and the world is nothing more than this actuality. But since this _law that has being_ is valid without qualification, the obedience of self-consciousness is not the service owed to a master whose orders would be so much caprice, and in which it would not cognize itself. The laws are, instead, thoughts belonging to its own absolute consciousness, which it itself immediately _has_. Nor does it _believe_ in them either, for faith does indeed look upon the essence as well, but upon an alien essence. Ethical _self_-consciousness is _immediately_ at one with the essence through the _universality_ of its _self_; faith, in contrast, takes its start from the _singular_ consciousness, and is that consciousness's movement of forever going toward this unity without ever reaching the presence of its essence. — The former consciousness, by contrast, has sublated itself as singular, this mediation has been carried out, {{{ 373 }}} and it is only by its having been carried out that such consciousness is the immediate self-consciousness of the ethical substance.

So the difference of self-consciousness from the essence is transparent through and through. {{ 236 }} In consequence the _differences within the essence_ itself are not contingent determinatenesses; because essence and self-consciousness form a unity, and inequality could arise from self-consciousness alone, these differences are the masses of an articulation permeated by the life of that unity, spirits that are clear to themselves and not split in two, unblemished heavenly shapes which in their differences maintain the unprofaned innocence and concord of their essence. — Toward them self-consciousness is, in the same way, a simple and clear _relationship_. They _are_, and nothing more, — this is what constitutes its consciousness of its relationship. Thus for the Antigone of Sophocles they count as the _unwritten_ and _infallible_ right of the gods, which lives not merely today and yesterday but everlastingly, and nobody knows whence it appeared. They _are_. Should I inquire into how they arose and narrow them down to the point at which they originated, I have already passed beyond them; for from that moment on it is I who am the universal, and they that are conditioned and restricted. If they are to legitimate themselves to my insight, then I have already shaken their unshakable being-in-itself, and I regard them as something that may be true and may equally well not be true for {{{ 374 }}} me. Ethical conviction consists precisely in this, that one stands unswervingly fast by what is right and refrains from all moving, shaking, and tracing it back. — Someone lodges a deposit with me; it _is_ another person's property, and I recognize it as such _because that is how it is_, and I hold myself unwavering within this relationship. Were I to hold on to the deposit for myself, I would commit no contradiction at all by the principle of my testing, namely tautology; for in that case I would no longer take it to be another person's property, and holding on to something that I do not take to be another person's property is entirely consistent. Altering _the view_ is no contradiction, for what matters is not the view qua view but the object and the content, and it is this that is supposed not to contradict itself. To the same degree that I am able — as indeed I do whenever I give something away — to replace the view that something is my property with the view that it is another person's property, without incurring any guilt of contradiction thereby, I am equally able to travel the road in the reverse direction. — Hence something is not right on the grounds that I find it free of self-contradiction; rather, because it is the right, it is right. That something _is_ the property of the other lies _at the foundation;_ I have no reasoning to carry on about it, nor am I to seek out all sorts of thoughts, connections, and considerations or let them come to mind; I am to think neither {{{ 375 }}} of law-giving nor of testing; by such movements of my thought I would displace the relationship just named, seeing that in fact I could, as I pleased, fit the opposite quite as well to my indeterminate tautological knowledge and so make that into the law. {{ 237 }} Whether this determination or its opposite is the right one is instead settled _in_ and _for itself_; left to myself I could make whichever one I wanted into the law, or just as easily neither, and once I set about testing I am already traveling an unethical road. Because the right is for me _in_ and _for itself_, I stand within the ethical substance; the substance is thus the _essence_ of self-consciousness, while self-consciousness is _its actuality_ and _existence_, its _self_ and _will_.
