## b. The Living Work of Art

The people that approaches its god in the cult belonging to the religion of art is the ethical people, which knows its state and that state's actions as its own will and its own accomplishment. This spirit, which steps up against the self-conscious people, {{{ 670 }}} is therefore not the light-essence that, selfless as it is, holds no certainty of the singulars within itself and is instead merely their universal essence and the imperious power in which they vanish… So the cult that belongs to the religion of this simple, shapeless essence returns to its adherents nothing in general beyond this, that they make up the people belonging to their god; it wins for them only their subsistence and simple substance at large, and not their actual self, which is on the contrary cast out. For the god they revere is empty depth rather than spirit. In the religion of art, by contrast, the cult does without that abstract _simplicity_ which belongs to the essence, and hence without the essence's _depth_. But the _essence_ which is _united with the self immediately_ is _in itself_ spirit and _knowing truth_, though not yet truth that has been known, or truth that knows itself in its own depth. {{ 386 }} Since the essence here has the self on it, its appearance is friendly toward consciousness, and within the cult consciousness obtains not merely the universal warrant of its subsistence but its existence as self-conscious within that essence as well; conversely, the essence has no selfless actuality in a repudiated people whose substance alone is granted recognition, but has its actuality in the people whose _self_ is recognized in its substance.

So there emerges out of the cult a self-consciousness satisfied within its essence, and a god who has entered into it as into his abode. For itself this _abode_ is substance's night, that is, substance's pure individuality, {{{ 671 }}} yet no longer the artist's tense individuality, which has not yet come to terms with an essence that is turning _objective_, but rather the satisfied night, which possesses its pathos on itself without needing anything, since it is returning from intuition, from objectivity that has been sublated. — Taken for itself, this _pathos_ is the essence of the _rising_, an essence that has by now, however, _gone down_ within itself and possesses on itself its going-down, namely self-consciousness and with it existence and actuality. — At this point it has traversed the movement of its actualization. Setting itself down from its pure essentiality into an objective natural force together with that force's expressions, it is an existence meant for the other, for the self that consumes it. In its fruit the quiet essence of selfless nature attains the stage at which nature, dressing itself and digested, offers itself to selfish life; in the usefulness of being edible and drinkable it arrives at its supreme perfection, since in that it is the possibility of an existence that is higher, and it touches upon spiritual existence; — grown in part into quietly potent substance, in part into spiritual fermentation, the earth-spirit in its metamorphosis has ripened over there into the feminine principle of nourishment, over here into the masculine principle of the self-propelling force of self-conscious existence.

So in this enjoyment that rising light-essence is betrayed for what it is; the enjoyment is the mystery {{{ 672 }}} of that essence. For the mystical does not consist in a secret's being hidden, nor in ignorance, but consists in this, that the self knows itself as one with the essence, so that the essence is thereby revealed. The self alone is revealed to itself; or rather, whatever is revealed is revealed only within an immediate certainty of itself. Within that certainty, however, the simple essence has been posited by the cult; as a usable thing it not only has the existence that is seen, felt, smelled and tasted, but is an object of desire as well, and actual enjoyment makes it one with the self, so that it is thereby wholly betrayed to the self and revealed to it. — Whatever is said to be revealed to reason and to the heart in truth remains secret, for there is still lacking the actual certainty that belongs to immediate existence, both the objective certainty and the enjoying one, which within religion is not merely the thoughtless immediate certainty but at the same time the purely knowing certainty of the self.

{{ 387 }} What has thereby been revealed through the cult to self-conscious spirit within spirit itself is the _simple_ essence, revealed as the movement whereby it partly steps up out of its nocturnal concealment into consciousness, so as to be the quietly nourishing substance of consciousness, and partly loses itself once more in the subterranean night, in the self, lingering above only with a quiet maternal longing. — The pure drive, though, is the many-named light-essence of the rising {{{ 673 }}} and its reeling life, a life which, having likewise let go of its abstract being, first gathered itself into the fruit's objective existence, then gave itself over to self-consciousness and reached its proper actuality within it, — and which now wanders about as a crowd of ecstatic women, nature's untamed reeling in self-conscious shape.

As yet, however, the only absolute spirit betrayed to consciousness is the one that is this simple essence, not the one that is spirit on itself, that is, only the _immediate_ spirit, nature's spirit. Its self-conscious life is therefore nothing but the mystery of bread and of wine, of Ceres and of Bacchus, and not that of the other gods, the properly higher ones, whose individuality encloses self-consciousness as such within itself as an essential moment. So spirit has not yet sacrificed itself to consciousness as _self-conscious_ spirit, and the mystery of bread and of wine has not yet become the mystery of flesh and of blood.

This unsteadied reeling of the god has to calm itself into an _object_, and the enthusiasm that never arrived at consciousness has to bring forth a work that steps up against it, just as the statue did against the enthusiasm of the earlier artist, as a work equally complete, yet not as one lifeless on itself but as a _living_ self. — A cult of this kind is the festival that the human being gives in his own honor, though into such a {{{ 674 }}} festival he does not yet put the significance of absolute essence; for what has so far been revealed to him is the _essence_, not yet spirit; not, that is, something which _essentially_ assumes human shape. But this cult lays the ground for that revelation and spreads out its moments singly. Thus here we have the essence's _abstract_ moment of living _corporeality_, as earlier we had the unity of the two in unconscious rapture. So the human being puts himself in the statue's place, as a shape trained and worked up into wholly free _movement_, while the statue is wholly free _repose_. {{ 388 }} While each singular individual knows how to present himself at the very least as a torchbearer, one among them rises out of the rest, one who is movement given shape, the polished working-out and fluid force of every limb; — an ensouled and living work of art, who joins strength to his beauty and to whom there falls, as the prize of his force, that ornament with which the statue used to be honored, and, in the stone god's stead, the honor among his people of being the supreme bodily presentation of their essence.

In both of the presentations that have just come up, the unity of self-consciousness and the spiritual essence is at hand, yet the balance between them is still missing. Within Bacchic enthusiasm the self is outside itself, whereas within beautiful corporeality the spiritual essence is. The dullness of that consciousness and its wild stammering must be received into the {{{ 675 }}} clear existence belonging to the latter, and the latter's spiritless clarity into the former's inwardness. Language is once again the complete element, the element in which inwardness is outward to the same degree that outwardness is inward, though it is neither the oracle's language, wholly contingent and singular in what it contains, nor the hymn of feeling that praises the singular god alone, nor the contentless stammering of Bacchic frenzy. Rather, language has gained its clear and universal content; its _clear_ content, since out of that first wholly substantial enthusiasm the artist has worked himself through to a shape that is an existence of his own, permeated in all its stirrings by the self-conscious soul and living along with it; — its _universal_ content, since in this festival, which is the honor of the human being, the one-sidedness of the statues disappears, statues that hold within them merely a national spirit, one determinate character of divinity. The beautiful fighter does count as the honor of his own particular people, and yet he is a corporeal singularity in which the fullness and gravity of meaning have gone under, along with the inner character of that spirit which bears the particular life, the concerns, the needs and the customs of his people. In this externalization into full corporeality spirit has set aside those particular impressions and resonances of nature which it used to enclose within itself while it was the people's actual spirit. Its people is therefore {{{ 676 }}} no longer conscious of its own particularity in him, but conscious rather of the setting aside of that particularity and of the universality that its human existence has.
