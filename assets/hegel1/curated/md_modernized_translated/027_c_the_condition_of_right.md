## {{{ 422 }}} c. The Condition of Right

The universal unity into which the immediate living unity of individuality and of substance goes back is the spiritless communal being, which has ceased to be the individuals' substance devoid of self-consciousness and in which they now count, in keeping with their singular being-for-itself, as self-essences and substances. The universal splintered into the atoms of the absolutely many individuals: this spirit that has died is an _equality_ where _all_ count as _each_, as _persons_. {{ 261 }} — What in the world of ethical life bore the name of the hidden divine law has in truth stepped out of its inner being into actuality; there the _singular_ was valid and actual only as the _family's_ universal _blood_. Taken as _this_ singular he was the _selfless departed_ spirit; but now he has stepped forth out of his unactuality. Since the ethical substance is nothing but _true_ spirit, spirit goes back into the _certainty_ of itself; it is that substance as the _positive_ universal, whereas its actuality consists in being a _negative_ universal _self_. — The powers and the shapes of the ethical world, we saw, sank into the simple necessity of empty _fate_. That power of theirs is the substance that reflects itself into its own simplicity; but the absolute essence in its self-reflection, precisely that necessity of empty fate, {{{ 423 }}} is nothing other than the _I_ of self-consciousness.

Accordingly the I now counts as the essence that is _in and for itself_; its substantiality is this _being-recognized_; but that substantiality is _abstract universality_, since what fills it is _this brittle self_ and not the self that has been dissolved in the substance.

Personality, then, has here stepped out of the ethical substance's life; it is the _actually valid_ self-subsistence of consciousness. The _unactual thought_ of personality, which comes about through _renunciation_ of _actuality_, has already appeared as _stoic_ self-consciousness; and as that consciousness proceeded from mastery and servitude, taken as _self-consciousness_ in its immediate existence, so too personality has emerged from immediate _spirit_ — which is the universal ruling will of all and equally their serving obedience. The _in-itself_ that stoicism possessed only in _abstraction_ has now become an _actual world_. Stoicism amounts to nothing but the consciousness that brings the principle of the condition of right, spiritless self-subsistence, to its abstract form; in fleeing _actuality_ it reached no more than the thought of self-subsistence; it is absolute for _itself_ precisely because it attaches its essence to no existence at all, but instead surrenders every existence and confines its essence to the unity of pure thinking alone. In just the same way, the right of the person is bound neither to a {{{ 424 }}} wealthier or mightier existence of the individual as such, nor to a living universal spirit, but instead to its abstract actuality as pure One, or to it as self-consciousness in general.

And as the _abstract_ self-subsistence of stoicism presented its own realization, so this latter will in turn repeat the movement of the first. {{ 262 }} The former passes over into the skeptical confusion of consciousness, into a prattling of the negative which, shapeless, strays from one contingency of being and of thought to the next, dissolving them, to be sure, in absolute self-subsistence, yet just as much generating them again, and which in fact is nothing but the contradiction of the self-subsistence and non-self-subsistence of consciousness. — Likewise the personal self-subsistence of _right_ is rather this same universal confusion, this same reciprocal dissolution. For self-consciousness, taken as the person's pure _empty One_, is what counts as the absolute essence. Confronted by this empty universality, the substance takes on the form of _fulfillment_ and of _content_, and that content is now left completely free and disordered; for the spirit that once subjugated it and bound it together within its unity is no longer present. — In its _reality_, therefore, this empty one of the person is a contingent existence and an essenceless moving and doing that arrives at no subsistence. Like skepticism, then, the formalism of right {{{ 425 }}} is by its concept devoid of any peculiar content; it comes upon a manifold subsistence, namely possession, and stamps upon it, as skepticism did, that same abstract universality through which it bears the name _property_. But whereas actuality so determined is in skepticism called _semblance_ in general and has only a negative value, in right it has a positive one. The negative value lies in this, that the actual carries the significance of the self as thinking, as what is universal _in itself_, while the positive one lies in this, that the actual is _mine_ in the significance of the category, as _a recognized_ and _actual_ validity. — Both amount to the same _abstract universal_; the content that is actual, or the _determinateness_ of what is mine — be it of an outward possession, or of the inner wealth or poverty of spirit and of character, finds no place in this empty form and is no concern of it. It therefore belongs to a _power of its own_, one that is something other than the formal universal, the power of contingency and arbitrariness. — In its very actual validity, therefore, the consciousness of right undergoes instead the loss of its own reality and its utter inessentiality, and to call an individual a _person_ is to voice contempt.

The content's free power gives itself this determination: that the scattering of the personal atoms into absolute _multiplicity_ is, by the very nature of such determinateness, at once collected into _one_ point foreign to them and equally {{{ 426 }}} spiritless, a point which on the one hand, like the brittleness of their personality, is purely singular actuality, but which, set against their empty singularity, at once carries the significance of all content and thereby of the real essence for them, and which, set against their supposedly absolute yet in itself essenceless actuality, constitutes the universal power and the absolute actuality. In this way this master of the world is for himself the absolute person, a person that at the same time encompasses all existence within itself, and for whose consciousness there exists no higher spirit. {{ 263 }} A person he is; but the lonely person who has stepped over against _all_; and it is these all that constitute the valid universality of the person, since the singular as such has truth only as the universal multiplicity of singularity, and cut off from that multiplicity the lonely self is in fact the unactual self, the self without force. — Simultaneously it is the content's consciousness, the content that has stepped over against that universal personality. This content, however, once released from its negative power, is the spiritual powers in their chaos: unfettered, they move against one another as elemental essences in wild excess, raving and destructive; their self-consciousness, devoid of force, is the powerless enclosure and the ground of their tumult. Knowing himself in this manner as the sum of all actual powers, this master of the world is that monstrous self-consciousness which knows itself as the actual god; yet because he is nothing more than the formal {{{ 427 }}} self, incapable of taming those powers, his movement and self-enjoyment are an excess just as monstrous.

In the destructive violence that he wields against the self of his subjects, which stands over against him, the master of the world has actual consciousness of what he is, namely the universal power of actuality. For his power is not that _oneness_ of spirit in which the persons would cognize their own self-consciousness; on the contrary, as persons they are for themselves and exclude continuity with others out of the absolute brittleness of their punctuality; hence their relationship is a merely negative one, to each other as much as to him, in whom their relation, their continuity, consists. As this continuity he constitutes the essence and the content of their formalism; but a content foreign to them, a hostile essence that in fact sublates the very thing which counts for them as their essence, their contentless being-for-itself; — and inasmuch as he is the continuity of their personality, it is precisely this that he destroys. The personality of right therefore experiences, as the content alien to it asserts its validity within it, and it asserts its validity in them because it is their reality, — rather its own substancelessness. The destructive rummaging in this essenceless ground gives itself, by contrast, the consciousness of its universal dominion, but this self is mere devastation, hence only outside itself, and is rather the casting away of its self-consciousness.

{{{ 428 }}} Such is the constitution of the side on which self-consciousness as absolute essence is _actual_. But out of this actuality the _consciousness thrust back into itself_ thinks this unessentiality of its own; earlier we saw the stoic self-subsistence of pure thinking pass through skepticism and arrive at its truth within the unhappy consciousness, — the truth as to how things stand with its being in and for itself. If this knowledge then appeared only as a one-sided view held by consciousness as such, here its _actual_ truth has entered on the scene. {{ 264 }} It consists in this: that the reality estranged from self-consciousness is precisely this _universal validity_ of its own. Such _validity_ is the self's universal actuality, though immediately it is just as much the inversion; it is the loss of the self's essence. — The actuality of the self that was absent from the ethical world has now been gained through its return into the _person_; what was at one in that world now comes forward developed, yet estranged from itself.
