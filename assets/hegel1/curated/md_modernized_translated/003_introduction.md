## Introduction

{{ 53 }} It is a natural representation that philosophy, before it takes up the matter itself, namely the actual cognition of what is in truth, must first come to terms with cognition, which is taken to be the tool whereby one seizes the absolute, or the means through which one glimpses it. The worry seems warranted: partly, that there might be various kinds of cognition, and among them one more adept than another at reaching this final purpose, so that one would choose wrongly among them, — partly, too, that since cognition is a capacity of a determinate kind and compass, one grasps clouds of error rather than the sky of truth so long as its nature and its limit go without closer determination. Indeed this worry is bound to change into the conviction that the entire enterprise of gaining for consciousness, through cognition, whatever is in-itself is by its own concept preposterous, and that a boundary falls between cognition and the absolute which parts them utterly. For should cognition be the tool for taking possession of the absolute essence, it strikes one at once that applying a tool to a matter, far from leaving it {{{ 4 }}} as it is for itself, undertakes a shaping and an alteration of it. Or should cognition not be a tool of our activity but, in a manner of speaking, a passive medium across which the light of truth travels to reach us, then even so we do not obtain truth as it is in itself, but as it stands by way of and within this medium. Either way what we employ is a means that immediately yields the opposite of its purpose; or rather, what is preposterous is that we should resort to a means at all. Admittedly this defect looks remediable through an acquaintance with how the _tool_ operates, since such acquaintance allows us to deduct from the result that share of the representation we get of the absolute through the tool which belongs to the tool itself, and so to keep the true unadulterated. Yet this improvement would in fact deposit us just where we already were. Strip from a shaped thing whatever the tool has wrought upon it, and the thing, — here the absolute — stands to us again at exactly what it was worth before an exertion thereby shown superfluous. Were the absolute merely to be brought nearer to us by the tool, without anything in it being altered, as a bird is by a limed twig, then, if it were not already in and for itself with us and willing to be so, it would surely mock this ruse; for in {{{ 5 }}} that case cognition would be a ruse, since through its manifold exertions it gives itself the air of doing something quite other than producing a merely immediate and hence effortless relation. {{ 54 }} Or if testing the cognition that we represent to ourselves as a _medium_ teaches us the law by which it refracts rays, it is likewise of no use to subtract that refraction from the result; for cognition is not the bending of the ray but the ray itself by which truth touches us, and with this subtracted, all that would have been indicated to us is the bare direction, or the empty place.

Meanwhile, once the worry about lapsing into error has planted a mistrust of science, which sets about the work itself without scruples of this sort and actually cognizes, there is no seeing why the reverse should not hold: why a mistrust of that mistrust should not be planted, and the worry raised that this fear of erring is itself already the error. For it does presuppose something as truth, a good deal in fact, and props its scruples and its conclusions on what would itself first have to be tested for whether it is truth. What it presupposes is this: _representations_ of _cognition_ as a _tool_ and as a _medium_, along with a _difference of ourselves from this cognition_; and chiefly this, that the absolute is stationed _on one side_ while _cognition on the other side_, on its own and cut off from the absolute, is nonetheless something real, which is to say that cognition, {{{ 6 }}} standing as it does outside the absolute and therefore surely outside truth as well, is nevertheless truthful; a supposition by which whatever names itself fear of error rather betrays itself as fear of the truth.

This consequence arises because only the absolute is true, or only the true is absolute. It can be rejected by way of the distinction that a cognition which does not, as science would have it, cognize the absolute may still be true, and that cognition in general, though incapable of grasping the absolute, might still be capable of some other truth. But by now we can see that talk going back and forth like this comes down to a murky distinction between an absolute true and some other true, and that words like the absolute and cognition and the rest presuppose a meaning that it is our first business to attain.

Instead of bothering with such useless representations and turns of phrase about cognition as a tool with which to master the absolute, or as a medium letting us glimpse the truth and so on, — relationships that all these representations of a cognition cut off from the absolute and an absolute cut off from cognition no doubt amount to, — instead of the excuses that the incapacity of science draws from presupposing {{{ 7 }}} such relationships, so as at once to free itself from the labor of science and to lend itself the look of a serious and zealous effort, — as well as instead of wrangling with answers to all of this, these representations could be thrown out straightaway as contingent and arbitrary, and the use of words that goes with them, such as the absolute, cognition, also the objective and the subjective, and countless others whose meaning is presupposed as commonly familiar, could even be viewed as a fraud. {{ 55 }} For the pretense, partly that their meaning is commonly familiar, partly also that one is oneself in possession of their concept, looks rather like a way of sparing oneself the main task, namely that of supplying this concept. With better right, by contrast, the effort of paying any attention whatever to such representations and turns of phrase, which are meant to ward science off, could be dispensed with, since they constitute nothing but an empty appearance of knowledge, one that disappears the moment science steps forward. But in the very fact that it steps forward, science is itself an appearance; its stepping forward is not yet science carried through and spread out in its truth. It makes no difference here whether one represents _it_ as being the appearance because it steps forward _alongside something else_, or calls that other, untrue knowledge its appearing. Science, however, must {{{ 8 }}} rid itself of this semblance; and its only way of doing so is to turn against it. For science can neither merely reject a knowledge that is not truthful as a common view of things, assuring us that it is itself a wholly different cognition and that such knowledge is nothing at all for it, nor can it appeal to an intimation of something better within that knowledge itself. By that _assurance_ science would be declaring its _being_ to be its force; but untrue knowledge makes the very same appeal, _that it is_, while _assuring_ us that science is nothing to it; and _one_ dry assurance is worth precisely as much as the next. Still less can science appeal to the better intimation supposedly at hand within untruthful cognition, as the pointer within that cognition toward science itself; for on one side it would once more be appealing to a being, and on the other to itself as it stands within untruthful cognition, that is, to a bad manner of its being, and so to its appearance instead of to its being in and for itself. For this reason the exposition of appearing knowledge is to be undertaken here.

Since this exposition has only appearing knowledge for its object, it does not itself seem to be science moving freely in its own peculiar shape; from this standpoint it may instead be taken as the road of natural consciousness pressing on toward true knowledge {{{ 9 }}}, or as the road of the soul journeying through the whole series of its configurations, stations that its own nature has staked out for it in advance, so that it may purify itself into spirit by attaining, through the complete experience of itself, acquaintance with what it is in its own self.

{{ 56 }} Natural consciousness will prove to be merely the concept of knowledge, or knowledge that is not real. But because it takes itself immediately for real knowledge instead, this road bears a negative meaning for it, and what amounts to the realization of the concept counts with it rather as a loss of its own self; for along this road it loses its truth. It may accordingly be viewed as the road of _doubt_, or, more properly, as the road of despair; for what happens along it is not what people usually mean by doubting, a jostling of this or that presumed truth, after _which_ the doubt duly vanishes again and one comes back to the truth in question, so that at the end the matter is taken as it was before. It is instead the conscious insight into the untruth of appearing knowledge, for which what is most real is in truth only the unrealized concept. This skepticism that carries itself out to completion is therefore also not what a serious zeal for truth and science imagines it has made itself ready {{{ 10 }}} and equipped with for science; namely the _resolve_ not to surrender oneself in science to the authority of other people's thoughts, but to test everything for oneself and to go by one's own conviction alone, or better yet, to bring forth everything oneself and hold only one's own deed to be the true. The series of configurations which consciousness runs through on this road is rather the detailed history of the _cultivation_ of consciousness itself up to science. That resolve represents cultivation, in the simple manner of a resolve, as immediately settled and done with; this road, however, is the actual carrying out of it, over against that untruth. Following one's own conviction is admittedly more than submitting to authority; but the inversion of holding something to be so on authority into holding it to be so out of one's own conviction does not necessarily change the content of what is held, nor has truth thereby taken the place of error. Being stuck in the system of opining and of prejudice, whether on the authority of others or out of one's own conviction, differs only through the vanity that attaches to the latter way. Skepticism directed at the whole range of appearing consciousness, by contrast, is what first makes spirit competent to test what truth is, in that it brings about a despair over the so-called natural representations, thoughts, and opinions, {{{ 11 }}} which it makes no difference to call one's own or another's, and with which the consciousness that sets about testing _straight away_ is still filled and burdened, thereby being in fact incapable of what it means to undertake.

{{ 57 }} The _completeness_ of the forms of consciousness that is not real will emerge of itself through the necessity of the progression and of the interconnection. To make this comprehensible, one may note beforehand, in general terms, that when untruthful consciousness is exhibited in its untruth, the movement involved is not a merely _negative_ one. Natural consciousness, to be sure, holds just such a one-sided view of the exposition; and a knowledge that makes this one-sidedness into its essence ranks among the shapes of incomplete consciousness, a shape falling within the course of the road itself, where it will present itself. This one-sidedness is skepticism, which in the result never sees more than _pure nothing_, and leaves out of account that this nothing is the determinate nothing _of that_ _out of which it results_. The nothing, though, is in fact the truthful result only when it is grasped as the nothing of whatever it issues from; and so it is itself something _determinate_ and possesses a _content_. The skepticism that ends with the abstraction of nothing, or of emptiness, cannot advance any further from it but must wait to see whether and what novelty presents itself to it, so as to cast that novelty into the same empty {{{ 12 }}} abyss. If, on the other hand, one apprehends the result as it is in truth, namely as _determinate_ negation, then a new form has at once sprung up out of it, and within the negation the transition has been effected by which the progression through the full series of shapes comes about of itself.

The _goal_, however, is staked out for knowledge with the same necessity as the series of the progression; it lies at the point where knowledge has no further need to pass out beyond its own self, where it comes upon itself, and where concept corresponds to object, object to concept. The progression toward this goal is on that account unstoppable as well, nor is satisfaction to be found at any station short of it. Anything confined to a natural life cannot of its own power pass out beyond its immediate existence; something else drives it out past that existence, and this being torn away is its death. Consciousness, however, is for its own self its _concept_, and is thereby immediately the going beyond what is restricted and, since this restricted thing belongs to it, beyond its own self; along with the singular, the beyond is posited for it at the same time, even if only, as in spatial intuition, _alongside_ what is restricted. Consciousness thus suffers this violence at its own hands, spoiling for itself its restricted satisfaction. Feeling this violence, anxiety in the face of truth may well draw back and struggle to preserve what is threatened with loss. {{{ 13 }}} But it can find no rest; it may want to stand still in thoughtless inertia, yet thought cripples thoughtlessness and the restlessness of thought disturbs the inertia; or it may dig itself in as sentimentality, whose assurance is that everything is _good in its kind_; and that assurance suffers violence from reason just as much, since reason finds a thing not good precisely insofar as it is a kind. {{ 58 }} Or the fear of truth may screen itself, from its own eyes as much as from other people's, behind the semblance that it is precisely the burning zeal for truth itself which makes it so hard, indeed impossible, for it to come upon any truth other than the sole truth of vanity: that of being ever more clever than any thoughts one may have, whether one's own or another's; and this vanity, which knows how to foil every truth so as to withdraw out of it into itself, and which gorges on this understanding of its own, an understanding that can always dissolve every thought and, in place of all content, come up with nothing but the dry I, is a satisfaction that has to be left to its own devices, since it shuns the universal and pursues being-for-itself alone.

Since so much has now been said provisionally and in general about how the progression goes and why it is necessary, it may still be useful to recall something concerning _the method of carrying it out_. Once this exposition is represented as a _relating_ of _science_ to _appearing_ knowledge, and as an _investigation_ {{{ 14 }}} and a _testing of cognition's reality_, it seems unable to proceed without laying down some presupposition or other as its _standard_. For testing consists in applying an assumed standard, and, in the equality or inequality with that standard which then results for whatever is being tested, lies the decision that it is either correct or incorrect; and the standard as such, and science too if science were the standard, is in all this assumed to be the _essence_ or _the in itself_. Here, however, where science is only first stepping forward, neither science itself nor anything else has vindicated itself as being the essence or the in itself; and lacking any such thing, no testing seems possible at all.

This contradiction and its removal will emerge more determinately if we first recall the abstract determinations of knowledge and of truth as they occur in consciousness. Consciousness, namely, _distinguishes_ from itself something to which it at the same time _relates_ itself; or, as the point is put, something is _for it_; and the determinate side of this _relating_, of the _being_ that something has _for a consciousness_, is _knowledge_. From this being for another, however, we distinguish _being in itself_; what is related to knowledge is equally distinguished from knowledge and posited as _being_ outside this relation as well; the side of this in itself is called _truth_. What these determinations {{{ 15 }}} amount to in their own right need not occupy us any further at this point, for since appearing knowledge is our object, its determinations too are taken up at first just as they immediately offer themselves; and it is surely as they have been grasped here that they do offer themselves.

Now if we investigate the truth of knowledge, we seem to be investigating what knowledge is _in itself_. But in an investigation of this sort knowledge is _our_ object, something _for us_; hence the _in itself_ of it that would come out would rather be its being _for us_, and what we asserted to be its essence would be not its truth but only our knowledge of it. {{ 59 }} On such a showing the essence, or the standard, would lie within us, and whatever was to be measured against it, and to be decided about by that comparison, would be under no necessity to acknowledge it.

But the object we are investigating is of such a nature that it relieves us of this separation, or of this semblance of separation and presupposition. Consciousness supplies its standard from within its own self, so that the investigation turns into consciousness comparing itself with itself; for the distinction just now drawn falls inside consciousness. Within consciousness one thing is _for an_ other, or consciousness carries in it the determinateness of the moment of knowledge at all events; at the same time this other is for consciousness not merely _for it_ but also outside that relation, that is, _in itself_; {{{ 16 }}} the moment of truth. In whatever, then, consciousness within its own self declares the _in itself_ or the _true_ to be, we have the standard that consciousness itself sets up for measuring its knowledge against. If we call _knowledge_ the _concept_, and the essence or the _true_ what has being, the _object_, then the testing lies in watching whether concept corresponds to object. But if we instead call _the essence_, that is, the in itself, _the concept of the object_, while by the _object_ we understand the object as _object_, namely as it is _for another_, then the testing lies in watching whether the object, for its part, corresponds to its concept. It is plain enough that the two come to the same; what matters, though, is to hold this fast for the whole investigation, that both these moments, _concept and object, being for another_, and being in _its own self_, fall within the very knowledge we are investigating, so that we have no need to bring standards along with us or to apply _our_ notions and thoughts in the course of the investigation; dropping these is how we manage to consider the matter as it is _in_ and _for itself_.

Now an addition on our part is rendered superfluous not merely in this respect, that consciousness itself already harbors concept and object, standard and what gets tested; we are also spared the labor of comparing the two and the _testing_ proper, so that, since {{{ 17 }}} consciousness tests its own self, here too nothing is left to us but sheer watching. For consciousness is, on one side, consciousness of the object, and on the other, consciousness of its own self: consciousness of what for it is the true, and consciousness of its knowledge of that. Since both are _for it_, consciousness is their comparison itself; it comes to be _for it_ whether or not its knowledge of the object stands in correspondence with the object. Admittedly the object seems to be for consciousness only in the way consciousness knows it; consciousness seems, as it were, unable to get round behind the object to see how it is _not for consciousness_ but rather how it is _in itself_, and hence unable as well to test its knowledge against the object. Yet in the very fact that consciousness knows of an object at all, the difference is already at hand that for _it_ one thing is the _in itself_, while another moment is knowledge, the object's being _for_ consciousness. {{ 60 }} The testing rests on this distinction, which is present. If in this comparison the two fail to correspond, consciousness seems bound to alter its knowledge so as to make it conform to the object; but when the knowledge alters, the object itself in fact alters for consciousness too, since the knowledge at hand was essentially knowledge of that object; with the knowledge the object likewise becomes another, for it belonged essentially to this knowledge. In this way it dawns on consciousness that what {{{ 18 }}} previously was for it the _in itself_ is not in itself at all, or that it was only _***for it*** in itself_. So when consciousness finds, in the case of its object, that its knowledge does not correspond to that object, the object itself gives way as well; or the standard of the testing shifts once whatever it was meant to serve as standard for fails to hold up in the testing; and what gets tested is not knowledge alone but its standard along with it.

This _dialectical_ movement, which consciousness carries out on its own self, on its knowledge no less than on its object, _insofar as the new true object_ thereby _springs up_ for it, is precisely what goes by the name of _experience_. In this connection one further moment in the course just mentioned is to be brought out more closely, a moment that will shed new light on the scientific side of the exposition that follows. Consciousness knows _something_, and what it has there as the essence, as the _in itself_, is this object; but the object is the _in itself_ for consciousness as well, and thereby the ambiguity of this true enters in. Two objects, we see, are now before consciousness: the one is the first _in itself_, the other _the being-for-consciousness of this in itself_. This second one looks at the outset like nothing but consciousness's reflection into its own self, a representing not of an object but merely of its knowledge of that _first_ object. But as was shown a moment ago, in the course of this the first {{{ 19 }}} object alters for consciousness; it stops being the in itself and turns into something that is only the _in itself for consciousness_; and so what is then the true is this: _the being-for-consciousness of this in itself_, which is to say that here we have the _essence_, or consciousness's _object_. This new object contains the nullity of the first; it is the experience made about that first object.

One moment attaches to this exposition of how experience runs its course on account of which the exposition seems not to square with what experience is ordinarily taken to mean. The transition, namely, which starts from the first object together with the knowledge of it and arrives at the other object _about which_ experience is said to have been made, was specified thus: that the knowledge of the first object, or what the first in itself is _for_ consciousness, is itself to become the second object. {{ 61 }} Whereas it otherwise seems that we make the experience of the untruth of our first concept _on another_ object, one we happen to come upon contingently and externally, so that what falls within us is nothing but the pure _apprehending_ of what is in and for itself. On the view just given, though, the new object exhibits itself as having come to be through a _reversal of consciousness_ itself. Our own addition is this way of regarding the matter, through which the series of consciousness's experiences is raised into a scientific course; and it is an addition not present for the consciousness we are observing. But {{{ 20 }}} this is in fact the very circumstance already discussed above when the relationship of the present exposition to skepticism came up, namely that the result reached in each case on an untruthful knowledge must not collapse into an empty nothing, but has necessarily to be apprehended as the nothing _of that whose result_ it is; a result which holds within it whatever of truth the preceding knowledge has. This presents itself here in the following way: since what at first appeared as the object descends, for consciousness, into a knowledge of it, and the _in itself_ turns into a _being of the in itself for consciousness_, this is the new object, with which a new shape of consciousness also steps forward, one for which something other is the essence than was for the preceding shape. It is this circumstance that guides the whole succession of the shapes of consciousness in their necessity. What goes on for us, as it were, behind consciousness's back is only this necessity itself, the _emergence_ of that new object as it offers itself to consciousness, which has no idea what is happening to it. There thereby enters into its movement a moment of the _in itself_, that is, of _being-for-us_, a moment that does not present itself to the consciousness caught up in the experience itself; the _content_, however, of what emerges for us is _for it_, and we comprehend only the formal side of it, its pure emerging; _for it_ {{{ 21 }}} what has emerged is only an object, while _for us_ it is at the same time a movement and a becoming.

Through this necessity, this road to science is itself already _science_, and, in respect of its content, therefore the science of the _experience of consciousness_.

The experience that consciousness undergoes concerning itself can, by its concept, comprehend within itself nothing short of the whole system of consciousness, the whole kingdom of spirit's truth, in such a way that the moments of that truth exhibit themselves under this peculiar determinateness, not as abstract, pure moments, but as they stand for consciousness, or as consciousness comes forward itself in its relating to them, so that the moments of the whole amount to _shapes of consciousness_. {{ 62 }} As consciousness drives itself onward to its true existence, it will reach a point at which it lays aside the semblance of being burdened with something alien that is only for it and is there as another, or where appearance becomes equal to essence, so that its exposition coincides at just this point with the proper science of spirit, and finally, in grasping this its own essence, consciousness will designate the nature of absolute knowing itself.
