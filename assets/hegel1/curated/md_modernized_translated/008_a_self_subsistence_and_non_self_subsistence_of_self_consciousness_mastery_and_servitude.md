## {{{ 114 }}} A. Self-Subsistence and Non-Self-Subsistence of Self-Consciousness; Mastery and Servitude

Self-consciousness is _in_ and _for itself_ insofar as, and because, it is for another something in and for itself; that is, its being lies wholly in being recognized. The concept of this its unity in its doubling, the concept of the infinity that realizes itself within self-consciousness, is an intertwining of many sides and many senses, so that its moments must partly be held rigorously apart, and partly, within this very distinguishing, be grasped and cognized as at once undistinguished as well, or always in the significance opposed to their own. That what is distinguished carries a double sense has its ground in the essence of self-consciousness, which is to be infinite, that is, immediately the contrary of whatever determinateness it gets posited in. What sets the movement of _recognition_ before us is the unfolding of this spiritual unity's concept in its doubling.

Another self-consciousness is there for self-consciousness; it has come to be _outside itself_. {{{ 115 }}} This carries a doubled meaning: _first_, it has lost its own self, since what it finds itself to be is an _other_ essence; _second_, the other has thereby been sublated by it, for what it sees as essence is not the other either, but _its own self_ in the other.

It must sublate this, _its otherness_; that is the sublating of the first double sense and hence a second double sense in its own right: _first_, it must aim at sublating _the other_ self-subsistent essence, thereby gaining certainty of _itself_ as the essence; _second_, its aim in this is to sublate _its own self_, since this other is itself.

This double-sensed sublating of its double-sensed otherness is just as much a double-sensed return _into itself;_ for _first_, by the sublating it recovers its own self, becoming equal to itself once more through the sublating of _its_ otherness; but _second_, the other self-consciousness is in the same way restored to itself by it, for it was present to itself in the other, and in sublating this, _its_ being in the other, it releases the other back into freedom.

{{ 110 }} This movement whereby self-consciousness relates itself to another self-consciousness has, however, been depicted in this fashion, as _the doing of the one;_ but that doing of the one itself carries the doubled meaning of being _its own doing_ quite as much as _the doing of the other;_ for the other is equally self-subsistent, sealed within itself, and nothing is in it that is not there through itself. The first {{{ 116 }}} does not have the object before it in the way the object at first is merely for desire, but rather has a self-subsistent object that is for itself, and over that object it can by itself accomplish nothing unless the object does to its own self what the first does to it. The movement is thus, plainly and simply, the doubled movement of both self-consciousnesses. What each sees _the other_ doing is the very same thing that _it_ does itself; each does on its own part what it requires of the other; and hence does what it does _only_ to the extent that the other does the same; a doing that stayed one-sided would be pointless, since what is to happen can be brought off by the two alone.

The doing, then, is double-sensed not only in that it is a doing directed just as much _upon itself_ as _upon the other_, but also in that, undivided, it is just as much _the doing of the one_ as _of the other_.

In this movement we watch the process that displayed itself as the play of forces recur, but now within consciousness. What there held only for us holds here for the extremes themselves. Self-consciousness is the middle, and it decomposes into the extremes; each extreme, for its part, consists in this exchange of its own determinateness, this absolute transition into its opposite. As consciousness, though, it certainly does get _outside itself_, and yet in this being-outside-itself it is at the same time held back within itself, _for itself_, and its outside-itself is _for it_. For it there is this: that it immediately _is_ another consciousness and {{{ 117 }}} _is not;_ and likewise that this other is only for itself insofar as it sublates itself as something that is for itself, and is for itself only inside the other's being-for-itself. To the other, each is the middle by means of which each mediates and joins together with itself, and each is, both to itself and to the other, an essence that immediately is for itself, an essence which at once is so for itself only by way of this mediation. They _recognize_ themselves as _mutually recognizing one another_.

We must now consider this pure concept of recognition, of the doubling of self-consciousness within its unity, in respect of the way its process makes its appearance for self-consciousness. It will first set forth the two in the aspect of their _inequality_, or the stepping-out of the middle into the extremes, which as extremes are opposed to each other, the one being merely something recognized, the other merely something recognizing.

To begin with, self-consciousness is simple being-for-itself, self-equal by excluding all that is _other from itself;_ what counts for it as essence and absolute object is _I;_ and it is _a singular_ in this _immediacy_, or in this _being_ that belongs to its being-for-itself. {{ 111 }} Whatever is other for it stands there as an unessential object, one stamped with the character of the negative. The other, however, is a self-consciousness too; one individual steps forth confronting another individual. Stepping forth thus _immediately_, they are for one another after the manner of ordinary objects; _self-subsistent_ shapes, consciousnesses submerged in the _being_ {{{ 118 }}} of _life_ — for it is as life that the object with being has determined itself here — consciousnesses which, _for one another_, have not yet carried through the movement of absolute abstraction, the movement of wiping out every immediate being and of being nothing but the pure negative being which self-equal consciousness is, and which have not yet set themselves before one another as pure _being-for-itself_, that is, as _self_-consciousness. Each is indeed certain of its own self, though not of the other, so that the certainty it has of itself possesses as yet no truth; for its truth would lie solely in this, that its own being-for-itself had presented itself to it as a self-subsistent object, or, what amounts to the same, that the object had presented itself as this pure certainty of its own self. By the concept of recognition, however, this is possible only in the following way: that just as the other is for it, so it is for the other, and each in its own self, through its own doing and once again through the doing of the other, brings this pure abstraction of being-for-itself to completion.

What the _presentation_ of itself, taken as self-consciousness's pure abstraction, amounts to, however, is showing itself to be the pure negation of its own objective manner, or showing that it is tied to no determinate _existence_, not tied to the universal singularity of existence at all, not tied to life. This presentation is the _doubled_ doing: the doing of the other, and the doing through one's own self. Insofar as it is the doing _of the other_, each therefore aims at {{{ 119 }}} the death of the other. But therein the second is also present, _the doing through one's own self_, since the former includes within itself the staking of one's own life. The relationship of the two self-consciousnesses is accordingly determined thus: that through a struggle of life and death they _corroborate_ themselves and each other. — They must enter this struggle, for they have to raise the certainty of their own selves, of _being for themselves_, into truth in the other and in themselves. And the staking of life is the sole thing by which freedom, by which it is corroborated, that for self-consciousness the essence is neither _being_, nor the _immediate_ manner in which it steps forth, nor its submersion in the spread of life, — but rather that nothing is to be found in it which would not be for it a moment that vanishes, that it is nothing but pure _being-for-itself_. The individual who has not risked life can no doubt be recognized as a _person;_ but it has not attained the truth of being thus recognized as a self-subsistent self-consciousness. {{ 112 }} Equally, each must go for the death of the other just as it stakes its own life; for the other counts to it as no more than its own self; its essence presents itself to it as another, it is outside itself; it must sublate its being-outside-itself; the other is a consciousness with being, entangled in manifold ways; it must intuit its otherness as pure being-for-itself, or as absolute negation.

{{{ 120 }}} This corroboration by way of death, however, sublates the truth that was meant to issue from it, and with that truth it sublates the certainty of one's own self altogether; for if life is the _natural_ position of consciousness, self-subsistence lacking absolute negativity, then death is its _natural_ negation, a negation lacking self-subsistence, and one that therefore remains without the significance recognition calls for. Death has indeed produced the certainty that both risked their lives and held life in contempt, in themselves and in the other; yet not for those who survived this struggle, who sublate their consciousness as posited in that alien essentiality, natural existence, or who sublate themselves and are sublated as the _extremes_ that wanted to be for themselves. With this, though, the essential moment disappears from the play of exchange, namely the decomposing of itself into extremes of opposed determinatenesses; the middle collapses into a dead unity, one decomposed into dead extremes that merely have being and are not opposed; and the two do not give and receive each other back mutually through consciousness, but only release each other indifferently, as things. What they have done is abstract negation, not the negation of consciousness, which _sublates_ in such a way that it _stores up_ and _preserves_ what has been sublated, and so survives its own being sublated.

{{{ 121 }}} In this experience self-consciousness comes to find that life matters to it just as essentially as pure self-consciousness does. Within immediate self-consciousness the absolute object is the simple I, which for us, or in itself, is nevertheless absolute mediation and has subsisting self-subsistence for its essential moment. That simple unity dissolves, and this dissolution is what the first experience results in; through it a pure self-consciousness is posited, alongside a consciousness that is not purely for itself but rather for another, that is, a consciousness that _has being_, or consciousness having the shape of _thinghood_. Both of these moments are essential; — inasmuch as they are at first unequal and opposed, with no reflection of theirs into unity having yet come about, they stand as two opposed shapes of consciousness: the self-subsistent one, whose essence is being-for-itself, and the non-self-subsistent one, whose essence is life, or being for another; the first of these is the _master_, the second the _servant_.

The master is consciousness that is _for itself_, no longer the mere concept of such consciousness but consciousness being for itself whose mediation with itself runs through an _other_ consciousness, one whose essence involves its being synthesized with self-subsistent _being_, or with thinghood as such. {{ 113 }} Two moments are what the master relates to: a _thing_ as such, {{{ 122 }}} which is the object of desire, and the consciousness for which what is essential is thinghood; and since a) he is, as the concept of self-consciousness, an immediate relation of _being-for-itself_, while b) he is now at the same time mediation, a being-for-itself that is for itself only through another, he relates a) in an immediate way to both, and b) in a mediated way to each of them through the other. The master relates _to the servant in a mediated way, through self-subsistent being;_ for this is exactly what holds the servant bound; it is the chain he could not abstract from during the struggle, which is why he proved to be non-self-subsistent, proved to have his self-subsistence in thinghood. The master, by contrast, holds power over this being, since in the struggle he proved that it is valid for him merely as something negative; and as he is the power over it while this being is the power over the other, he keeps that other beneath him in this syllogism. In the same way the master relates _to the thing in a mediated way, through the servant;_ being self-consciousness as such, the servant likewise relates negatively to the thing and sublates it; but for him the thing is self-subsistent at the same time, so that his negating cannot finish it off to the point of annihilation, and he merely _works on_ it. For the master, on the other hand, this mediation _yields_ the _immediate_ relation as the pure negation of the thing, that is, _enjoyment;_ what desire did not manage, he manages: to have done with the thing and to satisfy himself in enjoyment. {{{ 123 }}} Desire did not manage this, on account of the thing's self-subsistence; but the master, having pushed the servant in between himself and the thing, thereby joins together only with the thing's non-self-subsistence and enjoys it purely; the side of self-subsistence he leaves to the servant, who works on it.

In these two moments the master comes to be recognized by another consciousness; for in them the other consciousness posits itself as the unessential one, first in working on the thing, then in its dependence on a determinate existence; in neither can it become master over being and reach absolute negation. Present here, then, is this moment of recognition: the other consciousness sublates its own being-for-itself and in doing so does itself what the first does to it. So too the other moment, that this doing of the second is the first one's doing of its own; for what the servant does is in truth the master's doing; to the master, being-for-itself alone is essence; he is pure negative power, to which the thing is Nothing, and hence the pure essential doing within this relationship; the servant's doing, however, is not pure but unessential. But recognition proper lacks the moment in which the master would also do to himself what he does to the other, and the servant would also do to the other what he does to himself. What has thereby {{{ 124 }}} come about is a one-sided and unequal recognition.

{{ 114 }} Here the unessential consciousness is, for the master, the object in which the _truth_ of his certainty of his own self consists. But it comes to light that this object fails to answer to its concept, and that in the very place where the master has brought himself to completion, what has come to be for him is something else entirely than a self-subsistent consciousness. It is not such a consciousness that is there for him, but a non-self-subsistent one; so he has no certainty _of being-for-itself_ as the truth; on the contrary, his truth is the unessential consciousness and the unessential doing that belongs to it.

Accordingly, the _truth_ of the self-subsistent consciousness is the _servile consciousness_. The latter does indeed appear at first to be _outside_ itself, and appears not to be self-consciousness's truth. Yet mastery showed its essence to be the inverted form of what it wills to be, and likewise servitude, once it reaches its completion, will surely turn into the contrary of what it immediately is; being a consciousness _pressed back_ into itself, it will withdraw into itself and reverse itself into true self-subsistence.

We have seen only what servitude is within the relationship of mastery. But it is self-consciousness, and what it accordingly is in and for its own self is what we must now consider. At first the master is the essence for {{{ 125 }}} servitude; hence _the self-subsistent consciousness that is for itself_ counts for it as _the truth_, a truth that, _***for it***_, is nevertheless not yet _in it_. Yet this truth of negativity in its purity and of _being-for-itself it does in fact have in its own self;_ for it has _experienced_ this essence within itself. For this consciousness has been anxious not over this or that, nor for this or that moment, but over its whole essence; since what it has felt is the fear of death, of the absolute master. Therein it was inwardly dissolved, it shook through and through within its own self, and everything fixed quaked inside it. But this pure universal movement, the absolute becoming-fluid of all subsistence, is what constitutes the simple essence of self-consciousness, namely absolute negativity, _pure being-for-itself_, which is thereby _in_ this consciousness. Pure being-for-itself as this moment is _for it_ too, since in the master it is its _object_. Furthermore, it is not merely this universal dissolution _in general_, for in serving it brings that dissolution about _actually;_ therein it sublates, across all the _singular_ moments, its attachment to natural existence, and works that existence away.

The feeling of absolute power, however, whether taken in general or in the singular details of service, amounts only to dissolution _in itself_, and even granted that fear of the master makes the start of wisdom, consciousness in that fear is _for its own self_, not _being-for-itself_. Through work, though, it arrives at its own self. At the moment {{{ 126 }}} that answers to desire in the master's consciousness, it did look as though the side of the unessential relation to the thing had fallen to the serving consciousness, since there the thing retains its self-subsistence. {{ 115 }} Desire had kept back for itself the pure negating of its object and, along with that, the unmixed feeling of self. For just that reason, however, the satisfaction is itself nothing but a vanishing, since it lacks the _objective_ side, or _subsistence_. Work, by contrast, is _inhibited_ desire, a vanishing _held up_, or, to put it otherwise, it _shapes_. What relates negatively to the object turns into that object's _form_, and into something _enduring;_ precisely because for the one who works the object possesses self-subsistence. This _negative_ middle, the forming _doing_, is at once _singularity_, the pure being-for-itself of consciousness, which in work now steps outside consciousness into the element of what endures; by this route, then, the working consciousness arrives at the intuition of self-subsistent being _as its own self_.

Forming, however, carries not merely that positive significance whereby the serving consciousness, within it, becomes for itself, as pure _being-for-itself_, something _that has being;_ it carries a negative significance as well, directed against its first moment, fear. For in shaping the thing, its own negativity, its being-for-itself, becomes an object to it only through its sublating the opposed _form_ that has being. But that alien essence, the one it trembled {{{ 127 }}} before, is exactly this objective _negative_. Now, however, it destroys this alien negative and posits _itself_ as such a negative in the element of what endures; and thereby it becomes, _for its own self_, something _that is for itself_. In the master, being-for-itself is for it _another_, or is only _for it;_ under fear, being-for-itself lies _in its own self;_ in shaping, being-for-itself comes to be for it as _its own_, and it becomes conscious that it is itself in and for itself. Being _posited outward_ does not make the form into something other than it; for this very form is its pure being-for-itself, which therein becomes truth for it. Through this rediscovery of itself by its own means, then, it becomes _a mind of its own_, and precisely in the work in which it had seemed to be only _a mind belonging to another_. — For this reflection both moments are necessary, fear and service in general as well as shaping, and both at once in a universal manner. Lacking the discipline of service and obedience, fear stops short at the merely formal and does not spread over the conscious actuality of existence. Lacking shaping, fear stays inward and mute, and consciousness does not come to be for its own self. Should consciousness form without that first absolute fear, it is nothing but a vain mind of its own; for the form or negativity belonging to it is not negativity _in itself;_ and its forming can therefore not give it consciousness of itself as the essence. If what it has endured is not absolute fear but only some anxiety, the negative essence has stayed something external {{{ 128 }}} to it, and its substance has not been infected by that essence through and through. {{ 116 }} Since not every filling of its natural consciousness has been set tottering, it still belongs _in itself_ to determinate being; the mind of its own is _obstinacy_, a freedom that still remains standing inside servitude. Just as the pure form cannot become essence for it, so too, viewed as a spreading beyond the singular case, that form is no universal shaping, no absolute concept, but a skill holding sway over some things only, and not over that power which is universal, nor over the objective essence as a whole.
