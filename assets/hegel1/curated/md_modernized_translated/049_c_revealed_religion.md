## {{{ 699 }}} C. Revealed Religion

{{ 400 }} By way of the religion of art, spirit has passed out of the form of _substance_ into that of the _subject_, since it is art that _brings_ spirit's shape _forth_ and so plants in that shape the _doing_, that is, the _self-consciousness_ which inside the terrifying substance does nothing but disappear and which, in trust, never lays hold of itself. The statue is where this becoming-human of the divine essence begins, a statue carrying on it only the _outer_ shape of the self, while the _inner_ side, its activity, lies outside it; in the cult, though, the two sides have fused, and where the religion of art issues, this fully achieved unity has crossed over to the extreme of the self as well; every essentiality has drowned in the spirit that, within the singularity of consciousness, is wholly certain of itself. Here is how the proposition that utters this levity goes: _the self is the absolute essence_; and what had been essence, namely substance, on {{{ 700 }}} which the self figured as accidentality, has dropped to the rank of predicate, while spirit, in _this self-consciousness_, confronted by nothing in the form of essence, has forfeited its _consciousness_.

As is plain enough on its own, this proposition, that _the self is the absolute essence_, has its home in the non-religious spirit, the actual one, and we need to call to mind which of that spirit's shapes gives it voice. That shape will contain, at one and the same time, the proposition's movement and its reversal, the reversal that tunes the self down into predicate and elevates substance into subject. In this way, namely: the reversed proposition does not turn substance into subject _in itself_ or _for us_, which would come to the same as setting substance up again so that spirit's consciousness gets driven back to where it started, to natural religion; the reversal is instead accomplished _for_ and _by self-consciousness_ itself. Since self-consciousness surrenders itself knowingly, it is kept intact in its externalization and stays the subject of substance, though, having externalized itself just as much, it also possesses the consciousness of substance; or: since its self-sacrifice is what _produces_ substance as subject, what it produces goes on being its own self. {{ 401 }} The upshot attained is this: given the two propositions, — in the one, that of the first substantiality, the subject does no more than disappear, — and in the second, substance counts as nothing but predicate, so that in each of them the two sides stand with their worth unequal in opposite directions, — what emerges is the {{{ 701 }}} union and mutual penetration of both natures, wherein each, at equal worth, is _essential_ every bit as much as it is a mere _moment_; by this route, then, spirit is _consciousness_ of itself and of its _objective_ substance quite as much as it is simple _self-consciousness_ abiding within itself.

Ethical spirit is what the religion of art belongs to, the spirit whose downfall we watched earlier in the _condition of legal right_, that is, in the proposition: _the abstract person_, the self as such, _is absolute essence_. Within ethical life the self lies sunk in its people's spirit; it is universality _filled out_. But _simple singularity_ lifts free of this content, and levity purifies it into the person, into the abstract universality of right. There the _reality_ of ethical spirit is lost, and the content-empty spirits of the peoples as individuals stand assembled in one single pantheon, not in a pantheon of representation, whose powerless form leaves each to itself, but in the pantheon of an abstract universality, of the pure thought that strips them of their bodies and grants being-in-and-for-itself to the spiritless self, the singular person.

Yet by its emptiness this self has set the content free; consciousness is the essence only _within itself_; its own _existence_, the legal recognition accorded the person, is abstraction left unfulfilled; what it possesses, then, is rather no more than the thought of itself, or, taken as it _is there_, knowing itself as an object, it amounts to the _unactual_. Hence it is nothing but the stoic _self-subsistence_ {{{ 702 }}} of _thinking_, and this self-subsistence, on its way through the movement of skeptical consciousness, finds its truth in the shape that came to be called the _unhappy self-consciousness_.

The unhappy self-consciousness knows what to make of the abstract person's actual validity, and equally of that person's validity within pure thought. Such validity it knows rather as total loss; it is itself that loss made conscious of itself, and the externalization of what it knows of itself. — We can see that this unhappy consciousness forms the reverse side, and the completion, of the consciousness that is entirely happy within itself, the comic one. All divine essence recedes into that comic consciousness; or, put otherwise, it is the total _externalization_ of _substance_. The unhappy consciousness, by contrast, is the opposite case: the tragic fate of a _certainty of itself_ that was to be in and for itself. This is the consciousness that all _essentiality_ is lost within _this certainty_ of itself and that just this knowing of itself is lost as well — substance no less than self; it is the pain uttering itself in the harsh saying, _that God has died_.

Within the condition of legal right, then, the ethical world together with its religion has gone under in comic consciousness, while the unhappy consciousness is what knows this _whole_ loss. {{ 402 }} Gone for it is the self-worth attaching to its immediate personality, and gone as well the worth of its mediated, _thought_ personality. Silenced too {{{ 703 }}} is the trust placed in the gods' eternal laws, and silenced are the oracles that professed knowledge of the particular. The statues are corpses now, the animating soul having fled them, as the hymn is words whose faith has fled; the gods' tables stand bare of spiritual food and drink, and consciousness no longer recovers from its games and festivals that joyous unity of itself with the essence. What the Muse's works lack is the force of that spirit for which certainty of itself came forth out of the pulverizing of gods and human beings. What they now are is what they are for us, — lovely fruit broken off the tree, held out to us by a kindly fate, the way a girl hands such fruit across; that fate does not supply the actual life of their existence, nor the tree they grew on, nor the earth and elements that constituted their substance, nor the climate that constituted their determinateness, nor the turning seasons that ruled the process by which they came to be. — Fate, in giving us the works of that art, thus withholds their world, withholds the springtime and summer of ethical life where they came into bloom and ripened, and yields only a recollection of that actuality, wrapped up and enveloped. — Our doing, as we enjoy them, is therefore not the doing of worship, by which our consciousness would come into its complete and filling truth; it is rather the external doing that brushes off these fruits a {{{ 704 }}} raindrop, perhaps, or a speck of dust, and that puts up, in the place once held by the inner elements of the surrounding, generating, inspiriting actuality of the ethical, the rambling scaffolding built from the dead elements of their external existence, of language, of the historical, and so on, not so as to live our way into them, but only so as to represent them within ourselves. But the girl who holds out the plucked fruit surpasses the fruit's own nature as that nature lay spread out into its conditions and elements, into air, light, the tree, and so on, where it offered the fruit immediately, since she draws all of this together, at a higher level, into the beam of a self-conscious eye and into the gesture of holding out; in the same way, the spirit of fate that holds those artworks out to us surpasses the ethical life, the actuality, of that people, since it is the _re-collection_ of a spirit that in them still stands _externalized_, — it is the spirit of that tragic fate which assembles every one of those individual gods and attributes of substance into the single pantheon, into a spirit self-conscious of being spirit.

Every condition of spirit's coming forth lies ready to hand, and that totality of conditions is what constitutes spirit's _becoming_, its _concept_, or its coming forth as something _in-itself_. {{ 403 }} — Art's productions form a circle that takes in the shapes taken by absolute substance's externalizations: substance appears under individuality's form, as a thing, as an object that _is_, an object {{{ 705 }}} for sensuous consciousness, — as pure language, or as the becoming of a shape whose existence never steps out beyond the self and which is an object purely in the act of _vanishing_; — as _unity_ with universal _self-consciousness_, immediate in the inspiration of that consciousness and mediated in the doing of the cult; — as beautiful _selflike corporeality_, and lastly as an existence lifted into _representation_, spread out into a world that finally draws itself together into universality, which is likewise _pure certainty of itself_. — Those forms, and, over against them, the _world_ of the _person_ and of right, with the devastating wildness of the content's elements once they are set loose, and equally the _thought_ person of stoicism along with the groundless unrest of skeptical consciousness, make up the periphery of shapes that stand round the birthplace of the spirit now becoming self-consciousness, expectant and pressing in; the pain and longing of unhappy self-consciousness, penetrating everything, is their center point and the birth pang they share in that coming forth, — namely the simplicity of the pure concept, which holds those shapes within it as its moments.

The pure concept carries on it the two sides that were set out above as the two reversed propositions; the first of them is that _substance_ externalizes itself and becomes self-consciousness, the second, in reverse, that _self-consciousness_ externalizes itself {{{ 706 }}} and turns itself into thinghood, that is, into the universal self. The two sides have met each other halfway in this manner, and out of that their true union has arisen. What substance's externalization, its becoming self-consciousness, gives expression to is the passage over into the opposite, the unconscious passage of _necessity_, that is, the fact that substance _in itself_ is self-consciousness. In reverse, self-consciousness's externalization gives expression to this: that self-consciousness _in itself_ is universal essence, or, since the self is that pure being-for-itself which stays with itself in its own opposite, that _for it_ substance is self-consciousness and, just by that, spirit. Of this spirit, which abandons the form of substance and comes into existence with the shape of self-consciousness, one may therefore say, — supposing one wants to employ the relations borrowed from natural procreation, — that its mother is _actual_ while its father has being that is _in-itself_; for _actuality_, or self-consciousness, together with the _in-itself_ as substance, are the two moments of spirit, and it is by their mutual externalization, each turning into the other, that spirit comes into existence as the unity the two of them constitute.

To the extent that self-consciousness takes hold, one-sidedly, of _its own_ externalization alone, granted that its object is by now self no less than being and that it knows every existence as spiritual essence, the true spirit has all the same not yet come to be for it, to the extent, that is, that being at large, {{{ 707 }}} or substance, did not on its own side and _in itself_ likewise externalize itself and turn into self-consciousness. {{ 404 }} For then every existence counts as spiritual essence merely _as seen from the standpoint of consciousness_, and not on its own account. On this footing spirit is merely _imagined into_ existence; such imagining is the _fanatical enthusiasm_ that slips under nature and history alike, under the world as much as under the mythical representations of the earlier religions, an inner sense different from the one they immediately present to consciousness in their appearance, and, where the religions are concerned, different from the one that the self-consciousness whose religions they were knew in them. This significance, however, is one borrowed, a robe that leaves the bareness of the appearance uncovered and earns itself neither faith nor reverence, remaining instead the clouded night and the private rapture belonging to consciousness.

If this significance of the objective is not to be mere imagining, it must be _in itself_, which means, _first_, that for consciousness it springs from the _concept_ and comes forth in its own necessity. That is how the self-knowing _spirit_ sprang up for us: out of the cognition of _immediate consciousness_, the consciousness whose object _is_, by way of that consciousness's necessary movement. This concept, which, being immediate, likewise had for its consciousness the shape of _immediacy_, has _secondly_ given itself, _in itself_, the shape of self-consciousness, that is, in keeping with the very {{{ 708 }}} necessity of the concept, at the point where _being_, or _immediacy_, which is the object of sensuous consciousness, an object void of content, externalizes itself and comes to be I for consciousness. — Distinct from the _thinking in-itself_, that is, from the _cognition_ of _necessity_, stands the _immediate in-itself_, or _necessity as existent_, itself; — a distinction which nevertheless falls within the concept and not beyond it, since the concept's _simple unity_ is _immediate being_ itself; the concept is what externalizes itself, the becoming of _intuited necessity_, quite as much as it is at home in that necessity, knowing and comprehending it. — When we speak of the _immediate in-itself_ of the spirit that confers on itself the shape of self-consciousness, this says no more than that the world-spirit in its actuality has arrived at this knowledge of itself; only then does that knowledge enter spirit's consciousness as well, and enter as truth. How the first of these came about has already shown itself above.

The fact that absolute spirit has _in itself_ conferred on itself the shape of self-consciousness, and with that for its _consciousness_ too, shows up now as the _faith of the world_: that spirit _is there_ as a self-consciousness, that is, as an actual human being, that it exists for immediate certainty, that believing consciousness _sees_ and _feels_ and _hears_ this divinity. {{ 405 }} So understood, it is no imagining; the thing _really is so_. Consciousness in that case does not set out from what is inward in it, from _its_ thought, joining _within itself_ the thought of God to {{{ 709 }}} existence; it sets out instead from the immediately present existence, and there it cognizes God. — Within the content of the concept the moment of _immediate being_ is on hand in this way: religious spirit, as all essentiality returns into consciousness, has turned into a _simple_ positive self, exactly as actual spirit as such, within unhappy consciousness, has turned into precisely this _simple_ self-conscious negativity. By this route the self of the spirit that is there acquires the form of immediacy in its completeness; it is not posited as thought, nor as represented, nor as brought forth, which is what happens to the immediate self in natural religion on the one hand and in the religion of art on the other. Rather, this God is sensuously intuited, immediately, as self, as a singular human being who is actual; on no other terms _is_ he self-consciousness.

The absolute religion has for its simple content this becoming-human of the divine essence, the fact that the essence bears the shape of self-consciousness essentially and immediately. In that religion the essence is known as spirit; the religion is essence's own consciousness about itself, that it is spirit. Spirit, after all, is knowledge of itself in its own externalization: the essence whose being consists in the movement of holding on to equality with itself throughout its otherness. But that is substance, in so far as substance stands reflected into itself within its accidentality as well, not indifferent toward that accidentality as toward something unessential, something found in an alien element, {{{ 710 }}} but rather _within itself_ there, that is, in so far as substance is _subject_ or _self_. — For that reason the divine essence stands _revealed_ in this religion. Its being revealed manifestly amounts to this, that what it is gets known. And it does get known precisely in being known as spirit, as an essence whose essential character is to be _self-consciousness_. — For _consciousness_, something in its object stays secret whenever that object is an _other_ for it, something _alien_, and whenever consciousness does not know the object as _itself_. Such secrecy comes to an end once the absolute essence is consciousness's object as spirit; for then, in its relation to consciousness, it stands there as _self_, that is, consciousness immediately knows itself in it, or is revealed to itself in it. Consciousness itself is revealed to itself nowhere but in the certainty it has of its own self; the object in question is the _self_, and the self is nothing alien: it is unity with itself that cannot be severed, the immediately universal. It is the pure concept, it is pure thinking, that is, _being-for-itself_, which is immediately _being_ and hence _being for another_, and which as this _being for another_ has immediately gone back into itself and is at home with itself; hence what is revealed truly and alone is just this. Goodness, justice, holiness, being creator of heaven and of earth, and so on are a subject's _predicates_, — universal moments that find their hold at this point and that exist only when consciousness first goes back into thinking. {{ 406 }} — So long as _they_ are what is known, the ground and essence of them, the _subject_ itself, remains unrevealed, {{{ 711 }}} and they are likewise _determinations_ of the universal rather than _this universal_ itself. Yet as _self_ the _subject_ itself, and with it _this pure universal_, is revealed, for the self is just this inwardness reflected into itself, immediately there, and it is the certainty belonging to whichever self it is there for. This — being, in accordance with its _concept_, what is revealed — is therefore spirit's true shape, and this shape of its, the concept, is likewise its essence and substance and nothing besides. Spirit is known as self-consciousness and lies open to self-consciousness immediately, since spirit is that very self-consciousness; divine nature and human nature are one and the same, and it is this unity that gets intuited.

Here, then, consciousness, that is, the manner in which the essence is for consciousness itself, its shape, really does match its self-consciousness; that shape is a self-consciousness in its own right; with that it is at once an object that _is_, and this _being_ carries just as immediately the significance of _pure thinking_, of the absolute essence. — Being there as an actual self-consciousness, the absolute essence looks as though it had _stepped down_ from its eternal simplicity, whereas in truth only in doing so has it reached its _highest_ essence. For not until the concept of the essence has won its simple purity is it the absolute _abstraction_ which is _pure thinking_, and therewith the pure singularity of the self, just as, for the sake of that simplicity, it is the _immediate_, that is, _being_. — What goes by the name of sensuous consciousness is exactly this pure _abstraction_; {{{ 712 }}} it is that thinking to which _being_ counts as the _immediate_. What is lowest is therefore also what is highest: the revealed, having come out wholly onto the _surface_, is in just that respect the _deepest_. That the highest essence is seen, heard, and so forth as a self-consciousness which has being, this is in fact the completing of its concept; and through that completion the essence is _there_ as immediately as it is essence.

Immediate existence of this kind is at the same time not merely immediate consciousness and nothing more; it is religious consciousness; undividedly, the immediacy signifies not only a self-consciousness that _is_, but the purely thought or absolute _essence_. Religious consciousness is conscious of the same thing that we in our concept are conscious of, namely that _being is essence_. This _unity_ of being and essence, of a _thinking_ that immediately _is existence_, is, just as much as it is the _thought_ of this religious consciousness, its _mediated_ knowledge, also _its immediate_ knowledge; for being and thinking in this unity make up _self_-consciousness, and this unity is itself _there_, or the _thought_ unity has at the same time the shape of what it is. {{ 407 }} Here, then, God stands _revealed_ as _he is; he is_ so _there_ as he is _in himself_; he is there, as spirit. Pure speculative knowledge is the sole place where God can be reached; he is only within it and is only it itself, since he is spirit; and that speculative knowledge is what revealed religion knows. Speculative knowledge knows him as _thinking_, as pure essence, knows this thinking as being and as {{{ 713 }}} existence, and knows existence as its own negativity and thereby as self, as _this_ self and as universal self; and just this is what revealed religion knows. — Everything the earlier world hoped for and expected pressed toward this revelation alone, toward intuiting what the absolute essence is and finding itself within it; the joy of seeing itself in the absolute essence comes to self-consciousness and takes hold of the entire world, for the essence is spirit, it is that simple movement of the pure moments which expresses precisely this, that the essence gets known as spirit only in being intuited as _immediate_ self-consciousness.

The concept of a spirit that knows itself as spirit is, taken by itself, still the immediate concept, undeveloped as yet. Essence is spirit, which is to say it has appeared, it stands revealed; being revealed in this first way is itself _immediate_; yet immediacy is equally pure mediation, that is, thinking, and must therefore exhibit as much on itself as immediacy. — Looked at with more determinateness: within the immediacy of self-consciousness, spirit is _this singular_ self-consciousness, standing over against the _universal_ one; it is an excluding One which, in the eyes of the consciousness _for which_ it is there, still wears the undissolved form of a _sensuous other_; that consciousness has not yet come to know spirit as its own, or spirit, while being a _singular_ self, is not yet there as a universal self as well, as the self of all. Put otherwise, the shape lacks as yet the form of the _concept_, that is, the form of the {{{ 714 }}} universal self, of that self which in its immediate actuality is at the same time something sublated, is thinking, universality, without forfeiting the actuality in the universality. — The form of this universality that comes next, itself immediate, is not yet the form _of thinking_ as such, _of the concept qua concept_; it is the universality of actuality, the totality of selves, and the lifting of existence into representation; much as everywhere, and to give one determinate example, the sublated _sensuous This_ is at first the thing of _perception_ and not yet the _universal_ that belongs to the understanding.

So this singular human being, in whom the absolute essence stands revealed, carries out on himself as a singular the movement belonging to _sensuous being_. He is God present here _immediately_; by that very fact his _being_ passes over into _having-been_. For the consciousness to which he has this sensuous presence, seeing him and hearing him come to an end; it _has_ seen and heard him; and only because it merely _has_ seen and heard him does it itself become spiritual consciousness, or, just as he formerly rose up for it as _sensuous existence_, he has now risen up _in spirit_. {{ 408 }} — For so long as it sees and hears him sensuously it is nothing but immediate consciousness, which has left the inequality of objectivity unsublated and has not withdrawn it into pure thinking, and which knows this objective singular while failing to know itself as spirit. As the immediate existence of the one known as absolute {{{ 715 }}} essence vanishes, what is immediate takes on its negative moment; spirit stays the immediate self of actuality, though now as _the universal self-consciousness_ of the community, a self-consciousness that reposes in a substance of its own while that substance is universal subject within it; the complete whole of that spirit is not the singular taken on his own, but the singular together with the community's consciousness, and what he is for the community.

_Pastness_ and _remoteness_, however, make up only that incomplete form in which the immediate manner gets mediated, or posited as universal; that manner has been dipped into the element of thinking only on the surface, is kept there _as_ a sensuous manner, and is not posited as one with what thinking itself is by nature. It has been lifted no further than into _representing_, since representing is precisely the synthetic linking of sensuous immediacy with the universality of that immediacy, that is, with thinking.

The determinateness in which spirit becomes conscious of itself in this community of its own is constituted by this _form of representing_. It is not yet spirit's self-consciousness ripened into its concept taken as concept; mediation remains unfinished. So in this linking of being and thinking the defect is present that the spiritual essence is still afflicted with an unreconciled splitting-in-two into a here below and into a beyond. The _content_ is the true content, but every one of its moments, posited as they are in the element of representing, has the character of going uncomprehended and of appearing instead as sides that are entirely {{{ 716 }}} self-subsistent and relate to one another _externally_. If the true content is to acquire its true form for consciousness as well, consciousness needs a higher culture: it must lift its intuition of the absolute substance up into the concept and must, _for itself_, bring its consciousness level with its self-consciousness, as has already occurred for us, or _in itself_.

This content is now to be considered as it stands within spirit's consciousness. — Absolute spirit is _content_, and as such it has the shape of its _truth_. Its truth, though, does not consist merely in being the community's substance, the community's _in-itself_, nor merely in rising up out of that inwardness into the objectivity that belongs to representing; it consists in coming to be an actual self, in reflecting itself into itself, and in being subject. That, then, is the movement spirit carries out within its community; that is spirit's life. What spirit, in revealing itself, is _in and for itself_ therefore does not come to light by having its rich life in the community unwound, so to speak, and traced back to its first thread, say to the representations of the first, incomplete community, or indeed to whatever the actual human being said. {{ 409 }} Underlying such tracing back is the instinct to reach the concept; but it mistakes the _origin_, taken as the _immediate existence_ of the first appearance, for the _simplicity_ of the _concept_. {{{ 717 }}} What such impoverishing of spirit's life brings about, when the community's representation and the community's doing toward that representation are cleared away, is therefore not the concept but sheer externality and singularity instead, the historical manner of the immediate appearance together with a spiritless recollection of one singular shape that is merely meant, and of its past.

Spirit is the content of its consciousness at first under the form _of pure substance_, or, what is the same, it is what its pure consciousness contains. The movement of this element of thinking is a climbing down to existence, that is, to singularity. Between the two, the middle is their synthetic linking, the consciousness of becoming-other, or representing as such. Third comes the return out of representation and out of otherness, in other words the element of self-consciousness itself. — Spirit is made up of these three moments; its stepping apart within representation consists in being in a _determinate_ manner, and this determinateness is nothing but one of spirit's moments. Spirit's movement carried out in full therefore consists in spreading its nature through each of its moments as through an element; and because each of these circles completes itself within itself, spirit's reflection into itself is at once its passing over into the next circle. _Representation_ forms the middle standing between self-consciousness as such and pure thinking, and it is no more than _one_ determinateness among them; at the same time, {{{ 718 }}} as has come to light, the character it has of being the synthetic linking lies spread across every one of these elements and is the determinateness they share.

The content that is to be considered has already turned up in part as the representation belonging to the _unhappy_ and to the _believing_ consciousness; — in the former, though, with the determination of a content _brought forth out of consciousness_ and _yearned after_, a content in which spirit can find neither satiety nor rest, because the content is not yet spirit's own _in itself_ or as its _substance_; — in the latter, by contrast, the content has been considered as the world's selfless _essence_, as the essentially _objective_ content of a representing, — a representing that takes flight from actuality altogether and is therefore without the _certainty of self-consciousness_, which parts from it partly as the vanity of knowledge, partly as pure insight. — The community's consciousness, on the other hand, has this content for its _substance_, just as that content is the community's _certainty_ of its own spirit.

{{ 410 }} Represented at first as substance within the _element of pure thinking_, spirit is thereby, immediately, the simple, self-equal, eternal _essence_, an essence that carries not the abstract _significance_ of essence but the significance of absolute spirit. Only, spirit consists in being the actual, and not in being a significance, an inward something. The simple eternal essence would accordingly be spirit in an empty word only, were it to remain with the representation and with the expression of the {{{ 719 }}} simple eternal essence. Being the abstraction, however, the simple essence is in fact _negative in its own self_, and specifically the negativity of thinking, negativity such as it is in itself within _essence_; that is to say, it is the absolute _difference_ from itself, its pure becoming-other. Taken as _essence_ it is _in itself_ only, or for us; but since this purity is precisely abstraction, precisely negativity, it is _for its own self_, it is the _self_, the _concept_. — It is therefore _objective;_ and where representation apprehends and utters the _necessity_ of the concept just stated as a _happening_, what gets said is that the eternal essence _begets_ another for itself. Yet within this otherness it has returned into itself just as immediately; for the difference in question is difference _in itself_, that is, immediately it differs from nothing but itself, and so it is the unity that has come back into itself.

Three moments accordingly come apart from one another: _essence_; _being-for-itself_, which is essence's otherness and for which essence is; and _being-for-itself_, or knowing oneself, _in the other_. In its being-for-itself the essence intuits nothing but itself; throughout this externalization it stays with itself alone, and the being-for-itself that shuts itself off from the essence is the _knowledge_ the _essence has of its own self_; it is the word which, once spoken, externalizes the speaker and leaves him emptied out, and which is heard just as immediately, the existence of the word being nothing other than this hearing {{{ 720 }}} of itself. So the differences that get made are dissolved as immediately as they are made, and made as immediately as they are dissolved, and what is true, what is actual, is just this movement circling within itself.

It is this movement within its own self that utters the absolute essence as _spirit_; taken without being grasped as spirit, the absolute essence is merely the abstract void, and spirit, taken without being grasped as this movement, is merely an empty word. When spirit's _moments_ are taken hold of in their purity, they are those restless concepts whose whole being lies in being their own opposite in their own selves and in finding their rest within the whole. The community's _representing_, however, is not this _comprehending_ thinking; it holds the content while lacking the content's necessity, and in place of the concept's form it carries the natural relations of father and son over into the kingdom of pure consciousness. {{ 411 }} Since it relates itself _representationally_ in this way within thinking itself, the essence is revealed to it well enough, yet on account of that synthetic representation the essence's moments partly fall apart from one another for it, so that they are not related through a concept of their own, and partly it draws back from this pure object of its and relates to the object only externally; the object has been revealed to it by an alien source, and within this thought of spirit it fails to cognize itself, fails to cognize the nature of pure self-consciousness. {{{ 721 }}} In so far as one has to go beyond the form of representing and beyond those relations borrowed from what is natural, and thereby beyond taking the moments of the movement which spirit is as isolated, unshakeable substances or subjects rather than as moments that pass over, — this going beyond is to be viewed, as was recalled earlier with respect to another side of the matter, as a pressing of the concept; but being mere instinct, it misconstrues itself, casts away the form and the content along with it, and, what amounts to the same, degrades the content into a historical representation, an heirloom of tradition; here what is purely external in faith is all that is kept, kept as something dead and without cognition, while the _inner_ side of faith has disappeared, since that inner side would be the concept knowing itself as concept.

Absolute spirit, as represented in _pure essence_, is admittedly not the _abstract_ pure essence; on the contrary, that essence, precisely by being no more than a moment within spirit, has sunk to the rank of an _element_. Yet the presentation of spirit within this element carries on it, as regards form, the very defect that _essence_ as essence carries. Essence is the abstract, and hence the negative of its own simplicity, another; in the same way _spirit_ within essence as element is the _form_ of _simple unity_, and just for that reason is quite as essentially a becoming-other. — Or, what comes to the same, how the eternal essence stands toward its being-for-itself is {{{ 722 }}} the simple, immediate relating of pure thinking; within this _simple_ intuiting of its own self in the other, then, _otherness_ is not posited as otherness; the difference here is difference such as immediately amounts, in pure thinking, to _no difference_; it is a recognition _of love_, in which the two were not set _in opposition_ to each other according to their essence. — Spirit as uttered within the element of pure thinking is itself essentially this: to be not in that element alone but to be _something actual_, for _otherness_ lies within its very concept, that is, the sublating of the pure concept that is merely thought.

Since it is the abstract, the element of pure thinking is itself rather the _other_ of its own simplicity, and for that reason it passes over into the element proper to _representing_, — an element where the moments of the pure concept acquire a _substantial_ existence over against one another quite as much as they are _subjects_ that do not, for some third party, hold the indifference of being toward each other, but, reflected into themselves, set themselves apart from one another and into opposition.

{{ 412 }} Spirit, then, being merely eternal or abstract, makes itself into _another_, or enters existence, and immediately enters _immediate existence_. A _world_ is accordingly what it _creates_. This _creating_ is representation's word for the _concept_ itself in its absolute movement, or for the fact that the simple, that is, pure thinking, which has been declared absolute, is instead the negative, precisely because it is the abstract, and so is what is opposed to itself, the _other_; — or, to put the matter in still another {{{ 723 }}} way, because that which has been posited as _essence_ amounts to simple _immediacy_ or _being_, and yet, as immediacy or being, goes without the self and so, since inwardness is wanting, is _passive_, or _being for another_. — _Being for another_ of this sort is at once _a world_; spirit, taken in respect of the determination _being for another_, is the quiescent subsistence of those moments that were previously shut up inside pure thinking, and hence is the dissolving of their simple universality and their falling apart into a particularity of their own.

The world, however, is not merely this spirit thrown apart into completeness and into the external ordering of that completeness; because spirit is in essence the simple self, that self is present in the world just as much: the _existent_ spirit, which is the singular self that has consciousness and that distinguishes itself from itself as other, or as world. — Posited at first in such immediacy, this singular self has not yet become _spirit for itself_; hence it _is_ not _as_ spirit, and while it may be called _innocent_, it can hardly be called _good_. If it is really to be self and spirit, it must, precisely as the eternal essence displays itself as that movement in which it is self-equal within its otherness, first become, to its own self, _another_. Given that this spirit is determined as existing at first only immediately, or as dispersed into the manifoldness that its consciousness has, its becoming-other is the going-_within-itself_ of knowledge as such. Immediate existence turns over into thought, or {{{ 724 }}} merely sensuous consciousness turns over into the consciousness of thought; and since this is thought that issues from immediacy, thought that is _conditioned_, it is no pure knowledge but rather a thought that carries otherness in it, and so the thought of _good_ and _evil_ that stands opposed to itself. Man is represented in such a way that this _happened_, as something not necessary, — that by plucking from the tree of the cognition of _good_ and _evil_ he lost the form of self-equality, and that he was expelled from the state of innocent consciousness, from a nature that offered itself without labor, and from paradise, the garden of the animals.

Since this going-within-itself of existent consciousness determines itself immediately as a becoming _unequal_ to itself, _evil_ shows up as the first existence of consciousness once it has gone within itself; and since the thoughts of _good_ and of _evil_ are opposed outright, with that opposition not yet dissolved, this consciousness is in essence nothing but the evil one. {{ 413 }} At the same time, though, and just because of this opposition, the _good_ consciousness is also at hand over against it, along with the relationship the two have to each other. — Insofar as immediate existence turns over into _thought_, and that _being-within-itself_ is thereby determined more closely as partly thinking itself and partly the moment of the essence's _becoming-other_, the becoming-evil can be shifted still further back, beyond the existent world, into the first realm of thinking already. One may accordingly say {{{ 725 }}} that the firstborn son of light already, in going within himself, is this evil, and that he fell away, whereupon another was at once begotten in his place. A form of that kind, such as _falling away_, and likewise _son_, belongs merely to representation, not to the concept, and it moreover degrades the moments of the concept downward into representing, or inversely carries representing over into the realm of thought. — It is just as indifferent whether one coordinates a manifold of further shapes with the eternal essence's simple thought of _otherness_ and transposes the _going-within-itself_ into them. Such coordination has at the same time to be approved, since through it this moment of _otherness_ expresses difference as well, as it ought to; and indeed not as plurality at large but at the same time as determinate difference, so that the one part, the son, is the simple that knows itself as essence, whereas the other part is the externalization of being-for-itself, an externalization that lives only in praising the essence; into this part, in turn, one may place the taking back of externalized being-for-itself and the going-within-itself of evil. To the extent that otherness falls apart into two, spirit would be more determinate in its moments and, if these are counted, would be expressed as a fourfold unity, or even as a fivefold unity, since the multitude in turn falls apart into two parts, those that stayed good and those that turned evil. — To _count_ the moments can be regarded as useless altogether, since on one side what is differentiated {{{ 726 }}} is itself no less merely _one_, namely just the _thought_ of difference, a thought that is only one thought insofar as it is _this_ differentiated item, the second over against the first, — while on the other side the thought that gathers the many into one must be dissolved out of the universality it has and split into more than three or four differentiated items; — and this universality, set against the abstract one's absolute determinateness, the principle of number, appears, in relation to number itself, as indeterminateness, so that there could be talk only of _numbers_ at large, that is, not of any _count_ of the differences, which makes any thought here of number and counting wholly superfluous, just as elsewhere a mere difference of magnitude and multitude is without concept and says nothing.

_Good_ and _evil_ were the determinate differences of thought that had come out. {{ 414 }} Since their opposition remains undissolved, and since they are represented as essences of thought, each self-subsistent on its own, man is the essenceless self, the synthetic ground on which they exist and fight. But these universal powers belong no less to the self; or rather, the self is what makes them actual. In keeping with this moment, then, it comes about that, just as evil amounts to nothing but the going-within-itself of spirit's natural existence, so conversely the good enters actuality and shows up as a self-consciousness that exists. — What in spirit as purely thought is merely intimated as the becoming-_other_ of the {{{ 727 }}} divine essence in general draws nearer here to its realization for representing; for representing, that realization consists in a self-abasement of the divine essence, which renounces its abstraction and its unactuality. — The other side, evil, representing takes to be a happening foreign to the divine essence; and to grasp evil within that essence itself, _as its wrath_, is the highest and hardest exertion of a representing that wrestles with itself, an exertion which, going without the concept, stays fruitless.

The estrangement of the divine essence is thus posited in its doubled manner; spirit's self and spirit's simple thought are the two moments, and their absolute unity is spirit itself; its estrangement consists in the two stepping apart, the one holding a value unequal to that of the other. The inequality is on that account a doubled one, and two combinations arise, the moments common to which are the ones just given. Within the first, the _divine essence_ is what counts as essential, whereas natural existence together with the self counts as unessential and as what is to be sublated; within the second, by contrast, _being-for-itself_ is what counts as essential, and the simple divine as unessential. Their middle, still empty, is _existence_ at large, the bare commonality of its two moments.

This opposition is not dissolved so much by the struggle of the two, which are represented as separate {{{ 728 }}} and self-subsistent essences. Their _self-subsistence_ implies that each of them must, _in itself_, by its concept, dissolve within its own self; the struggle first falls at the point where the two cease being these blends of thought with self-subsistent existence and stand facing each other merely as thoughts. For then, as determinate concepts, they are essentially only within the opposed relation; as self-subsistent, by contrast, their essentiality lies outside the opposition, so that their movement is free and belongs to them. And so, just as the movement of the two is the movement _in itself_, since it has to be considered in the two themselves, it is likewise begun by whichever of them is determined over against the other as what is in itself. {{ 415 }} This is represented as a voluntary deed; but the necessity of its externalization lies in the concept that what is in itself, determined so only within the opposition, precisely therefore has no true subsistence; — hence it is the one for which the simple, and not being-for-itself, counts as the essence that externalizes its own self, goes into death, and thereby reconciles the absolute essence with its own self. For it is in this movement that it displays itself as _spirit_; the essence in its abstractness stands estranged from itself, possessing natural existence and selflike actuality; that otherness of its own, its sensuous presence, is withdrawn again through the second becoming-other and posited as sublated, as _universal_; by this the essence has become within actuality {{{ 729 }}} its own self; actuality's immediate existence has stopped being something alien or external to the essence, since it is sublated and universal; this death is therefore the essence's arising as spirit.

The self-conscious essence's immediate presence, once sublated, is that essence in the guise of universal self-consciousness; the concept of the sublated singular self, which is absolute essence, therefore expresses immediately the constituting of a community that has lingered until now in representing and now returns into itself as into the self; and with that, spirit passes over out of the second element of its determination, representing, into the _third_, self-consciousness as such. — Let us look further at the way that representing conducts itself as it proceeds; we then see this expressed first of all: that the divine essence takes on human nature. In that it is already _pronounced_ that the two, _in themselves_, are not separate; — just as, where it is said that the divine essence externalizes itself _from the beginning_, that its existence goes within itself and turns evil, it is not pronounced but is nevertheless _contained_ that this evil existence is, _in itself_, nothing foreign to that essence; the absolute essence would carry only this empty name were there in truth an _other_ to it, were there a _falling away_ from it; — the moment of _being-within-itself_ makes up, rather, the _self_ of spirit in its essential moment. — That _being-within-itself_, and thereby _actuality_ for the first time, belongs to the essence itself: this, which for us is _concept_, and so far as it is concept, {{{ 730 }}} appears to representing consciousness as a _happening_ beyond comprehension; the _in-itself_ assumes for that consciousness the form of _indifferent being_. But the thought that those moments which seem to flee one another, the absolute essence and the self that is for itself, are not separate does occur to this representing _too_, — for representing possesses the true content, — yet only afterwards, — within the externalization of the divine essence that becomes flesh. This representation, still _immediate_ in this manner and hence not spiritual, or which knows the essence's human shape as yet only as a particular one and not as universal, turns spiritual for this consciousness through the movement in which the shaped essence sacrifices its immediate existence once again and goes back to the essence; the essence as _reflected within itself_ is spirit only then. — What is represented here, then, is the _reconciliation_ between the divine essence and the _other_ at large, and determinately with the _thought_ of that essence, with _evil_. {{ 416 }} — If this reconciliation is expressed in accordance with _its concept_ by saying that it consists in this, that _in itself_ _evil is the same_ as _good_, or again that the divine essence is _the same_ as the whole compass of nature, while nature severed from the divine essence is only _nothingness_, — then this must be regarded as a spiritless way of expressing oneself, one bound to give rise to misunderstandings. — Given that evil is _the same_ as good, evil is precisely then not evil, nor is good good; rather both are sublated, evil at large being the {{{ 731 }}} being-for-itself that is within itself, and good being the selfless simple. Once both are pronounced in this way according to their concept, their unity becomes evident at the same time; for the being-for-itself that is within itself is simple knowledge, and the selfless simple is likewise the pure being-for-itself that is within itself. — Just as much, therefore, as one has to say that on this concept of theirs good and evil, that is, to the extent that they are not good and not evil, are _the same_, just as much must one say that they are _not_ the same, that they are simply _different_, since simple being-for-itself, or equally pure knowledge, are alike pure negativity, or absolute difference within their own selves. — Only these two propositions together complete the whole; against the asserting and assuring of the first, holding fast to the second must be set with unconquerable stubbornness; since the two have equal right, they have equal wrong as well, and their wrong lies in treating abstract forms of this kind, namely the forms _the same_ and _not the same_, the forms _identity_ and _non-identity_, as things that are true, fixed, actual, and in resting upon them. Truth lies neither with the one nor with the other, but precisely in their movement: that the simple same is abstraction and thereby absolute difference, while this difference, as difference in itself, differentiated from its own self, is self-equality. The very same holds of the _selfsameness_ between the divine essence and nature at large, and of human nature {{{ 732 }}} especially; the divine essence is nature to the extent that it is not essence; nature, according to its essence, is divine; — but spirit is that in which both abstract sides are posited as in truth they are, namely as _sublated_, — a positing that no judgment, and no spiritless _is_, the judgment's copula, can express. — Nature likewise is _nothing outside_ its essence; but this nothing itself just as much _is_; it is absolute abstraction, hence pure thinking, or being-within-itself, and taken with the moment of its opposition to spiritual unity it is _evil_. What makes these concepts difficult is solely the clinging to the _is_ and the forgetting of thinking, within which the moments no less _are_ than they _are not_, — within which they are only the movement that spirit is. {{ 417 }} — What has come to be for representing consciousness in that reconciliation is precisely this spiritual unity, the unity in which differences are present only as moments, only as sublated; and since it is the universality that self-consciousness has, the latter has stopped being a representing one; the movement has gone back into it.

Spirit is accordingly posited within the third element, within _universal self-consciousness_; it is its own _community_. The community's movement, as the movement of a self-consciousness that distinguishes itself from its representation, is to _bring forth_ what _in itself_ has come to be. The divine man who has died, the human God as such, is _in itself_ universal self-consciousness; {{{ 733 }}} it has to become this _for this self-consciousness_. Or, since it makes up the One side of representation's opposition, namely the evil side, for which what counts as the essence is natural existence and being-for-itself in its singularity, that side, which is represented as self-subsistent rather than as a moment, has for the sake of its self-subsistence to raise itself, in and for its own self, to spirit, or to exhibit spirit's movement in itself.

That side _is_ _natural spirit_; out of this naturalness the self has to draw back and go within itself, which would mean turning _evil_. But that side is already evil _in itself_; the going-within-itself therefore consists in _becoming convinced_ that evil is what natural existence is. The world's _existent_ becoming-evil and being-evil fall within representing consciousness, and so does the _existent_ reconciliation of the absolute essence; within _self-consciousness_ as such, by contrast, what is represented falls, as regards form, only as a sublated moment, since the _self_ is the negative, and so falls as _knowledge_, — a knowledge that is a pure deed of consciousness within its own self. — In the content too this moment of the _negative_ must find expression. For since the essence has _in itself_ already been reconciled with its own self and is a spiritual unity within which the parts of the representation stand as _sublated_, as _moments_, this presents itself in the fact that every part of the representation acquires here the _opposite_ significance from the one it previously had; each significance thereby fills itself out in the other, and only thus does the content {{{ 734 }}} become a spiritual one; since determinateness is just as much its own opposite, the unity in otherness, the spiritual unity, is completed, in the way that for us, or _in itself_, the opposed significances united earlier and even sublated those abstract forms, _the same_ and what is not _the same_, _identity_ as well as _non-identity_.

{{ 418 }} If, then, within representing consciousness the becoming-_inward_ of natural self-consciousness was the _existent evil_, the _becoming-inward_ within the element of self-consciousness is _knowledge_ of _evil_ as something that _in itself_ is there in existence; such knowledge is indeed a becoming-evil, yet only the becoming of the _thought_ of _evil_, and for that reason it is acknowledged as the first moment of reconciliation. Since it is a going back into itself away from the immediacy of nature, nature being determined as evil, it is a leaving of nature behind, the dying away of sin. What consciousness leaves behind is not natural existence as such, but that existence at the same time as something known to be evil. The immediate movement of _going-within-itself_ is just as much a mediated one; — it has itself for its presupposition, or is its own ground; the ground of the going-within-itself is, namely, that nature has already gone within itself in itself; for the sake of evil man must go within himself, but _evil_ is itself that going-within-itself. — This first movement is for just that reason itself only the immediate one, or its _simple concept_, since it is the very thing that is its {{{ 735 }}} ground. The movement, or the becoming-other, has therefore still to make its entrance in its more proper form.

Alongside this immediacy, then, the _mediation_ of representation is necessary. _In itself_, _knowledge_ of nature as spirit's untrue existence, and this universality of the self that has become inward, are spirit's reconciliation with its own self. For a self-consciousness that does not comprehend, this _in-itself_ acquires the form of something _existent_ and _represented to it_. Comprehension is therefore not for it a seizing of this concept, which knows sublated naturalness as universal and hence as reconciled with itself, but a seizing of that _representation_ according to which, through the _happening_ of the divine essence's own externalization, through its accomplished becoming-human and its death, the divine essence has been reconciled with its existence. — Seizing this representation now expresses more determinately what was earlier called, within it, the spiritual resurrection, or the becoming of its singular self-consciousness into the universal one, into the community. — Taken _as death_, the _death_ of the divine man is _abstract_ negativity, the movement's immediate result, a movement that ends only in _natural_ universality. This natural significance death loses within spiritual self-consciousness, or death becomes the concept just given; out of what it immediately signifies, out of the non-being of _this singular one_, death is transfigured {{{ 736 }}} into the _universality_ of a spirit that lives in its community, dying and rising again there daily.

What belongs to the element of _representation_, namely that absolute spirit, as _a singular_ one, or rather as a _particular_ one, represents spirit's nature in its existence, is accordingly transposed here into self-consciousness itself, into the knowledge that maintains itself within its _otherness_; this knowledge therefore does not _die_ in actuality, in the way that _the particular one is represented_ as having _actually_ died, but rather its particularity dies away within its universality, that is, within its _knowledge_, which is the essence that reconciles itself with itself. {{ 419 }} The _element of representing_ that immediately precedes is thus posited here as sublated, or has gone back into the self, into the concept of the self; what in that element was merely existent has become subject. — Precisely thereby the _first element, pure thinking_, and the spirit eternal within it are no longer a beyond over against representing consciousness or over against the self; instead, the whole's return into itself is precisely this holding of all moments within itself. — The mediator's death, once the self has seized it, is the sublating of his _objectivity_, that is, of the _particular being-for-itself_ he had; that _particular_ being-for-itself has turned into universal self-consciousness. — The _universal_, on the other side, has by that very means become self-consciousness, and the pure or unactual spirit belonging to mere thinking has become _actual_. — That death of the mediator is a death not only of his _natural side_, that is, of his particular {{{ 737 }}} being-for-itself; it is not only the already dead husk, drawn off from the essence, that dies, but the _abstraction_ of the divine essence as well. For inasmuch as his death has not yet brought the reconciliation to completion, he is the one-sided element that knows the simplicity of thinking to be the _essence_ over against actuality; this extreme, the self, does not yet have a value equal to the essence's; the self has that only in spirit. This representation's death therefore carries within it the dying of that _abstraction of the divine essence_ that is not posited as self. It is the unhappy consciousness's painful feeling that _God himself has died_. This harsh expression expresses the innermost knowledge that simply knows itself, consciousness returning into the night's depth, the night of I = I, a night that distinguishes and knows nothing further outside itself. This feeling is thus in truth the loss of _substance_ and the loss of substance's standing over against consciousness; yet it is at the same time substance's pure _subjectivity_, the pure certainty it has of its own self, which substance lacked as object, as the immediate, as pure essence. This knowledge, then, is the _inspiriting_ whereby substance becomes subject, whereby its abstraction and its lifelessness have died, and whereby it has thus become _actual_, a simple and universal self-consciousness.

Spirit is thus a spirit that knows _itself_; it knows _itself_, and that which is object to it, is; in other words its {{{ 738 }}} representation is the _content_ that is true and absolute, and this content, as we saw, gives expression to spirit itself. At the same time spirit is not merely self-consciousness's _content_, and not merely an object _for it_; it is _actual spirit_ besides. It is this in running through the three elements that its nature has; this movement through its own self constitutes its actuality; — what moves is spirit, spirit is the movement's subject, and spirit is equally _the moving_ itself, the substance that the subject passes through. {{ 420 }} Just as the concept of spirit had come to be for us when we entered religion, that is, as the movement of a spirit certain of itself, a spirit that forgives evil and in doing so gives up its own simplicity and hard immutability, or the movement whereby what is absolutely _opposed_ recognizes itself as _the same_ and this recognizing breaks forth as the _yes_ standing between these extremes, — upon this concept religious consciousness, for which the absolute essence is manifest, _directs_ its _intuition_; and it sublates the _distinction_ between its _self_ and what it has _intuited_, is the substance just as it is the subject, and so _is_ itself spirit, precisely because and insofar as it is this movement.

This community, however, is not yet completed in this self-consciousness of its own; its content stands before it in the form of _representing_ generally, and this splitting-in-two still clings to the community's _actual spirituality_ as well, to its return out of its representing, in the way that pure thinking as an element was itself afflicted {{{ 739 }}} with it; it does not have consciousness about what it is besides; it is the spiritual self-consciousness that is not an object to its own self as this self-consciousness, that does not open itself up into a consciousness of its own self; instead, to the degree that it is consciousness, it has the representations that were considered. — At its final turning point we watch self-consciousness become _inward_ to itself and arrive at _knowledge_ of _being-within-itself_; we watch it externalize its natural existence and win pure negativity. But the _positive_ significance, namely that this negativity, this pure _inwardness_ of _knowledge_, is no less the _self-equal essence_, — or, again, that substance herein arrives at being absolute self-consciousness, this is something _other_ for devotional consciousness. It seizes this side, that knowledge's pure becoming-inward is, _in itself_, absolute simplicity, is substance, taking it as the representation of something that is not so in accordance with the _concept_, and rather as the act of an _alien_ satisfaction. Or, put otherwise, it is not present for it that the pure self's depth is the violence by which the _abstract essence_ gets drawn down out of its abstraction and raised to selfhood by the power that this pure devotion has. — The self's doing thereby keeps this negative significance over against consciousness, because the externalization of substance is, from substance's side, an _in-itself_ for consciousness, one that consciousness does not equally grasp and comprehend, or does not find in _its own_ doing as such. — Since, _in itself_, this unity {{{ 740 }}} between essence and self has come about, consciousness also still has this _representation_ of its reconciliation, but has it as representation. It reaches satisfaction by _externally_ attaching to its own pure negativity the positive significance that it is united with the essence; its satisfaction therefore stays burdened, itself, with the opposition of a beyond. {{ 421 }} Reconciliation of its own therefore enters its consciousness as a _distant_ thing, a distance belonging to the _future_, just as the reconciliation accomplished by the other _self_ appears as a distance belonging to the _past_. Just as the _singular_ divine man has a father who is _in itself_ and has only an _actual_ mother, so the universal divine man, that is, the community, has its _own doing_ and _knowing_ for its father, while _eternal love_ is its mother, a love that it merely _feels_ and does not intuit as an actual immediate _object_ within its consciousness. Its reconciliation is therefore in its heart, yet still split in two from its consciousness, and its actuality still broken. That which steps into its consciousness as being the _in-itself_, that is, as the side of _pure mediation_, is the reconciliation lying beyond; while that which is _present_, the side of _immediacy_ and of _existence_, is that world which has yet to await its transfiguration. The community is indeed reconciled _in itself_ with the essence; and of the _essence_ it is indeed known that it no longer cognizes its object as estranged from itself, but as equal to itself within its love. But {{{ 741 }}} this immediate presence does not yet have the shape of spirit for self-consciousness. The community's spirit is thus, within its immediate consciousness, severed from its religious consciousness, which does pronounce that the two are not separate _in themselves_, but pronounces an _in-itself_ that is not realized, or that has not yet likewise become absolute being-for-itself.
