## b. The Truth of the Enlightenment

The muffled weaving of spirit, which no longer distinguishes anything within itself, has thus, within itself, stepped beyond consciousness, whereas consciousness has by contrast gained clarity about itself. — In respect of its necessity and its condition, what determines the first moment of such clarity is {{{ 523 }}} this: that pure insight, which _in itself_ is the concept, makes itself actual; and it does this by positing otherness, or determinateness, upon insight itself. So taken, insight is negative pure insight, that is, the negation of the concept; this negation is just as pure; and with it there has arisen the _pure thing_, the absolute essence, which apart from this carries no further determination. Spelled out more closely: as absolute concept, insight is a distinguishing of distinctions which are distinctions no longer, a distinguishing of abstractions, of pure concepts, that no longer carry themselves and owe their hold and their distinctness entirely to _the whole of the movement_. What such distinguishing of the undistinguished amounts to is just this: that the absolute concept turns itself into its own _object_ and, in the face of that _movement_, sets itself up as the _essence_. In doing so the essence goes without the aspect in which the abstractions or distinctions get _held apart_, and so what it turns into is _pure thinking_ as _pure thing_. — This, accordingly, is just that muffled, unconscious weaving of spirit inside itself into which faith sank down when it forfeited its differentiated content; — and it is likewise that _movement_ belonging to pure self-consciousness, for which it is meant to be the utterly alien beyond. For, since this pure self-consciousness is movement in pure concepts, in distinguishings that are not distinctions at all, it does in fact collapse together with the unconscious weaving, that is, with pure _feeling_ or with pure _thinghood_. — The self-estranged {{{ 524 }}} concept, however — for it still stands here at the level of this estrangement — does not cognize this _same essence_ in both sides, in the movement of self-consciousness and in its absolute essence, — does not cognize the _same essence_ of the two, which is in fact their substance and their subsistence. {{ 312 }} Failing to cognize this unity, it takes the essence to be valid only under the form of an objective beyond, whereas the distinguishing consciousness, which on these terms keeps the in-itself outside itself, counts as a finite consciousness.

Over that absolute essence the Enlightenment enters into the very conflict with itself that it previously had with faith, and it splits into two parties. A party first corroborates itself as the _victorious_ one by breaking apart into two parties; for in doing so it shows that it possesses in itself the very principle it was fighting, and has thereby sublated the one-sidedness with which it earlier came forward. The interest formerly shared between it and the other party now falls wholly within it and forgets the other, since inside itself it finds the opposition that keeps it occupied. At the same time, though, the opposition has been lifted into that higher, victorious element in which it presents itself purified. So that the discord arising within one party, which looks like a misfortune, proves instead to be its good fortune.

Pure essence itself has no distinction in it; distinction therefore accrues to it in such a way that two such pure essences come forth for consciousness, or {{{ 525 }}} rather a twofold consciousness of pure essence comes forth. — Pure absolute essence has its place only within pure thinking; indeed it simply is pure thinking, and hence it is nothing but the negative essence, lying flatly _beyond_ the finite, beyond _self-consciousness_. Yet on these terms it is precisely _being_, the negative belonging to self-consciousness. And as that _negative_, it is _also_ related to self-consciousness; it is _external being_, and, in being related to that in which distinctions and determinations fall, it receives distinctions upon itself, namely that of being tasted, of being seen, and so forth; and the relationship is _sense_-certainty and perception.

Suppose we take our start from this _sensuous_ being, which is where that negative beyond necessarily ends up, and abstract from the determinate modes of consciousness's relation to it; pure _matter_ is then what remains over, the muffled weaving and stirring within itself. The essential point to observe here is that _pure matter_ is merely the residue that _remains_ once we _abstract_ from sight, touch, taste, and the rest, which is to say, it is not the seen, the tasted, the felt, and the rest; what gets seen, felt, and tasted is never _matter_ but rather the color, a stone, a salt, and so forth; matter is instead the _pure abstraction_; and by this route the _pure essence_ of _thinking_ comes to be at hand, or pure thinking itself, as the absolute that is undifferentiated within, undetermined, and devoid of predicates.

{{{ 526 }}} The one Enlightenment calls the absolute essence that predicateless absolute which lies beyond actual consciousness, in thinking, and from which we set out; — the other calls it _matter_. If the two were distinguished as _nature_ on the one hand and spirit or _God_ on the other, then the unconscious weaving within itself would lack, in order to be nature, the wealth of unfolded life; — and spirit or God would lack the consciousness that distinguishes itself within itself. {{ 313 }} As we have seen, the two are flatly one and the same concept; what separates them lies not in the matter itself but purely in the divergent points of departure taken by the two formations, and in the fact that each halts at a point of its own within the movement of thinking. Were they to step past that point, they would meet and would cognize as one and the same what counts for the one, on its own profession, as an abomination and for the other as folly. For to the one, the absolute essence lies within its pure thinking, or immediately for pure consciousness, outside the finite consciousness, as the _negative_ beyond of the latter. Suppose it reflected that this simple immediacy of thinking is nothing other than _pure being_, and, further, that whatever stands as _negative_ for consciousness bears at once a relation to consciousness, seeing that in the negative judgment it is precisely the _is_ (copula) that holds the two separated terms together as well, — then there would follow a relation of this beyond, taken under the determination of an _external existent_, to consciousness, {{{ 527 }}} and so an identity with what goes by the name of _pure matter_; the lacking moment of _presence_ would thereby have been won. — The other Enlightenment sets out from sensuous being, proceeds to _abstract_ away the sensuous relation of tasting and seeing and the rest, and makes of it the pure _in-itself_, _absolute matter_, that which is neither felt nor tasted; this being has in this way become the predicateless simple, essence of _pure consciousness_; and it is the pure concept as existing _in itself_, or _pure thinking within itself_. Within its own consciousness this insight does not take the countervailing step from _what exists_, which is _purely_ existent, across to the thought, which is the same as the _purely_ existent, nor therefore the step from what is purely positive to what is purely negative; and yet the positive is _pure_ at all only through negation, while the _purely_ negative, precisely as pure, is self-equal within itself and positive on just that account. — Or rather, neither of them has reached the concept of Cartesian metaphysics, that _being in itself_ and _thinking_ are the same, nor the further thought that _being, pure being_, is nothing _concrete and actual_ but the _pure abstraction_; and conversely that pure thinking, self-equality, or essence, is on one side the _negative_ of self-consciousness and hence _being_, and on the other, as immediate simplicity, likewise nothing other than _being_; _thinking_ is _thinghood_, or, equally, _thinghood_ is _thinking_.

{{{ 528 }}} At this point essence carries the _splitting-in-two_ within it only to the extent that it belongs to two sorts of consideration; on one side essence must carry the distinction in its very self, on the other side it is just here that the two sorts of consideration run together into one; for those abstract moments, pure being and the negative, through which the two sorts differ, are then united in the object that these ways of considering have. — The universal they share is the abstraction of a pure quivering within itself, or of pure thinking of itself. {{ 314 }} This simple movement rotating on its own axis must throw itself apart, because it is movement at all only in distinguishing its own moments. What such distinguishing of the moments leaves behind is the unmoved, the hollow shell of pure _being_, which is actual thinking no longer, no longer any life within itself; for as the distinction, it is the whole of the content. But this distinguishing, which posits itself _outside_ that _unity_, is thereby the alternation among the moments _in-itself_, _being for another_, and being-_for-itself_, an alternation _that does not return into itself_; — it is actuality in the shape it wears as object for pure insight's actual consciousness, — it is _utility_.

However poorly utility may show up in the eyes of faith, or of sentimentality, or even of that abstraction which styles itself speculation and fixes the _in-itself_ for itself, utility is nonetheless where pure insight brings its realization to completion and where insight is its own _object_, {{{ 529 }}} an object it no longer disavows and one that does not carry for it the worth of something empty or of a pure beyond. For, as we saw, pure insight is the existent concept itself, the self-equal pure personality, and it draws distinctions inside itself in such fashion that each distinguished term is itself pure concept, which is to say, is immediately not distinguished at all; pure insight is a simple, pure self-consciousness standing _for itself_ every bit as much as _in itself_, within one immediate unity. Hence what insight has as _being-in-itself_ is no abiding _being_; it immediately stops being anything in its distinctness, and a being of that sort, one that immediately has no hold, is not _in itself_ but essentially _for another_, namely the power by which it gets absorbed. Yet this second moment, set over against the first, against being-_in-itself_, disappears every bit as immediately as the first did; or rather, as _being only for another_ it is _disappearing_ itself, and thereby it is what has _returned_ into itself, it is _being-for-itself posited_. But this simple being-for-itself, as self-equality, is rather _a being_, and hence _for another_. — The useful gives expression to this nature that pure insight has in the _unfolding of its moments_, that is, to pure insight as _object_. The useful is something that subsists _in itself_, a thing, and yet such being-in-itself amounts to no more than a pure moment; the useful is therefore absolutely _for another_, though it is for another only in the measure in which it is in itself; and these opposed moments have gone back into the inseparable unity of {{{ 530 }}} being-for-itself. Now although the useful does give expression to the concept of pure insight, it does so not as that insight as such but as insight in the form of _representation_ or as its _object_; it is nothing more than the tireless alternation of those moments, of which one is admittedly the very having-returned-into-itself, yet only as being-_for-itself_, that is, as an abstract moment stepping aside over against the rest. The useful is not itself that negative essence which holds these moments, opposed as they are, at once _undivided_ under _one_ and _the same regard_, or holds them in itself as a _thinking_, in the way they are when they are pure insight; the useful does carry within it the moment of _being-for-itself_, yet not in such fashion that it _reaches out over_ the other moments, the _in-itself_ and being for another, and would thereby be the _self_. {{ 315 }} So in the useful pure insight has for its _object_ its own concept in that concept's _pure_ moments; it is the consciousness of this _metaphysics_, though not yet the comprehension of it; the _unity_ of _being_ and of the _concept_ itself has still not been arrived at. Since for pure insight the useful still wears the form of an object, insight goes on having a _world_, one that no longer exists in and for itself and yet is still a world, which it marks off from itself. Yet since the oppositions have stepped out to the sharp point of the concept, the next stage will be that they collapse together and that the Enlightenment comes to experience the fruit of its own deeds.

{{{ 531 }}} If we consider the object now reached in relation to this whole sphere, the actual world of culture had gathered itself up into the _vanity_ of self-consciousness, — into the _being-for-itself_ that still has that world's confusion for its content and that is still the _singular_ concept, not yet the concept that is _universal_ for itself. Returned into itself, however, that concept is _pure insight_, — pure consciousness in the guise of the pure _self_, that is, negativity, while faith is that very same pure consciousness in the guise of _pure thinking_, that is, positivity. In that self faith possesses the moment which completes it; — but since faith goes under through this very supplement, pure insight is now where the two moments come into view for us: as the absolute essence, purely _thought_ or negative — and as _matter_, the positive _existent_. — What this completeness still lacks is that _actuality_ of self-consciousness proper to the _vain_ consciousness, — the world from which thinking lifted itself up to itself. In utility this lack is made good to the extent that pure insight has won positive objectivity there; insight is on that account an actual consciousness, satisfied inside itself. Such objectivity now constitutes insight's _world_; it has turned into the truth of the whole preceding world, the ideal one as much as the real. The first world of spirit is the outspread realm of its self-dispersing existence and of the isolated _certainty_ of itself; just as nature scatters its life into {{{ 532 }}} shapes of endless variety, with no _genus_ of those shapes at hand. The second world holds the _genus_, and constitutes the realm of _being-in-itself_, of _truth_ as against that certainty. The third, though, the useful, is that _truth_ which is at the same time the _certainty_ of itself. What the realm of _faith_'s truth lacks is the principle of _actuality_, of certainty of its own self as this _singular_. Actuality, on the other hand, certainty of its own self as this singular, is what lacks the _in-itself_. Both worlds are unified in what pure insight has for its object. {{ 316 }} The useful is that object, insofar as self-consciousness sees clean through it and finds in it its own _singular certainty_, its enjoyment (its _being-for-itself_); in this fashion self-consciousness _sees_ its way _into_ the object, and such insight holds within it the _true_ essence of the object (namely, being something seen through, or being _for another_); insight is therefore itself _true knowledge_, and self-consciousness possesses with equal immediacy its own universal certainty, its _pure consciousness_, within this relationship, a relationship in which, accordingly, _truth_ is bound up with presence and _actuality_ alike. Both worlds are reconciled, and heaven has been transplanted down onto the earth.
