## {{{ 59 }}} III. Force and the Understanding, Appearance and Supersensible World

Sense-certainty's dialectic robbed consciousness of hearing, of sight, and of the rest, and in perceiving it arrived at thoughts, though only in the unconditioned universal does it first gather these thoughts into one. Taken as a quiescent simple essence, this unconditioned would once again amount to nothing but the _extreme_ of _being-for-itself_ that steps off to one side, since the unessential would then confront it; and standing in that relation it would rank as unessential itself, leaving consciousness inside perceiving's deception after all; what has emerged, however, is something that has withdrawn into itself out of precisely such a conditioned being-for-itself. — True object of consciousness though it now is, this unconditioned universal still stands there merely as consciousness's _object_; of its _concept_ as _concept_ consciousness has as yet taken no hold. The two call for an essential distinction; out of the relation to another the object has, for consciousness, withdrawn into itself, {{{ 60 }}} and become thereby concept _in itself_; yet consciousness is not the concept for its own self, which is why what it fails to find in that reflected object is itself. _For us_, the coming-to-be of this object out of consciousness's movement took a form in which consciousness is woven into that coming-to-be, so that one and the same reflection, a single reflection, occurs on either side. Since, though, what filled this movement as consciousness's content was the objective essence alone and never consciousness as such, the result has to be posited for consciousness with an objective significance, consciousness meanwhile still drawing back from what has arisen, so that this, in its objectivity, counts for it as the essence.

With this the understanding has of course sublated both untruths, the object's and its own; and what has arisen for it in consequence is the concept of the true; a true that is _in itself_, one that is not yet concept, or that goes without the _being-for-itself_ belonging to consciousness, and that the understanding, not knowing itself within it, simply allows to proceed. On its own account this true drives its essence forward, so that consciousness plays no part in the free realization of it but watches from outside and apprehends it purely. {{ 83 }} _We_ therefore have for the time being to step into consciousness's place and to be the concept that develops what is contained in the result; only on this developed object, which offers itself to consciousness {{{ 61 }}} as something that is, does consciousness become for itself a comprehending consciousness.

What resulted was the unconditioned universal, to begin with in a negative, abstract sense, inasmuch as consciousness denied its own one-sided concepts and drew away from them, that is, surrendered them. In itself, though, the result carries a positive significance: within it the unity _of being-for-itself_ and _of being-for-an-other_, the absolute opposition, stands posited straightaway as one and the same essence. To begin with, the point looks as if it bore merely on how the moments stand to one another in respect of form; yet being-for-itself and being-for-another equally make up the _content_ itself, seeing that the opposition, truly taken, admits of no nature besides the one the result brought out, namely that whatever perceiving held to be true as content belongs in fact to form alone and dissolves into the unity of form. At the same time this content is universal; no content whatever could, by virtue of its particular constitution, exempt itself from returning into this unconditioned universality. Such a content would be some determinate way of being for itself and of relating to another. But _being for itself_ and _relating to another as such_ make up its _nature_ and _essence_, whose truth is to be an unconditioned universal; and the result is universal without qualification.

{{{ 62 }}} Since, though, consciousness has this unconditioned universal before it as an object, form and content come apart within it, and under the guise of content the moments wear the very aspect in which they presented themselves at the start: on one side a universal medium of many subsisting matters, on the other a One reflected into itself, where the self-subsistence of those matters is wiped out. The former is the dissolution of the thing's self-subsistence, or the passivity that is a being for another, whereas the latter is being-for-itself. We have to see how these moments present themselves within the unconditioned universality that is their essence. It is clear at once that, precisely because they are only within this universality, they no longer lie apart from one another at all, but are essentially sides that sublate themselves in their own right, so that only their passing over into each other is posited.

One moment, then, makes its appearance as the essence that has stepped aside, as a universal medium, or as that wherein self-subsistent matters subsist. But the _self-subsistence_ of these matters is nothing other than this medium; that is, this _universal_ is through and through the _multiplicity_ of such diverse universals. To say that in its own self the universal stands in undivided unity with this multiplicity means that each of these matters is where the other is, that they run through one another — yet never touch, since conversely the many differentiated items are equally self-subsistent. {{ 84 }} With this their pure porosity {{{ 63 }}} or their being sublated is posited at the same time. This being sublated once more, or the drawing back of such diversity into _pure being-for-itself_, comes to nothing but the medium, and the medium is what the _self-subsistence_ of the differences amounts to. Or: what is posited as self-subsistent passes over immediately into its unity, and its unity immediately into the unfolding, and this back again into the reduction. This movement, however, is what is called _force_; the one moment of it, namely force as the spreading out of the self-subsistent matters in their being, is force's _expression_; force as the having-vanished of those matters is force _pressed back_ into itself out of its expression, or _force proper_. But, in the first place, force pressed back into itself _must_ come to expression; and in the second, within that expression it is no less force existing _within itself_ than, in this being-within-itself, it is expression. — Since we thus hold both moments in their immediate unity, it is really the understanding, to which the concept of force belongs, that is _the concept_ carrying the differentiated moments as differentiated; for _in force itself_ they are not supposed to be differentiated; the difference is thus only in thought. — Or: in what precedes only the concept of force has so far been posited, not its reality. Force, in truth, is the unconditioned universal, being in its own self exactly what it is _for an other_; or, put differently, it carries the difference — difference being nothing else than the being {{{ 64 }}} _for an other_ — within its own self. If force is to be in its truth, thought must let it go entirely and posit it as the substance of these differences, meaning _at the one point, force_, as this whole force, abiding essentially _in and for itself_, and _then_ its _differences_ as _substantial_, as moments that subsist for themselves. Force as such, or force pressed back into itself, is thus for itself an _excluding_ One, and to it _the_ unfolding of the matters counts as _another subsisting essence_; _so_ two differentiated self-subsistent sides stand posited. But force is the whole as well, or it abides as what its concept makes it, namely: these _differences_ persist as pure forms, superficial _vanishing moments. The differences_ separating force proper, _pressed back_ into itself, from the _unfolding_ of the self-subsistent matters would at the same time be nothing at all had they no _subsistence_, just as force would fail to be unless it _existed_ in this opposed manner; but that it exists in this opposed manner says nothing beyond this, that both moments are themselves at once _self-subsistent_. — This movement, then, in which the two moments continually make themselves self-subsistent and again sublate themselves, is what has to be considered. — Broadly it is evident that this movement is none other than perceiving's own movement, where both sides, the perceiving one and the perceived, {{{ 65 }}} are at once one and undifferentiated in the _apprehending_ of the true, while each side is nonetheless _reflected_ into itself, existing for itself. {{ 85 }} In the present case these two sides are moments of force; they stand within a unity no less than that unity, which shows up as the middle set against the extremes that are for themselves, keeps decomposing into precisely those extremes, which owe their being to nothing else. — The movement that earlier showed itself as the self-annihilation of contradictory concepts accordingly wears the _objective_ form here, being force's movement, and its result is the emergence of the unconditioned universal as something _non-objective_, or as the _inner_ of things.

Force, as it has been determined, is, insofar as it is represented as force _as such_ or as _reflected into itself_, the one side of its concept; but it is so as a substantiated extreme, and indeed as the one posited under the determinateness of the One. The _subsistence_ of the unfolded matters is thereby shut out of force, and is _other_ than force. Given the necessity that _force itself_ should be this _subsistence_, that is, that it should _express_ itself, the way its expression represents itself is that _the other in question_ _comes up_ to it and solicits it. But in fact, since it _necessarily_ expresses itself, it has within its own self what was posited as another essence. What has to be withdrawn is the positing of force as _a One_ and of its essence, expressing itself, as something else that approaches it from outside; force is instead itself the universal {{{ 66 }}} medium wherein the moments have their subsistence as matters; or _it has expressed itself_, and the supposed other, the soliciting one, is rather force itself. So force exists at present as the medium of matters unfolded. But equally essentially it wears the form of the subsisting matters' sublatedness, or it is essentially _One; this being-One_ is thus _now_, given that _force_ has been posited as the matters' medium, _other than force_, and force keeps this essence of its own outside itself. But since necessity requires it to be what it has _not yet_ been posited as, _this other comes up_ and solicits it toward reflection into itself, or sublates its expression. In truth, however, _force itself_ is this being-reflected-into-itself, this sublatedness of the expression; being-One disappears just _as_ it showed up, that is, as _another; force is that itself_, force is force pressed back into itself.

Whatever steps forward as other, soliciting force toward expression as well as toward its return into itself, proves immediately to be _force itself_; for that other exhibits itself no less as universal medium than as One, each of these shapes stepping forward only ever as a moment that vanishes. So force, in having another for itself and in being for another, has by no means yet stepped outside its concept. But at the same time two forces are present; the concept of both is indeed the same, but it has gone out of its unity into duality. Instead of the opposition {{{ 67 }}} remaining throughout essentially only a moment, it seems, through this splitting in two, to have withdrawn into wholly _self-subsistent forces_ beyond the dominion of unity. {{ 86 }} What this self-subsistence amounts to is to be looked at more closely. To begin with, the second force steps forward as the soliciting one, and, as regards its content, as universal medium, set against the force determined as solicited; but the former, being essentially the alternation of these two moments and being force in its own right, is in fact universal medium _only first_ _through being solicited to it_, and equally is negative unity, that which solicits force to go back into itself, only _in that it is solicited_. So this difference too, which obtained between the pair, that one of them should be the _soliciting_ one and the other the _solicited_, converts into that same mutual exchange of determinatenesses.

So the play of the two forces consists in their being determined in this opposed way, in their being for one another under that determination, and in the absolutely immediate interchange of the determinations — a transition, and only by it do there exist those determinations under which the forces look as though they came forward as _self-subsistent_. The soliciting one, for example, is posited as universal medium, and against it the solicited one as force pressed back into itself; but the former is universal medium only because the other is force pressed back into itself; {{{ 68 }}} or rather the latter is what solicits the former, and only thereby makes it the medium. The former owes its determinateness wholly to the other, and it solicits only to the extent that the other solicits it into soliciting; and just as immediately it loses the determinateness thus conferred on it, since that determinateness crosses over to the other, or rather has crossed over to it already; the alien thing soliciting force steps forward as universal medium, yet only because force has solicited it into this; which means, however, that _force posits_ it so, and _is_ rather _itself essentially_ universal medium; force posits the soliciting one so for the reason that this second determination belongs essentially to _it_, that is, because _it is rather itself this other_.

For the sake of rounding out our grasp of this movement's concept, one may point out in addition that the differences themselves turn up within a doubled difference: _first_ as differences _of content_, the one extreme being force reflected into itself and the other the medium of the matters; _second_ as differences of _form_, the one soliciting, the other solicited, that one active and this one passive. By the content-difference they _are_ differentiated at all, that is, for us; by the form-difference, though, they are self-subsistent, cutting themselves off from one another in their relation and set in opposition. That in respect of {{{ 69 }}} both these sides the extremes are thus nothing _in themselves_, but that these sides, in which their differentiated essence was supposed to consist, are only vanishing moments, an immediate passing over of each into its opposite, all this comes about for consciousness in the perception of the movement of force. {{ 87 }} For us, though, as was recalled above, there was this further point: that in themselves the differences, taken as _differences of content and of form_, vanished, and that where form is concerned the _active, soliciting_, or _being-for-itself_ coincided in essence with what, where content is concerned, was force pressed back into itself; while the passive, _solicited_, or being-for-an-other belonging to form coincided with what, on content's side, showed up as the universal medium of the many matters.

What follows from this is that, by doubling into two forces, the concept of force turns _actual_, and it follows how this comes about. These two forces exist as essences that are for themselves; but their existence is a movement against each other of such a kind that their _being_ is rather a pure _being posited through an other_, that is, that their being has rather the pure significance of _vanishing_. They do not stand as extremes that would hold on to something firm on their own account and dispatch merely an external property into the middle and into their mutual contact; on the contrary, whatever they are, they are so exclusively within that middle and that contact. In it there is immediately just as much force's being pressed back into itself, or _being-for-itself_, as {{{ 70 }}} the expression, the soliciting as much as the being solicited; these moments are thus not distributed between two self-subsistent extremes that would offer each other only an opposed tip; rather, their essence is simply this, that each is only through the other and immediately ceases to be what it thus is through the other, in the very act of being it. They therefore in fact have no substances of their own that would bear and sustain them. Rather it is the _concept_ of force that keeps itself intact as _the essence_ within force's own _actuality_; _force as actual_ lies wholly in the _expression_ alone, which is at once nothing but a self-sublating. Represented as free of its expression and as being for itself, this _actual_ force is force pressed back into itself; but this determinateness is in fact, as has emerged, itself only a moment of the _expression_. So the truth of force stays nothing more than the _thought_ of force; and with no support to sustain them, its actuality's moments, its substances along with its movement, come crashing together into an undifferentiated unity, a unity that is not force pressed back into itself, that being itself only such a moment, but rather _its concept, as concept_. Force's realization accordingly amounts at once to a forfeiting of reality; therein force has turned into something wholly other, namely this _universality_, which the understanding cognizes at the outset, immediately, as force's essence, and which likewise establishes itself as force's essence in {{{ 71 }}} the reality force was supposed to have, in the actual substances.

{{ 88 }} So long as we take _the first_ universal to be the understanding's _concept_, wherein force has not yet come to be for itself, the second now counts as force's _essence_ in the way that essence displays itself _in_ and _for itself_. Or, turning it around: should the first universal be taken as the _immediate_, which was to serve consciousness as an _actual_ object, then this second stands determined as the _negative_ of sensuously objective force; it is force as force, in its genuine essence, exists solely as _object of the understanding_; the former would be force pressed back into itself, force as substance; whereas this second is the _inner_ of things, taken as _inner_, and that coincides with the concept as concept.

The true essence of things has by now determined itself so as not to be there immediately for consciousness; consciousness instead relates to the inner by way of mediation and, being understanding, _peers through this middle formed by the play of forces into the genuine background of things_. That middle which locks together understanding and inner, the two extremes, is force's developed _being_, and for the understanding this being has by now become a _vanishing_. It is therefore called _appearance;_ for we call shine that _being_ which immediately in its own self is a _non-being_. But it is not merely a shine; it is appearance, a _whole_ of shine. It is this _whole_, taken as whole or as _universal_, that makes up the {{{ 72 }}} _inner_, the _play of forces_ in its _reflection_ into itself. Within it the essences of perception stand _posited_ for consciousness objectively just as they are in themselves, that is, as moments changing straightaway into their opposite, restless and without being: the One straightaway into the universal, the essential straightaway into the unessential, and the other way round. This play of forces is thus the developed negative, whereas the truth of it lies on the positive side, in the _universal_, in the object having being _in itself_. — That this object has _being_ _for_ consciousness comes about by mediation, through the movement of _appearance_, where the _being of perception_ and whatever is sensuously objective carries a merely negative significance, so that out of it consciousness reflects into itself as into the true, yet, as consciousness, turns this true once more into an objective _inner_, holding apart the reflection of things from the reflection into its own self; just as the mediating movement remains for it an objective one too. {{ 89 }} To consciousness, then, this inner is an extreme standing over against it; and it counts as the true for consciousness just because within it, as within the _in-itself_, consciousness possesses at the same time its own certainty, the moment of its being-for-itself; of this ground, however, consciousness is as yet unaware, since the _being-for-itself_ the inner was to have in its own self could only be the negative movement, and that movement is for consciousness still the _objective_ {{{ 73 }}} appearance that vanishes, not yet a being-for-itself of its _own_; so the inner is indeed concept for consciousness, only the nature of the concept is something consciousness does not yet know.

Within the _inner true_, that _absolutely universal_ which has been cleansed of the _opposition_ between universal and singular and has come about _for the understanding_, a _supersensible_ world as the _true_ world first discloses itself over the _sensuous_ world as the _world of appearance_, the enduring _beyond_ over the vanishing _here below_; an in-itself that constitutes reason's initial and hence still imperfect appearance, or merely the pure element wherein truth holds its _essence_.

_Our object_ from here on is accordingly the syllogism whose extremes are the inner of things and the understanding, with appearance serving as its middle; the movement of that syllogism, though, delivers a closer determination both of what the understanding catches sight of within the inner across the middle, and of the experience it undergoes regarding this relation of being joined together.

For consciousness the inner remains a _pure beyond_, since consciousness has not yet found itself within it; the inner is _empty_, being nothing but appearance's nothing and, on the positive side, the simple universal. That the inner should be in this way concedes the point at once to whoever claims that things' inner admits of no cognition; the ground for saying so, however, would have to be conceived otherwise. Of this inner, {{{ 74 }}} in its immediate state here, we do indeed have no acquaintance, though not because reason sees too little a way, or is confined, or bears whatever other name one likes to give it; of which nothing is known at this stage, our penetration not having gone so deep; but rather on account of the plain nature of the matter itself, namely that in the _empty_ nothing gets cognized, or, put from the opposite side, that the inner has been determined precisely as consciousness's _beyond_. — Naturally it comes to the same thing whether a blind man is set down amid the riches of the supersensible world, — assuming it has any, whether their content belongs to that world itself or consciousness is that content, — or a sighted man is set down in pure darkness, or, if one prefers, in pure light, assuming the world is only that; in his pure light the sighted man sees as little as in his pure darkness, and precisely as much as the blind man sees amid the profusion of riches that might lie spread before him. {{ 90 }} If there were nothing more to the inner and to being joined with it through appearance, nothing would be left but to keep to appearance, that is, to take as true something we know is not true; or else, so that there may after all be something in the empty, which has indeed first arisen as an emptiness of objective things, but which, _as emptiness in itself_, must also be taken for the emptiness of all spiritual relations and of the differences of consciousness as {{{ 75 }}} consciousness, — so that there may after all be something in this _wholly empty_, which is also called the _holy_, to fill it with reveries, with _appearances_ that consciousness generates for itself; consciousness would have to accept such shabby handling, deserving nothing finer, given that reveries at least outrank its emptiness.

The inner, though, the supersensible beyond, has _arisen_; it _comes_ out of appearance, which is its mediation; or _its essence is appearance_, which in fact fills it out. What the supersensible amounts to is the sensuous and perceived posited according to its _truth_; and the _truth_ of what is _sensuous_ and perceived consists in being _appearance_. So the supersensible amounts to _appearance_ taken as _appearance_. — Should one suppose at this point that the supersensible is _thus_ the sensuous world, the world such as it stands _for immediate sense-certainty and perception_, the understanding of it is inverted; for appearance is rather _not_ the world of sensuous knowledge and perceiving as a world that is, but that same world posited _as sublated_, that is, in truth _as inner_. People are in the habit of saying that the supersensible is _not_ appearance; yet what they understand by appearance is not appearance at all, but instead the _sensuous_ world regarded as real actuality in its own right.

{{{ 76 }}} Our object, the understanding, stands at precisely this point: the inner has become for it no more than the universal, the as yet unfilled _in-itself_; the play of forces bears just this negative significance of not being in itself, and only the positive one of being what _mediates_, though outside the understanding. Its relation to the inner through the mediation, however, is its movement, through which the inner will become filled for it. — The play of forces is what is _immediate_ for it; _the true_, however, is for it the simple inner; the movement of force is therefore likewise the true only as something _simple_ in general. Regarding this play of forces, however, we have seen it to be so constituted that whichever force gets _solicited_ by another is equally the one _soliciting_ that other, which for its part turns into a soliciting force only in that way. Present here, likewise, is nothing but the immediate switching, the absolute exchange, of that _determinateness_ which constitutes the whole _content_ of whatever steps forward: to be either universal medium or negative unity. In its determinate coming forward it immediately ceases of itself to be what it comes forward as; by its determinate coming forward it solicits the other side, which thereby _expresses_ itself; that is, this other side is immediately now what the first was supposed to be. {{ 91 }} Both of these sides, the _relation_ of soliciting and the _relation_ of determinate opposed content, are, _each on its own_, the absolute inversion {{{ 77 }}} and exchange. But those two relations are in turn one and the same, and the _form_-difference, between being solicited and being soliciting, coincides with the _content_-difference: the solicited as such being the passive medium, while the soliciting one is the active, the negative unity, or the One. With this, any difference at all between the _particular forces_ said to be at work in this movement drops away; for such differences rested on nothing but the ones just named; and the difference between the forces sinks, along with those two, into a single one. Thus force, along with soliciting and being solicited, along with the determinateness of being a subsisting medium and a unity reflected into itself, amounts to nothing when taken singly, nor do these make up a variety of oppositions; what is at hand in this absolute alternation is solely the _difference as universal_, difference as that into which the many oppositions have reduced themselves. This _difference as universal_ is accordingly _what is simple within the play of force itself_, and that play's true; it is the _law of force_.

By relating to what is simple in the inner, that is, in the understanding, appearance in its absolute alternation turns into the _simple difference_. To begin with, the inner is nothing but the universal in itself; yet this _universal_, simple in itself, is no less essentially and absolutely the _universal difference_; for {{{ 78 }}} alternation itself is what yields it, or alternation is its essence; and alternation, once posited within the inner in the way it truly is, gets taken up into the inner as a difference no less absolutely universal, tranquil, and self-consistent. Put otherwise: the universal has negation as an essential moment of it, so that negation, or mediation, within the universal counts as _universal difference_. It is expressed in the _law_, as the _constant_ image of unsteady appearance. The _supersensible_ world is thus a _tranquil realm of laws_, beyond the perceived world, to be sure, since the latter exhibits the law only through constant change, yet just as much _present_ in that world and its immediate still likeness.

Granted, this realm of laws constitutes the understanding's truth, a truth whose _content_ lies in the difference the law contains; but the realm is also no more than the understanding's _first truth_, and it leaves appearance unfilled. The law is present in appearance, but it is not appearance's entire presence; under ever different circumstances it has an ever different actuality. So a side remains over to appearance, _for itself_, one that does not lie within the inner; in other words, appearance has not truly been posited yet as _appearance_, as a being-for-itself that is _sublated_. This defect of the law is bound to come out on the law's own face as well. {{ 92 }} The lack apparent in it seems to be this: difference itself is present in it, but only as universal, indeterminate difference. {{{ 79 }}} But insofar as it is not _the_ law in general but _a_ law, it has determinateness in it; and thus there are indeterminately _many_ laws. Yet such multiplicity is rather a defect in its own right; it runs counter to the understanding's principle, since for the understanding, as consciousness of the simple inner, the true lies in the _unity_ that is universal in itself. So the understanding is obliged rather to make the many laws coincide in _one_ law. Just as, for example, the falling of a stone and the motion of the celestial spheres have had their laws comprehended as _one_ law. With this coinciding, however, determinateness slips away from the laws; law grows steadily shallower, and what gets found is in fact no unity of _these determinate_ laws but a law that drops their determinateness; much as the one law uniting in itself the laws of terrestrial falling bodies and of celestial motion in fact fails to express the two of them. Gathering all laws into _universal attraction_ gives voice to no content beyond the _bare concept of law itself_, posited therein as something that _is_. Universal attraction says only this, that _everything has a constant difference_ from _something else_. The understanding imagines it has hit on a universal law here, one voicing universal actuality _as such_; what it has hit on, in fact, is merely the _concept_ of _law {{{ 80 }}} itself_, albeit in a manner that also states this much, that _all_ actuality is law-governed _in its own self_. The expression _universal attraction_ carries great weight, then, insofar as it takes aim at thoughtless _representation_, to which everything presents itself in contingency's shape and for which determinateness wears the form of sensuous self-subsistence.

Set against the determinate laws, then, stands universal attraction, the pure concept of law. So far as one takes this pure concept for the essence, for the true inner, the _determinateness_ of a determinate law still counts as belonging to appearance, or rather to sensuous being. Yet the pure _concept_ of law reaches past not only that law which is itself a _determinate one over against other determinate_ laws; it reaches _beyond law_ as such. The determinateness in question is really nothing more than a moment that vanishes, one that can no longer figure here as an essentiality; for the law alone is at hand as the true; and yet the _concept_ of law turns against _law_ itself. For within the law difference itself is apprehended _immediately_ and received into the universal, and along with that comes a _subsistence_ belonging to the moments whose relation the law voices, taken as essentialities that are indifferent and in themselves. {{ 93 }} But those portions of the difference {{{ 81 }}} within the law are at once determinate sides themselves; the pure concept of law, as universal attraction, has to be apprehended in its true meaning thus: within it, as within something absolutely _simple_, the _differences_ that belong to law as _such_ in their turn _go back into the inner as into a simple unity;_ that unity is law's inner _necessity_.

Law is on hand, then, in two ways at once: once as law whose differences find expression as self-subsistent moments; once in the form of a _simple_ having-gone-back-into-itself, which may once more be named _force_, though not as force pressed back into itself; force in general instead, force as the concept of force, an abstraction drawing into itself even those differences of attracting and being attracted. Thus _simple_ electricity, for instance, is the _force_; expressing the difference, however, falls to _the law_; and the difference in question is electricity positive and negative. With falling motion the _force_ is the simple, namely _gravity_, whose _law_ runs that the magnitudes of the motion's differentiated moments, the _time_ that has passed and the _space_ covered, relate as root to square. Electricity as such is not difference in itself, nor in its essence the doubled essence made of positive and negative electricity; {{{ 82 }}} which is why people habitually say it _has_ the law of _being_ so, indeed that it _has the property_ of so expressing itself. Admittedly that property is the sole and essential one this force has, or is _necessary_ for it. But necessity is here an empty word; force simply _must_ double itself in this way _because_ it _must_. Certainly, once _positive_ electricity is posited, _negative electricity is necessary in itself_ as well; for the _positive_ exists only by relating to a _negative_, or the positive constitutes _in its own self_ its difference from itself, and so does the negative. But that electricity as such should divide so is not what is necessary in itself; electricity as _simple force_ stands indifferent to its law, the law of _being_ positive and negative; and naming the one its concept and the other its being, we get a concept indifferent to that being; electricity merely _has_ this property, and this says just that the property is not necessary to it _in itself_. — The indifference assumes a different shape when one says that being positive and negative goes into the _definition_ of electricity, or that this is quite simply _its concept and essence_. Its being would then signify _its existence_ at large; yet what such a definition does not contain is the _necessity of its existence;_ either electricity is because one _comes across_ it, in which case it is in no way necessary, or its existence stems from other forces, in which case the necessity attaching to it is external. {{ 94 }} But in placing {{{ 83 }}} necessity in the determinateness of _being through another_, we fall back into the _multiplicity_ of determinate laws that we have just left in order to consider _law_ as law; only with law as law is its _concept_ as concept, or its necessity, to be compared, a necessity which, however, has so far shown itself in all these forms to be no more than an empty word.

Beyond the way just pointed out, there is still another in which law and force, or concept and being, stand indifferent to one another. In the law of motion, for example, motion must of necessity _divide_ itself into time and space, or again into distance and velocity. Since motion is only the relationship of those moments, motion, the universal, is here indeed divided _in its very self_; but nothing in these parts, in time and space, or in distance and velocity, expresses this derivation from a One; each stands indifferent to the other, for space gets represented as capable of being without time, time as capable of being without space, and distance at least as capable of being without velocity, — much as their magnitudes too are indifferent toward one another; since they do not relate to one another _as positive_ and _negative_, they are for that reason not connected by _their essence_. So the necessity of the _division_ is indeed at hand here, though not a necessity of the _parts_ for one another as such. But just for that reason the first necessity too is itself merely a false necessity held up before us; {{{ 84 }}} motion, namely, is not itself represented as something _simple_ or as pure essence, but _already_ as divided; time and space count as its _self-subsistent_ parts, as _essences in their very selves_, and distance and velocity as ways of being or of being represented, of which the one may well be without the other, so that motion amounts merely to their _superficial_ relation and not to their essence. Taken as simple essence, that is, represented as force, motion is of course _gravity_, which for its part harbors none of these differences within it at all.

In both cases, then, the difference is not a _difference in its very self;_ either the universal, force, is indifferent toward the division that the law contains, or else it is the parts of the law, the differences, that are indifferent toward one another. Yet the understanding _does have_ the concept of _this difference in itself_, and has it precisely in this: that the law, for one part of it, is the inner as what has being _in itself_, while _within it_ the law is at the same time _differentiated_; and that the difference in question is thereby _inner_ shows itself in the law's being _simple_ force, or being as the _concept_ of that force, hence a _difference of the concept_. But this inner difference still falls, as yet, only _within the understanding;_ it is not yet _posited on the matter itself_. {{ 95 }} What the understanding utters, then, is only its _own_ necessity: a difference it makes only by declaring, in the same breath, that the difference is no _difference of the matter itself_. Such a necessity, {{{ 85 }}} residing as it does merely in the word, thus amounts to a recital of the moments that make up the circle of that necessity; the moments do get distinguished, but their difference is at the same time declared to be no difference of the matter itself, and hence is at once sublated again; this movement goes by the name of _explaining_. A _law_ is uttered, and from it there is distinguished what is universal in it in itself, its ground, as _force_; but of this difference it is then said that it is no difference at all, and that the ground has exactly the constitution the law has. The singular event of lightning, for example, is apprehended as something universal, and this universal is uttered as the _law_ of electricity; whereupon the explanation gathers the _law_ together into _force_, taken as the essence of the law. Force is then given _such a constitution_ that in expressing itself it brings out opposed electricities, which vanish back into one another, that is, _force has precisely the constitution that the law has;_ the two, it is said, are not distinguished in the least. The differences here are the pure universal expression, that is, the law, and pure force; yet the content of both is _the same_, their constitution _the same_; difference taken as a difference of content, that is, of the _matter_, is thus withdrawn once more.

In this tautological movement the understanding, as it turns out, keeps to the tranquil unity that its object has, and the movement belongs only {{{ 86 }}} to the understanding, not to the object; it is an explaining that not merely explains nothing, but is so transparent that, in making as if to say something distinct from what has already been stated, it says nothing at all and merely repeats the same thing. On the matter itself this movement brings nothing new into being; it comes into view instead as the understanding's own movement. Within it, however, we now cognize just what the law was found to lack, namely absolute alternation itself; for this _movement_, considered up close, is immediately its own opposite. For it posits _a_ _difference_ which is not only _no difference_ for us, but which the movement itself sublates as difference. This is the very alternation that exhibited itself as the play of forces; in it lay the difference between soliciting force and solicited, between force expressing itself and force driven back into itself; but these were differences that in truth were none, and for just that reason sublated themselves again immediately. What lies before us is not mere unity, in which case _no difference would be posited_, but rather this _***movement***_, in which _a difference is indeed made but_, being none, _is sublated again_. — With explaining, then, the changing and alternating that formerly stood outside the inner and attached only to appearance has forced its way into the supersensible itself; our consciousness, however, has passed over from the inner as its object to {{{ 87 }}} the opposite side, into the _understanding_, and possesses the alternation there.

{{ 96 }} So understood, this alternation is not yet an alternation of the matter itself; it exhibits itself as _pure alternation_ precisely because the _content_ of the alternation's moments stays the same. But since the _concept_, being the understanding's own concept, coincides with what the _inner_ of things is, there arises for the understanding _this alternation as the law of the inner_. The understanding therefore _experiences_ this: that differences come to be which are no differences is a _law_ of _appearance itself_; or that the _like-named_ _repels_ itself from itself; and likewise that the differences are only such as are in truth none and sublate themselves; or that the _unlike-named_ _attracts_ itself. — A _second law_, then, whose content stands opposed to what was earlier called law, namely to difference that abides constantly the same; for what this new law expresses is rather the _becoming-unequal of what is equal_ and the _becoming-equal of what is unequal_. The concept expects of thoughtlessness that it hold the two laws together and become conscious of the opposition between them. — The second is admittedly a law as well, an inner self-equal being, though a self-equality rather of inequality, a constancy of what is inconstant. — In the play of forces it was precisely this law that emerged as just this absolute transition, as pure alternation; the _like-named_, force, _breaks up_ {{{ 88 }}} into an opposition that at first looks like a self-subsistent difference, though it turns out in fact _to be none;_ for what repels itself from itself is the _like-named_, so that what has been repelled essentially attracts itself, being _the same;_ the difference that was made, since it is no difference, thereby sublates itself once more. With this it exhibits itself as a difference _of the matter itself_, that is, as absolute difference; and that difference of the _matter_ is accordingly nothing but the like-named which has repelled itself away from itself and so posits an opposition that is none.

By this principle the first supersensible, that tranquil realm of laws, the immediate replica of the world we perceive, gets turned around into its opposite; law as such was what _stays equal_ to itself, and so were its differences; now, however, it is posited that both are rather the opposite of themselves; what is _equal_ to itself repels itself away from itself, while what is unequal to itself is what posits itself as equal to itself. Only with this determination, in fact, is the difference the _inner_ one, difference _in its very self_, since the equal is unequal to itself and the unequal equal to itself. — _This second supersensible world_ is in this way the _inverted_ world, and since one of its sides is already there in the first supersensible world, it is the _inverted_ counterpart of that _first_ one. With this the inner stands completed as appearance. {{ 97 }} For that first supersensible world amounted to nothing more than the _immediate_ raising {{{ 89 }}} of the world we perceive into the universal element; its necessary counter-image lay in that perceived world, which as yet kept _for itself the principle of alternation_ and _of change_; the first realm of laws went without this, but receives it as the inverted world.

By the law of this inverted world, then, the _like-named_ of the first world is what is _unequal_ to itself, and the _unlike_ of that world is just as much _unequal to itself_, or comes to be _equal_ to itself. At determinate moments this turns out as follows: whatever is sweet under the law of the first is sour in this inverted in-itself, and whatever is black in the one is white in the other. Whatever counts as the north pole of a magnet by the law of the former is, within its other supersensible in-itself (in the earth, that is), the south pole; and whatever is the south pole there is the north pole here. So too whatever counts as the oxygen pole in the first law of electricity turns into the hydrogen pole within its other supersensible essence; and conversely, whatever is the hydrogen pole there turns here into the oxygen pole. Within another sphere, revenge upon one's enemy counts, by the _immediate law_, as the supreme satisfaction of an individuality that has been injured. But _this law_, the law that toward anyone who does not treat me as a self-essence I show myself as essence and rather sublate him as essence, is _inverted_ by the other world's principle _into its opposite_: the restoring of myself as the essence by sublating the alien essence {{{ 90 }}} becomes self-destruction. Now once this inversion, the one exhibited in the _punishment_ of crime, has been turned into a _law_, it too is again merely the law of the one world, which has an _inverted_ supersensible world _standing over against_ it, one where whatever is held in contempt in the former comes to honor, and whatever stands in honor there falls into contempt. The punishment that by the _law of the first world_ disgraces and destroys a human being changes, within its _inverted world_, into a pardon that preserves his essence and brings him into honor.

Seen superficially, this inverted world is opposed to the first in such a way that it has the first outside of itself and thrusts it away as an _actuality_ that is inverted, with the _one_ being _appearance_ and the _other_ the _in-itself_, the _one_ being what it is _for an other_, and the _other_, by contrast, what it is _for itself;_ so that, taking up the earlier examples again, what tastes sweet would _properly_, or _inwardly_ in the thing, be sour, and what on the actual magnet of appearance is the north pole would, _in the inner or essential being_, be the south pole, while what presents itself as the oxygen pole on electricity as it appears would be the hydrogen pole on electricity as it does not appear. Or an action that is a crime in _appearance_ ought to be able to be really good _in the inner_ (a bad action could have a good intention), while punishment would be punishment only _in appearance_ and would, _in itself_, or in some {{{ 91 }}} other world, be a boon to the criminal. {{ 98 }} But oppositions of this kind, between inner and outer, between appearance and the supersensible, as between two sorts of actuality, are no longer to be found here. The repelled differences do not distribute themselves afresh over two such substances as would carry them and grant them a subsistence apart, whereby the understanding would drop back out of the inner into its earlier position. The one side, or substance, would once more be the world of perception, where one of the two laws would carry on its essential business, and facing it an inner world, _just such a sensuous world_ as the first, only in _representation;_ it could not be pointed to as a sensuous world, nor seen, nor heard, nor tasted, and would nonetheless be represented as just such a sensuous world. But in fact, if _the one that is posited_ is something perceived, and its _in-itself_, as the inversion of it, is likewise something _sensuously represented_, then the sour that the sweet thing would be in itself is a thing just as actual as the sweet thing, a _sour thing;_ the black that the white would be in itself is actual black; the north pole that the south pole is in itself is the north pole _present on that very magnet_; and the oxygen pole that the hydrogen pole is in itself is the _present_ oxygen pole of the same pile. _Actual_ crime, however, has _its inversion_, has _its in-itself_ as _possibility_, in _intention_ as such, {{{ 92 }}} though not in a good one; for an intention has its truth only in the deed itself. Crime according to its content, though, has its reflection into itself, its inversion, in _actual_ punishment; and punishment reconciles the law with the actuality that stands opposed to the law in crime. _Actual_ punishment, finally, carries its _inverted_ actuality within it in this sense, that it is an actualizing of the law by which the activity the law has as punishment _sublates itself_, so that from an active law it turns back into a _tranquil_ and valid law, and the movement of individuality against the law, and of the law against individuality, has died away.

From the representation of inversion, then, which makes up the essence of the one side of the supersensible world, one must clear away the sensuous representation that fixes the differences in a distinct element of subsistence, and must exhibit and apprehend purely this absolute concept of difference as inner difference, as the repelling of the like-named as like-named from itself, and as the equality of the unequal as unequal. It is pure alternation, or _the opposition within itself, the contradiction, that has to be thought_. For in a difference that is inner, what is opposed is not merely _one of two_; — otherwise it would be a _being_ and not something opposed; — rather, it is what is opposed to something that is itself opposed, {{{ 93 }}} or the other is itself immediately there within it. True enough, I place the opposite _here_ and _over there_ that other thing whose opposite it is, putting the _opposite_ thus on one side, in and for itself, apart from the other. {{ 99 }} But precisely on that account, since what I have here is _the opposite in and for itself_, this opposite is the opposite of its own self, or in fact it holds the other immediately within itself. — The supersensible world, being the inverted one, has thereby reached across the other world and has it within its own self; for itself it is what is inverted, that is, the inversion of its own self; it is at once itself and what is opposed to it in a single unity. Only in this fashion is it the difference that is _inner_, or difference _in its very self_, or is it as _infinity_.

Through infinity we see the law brought to completion as necessity within its own self, and all the moments of appearance taken up into the inner. To say that what is simple in the law is infinity means, in light of what has emerged, _α_) that it is something _self-equal_ which is nevertheless _difference_ in itself; or that it is a like-named which repels itself away from itself, which splits itself in two. What went by the name of _simple_ force _doubles_ itself, and it is by virtue of its infinity that it is the law. _β_) What has been split in two, making up the parts represented in the _law_, presents itself as something that subsists; and taken apart from the concept of inner difference, these parts are space and {{{ 94 }}} time, or else distance and velocity, which come on the scene as moments of gravity, indifferent toward one another and toward gravity itself and without necessity for either, just as simple gravity is indifferent toward them, or simple electricity toward the positive and the negative. _γ_) Through the concept of inner difference, however, this unequal and indifferent stuff, space and time and so forth, is a _difference_ that is no _difference_, only a difference of the _like-named_, and unity is its essence; as positive and negative they stand inspirited toward one another, and their being consists rather in this, that they posit themselves as non-being and sublate themselves in the unity. The two distinguished terms both subsist, they are _in themselves_, they are _in themselves as opposites_, that is, the opposite of themselves, they have their other within them and are but one unity.

This simple infinity, the absolute concept, may be named life's simple essence, the world's soul, the universal blood, which, omnipresent, is neither dimmed nor broken by any difference, which is rather itself all the differences together with their being sublated, and which therefore pulses within itself without stirring, trembles within itself without being disquieted. It is _self-equal_, for its differences are tautological, differences that are none. {{ 100 }} This self-equal essence therefore relates solely to itself; _to itself_: then what the relation goes out toward is another, and the _relating {{{ 95 }}} to itself_ is rather _the splitting-in-two_, or that very self-equality is inner difference; those thus _split in two_ are accordingly _in and for their own selves_, each one an opposite — of _another_: then the _other_ is already uttered in it along with it; or, again, it is the opposite not _of an other_ but only _the pure opposite_: then it is within its own self the opposite of itself; or it is no opposite whatever, but is purely for itself, a pure essence equal to itself with no difference in it: then we have no need to ask, still less to take the torment of such a question for philosophy's business, or even to hold it unanswerable for philosophy — _how_ difference or otherness arises out of this pure essence, how it comes _out_ of it; for the splitting-in-two has already happened, difference has been excluded from what is self-equal and placed at its side; and so what was meant to be _the self-equal_ is already one of the terms split in two rather than the absolute essence. To say that _the self-equal splits itself in two_ therefore means just as much that it sublates itself as something already split in two, that it sublates itself as otherness. The _unity_ of which it is customarily said that difference can never emerge from it is in truth itself only the one moment of the splitting-in-two; it is the abstraction of simplicity, an abstraction that stands over against difference. But in being the abstraction, {{{ 96 }}} in being only one of the opposites, it has thereby already been said of it that it is the splitting-in-two; for if unity is something _negative_, something _opposed_, then it is posited precisely as that which has opposition within it. The differences between _splitting-in-two_ and _becoming self-equal_ are for that reason equally nothing but _this movement of self-sublating;_ for since what is self-equal, which is first supposed to split itself in two or turn into its opposite, is an abstraction, is _already itself_ something split in two, its splitting-in-two is a sublating of what it is, and hence the sublating of its being-split-in-two. _Becoming self-equal_ is equally a splitting-in-two; whatever becomes _self-equal_ thereby steps over against the splitting-in-two, that is, it thereby places itself _to one side_, or rather it _becomes_ something _split in two_.

Infinity, this absolute unrest belonging to pure self-movement, whereby whatever is determined in any manner whatever, for example as being, is rather the opposite of that determinateness, has admittedly been the soul of everything up to now, but only in the _inner_ has it stepped forth freely on its own. Appearance, or the play of forces, exhibits infinity already, yet infinity first steps forth freely as _explaining_; and when at last it is an object for consciousness _as what it is_, then consciousness is _self-consciousness_. {{ 101 }} The understanding's _explaining_ at first does no more than describe {{{ 97 }}} what self-consciousness is. It sublates those differences that the law contains, differences already made pure yet still indifferent, and posits them within one unity, force. But this becoming-equal is just as immediately a splitting-in-two, for the only way the differences get sublated and the One of force posited is that the understanding makes a new difference, between law and force, which at the same time is none at all; and toward this, that the difference is likewise no difference, the understanding goes on itself to sublate this difference again, letting force be constituted exactly as the law is. — This movement, this necessity, is still, so far, the understanding's own necessity and movement, or _as such_ it is _not the understanding's object_; what the understanding has for objects within it are positive and negative electricity, distance, velocity, attractive force, and a thousand other things, which make up what the moments of the movement contain. Explaining carries so much self-satisfaction precisely because consciousness in it, if one may put it so, stands in immediate conversation with itself and enjoys only itself, seeming all the while to be busy with something else, whereas in fact it is only busying itself with itself.

Within the opposed law, taken as the inversion of the first, that is, within inner difference, infinity does indeed become an _object_ for the understanding, but the understanding misses infinity as such once again, {{{ 98 }}} in that it parcels out difference in itself, the self-repelling of the like-named and the unequals that attract each other, once more between two worlds, between two substantial elements; the _movement_ as experience has it counts for the understanding here as an occurrence, while the like-named and the unequal count as _predicates_ whose essence is a substrate that has being. What is an object for it under a sensuous covering is an object for us in its essential shape, as pure concept. This apprehending of difference such as it _in truth_ is, this apprehending of _infinity_ as such, occurs _for us_, or _in itself_. Setting out the concept of infinity is the business of science; consciousness, however, holding that concept as it does _immediately_, comes forward once again as a form of its own, as a new shape of consciousness which fails to recognize its essence in what went before and takes that essence for something wholly different. — Since this concept of infinity is its object, consciousness is accordingly consciousness of a difference that is just as _immediately_ sublated; it is _for its own self_, it is the _distinguishing of the undistinguished_, or _self-consciousness_. I _draw a distinction between myself and myself_, and _in doing so it is immediately there for me that what has been distinguished is not distinguished_. I, as the like-named, repel myself away from myself; but this distinguished term, posited as unequal, is immediately, in the very act of being distinguished, no difference so far as I am concerned. Consciousness of something other, of an object {{{ 99 }}} at all, is of course itself necessarily _self-consciousness_, reflectedness into itself, consciousness of its own self in its otherness. {{ 102 }} The _necessary advance_ beyond the shapes of consciousness dealt with so far, shapes for which what was true was a thing, something other than themselves, expresses precisely this: that consciousness of the thing is possible only for a self-consciousness, and moreover that self-consciousness is the sole truth of those shapes. But this truth is at hand for us alone, not yet for consciousness. Self-consciousness, though, has so far come to be only _for itself_, not yet _as unity_ with consciousness as such.

We see that within the inner of appearance the understanding in truth experiences nothing else than appearance itself, though not appearance as it is in the play of forces, but appearance in its absolutely universal moments and in their movement, and that in fact it experiences nothing but _itself_. Having risen above perception, consciousness presents itself as closed together with the supersensible by way of the middle term of appearance, and through that middle it gazes into this background. The two extremes, the one being the pure inner, the other the inner that gazes into this pure inner, have now coincided, and just as they have vanished as extremes, so too has the middle vanished as anything other than they. The curtain, then, has been pulled away from before the inner, and what is at hand is the inner's gazing into the inner: the gazing of the _undistinguished_ like-named, which {{{ 100 }}} repels itself, posits itself as an inner that is _distinguished_, but _for which_ the _undistinguishedness_ of the two is there just as immediately, _self-consciousness_. It shows itself that behind the so-called curtain, which is supposed to cover over the inner, there is nothing to be seen unless _we_ ourselves go behind it, as much in order that seeing may occur as that there may be something behind it which can be seen. Yet it emerges at the same time that one cannot go straight behind it without ceremony; for this knowledge of what _the representation_ of appearance and of its inner has as its truth is itself only the result of a movement full of circumstance, one whereby the modes consciousness takes, namely meaning, perceiving, and understanding, disappear; and it will emerge likewise that cognizing _what consciousness knows in the act of knowing itself_ calls for still further circumstances, the unfolding of which is what follows.
