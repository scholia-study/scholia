## {{{ 243 }}} c. Observation of the Relation of Self-Consciousness to its Immediate Actuality; Physiognomy and Phrenology

Observation in psychology discovers no law for the relationship in which self-consciousness stands to actuality, that is, to the world set over against it, and, since the two are indifferent toward each other, it is driven back to the _peculiar determinateness_ that belongs to real individuality, an individuality that is _in_ and _for itself_, one that holds the opposition of being-_for-itself_ to being-_in-itself_ extinguished within their absolute mediation. It is the object that has now become the object of observation, or to which observation passes over.

The individual is in and for its own self: it is _for itself_, or it is a free doing; but it is also _in itself;_ or it itself has a determinate _being_ that is _original_ — a determinateness which, by its concept, is the same as what psychology wanted to find outside it. {{ 172 }} _At the individual itself_, then, the opposition emerges: to be this doubled thing, consciousness in its movement {{{ 244 }}} and the fixed being of an actuality that appears, an actuality immediately _its own_ at the individual. This _being_, namely the _body_ belonging to the determinate individuality, is the _originality_ of that individuality, its not-having-done-anything. Yet inasmuch as the individual is at the same time only what it has done, its body is also the expression of itself that it has _brought forth_; it is at once a _sign_, one that has not stayed an immediate matter but through which the individual only indicates what it _is_, in the sense that it puts its original nature into effect.

Looking at the moments present here in their relation to the view that preceded, we find here a human shape that is universal, or at any rate the universal shape of a climate, a region of the world, a people, in the way that earlier there were the same universal customs and culture. Added to this are the particular circumstances and the particular situation within universal actuality; here, this particular actuality is the particular formation of the individual's shape. — Seen from the other side, just as earlier the free doing of the individual and the actuality that counted as _its own_ were set over against the actuality already at hand, so here the shape stands as the expression of _its_ actualization posited through the individual itself, the features and forms of its self-active essence. But the actuality, both universal and particular, which observation earlier came upon outside the individual, is here the individual's own actuality, its innate body, and it is precisely into this body {{{ 245 }}} that the expression pertaining to its doing falls. In the psychological consideration, the actuality that has being in and for itself and the determinate individuality were supposed to be related to each other; here, by contrast, it is the _whole_ determinate _individuality_ that observation has for its object; and each side of the opposition within it is itself this whole. What belongs to the outer whole, then, is not only the _original being_, the innate body, but equally the formation of that body, a formation that is the work of the inner's activity; the body is a unity of uncultivated and cultivated being, and it is the individual's actuality permeated by being-for-itself. This whole, which gathers into itself the determinate, original, fixed parts along with the features that come about solely through doing, _is_; and this _being_ constitutes the _expression_ of the inner, that is, of the individual which has been posited as both consciousness and movement. — This _inner_, equally, is no longer the formal, contentless or indeterminate self-activity whose content and determinateness would lie, as they did earlier, in outer circumstances; it is instead an original character already determinate in itself, and activity is merely its form. It is the relationship between these two sides, then, that comes under consideration here: how it is to be determined, and what this _expression_ of the inner within the outer is supposed to mean.

{{ 173 }} In the first place, this outer does not make the inner visible in the manner of an _organ_, nor make it a being for another at all; for so far as the inner lies within the organ, {{{ 246 }}} it is the _activity_ itself. Mouth in speech, hand at work, and, if one likes, the legs as well, are the organs that actualize and bring to completion; in them the doing is present _as doing_, or the inner taken as such; but the externality which the inner gains through them is the deed, an actuality cut off from the individual. Speech and labor count as expressions in which the individual, instead of holding on to itself and retaining itself within itself, lets the inner come entirely outside itself and hands it over to what is other. One may therefore say with equal justice that these expressions say too much of the inner and that they say too little of it; _too much_, — since the inner itself breaks out in them, no opposition is left standing between them and it, and what they give is not only an _expression_ of the inner but that inner itself, immediately; _too little_, — since in speech and in action the inner turns itself into something other, it thereby delivers itself over to the element of transformation, which inverts the word once spoken and the deed once accomplished and turns them into something different from what, in and for themselves, they are as actions of this determinate individual. Not only do the works of actions, through this externality, lose the character of being something enduring over against other individualities, on account of the influence of others; but also, since they relate to the inner they contain as a separated, indifferent outer, {{{ 247 }}} they can, as inner, be something other _through the individual_ itself than they appear to be, — either because the individual deliberately makes them, for appearance's sake, into something other than they are in truth, — or because it is too inept to give itself the outer side it actually wanted and to fix that side so firmly that its work cannot be inverted by others. Doing, then, as a completed work, carries a doubled and opposed significance: it is either the _inner_ individuality and _not_ that individuality's _expression_, or else, as something outer, an actuality _free_ of the inner, an actuality wholly other than the inner is. — Because of this ambiguity we have to look around for the inner in the way it _still_ is, yet visibly or outwardly, _at the individual itself_. In the organ, however, it is only as the immediate _doing_ itself, which attains its externality in the deed, and the deed either represents the inner or does not. The organ, considered in terms of this opposition, therefore does not afford the expression that is being sought.

{{ 174 }} Suppose now that the outer shape were able to give expression to inner individuality only in so far as it is not organ and not _doing_, and so is a whole at _rest_: then it would relate as a subsisting thing that quietly received the inner into its passive existence as something alien and so became that inner's _sign_; — an outer, contingent expression whose _actual_ side is on its own account without significance, {{{ 248 }}} — a language whose tones and combinations of tones are not the matter at hand itself but are attached to it by free arbitrary choice and are contingent for it.

A connection as arbitrary as this, between terms that are an outer for one another, gives no law. Physiognomy, however, is meant to set itself apart from other bad arts and hopeless studies in this: that it takes the determinate individuality within the _necessary_ opposition between an inner and an outer, between character as a conscious _essence_ and that same character as shape in its being, and that it puts these moments into the relation to one another in which their concept places them, so that they must accordingly make up the content of a law. Astrology, chiromancy, and sciences of that sort, by contrast, seem to relate only an outer to an outer, something or other to what is alien to it. _This_ constellation at birth, and, if this outer is brought in closer to the body itself, _these_ lines of the hand, are _outer_ moments bearing on whether life is long or short, and on the destiny of the singular human being in general. As externalities they relate indifferently to one another and lack the necessity for each other that is supposed to reside in the relation between an _outer_ and an _inner_.

The hand, to be sure, seems not so much to be something outer for destiny as rather {{{ 249 }}} to relate to destiny as an inner. For destiny, in its turn, is nothing but the appearance of that which the determinate individuality is _in itself_ as inner original determinateness. — Now, in order to know what that individuality is in itself, the chiromancer, like the physiognomist, arrives at it by a shorter route than, for example, Solon, who held that this could be known only from and after the course of a whole life; he considered the appearance, whereas they consider the _in-itself_. That the hand must present the _in-itself_ of individuality with regard to its destiny can readily be seen from this, that after the organ of speech it is above all the hand through which the human being brings himself into appearance and actualization. It is the ensouled master builder of his fortune; the hand, one may say, _is_ what the human being _does_, for in the hand, as the active organ by which he brings himself to completion, he is present as the one giving soul, and since he is originally his own destiny, the hand will express this in-itself.

From this determination, that in the _organ_ of activity a _being_ is found _just as much_ as the _doing_ is within it, or that the inner being-_in-itself_ is itself _present_ at the organ and has a _being for_ others, there follows a view of the organ different from the previous one. {{ 175 }} For if the organs in general proved incapable of counting as _expressions_ of the inner, on the ground that in them the doing is present _as doing_, {{{ 250 }}} while the doing _as deed_ is merely something outer, so that inner and outer come apart in this manner and are, or may be, foreign to one another, then, on the determination just examined, the organ must once again be taken as the _middle_ of the two, since the very fact that the doing is _present_ at the organ at the same time makes up an _externality_ of that doing, and indeed an externality other than the deed is, for the former stays with the individual and at it. — This middle, this unity of inner and outer, is at first itself something outer as well; but then this externality is simultaneously drawn into the inner; being a _simple_ externality, it confronts the dispersed one, which is either only a _singular_ work or state, contingent as regards the individuality taken as a whole, or else, as _whole_ externality, is destiny shattered into a multiplicity of works and states. The _hand's simple lines_, then, and likewise the _sound_ and _range_ of the _voice_, taken as the individual determinateness of _speech_, — and speech once more as it acquires through the hand a more fixed existence than it does through the voice, namely _writing_, and writing in its particularity as _handwriting_ — all of this is _expression_ of the inner, so that, as the _simple externality_, it in turn relates over against the _manifold externality_ of acting and of destiny, relating to it as an _inner_. — So if the determinate nature and inborn peculiarity {{{ 251 }}} of the individual, along with whatever these have turned into through culture, are taken first as the _inner_, that is, as the essence of acting and of destiny, then this inner has its _appearance_ and externality _first_ in mouth, hand, voice, handwriting, as in the remaining organs too and their abiding determinatenesses; and only _then_ does it express itself _further_ outward, in its actuality in the world.

Now since this middle determines itself as that expression which is at once withdrawn into the inner again, its existence is not confined to the immediate organ of doing; it is rather the movement and form of the face and of bodily shaping in general, a movement that accomplishes nothing. These features and their movement are, according to this concept, the doing that is held back and remains at the individual, and, in accordance with the individual's relation to actual doing, they are the individual's own supervision and observation of that doing, _expression_ as _reflection upon_ the actual expression. — The individual is not mute toward its outer doing or in the midst of it, and this is because it is therein at the same time reflected into its own self, and it expresses this being-reflected-into-itself; this theoretical doing, or the individual's speech with itself about that doing, can be heard by others as well, for it is itself an expression.

{{ 176 }} In this inner, then, which remains inner within its expression, what gets observed is the _being_-reflected of the {{{ 252 }}} individual out of its actuality, and the question is how things stand with the necessity posited in this unity. — This being-reflected is at first different from the deed itself, and so can be, and can be taken to be, something _other_ than the deed is; one can tell from a person's face whether he is _serious_ about what he says or does. — Conversely, however, what is meant to serve as expression of the inner is at once an expression that _has being_, and with that it sinks itself into the determination of _being_, which for the self-conscious essence is utterly contingent. It is thus indeed an expression, yet at the same time only like a _sign_, so that the makeup of that by which the content is expressed is entirely a matter of indifference to the content expressed. Within this appearance the inner is indeed something invisible made _visible_, but without being tied to that appearance; it can just as well be in another appearance as another inner can be in this same one. — Lichtenberg therefore rightly says: _Suppose the physiognomist were once to catch hold of a human being; then all it would take would be a stout resolution to make oneself incomprehensible again for thousands of years_. — Just as, in the relationship that preceded, the circumstances lying to hand were something with being, out of which individuality took what _it_ had the power and the will to take, either surrendering to them or inverting them, for which reason they did not contain the necessity and the essence of {{{ 253 }}} individuality, — so here too the appearing immediate being of individuality is of such a kind that it either expresses individuality's being-reflected out of actuality and its being-within-itself, or else is for individuality merely a sign, one indifferent toward what is signified and hence in truth signifying nothing; it is for individuality its face and equally its mask, one that individuality can put off. — Individuality pervades its shape, moves within it and speaks in it; yet this entire existence likewise passes over into being a being indifferent toward the will and the action; individuality wipes out in it the significance it had before, that of holding individuality's being-reflected into itself or its true essence, and places that significance instead in the will and in the deed.

Individuality _gives up_ that _being-reflected into itself_ which is expressed in the _features_, and _places its essence_ in _the work_. In this it contradicts the relationship laid down by the instinct of reason, which devotes itself to observing self-conscious individuality, with respect to what is supposed to be individuality's _inner_ and _outer_. From this vantage point we arrive at the thought properly underlying the physiognomic — if one cares to call it that — _science_. {{ 177 }} The opposition into which this observing has fallen is, as regards its form, the opposition of the practical and the theoretical, both of them posited within the practical itself, — the opposition between individuality actualizing itself {{{ 254 }}} in action, taken in the most general sense, — and the same individuality as it is, in this very action, at the same time out of the action, reflected into itself, with the action as its object. Observing picks this opposition up in the same inverted relationship in which the opposition determines itself in appearance. For the _inessential outer_ it takes the _deed_ itself and the work, whether of speech or of a more firmly established actuality, — but for the _essential inner_, the _being-within-itself_ of individuality. Between the two sides that practical consciousness has at it, intending and the deed, — the _meaning_ it has about its action and the _action_ itself, — observation picks the former side as the true inner; and this inner, it holds, has an expression at the deed that is more or less _inessential_, but its true expression at its shape. The expression last named is the immediate sensuous presence of individual spirit; the inwardness that is claimed to be the true one is the ownness of the intention together with the singularity of being-for-itself; the two of them are spirit as _meant_. What observing has for its objects, then, is _meant_ existence, and it is among such objects that it searches out laws.

Immediate meaning concerning the meant presence of spirit is natural physiognomy: the rash judgment passed, at the first sight of a shape, upon the inner nature and the character that the shape has. This opinion has an object of such a {{{ 255 }}} kind that it lies in the object's essence to be in truth something other than merely sensuous immediate being. Admittedly what is present, and what is the object of observing, is indeed just this being-reflected-into-itself within the sensuous and out of it, visibility as the visibility of the invisible. Yet this sensuous immediate presence is the _actuality_ of spirit only as spirit is for opinion; and on this side observing knocks about with its meant existence, with physiognomy, handwriting, the tone of the voice, and so on. — It relates such existence to just such a _meant inner_. What is to be cognized is not the murderer or the thief but the _capacity for being one;_ with that, fixed abstract determinateness dissolves into the concrete infinite determinateness belonging to the _singular_ individual, which now calls for more artful depictions than those qualifications are. Such artful depictions do say more than qualification as murderers, thieves, or as good-hearted, unspoiled, and the like, but for their purpose of articulating meant being, or the singular individuality, they are by far not enough; just as little are the depictions of the shape that go beyond the flat forehead, the long nose, and the like. For the singular shape, like the singular self-consciousness, is inexpressible as meant being. {{ 178 }} The science of acquaintance with human beings, which goes after the human being as merely supposed, and likewise physiognomy, {{{ 256 }}} which goes after his supposed actuality and would elevate the unconscious judging of natural physiognomy into a knowledge, is therefore something without end or ground, something that can never get so far as to say what it means, since it only means, and its content is only something meant.

The _laws_ that this science goes out in search of are relations holding between these two meant sides, and so can themselves be nothing but an empty meaning. Moreover, since this supposed knowledge, which busies itself with the actuality of spirit, has precisely this for its object, namely that spirit reflects itself out of its sensuous existence into itself, and that determinate existence is for spirit an indifferent contingency, then it must know immediately, in the case of the laws it has discovered, that nothing is said by them, but that what is really going on is pure chatter, or that merely _an opinion of one's own_ is being given; an expression that has the truth of stating this as one and the same, — of saying one's _opinion_ and thereby bringing forward not the matter at hand but only an opinion _of one's own_. As far as their _content_ goes, however, these observations cannot differ from ones of this sort: "It always rains when we have the annual fair, says the shopkeeper; and always too when I dry the laundry, says the housewife."

Lichtenberg, who characterizes physiognomic observing in this way, also says the following: "if someone were to say: your conduct is indeed that of an honest {{{ 257 }}} man, but your figure shows me that you force yourself and are a rogue at heart; truly, to the end of the world such an address will be answered by every decent fellow with a box on the ear." — The reply is _telling_ for this reason, that it overturns what such a science of meaning presupposes first of all, namely that _the actuality_ of the human being is his face and so on. — The _true being_ of the human being is rather _his deed;_ in the deed individuality is _actual_, and the deed is what sublates the _meant_ in both of its sides. First, the meant as a bodily being at rest; in action, rather, individuality presents itself as the _negative_ essence, which _is_ only in so far as it sublates being. Then the deed likewise sublates the inexpressibility of opinion in respect of the self-conscious individuality, which in opinion is something infinitely determinate and determinable. In the accomplished deed this bad infinity is annihilated. The deed is something simply determinate, universal, something to be grasped in a single abstraction; it is murder, theft, or a benefaction, a brave deed, and so forth, and of it there can be _said_ what _it is_. The deed _is_ this, and this being of it is not a mere sign but the matter at hand itself. It _is_ this, and what the deed _is_, that the individual human being _is;_ in the simplicity belonging to _this being_ he is a universal essence with being for others, and he ceases to be merely something meant. {{{ 258 }}} {{ 179 }} He is indeed not thereby posited as spirit; but since it is a question of his _being_ as being, and since _on the one hand_ the doubled being, that of _shape_ and that of _deed_, stands opposed to itself, and both the one and the other are supposed to be his actuality, it is rather the deed alone that is to be asserted as his _genuine being_, — not his figure, which was supposed to express what he means with respect to his deeds, or what one supposed he might merely be able to do. Likewise, when _on the other hand_ his _work_ is set over against his inner _possibility_, capacity, or intention, it is the work by itself that counts as his true actuality, even should he deceive himself on the point and, turned inward out of his action, take himself in this inner to be something other than he is in the _deed_. Individuality that entrusts itself to the objective element, in becoming a work, does indeed thereby expose itself to being altered and inverted. But it is precisely this that constitutes the character of the deed, whether it is an actual being that holds up, or whether it is only a meant work that, null within itself, passes away. Objectivity does not alter the deed itself; it only shows _what_ the deed is, that is, whether it _is_ at all, or whether it _is nothing_. — The dissection of this being into intentions and refinements of that sort, by which the _actual_ human being, that is, his deed, is to be explained back into a merely meant being, just as he himself may well fabricate particular intentions about his own actuality, must {{{ 259 }}} be left to the idleness of opinion, which, if it wants to set its deedless wisdom to work, to deny the character of reason in the agent, and in this way to maltreat him by declaring, instead of the deed, rather the figure and the features to be his being, has to expect the above reply, which proves to it that the figure is not the _in-itself_ but can rather be an object of handling.

If we now survey the whole compass of relations in which self-conscious individuality admits of being observed as standing toward its outer, one of them will be left over that observation has still to make its object. In psychology it is the _outer actuality_ of _things_, which is to have at spirit its own self-conscious _counter-image_ and to render spirit comprehensible. In physiognomy, by contrast, it is in its _own_ outer, in a being which is _speech_ — the visible invisibility of its essence — that spirit is to be cognized. There still remains the determination of the side of actuality, that individuality articulates its essence in its immediate, fixed, purely existent actuality. — This last relation is therefore distinguished from the physiognomic in that the latter is the _speaking_ presence of the individual, which in its _acting_ expression at the same time presents itself as _reflecting_ into itself and as _contemplating_ itself, an expression which {{{ 260 }}} is itself movement, features at rest which are themselves in essence a mediated being. {{ 180 }} In the determination still to be considered, however, the outer is at last an actuality wholly at _rest_, one that is no sign speaking at its own self; separated from self-conscious movement, it presents itself for itself and is as a mere thing.

What first becomes clear about how the inner is related to this outer of its own is that this seems to have to be comprehended as the relationship of _causal connection_, since the relation of something with being in itself to something else with being in itself, taken as a _necessary_ relation, just is that relationship.

If spiritual individuality is to have an effect upon the body, then as cause it must itself be bodily. But the bodily in which it is present as cause is the organ, not, however, the organ of a doing directed at outer actuality, but of the self-conscious essence's doing within itself, turned outward only toward its own body; which organs these might be is not at once evident. Were one to think merely of organs in general, the organ of labor in general would lie ready to hand, and likewise the organ of the sexual drive, and so forth. Yet organs of that sort must be viewed as tools, or as parts, which spirit, being the one extreme, has as its middle over against the other extreme, an outer _object_. {{{ 261 }}} Here, however, what is understood is an organ in which the self-conscious individual, being an extreme, holds itself _for itself_ over against its own actuality opposed to it, an organ not turned outward at the same time but reflected in its action, and one at which the side of _being_ does not amount to a _being for another_. In the physiognomic relation the organ is indeed also considered as an existence that is reflected into itself and that speaks about the doing; but this being is an objective one, and physiognomic observation yields the result that self-consciousness confronts just this actuality of its own as something indifferent. This indifference vanishes once this being-reflected-into-itself is itself _efficacious_; through that, the existence in question acquires a necessary relation to it; but if it is to be efficacious upon existence, it must itself have a being that is nevertheless not properly objective, and it is as this organ that it is to be exhibited.

In ordinary life anger, for example, as such an inner doing, is located in the liver; Plato even grants the liver something still higher, which according to some is even the highest of all, namely prophecy, or the gift of articulating the holy and the eternal in a non-rational way. But the movement that the individual has in liver, heart, and so forth may not count as that individual's movement wholly reflected {{{ 262 }}} into itself; in these organs it is rather such that it has already struck inward into the body and has an animal existence turning outward against externality.

The _nervous system_, by contrast, is the immediate repose of the organic in its movement. {{ 181 }} The _nerves_ themselves are, to be sure, once again the organs of a consciousness already sunk into its outward direction; but brain and spinal cord may be taken as the abiding-within-itself — the non-objective, and likewise non-outgoing — immediate presence of self-consciousness. So far as the moment of being which belongs to this organ is a _being for another_, an existence, it is dead being, no longer the presence of self-consciousness. This _being within its own self_, however, is by its concept a fluidity in which whatever circles are cast into it dissolve immediately, and in which no difference expresses itself as one that _has being_. Meanwhile, just as spirit itself is not something abstract and simple but rather a system of movements within which it differentiates itself into moments while staying free in this very differentiating, and just as it articulates its body at large for various functions and assigns a singular part of it to one function alone, so too the fluid _being_ of its _being-within-itself_ can be represented as something articulated; and it seems that it has to be so represented, because the _being_ of spirit reflected into itself in the brain {{{ 263 }}} is once more only a middle between spirit's pure essence and the articulation of its body, a middle that must accordingly, taking after the nature of both and so from the side of the latter, in turn have at it the articulation that _has being_.

Spiritually organic being has, along with this, the necessary side of an existence that is _at rest and subsisting_; the former must withdraw as the extreme of being-for-itself and have the latter facing it as the other extreme, which is then the object upon which the former acts as cause. If, then, brain and spinal cord constitute that bodily _being-for-itself_ of spirit, skull and spinal column come in as the other extreme, the one set apart, namely the fixed thing at rest. — But since it is not the back but only the head that occurs to anyone who thinks of the proper place where spirit exists, we may, in examining a knowledge such as the one before us, content ourselves with this ground — not too poor a one for such a knowledge — in order to restrict this existence to the skull. Should the back occur to someone, on the ground that knowledge and doing are at times driven partly _in_ and partly _out_ through it, this would still prove nothing for taking the spinal cord as belonging to the indwelling place of spirit, and its column as the counter-image existence, since it would prove too much; for one may equally recall that {{{ 264 }}} other outer paths of approaching the activity of spirit, so as to rouse it or hold it back, are likewise resorted to. — The spinal column, then, if one likes, falls away _with justice_; and the claim that the skull by itself does not indeed contain the _organs_ of spirit is _constructed_ quite as well as many another doctrine of natural philosophy. For this was excluded earlier from the concept of this relation, and for that reason the skull was taken as belonging to the side of existence; or, if one may not be reminded of the _concept_ of the matter, then experience surely teaches that, whereas one sees with the eye as an organ, one does _not_ murder, steal, or write poetry with the skull, and so on. {{ 182 }} — For this reason one should also keep clear of the word _organ_ when it comes to that _significance_ of the skull which has still to be discussed. For although one is accustomed to say that for rational human beings it is not the word but the _matter_ that counts, this gives no permission to designate a matter with a word that does not belong to it, for this is at once ineptitude and deception, which merely supposes and pretends that it does not have the right _word_, and conceals from itself that in fact it lacks the matter, that is, the concept; if the concept were present, it would also have its right word. — For now only this much has been determined here: that the brain is the living head and the skull the caput mortuum.

{{{ 265 }}} In this dead being, then, the brain's spiritual movements and determinate modes would have to give themselves a presentation as outer actuality, which is nevertheless still at the individual itself. For their relationship to the skull, which, being dead, has no spirit dwelling inside it, what offers itself first is the outer, mechanical relationship fixed above, so that the organs properly so called — and they are at the brain — press it out round in one place, beat it broad or push it flat in another, or however else one may care to present this influence. Being itself a part of the organism, the skull must indeed be thought, like every bone, to have a living self-formation, so that, considered in this light, it is rather the skull that presses the brain from its side and sets the brain's outer limit; and being the harder of the two, it has the greater capacity for this. In that case, however, the same relationship would still remain in the determination of the activity of the two toward each other; for whether it is the skull that determines or the skull that is determined alters nothing in the causal connection as such, save that the skull would in that case be made into the immediate organ of self-consciousness, since the side of _being-for-itself_ would then be found in it as _cause_. But since _being-for-itself_, as _organic vitality in both_, falls to each in the same manner, the causal connection between the two in fact falls away. This continued formation of both would, however, hang together inwardly {{{ 266 }}} and would amount to a pre-established harmony of an organic kind, one that leaves the two mutually relating sides free toward each other and leaves each its own _shape_, which need not correspond to the shape of the other; and still more it leaves shape and quality free toward each other, — as the grape's form and the wine's taste are free toward each other. {{ 183 }} — But since the determination of _being-for-itself_ falls on the side of the brain, while the determination of _existence_ falls on the side of the skull, a causal connection between them is _also_ to be posited within the organic unity; a necessary relation of the two as outer for each other, that is, a relation itself outer, through which their _shape_ would be determined by each other.

There is room, however, for talk back and forth in a variety of ways about the determination under which the organ belonging to self-consciousness would be the cause active upon the side that faces it; for what is under discussion is how a cause is constituted when it is regarded in respect of its _indifferent_ existence, its shape and its magnitude, a cause whose interior and being-for-itself is precisely supposed to be such that immediate existence has no bearing on it. To begin with, the skull's organic self-formation is indifferent to mechanical action upon it, and since that formation consists in relating itself to itself, the relationship of these two relationships is precisely this indeterminateness and boundlessness {{{ 267 }}} itself. Suppose next that the brain did receive into itself the differences of spirit as differences that have being, and were a multiplicity of inner organs each taking up a space of its own — which contradicts nature, for nature grants the moments of the concept an existence proper to them and accordingly places the _fluid simplicity_ of organic life _purely on the one side_, while placing that life's _articulation_ and _division_ in its differences on the _other_ side just as much, so that, on the construal intended here, they show themselves as separate anatomical things — even so it would be left undetermined whether a moment of spirit, depending on whether it was originally the stronger or the weaker, would have to possess in the one case a _more expanded_ and in the other a _more contracted_ brain organ, or whether just the reverse holds. — Likewise whether its _development_ enlarged the organ or shrank it, whether it made it clumsier and thicker, or finer. Because the constitution of the cause is left undetermined, it is equally undetermined in what way the action on the skull comes about, whether by widening, or by constricting and letting collapse. Should this action be determined, _more genteelly_ perhaps, as a _stimulating_, it stays undetermined whether it happens after the manner of a cantharides plaster, swelling things up, or after that of vinegar, shrivelling them. — Plausible grounds can be adduced for every view of this kind, for the organic relation, {{{ 268 }}} which intervenes just as much, lets the one pass as readily as the other and is indifferent to all this understanding.

Observing consciousness, however, has no interest in wanting to pin down this relation. For what occupies the one side is at all events not the brain as an _animal_ part, but the brain qua _being_ of _self-conscious_ individuality. — Individuality, as a standing character and as conscious activity in motion, is _for itself_ and _within itself_; confronting this being-for-itself and being-within-itself stands its actuality, its existence for another; and it is being-for-itself and being-within-itself that constitutes the essence, the subject, which possesses in the brain a being _subsumed under_ it, one that acquires its worth solely from the significance dwelling within. {{ 184 }} The other side of self-conscious individuality, however, the side of its existence, is _being_ in the character of something self-subsistent, of a subject, or of a _thing_, namely a bone; _the human being's actuality and existence is his skull-bone_. — Such is the relationship, and such the understanding of it, which the two sides of this relation possess in the consciousness that observes them.

What now concerns this consciousness is the more determinate relation between these sides; broadly speaking, the skull-bone does signify the immediate actuality of spirit. But spirit, being many-sided, endows its existence with a correspondingly manifold significance; what has to be won is the {{{ 269 }}} determinateness of significance belonging to the several spots into which this existence is divided, and one must see how those spots bear in themselves the pointing toward it.

The bone of the skull is neither an organ of activity nor a movement that speaks; nothing is stolen or murdered and so forth with the skull-bone, nor does it so much as twist its expression at such deeds so as to become a speaking gesture. — Nor does this _existent_ carry the value of a _sign_. A facial expression and a gesture, a tone, and likewise a column, or a stake driven into the ground on a desert island, announce themselves at once as intending something other than what they immediately and _merely are_. They give themselves out straightaway as signs, since they bear on themselves a determinateness which points to something else by virtue of not belonging to them as their own. Faced with a skull one may, like Hamlet before Yorick's, let all manner of things come to mind; yet the skull-bone on its own is so indifferent and ingenuous a thing that nothing else is immediately to be seen in it or meant by it than just the bone itself; it may recall the brain and the brain's determinateness, and skulls of a different formation, but it recalls no conscious movement, since it possesses neither facial expression nor gesture, nor anything stamped upon it that would give notice of having come from a conscious deed; for it is that actuality which was to exhibit, on individuality, an {{{ 270 }}} other side of such a kind as to be no longer a being that reflects itself into itself, but purely _immediate being_.

Since, moreover, the bone does not itself feel, a more determinate significance for it might still seem obtainable in roughly this way: that determinate sensations in the neighborhood would make recognizable what is meant by it; and given that some conscious mode of spirit locates its feeling at a determinate spot of the skull, that place might, through its shape, hint at the mode and at what is particular to it. {{ 185 }} For instance, just as many people complain of a painful tension felt somewhere in the head when they think strenuously, or indeed merely when they _think_ at all, so too _stealing_, _murdering_, _writing poetry_, and so on could each be attended by a sensation of its own, one that would additionally have to occupy its own particular spot. That spot of the brain, being in this way more agitated and more active, would probably also develop the adjacent spot of the bone more strongly; or that spot of bone, out of sympathy or consensus, would for its part not be sluggish either, but would enlarge or diminish itself, or form itself in whatever manner. — What renders this hypothesis improbable, however, is that feeling in general is an indeterminate affair, and that the head, being the center, would have as its feeling the universal fellow-feeling with all suffering, so that the thief's, murderer's, or poet's head-tickling {{{ 271 }}} or head-pain would have others mixed in with it, and these could be told apart from one another, and from those that may be called merely bodily, as little as the disease admits of being determined out of the symptom of a headache, supposing we restrict the symptom's significance to the bodily alone.

In fact, no matter which side one considers the matter from, every necessary reciprocal relation drops away, along with any indication of one that would speak for itself. Should the relation be supposed to hold all the same, what remains, and remains as a necessity, is a _conceptless_, free, pre-established harmony between the corresponding determination on either side; for the one side _is to be spiritless actuality, a mere thing_. — Thus on the one side there stand a host of skull-spots at rest, on the other a host of spirit-properties whose multiplicity and determination will hang on whatever state psychology happens to be in. The more paltry the representation of spirit, the more this side of the business is made easy; for the properties grow fewer, and at the same time more segregated, harder and bonier, and by that very token more like determinations of bone and more readily compared with them. Still, though the paltriness of the representation of spirit makes much of it easier, a very great host always remains on either side; and what remains for observation is the utter contingency of their relation. Were each of the children of Israel {{{ 272 }}} to pick out for himself, from the sand by the sea to which they are said to correspond, the grain whose sign he is, the indifference and arbitrariness that gave each his own would be quite as strong as the indifference and arbitrariness that assigns skull-sites and bone-shapes to each soul-faculty, to each passion, and, what would have to be brought in here no less, to those shadings of character which a more refined psychology and acquaintance with human beings likes to talk about. — The murderer's skull has this — not organ, and not sign either, but this bump; yet this murderer has a whole crowd of other properties besides, as well as other bumps, and along with the bumps hollows too; one has one's pick among bumps and hollows. And conversely his murderous bent may be referred to any bump or hollow you please, and these in turn to any property you please; for the murderer is not just this abstract murderer, any more than he has but one protuberance and one hollow. {{ 186 }} The observations that are set up about this must therefore come out exactly as well as the shopkeeper's and the housewife's rain at the annual fair and on washday. Shopkeeper and housewife might just as well have observed that it always rains whenever this particular neighbor goes by, or whenever roast pork is served. Just as the rain is indifferent to those circumstances, so, for observation, is _this_ determinateness {{{ 273 }}} of spirit indifferent to _this_ determinate being of the skull. For among the two objects that this observing has, the one is a dry _being-for-itself_, a bony property of spirit, while the other is a dry _being-in-itself_; a thing as bony as either of them is completely indifferent to everything else; it is all one to the high bump whether a murderer happens to be its neighbor, and all one to the murderer whether flatness is close by him.

To be sure, the _possibility_ that a bump at some spot or other is bound up with some property, passion, and so forth remains left over, insuperably. One _can_ represent the murderer _to oneself_ as bearing a high bump right here at this spot on the skull, the thief as bearing one over there. On this side skull-science admits of great further extension; for to begin with it seems to restrict itself merely to the joining of a bump to a property _within the same individual_, so that both belong to this one individual. But even natural skull-science — for such a thing must exist just as surely as a natural physiognomy does — passes beyond this limit; it does not merely judge that a cunning man carries a fist-thick bump behind his ears, it also forms the representation that the bumps on the forehead belong not to the unfaithful wife herself but to the other marital individual. — In the same way {{{ 274 }}} one can equally well _represent_ to oneself the man who lodges under one roof with the murderer, or his neighbor as well, and further afield his fellow townsmen and so on, as carrying high bumps at some spot or other of the skull, quite as well as the flying cow that was first fondled by the crab that rode the donkey and then, and so on. — But take the _possibility_ in the sense not of what can be _represented_ but of _inner_ possibility, of the _concept_, and the object turns out to be an actuality that is, and is meant to be, a pure thing devoid of any such significance, one that can therefore carry such significance only in representation.

{{ 187 }} Yet suppose the observer, heedless of how indifferent the two sides are, does set about determining relations, kept in good heart partly by the general ground of reason that the _outer is the expression of the inner_, partly leaning on an analogy drawn from animal skulls — animals whose character may indeed be simpler than that of human beings, though for that very reason it grows correspondingly harder to say what character they have, since it cannot be easy for just anybody's representation to project itself properly into the nature of an animal — then, in vouching for the laws he claims to have discovered, the observer finds an _excellent aid_ in a distinction that must necessarily occur to us here too. — The _being_ of {{{ 275 }}} spirit at least cannot be taken as anything simply immovable and unshiftable. The human being is free; it is conceded that _original_ being consists only in _predispositions_, over which he has much power, or which require favorable circumstances in order to be developed, that is, an _original_ being of spirit is just as much to be pronounced a being that does not exist as being. So if observations were to contradict whatever anyone takes it into his head to vouch for as a law, — were the weather fine at the annual fair or on washday, shopkeeper and housewife could say that _properly_ it _ought_ to be raining, and that the _predisposition_ to rain is _present_ all the same; and skull-observing says the like, — that this individual _properly_ _ought_ to be as the skull declares it to be by the law, and does have an _original predisposition_ which, _however_, was never developed; the quality is not there, yet it _ought to be there_. — _Law_ and _ought_ rest on the observing of rain that actually falls and of a disposition that is actual given this determinateness of the skull; but where the _actuality_ is missing, _empty possibility_ passes for just as much. — This possibility, that is, the non-actuality of the law as set up, and with it the observations that run counter to it, are bound to enter in precisely because an individual's freedom, together with the circumstances that develop him, stands indifferent toward _being_ altogether, {{{ 276 }}} indifferent toward it as original and inner no less than as outer and bony, and because the individual can also be something other than what it inwardly and originally is, and other still than what a bone is.

We thus obtain the possibility that this bump or hollow of the skull designates both something actual and also merely a _predisposition_, indeterminately so, toward something or other, hence that it designates something non-actual; we see a bad excuse fare as it always fares, namely that it stands available for use against the very thing it is supposed to help out. We see the act of meaning brought by the nature of the matter to the point of itself saying, and saying _thoughtlessly_, the _opposite_ of what it holds fast to — of saying that something or other is indicated by this bone, but equally well is _not_.

What hovers before opinion itself in this evasion is the true thought that wipes opinion out, the thought that _being_ as such is in no way the truth of spirit. The predisposition already counts as an _original being_ having no part in spirit's activity, and the bone is, on its side, a being of just that kind. {{ 188 }} What has being apart from spiritual activity is for consciousness a thing, and so far from being consciousness's essence that it is rather the reverse of it, consciousness being _actual_ to itself only through the negation and obliteration of such being. — Seen from {{{ 277 }}} this side, passing a bone off for the _actual existence_ of consciousness has to be counted a total repudiation of reason; and passed off for it the bone is, once it is regarded as spirit's outer, since the outer is precisely actuality in the mode of being. It is no use to say that from this outer one _only_ draws an _inference_ to the inner, which is _something else_, and that the outer, far from being the inner itself, is merely the inner's _expression_. For within the relationship of the two to each other, what falls to the side of the inner is precisely the determination of what _thinks_ itself and is _thought_, while to the side of the outer falls the determination _of actuality in the mode of being_. — So when someone is told: you (your inner) are this, _because_ your _bone_ is constituted thus, that means nothing other than: I take a bone to be _your actuality_. The riposte to such a judgment by a box on the ear, mentioned in connection with physiognomy, in the first place dislodges the _soft_ parts from their appearance and their situation, and shows no more than that these are no genuine _in-itself_, not the actuality of spirit; — here the riposte would properly have to go the length of smashing the skull of anyone who judges so, in order to demonstrate, quite as palpably as his wisdom deserves, that for a human being a bone amounts to no _in-itself_ whatever, still less to _his_ true actuality. —

{{{ 278 }}} Such a science of the skull will be thrown out unexamined by the crude instinct of self-conscious reason — thrown out along with that other, observing instinct of the same reason, which, having ripened into an inkling _of cognition_, has laid hold of cognition in the spiritless manner of supposing the outer to be an expression of the inner. But the worse a thought is, the less it sometimes stands out just where its badness lies, and the harder it is to lay that badness out. For a thought is called worse in proportion as the abstraction that counts for it as the essence is purer and emptier. But the opposition that matters here has as its members individuality conscious of its own self, together with the abstraction of an externality grown wholly into a _thing_, — spirit's inner being, that is, apprehended as fixed, spiritless being and set over against a being of exactly the same sort. — With this, however, observing reason does seem in fact to have reached its apex, from which it must abandon itself and turn head over heels; for only what is wholly bad carries in itself the immediate necessity of inverting itself. {{ 189 }} — It is as with the Jewish people, of whom one may say that its standing immediately before the gateway of salvation is precisely why it is, and has been, the most reprobate of peoples; the self-essentiality that it was to be in and for itself it is not for itself, but relocates beyond itself; through this externalization it makes a higher {{{ 279 }}} existence _possible_ for itself, supposing it could take its object back into itself once more, than would have been open to it had it stayed put inside the immediacy of being; for spirit's greatness grows with the greatness of the opposition out of which it comes back into itself; and this opposition it creates for itself in sublating its immediate unity and in externalizing its being-for-itself. Yet where such a consciousness fails to reflect itself, the middle in which it stands is a hapless emptiness, since what was to have filled that middle has hardened into a fixed extreme. And so observing reason's final stage is at the same time its worst, and for that very reason its reversal is necessary.

For if we survey the series of relationships considered up to now, the series that makes up what observation has for content and object, it emerges that already in observation's _first mode_, in the observing of the relationships of inorganic nature, _sensuous being vanishes_ from view; the moments of that relationship come forward as pure abstractions, as simple concepts that were to have been tied fast to the existence of things, an existence which is nevertheless lost, so that the moment proves to be pure movement and something universal. This process, free and complete within itself, keeps the significance of something objective, yet it now steps forward as a _One_; within the process of what is inorganic, the One is the inner that does not exist; but existing as One, the process is the {{{ 280 }}} organic. — Confronting the universal, the One stands there as being-for-itself or negative essence, withdraws from the universal and stays free on its own account, so that the concept, realized solely in the element of absolute singularization, fails to find in organic existence its genuine expression, namely that of being there _as universal_, and stays instead an outer or, what comes to the same, an _inner_ of organic nature. — Only _in itself_ is the organic process free; _for its own self_ it is not; in _purpose_ the being-for-itself of its freedom sets in, and _exists_ as another essence, as a wisdom conscious of its own self and lying outside that process. Observing reason accordingly turns to that wisdom, to spirit, to the concept that exists as universality, or the purpose that exists as purpose, and from this point on its own essence is what stands before it as object.

It turns first to spirit's purity; but since it is an apprehending of its object, which moves in its differences, as something that has being, what it arrives at are _laws of thinking_, relations of what endures to what endures; and since the content of these laws consists of nothing but moments, the laws run off into self-consciousness's One. — Taken in the same way as something _that has being_, this new object is the _singular, contingent_ self-consciousness; observing thus keeps within the compass of spirit as merely meant, and of the contingent relationship between actuality that is conscious and actuality that is not. {{ 190 }} Only in its own self {{{ 281 }}} is spirit the necessity of that relation; observation accordingly closes in on spirit bodily, setting its willing and acting actuality alongside the actuality in which it is reflected into itself and contemplative, an actuality which is itself objective. This outer, though it is a language of the individual which the individual possesses on its own self, is at the same time, being a sign, indifferent toward the content it was to designate, just as whatever sets up the sign for itself is indifferent toward the sign.

Away from this shifting language, then, observation retreats at last to _fixed being_, and declares, in keeping with its concept, that externality is spirit's outer and immediate actuality neither as organ nor as language and sign, but as a _dead thing_. The very earliest observation of inorganic nature sublated the demand that the concept be present as a thing; this last mode restores that demand, and does so by making spirit's own actuality into a thing, or, put the other way round, by conferring on dead being the significance of spirit. — With this, what our concept of observation was has come to be pronounced by observation itself: that reason's certainty is in search of its own self as objective actuality. — Admittedly one does not thereby mean that spirit, in being represented by a skull, is being pronounced a thing; no materialism, {{{ 282 }}} as people call it, is supposed to lie in this thought, spirit being rather something else again than these bones; yet to say of spirit that it _is_ comes to nothing else than calling it a _thing_. So when _being_ as such, or thinghood, gets predicated of spirit, the truthful expression of that is that spirit is something of the sort that _a bone is_. Hence it has to count as a matter of the highest importance that we have hit upon the true expression for what is said of spirit when it is said, quite baldly, that _it is_. When elsewhere it is said of spirit, _it is_, has _a being_, is a _thing_, a singular _actuality_, then what is thereby _meant_ is not something one could see, or take into one's hand, or bump against, and so on; yet something of just that kind is what is _said_, and what in truth is said accordingly expresses itself thus: that _spirit's being is a bone_.

This result now carries a twofold significance, first the true one, insofar as it completes the result of the movement that self-consciousness went through before. Self-consciousness in its unhappiness externalized its self-subsistence and wrung its _being-for-itself_ out into a _thing_. By that it went back out of self-consciousness into consciousness, that is, into the consciousness whose object is a _being_, a _thing_; — but this, which is a thing, is self-consciousness itself; so it is the unity of the I with being, the _category_. {{ 191 }} Since the object is for consciousness determined {{{ 283 }}} in this way, _it has reason_. Consciousness, like self-consciousness, _is in itself_ reason properly speaking; but only that consciousness for which the object has determined itself as the category can be said to _have_ reason; — and distinct from this again is knowing what reason is. — The category is the _immediate_ unity of _being_ with _what is its own_, so it must run through both forms, and observing consciousness is precisely the consciousness before which the category sets itself out in the form of _being_. Consciousness, in its result, utters as a proposition what it is unconsciously certain of — the proposition lying in reason's concept. It is the _infinite judgment_ to the effect that the self is a thing — and it is a judgment that sublates its own self. — What this result has thus added to the category, in a determinate way, is that the category is this self-sublating opposition. In the form of _being_ or of _immediacy_ the _pure_ category is there for consciousness as an object still _unmediated_, merely _present at hand_, and consciousness is a relating just as unmediated. That infinite judgment has for its moment _immediacy_'s transition into mediation, or _negativity_. Accordingly the object present at hand is determined as a negative one, while consciousness over against it is determined as _self_-consciousness; or the category, which in observing ran through the form of _being_, is posited now under the form of {{{ 284 }}} being-for-itself; no longer will consciousness _find itself immediately_, but it will bring itself forth through its own activity. _It itself_ is for itself the purpose of its doing, whereas in observing it had to do only with things.

The result's other significance is that of conceptless observing, which we have already looked at. This observing knows no other way to grasp and pronounce itself than by ingenuously declaring the bone, as it is found to be a sensuous thing that does not at the same time lose its objectivity for consciousness, to be the _actuality_ of self-consciousness. Yet it has no clear consciousness that it is saying this, and it does not grasp its proposition in the determinateness of subject and predicate and of their relation, much less in the sense of the concept and of that judgment which is infinite and dissolves its own self. — Rather, out of a deeper-lying self-consciousness belonging to spirit, which shows up here as a natural decency, it conceals from itself how disgraceful the conceptless, naked thought is of taking a bone for the actuality of self-consciousness, and it whitewashes that thought by means of thoughtlessness itself, mixing in a variety of relationships that have no sense here, those of cause and effect, of sign, of organ, and so on, and hiding the glaring character of the proposition behind distinctions borrowed from them.

{{{ 285 }}} {{ 192 }} Brain fever and the like, regarded as spirit's being, already amount to an actuality merely thought and merely hypothetical, — not one that _exists_, not felt, not seen, not the true actuality; once they _are there_, once they come into view, they are dead objects, and at that point they no longer pass for spirit's being. Genuine objectivity, however, has to be an _immediate, sensuous_ one, so that spirit, in this objectivity taken as dead — for the bone is the dead element insofar as it occurs on the living being itself — gets posited as actual. — The concept of this representation is that reason is to itself _all thinghood_, including _thinghood that is purely objective_; but reason is so _in the concept_, or rather the concept alone is its truth, and the purer that concept, the more foolish is the representation into which it sinks when its content is present not as concept but as representation, — when the self-sublating judgment is taken without any consciousness of this its infinity, and taken instead as an abiding proposition whose subject and predicate each hold good on their own, the self fixed as self and the thing as thing, and yet the one is supposed to be the other. — Reason, being essentially the concept, is immediately split into its own self and its opposite, an opposition which for that very reason is just as immediately sublated. But when reason, thus presenting itself as its own self and as its opposite, is held fast in the wholly singular moment of this {{{ 286 }}} stepping apart, it has been apprehended unreasonably; and the purer the moments of that stepping apart are, the more garish is the appearance of this content, which alone either is there for consciousness or is alone pronounced by it ingenuously. — The _depth_ which spirit drives out from inside, yet only so far as its _representing consciousness_, leaving it to stand there — and the _ignorance_ of this consciousness as to what it is that it says, is the very same linking of high and low that nature naively expresses in the living creature when it links the organ of its highest completion, the organ of procreation, — with the organ of pissing. — The infinite judgment, taken as infinite, would be the completion of life that grasps its own self, whereas the consciousness of that life which remains within representation relates itself as pissing.
