## a. The Ethical World, Human and Divine Law, Man and Woman

The simple substance of spirit divides itself as consciousness. Or: the consciousness whose object is abstract, sensuous being makes its transition into perception, {{{ 384 }}} and the same holds for immediate certainty of the real, ethical being; and just as simple being becomes, for sensuous perception, a thing bearing many properties, so the case of acting becomes, for ethical perception, an actuality bearing many ethical relations. In the former case, however, the idle multiplicity of properties draws itself together into the essential opposition of singularity and universality, and even more so in the latter, which is purified, substantial consciousness: there the multiplicity of ethical moments becomes twofold, a law of singularity alongside a law of universality. {{ 242 }} Yet each of these masses of the substance remains the whole of spirit; if in sensuous perception things possess no other substance than those two determinations, singularity and universality, in the present case the two determinations articulate nothing more than a superficial opposition of the two sides to one another.

Within the essence under consideration here, singularity carries the significance of _self-consciousness_ as such, not that of some singular, contingent consciousness. In this determination, therefore, the ethical substance is the _actual_ substance, absolute spirit _realized across the multiplicity of consciousness as it exists_; it is the _communal being_ which, _for us_, already counted as the absolute essence when we entered upon the practical shaping of reason as such, and which now steps forth in its own truth, _for itself_, as an ethical essence that is conscious, and, further, as the _essence for the_ consciousness that {{{ 385 }}} is our object here. Spirit it is: _for itself_ insofar as it maintains itself in the _counter-shine of individuals_, — and _in itself_, or substance, insofar as it holds them within itself. In the guise of _actual substance_ it is _a people_; in the guise of _actual consciousness, a citizen_ of that people. Such a consciousness finds its _essence_ in the simple spirit, finds self-certainty in that spirit's _actuality_, the people as a whole, and finds immediately therein its _truth_, hence not in anything unactual, but in a spirit which _exists_ and _is valid_.

Human law is the name that fits this spirit, since its essential form is that of _an actuality conscious of itself_. Taken in universality it is the _familiar_ law and the _extant_ custom; taken in singularity it is self-certainty made actual in the _individual_ as such, and as self-certainty in the shape of _simple individuality_ it is government; its truth is _validity_ that lies open in the light of day, an _existence_ which, for immediate certainty, assumes the form of an existence released into freedom.

Over against this ethical power and its manifestness, however, there steps the _divine law_, a power of another kind. For the ethical _state power_, as the _movement_ of _doing conscious of itself_, finds its opposite in what is the _simple_, _immediate essence_ of ethical life; qua _actual universality_ it is a violence directed against individual being-for-itself; and qua actuality {{{ 386 }}} in general it still harbors, in the _inner_ essence, something other than what it is.

It has already been noted that whichever of the two opposed modes of existing the ethical substance takes, that mode contains it entire, with every moment of its content. So, where the communal being is that substance in the guise of actual doing conscious of itself, the other side carries the form of substance as immediate, as merely being. Thus, from one side, it is the inward concept, ethical life as such in its universal possibility, while from the other side it has the moment of self-consciousness in it no less. {{ 243 }} This moment, giving expression to ethical life within the element of _immediacy_ or of _being_, or a consciousness of itself that is _immediate_, a consciousness of itself as essence and equally as this self in another, that is to say, a _natural ethical_ communal being, — is the _family_. As the _unconscious_ concept still inward, it stands over against its actuality conscious of itself; as the _element_ of the actuality of the people, over against the people itself; as _immediate_ ethical _being_, over against the ethical life that forms and preserves itself through _labor_ for the universal — the Penates over against the universal spirit.

Even though the _ethical being_ of the family determines itself as the _immediate_, the family within itself is not an _ethical_ essence _insofar as_ it is the relationship _of nature_ among its members, or insofar as their relation is the _immediate one of singular actual beings_; for {{{ 387 }}} what is ethical is _universal_ in itself, while this relationship of nature is essentially no less a spirit, and is ethical only as a spiritual essence. We have to see wherein its own distinctive ethical life consists. — First of all, because the ethical is what is universal in itself, the ethical relation among family members is neither a relation of feeling nor the relationship of love. Now it seems that the ethical would have to be located in the relationship of the _singular_ family member to the _whole_ family as substance, such that this member's doing and actuality take the family alone as purpose and content. But the conscious purpose which the _doing_ of this whole has, insofar as that purpose bears on the whole itself, is itself the singular. The acquisition and preservation of power and wealth bears in part merely on need and belongs to desire; in part, in its higher determination, it becomes something merely mediate. Such a determination lies outside the family itself and aims at the truly universal, the communal being; toward the family it is rather negative, and consists in placing the singular outside of it, in subjugating his naturalness and his singularity, and in drawing him toward _virtue_, toward a life lived in the universal and for it. As for the _positive_ purpose that belongs to the family as its own, this is the singular as such. For this relation to be ethical, however, he may not appear under {{{ 388 }}} a _contingency_, neither the one who acts nor the one on whom the action bears, in the way this happens with some piece of help or some service. What the ethical action has for content must be substantial, whole and universal; it can therefore bear only on the _whole_ singular, or on him as a universal. And this again not in the sense that it is merely _represented_ that a _service_ promotes his whole happiness, while as immediate and actual action it does only something singular to him; — nor in the sense that, as education, in a _series_ of efforts, it really does take him as a whole for its object and bring him forth as its work, where, apart from the purpose that is negative toward the family, the _actual action_ has only a restricted content; — and just as little, finally, in the sense that it is a rescue in an emergency, by which the whole singular is in truth saved; for such a deed is itself an entirely contingent one, its occasion being a common actuality which may just as well be as not be. {{ 244 }} The action, then, that embraces the whole existence of the blood-relation and takes for its object and content him — not the citizen, since the citizen is no member of the family, nor the one who is to become a citizen and to _cease_ counting as _this singular_, but rather him, _this_ singular who belongs to the family, taken as a _universal_ essence lifted clear of sensuous, that is, singular actuality — has to do no longer with the _living_ one but with the _dead_ one, who out of the long series of his scattered existence {{{ 389 }}} has gathered himself into the one completed shape, and who out of the restlessness of contingent life has risen into the repose of simple universality. — Since he is _actual_ and _substantial_ only as citizen, the singular, insofar as he is not a citizen and belongs to the family, is only the _unactual_, marrowless shade.

This universality at which the singular as _such_ arrives is _pure being, death_; it is the _immediate natural having-become_, not the _doing_ of a _consciousness_. It falls to the family member as a duty, therefore, to supply this side, so that his ultimate _being_ as well, this _universal_ being, may not be left to nature alone as something devoid of reason, but may instead be something _done_, in which the right of consciousness is upheld. Or rather the sense of the action is this: since in truth the repose and universality of an essence conscious of itself does not belong to nature, the semblance of such a doing, which nature has arrogated to itself, is to fall away, and the truth is to be established. — What nature did to him is the side from which his becoming universal presents itself as the movement of a _being_. That movement does indeed fall within the ethical communal being and takes it as its purpose; death is the completion, the supreme labor that the individual as such undertakes on the communal being's behalf. But insofar as the individual is essentially _singular_, it remains contingent whether his death stood in immediate {{{ 390 }}} connection with his labor for the universal and issued from it, and in part, even where it did, death is the _natural_ negativity, the movement of the singular as something _that is_, a movement in which consciousness makes no return into itself so as to become self-consciousness; or, since the movement of what _is_ consists in its being sublated and arriving at _being-for-itself_, death is that side of splitting-in-two where the being-for-itself attained differs from the being that entered upon the movement. — Since ethical life is spirit whose truth is _immediate_, the sides into which its consciousness breaks apart also fall under this form of _immediacy_, and singularity crosses over into this _abstract_ negativity, which, having neither consolation nor reconciliation _in its own self_, must _essentially_ receive them by way of an _actual_ and _external action_. {{ 245 }} — Blood-relationship, then, supplements the abstract natural movement by supervening upon it with the movement of consciousness, breaking off nature's work and wresting the blood-relation from destruction, or better, since destruction, his becoming pure being, is necessary, by taking the deed of destruction upon itself. — What is thereby brought about is that even the _dead_, the universal _being_, becomes something returned into itself, a _being-for-itself_, or that powerless, purely _singular_ singularity is raised to _universal individuality_. The dead man, having released his _being_ from his {{{ 391 }}} _doing_ or negative one, is empty singularity, merely a passive _being for another_, given over to every base and reasonless individuality and to the forces of abstract materials, of which the former, by virtue of the life it has, and the latter, by virtue of their negative nature, are now more powerful than he. This dishonoring doing on the part of unconscious desire and of abstract essences the family holds off from him, putting its own doing in place of it, and it gives the kinsman in marriage to the earth's womb, to elemental, imperishable individuality; by this it makes him the companion of a communal being which for its part overpowers and holds bound the forces of the singular materials and the base living things that were breaking loose against him and bent on destroying him.

This final duty, then, constitutes the complete _divine_ law, or the positive _ethical_ action toward the singular. Every other relationship toward him that goes beyond mere love and is ethical falls under the human law, and its significance is negative: to lift the singular above his confinement within the natural communal being to which he belongs as an _actual_ one. Now granted that what human right has as content and as power is the ethical substance in its actuality and conscious of itself, the whole people, whereas divine right and law has the singular who lies beyond actuality, still he is not therefore powerless; {{{ 392 }}} his power is the _abstract_, the purely _universal_, the _elemental_ individual, which drags back into pure abstraction, as into its own essence, that individuality which tears itself loose from the element and constitutes the people's actuality conscious of itself, being no less the ground of that individuality. — How this power presents itself in the people itself will unfold further on.

Now within the one law, as within the other, there are also _differences_ and _stages_. For since both essences have in them the moment of consciousness, difference unfolds within them, and this is what constitutes their movement and their own distinctive life. Examining these differences displays the manner in which the ethical world's two _universal essences_ exercise their _activity_ and their _self-consciousness_, and displays their _interconnection_ and _transition_ into each other as well.

{{ 246 }} The _communal being_, the upper law openly valid in the light of the sun, has its actual vitality in _government_, in which it is an individual. It is the simple _self_ of the ethical substance in its entirety, _spirit made actual and reflected into itself_. Such simple force does, to be sure, permit the essence to spread itself out into its articulated members and to grant every part subsistence and a being-for-itself of its own. Herein lies the spirit's _reality_, its _existence_, and the _element_ of that reality is the family. Yet spirit is likewise the force of the whole, drawing these parts {{{ 393 }}} once more together into the negative one, making them feel their non-self-subsistence, and holding them in the consciousness that their life is theirs only within the whole. The communal being may thus organize itself on the one hand into the systems of personal self-subsistence and of property, of the law of persons and of things; and it may likewise articulate the modes of laboring for what are at first singular purposes — acquisition and enjoyment — into associations of their own and make them self-subsistent. The spirit of the universal association is what is _simple_, the _negative_ essence of these systems that isolate themselves. Lest they root themselves in this isolating and harden, and lest the whole thereby fall apart and the spirit disperse, government must periodically shake them inwardly by means of wars, must thereby injure and throw into confusion the order and the right to self-subsistence that they have arranged for themselves, and must let the individuals who, absorbed in all that, tear loose from the whole and strive after the inviolable _being-for-itself_ and security of the person, feel in that imposed labor their master, death. By thus dissolving the form of subsistence, spirit fends off any sinking out of ethical existence into natural existence, and it preserves the self of its consciousness and raises that self into _freedom_ and into its own _force_. — So the negative essence proves to be what is properly the _power_ of the communal being, the _force_ {{{ 394 }}} by which it preserves itself; and the communal being accordingly draws the truth and confirmation of its own power from the essence of the _divine law_, from the _subterranean realm_.

The divine law that holds sway in the family likewise has differences within it, whose relation constitutes the living movement of its actuality. Among the three relationships, however, that of man and wife, that of parents and children, that of siblings as brother and sister, the first is the _relationship_ of _man_ and _wife_, the _immediate_ cognizing of the one consciousness in the other, and the cognizing of mutual recognizedness. Since it is the _natural_ cognizing of oneself, not the ethical one, it is only the _representation_ and the _image_ of spirit, not actual spirit itself. — The representation, or the image, however, has its actuality in something other than itself; hence this relationship possesses its actuality not within itself but in the child instead, — another whose becoming the relationship is, and in which it vanishes; and this succession of generations rolling onward finds its subsistence within the people. {{ 247 }} — The piety that man and wife bear toward each other is thus mixed with feeling and with natural relation, and their relationship does not carry its return into itself within itself; and the same holds of the second, the _piety_ of _parents_ and _children_ toward each other. That of the parents toward {{{ 395 }}} their children is affected precisely by the emotion of having the consciousness of one's own actuality in the other, and of seeing being-for-itself come to be in the other without getting it back, since it remains an alien actuality of its own; — that of the children toward the parents, conversely, is affected by the emotion of having one's own becoming, or the in-itself, in another that is vanishing, and of attaining being-for-itself and a self-consciousness of one's own only through separation from the origin — a separation in which that origin runs dry.

These two relationships remain caught within the passing-over and the inequality of the sides distributed to them. — It is between _brother_ and _sister_, however, that the unmixed relationship takes place. The blood in them is one and the same, yet a blood that within them has arrived at its _repose_ and _equilibrium_. They therefore do not desire each other, nor have they given this being-for-itself to each other or received it from each other; rather, they are free individuality over against each other. As sister, then, the feminine possesses the highest _intimation_ of the ethical essence; it does not attain to _consciousness_ of that essence or to its actuality, since the family's law is the _inward_ essence, the essence that has being _in itself_, an essence lying outside the daylight of consciousness and remaining inward feeling and the divine that is lifted out of actuality. It is to these Penates that the feminine is tied, {{{ 396 }}} intuiting in them partly its universal substance and partly its singularity, yet so that this relation to singularity is not at the same time the natural relation of pleasure. — As _daughter_, the woman must now watch her parents vanish with natural movement and with ethical repose, since this relationship must be spent before she reaches the _being-for-itself_ she is capable of; in her parents, therefore, she does not intuit her being-for-itself in any positive manner. — The relationships of _mother_ and of _wife_, however, have singularity partly as something natural belonging to pleasure, partly as something negative that sees in them only its own disappearance, and for just that reason partly as something contingent that can be replaced by another. Within the house of ethical life what these relationships of the feminine rest upon is not _this_ man, not _this_ child, but _a man, children in general_, — not feeling but the universal. What distinguishes the ethical life of the feminine from that of the man lies precisely here, that in its determination for singularity and in its pleasure the feminine stays immediately universal and remains a stranger to the singularity of desire, whereas in the man the two sides come apart, and since as citizen he possesses the _self-conscious_ force of _universality_, he purchases with it the right of _desire_, while keeping himself free of desire at the same time. {{ 248 }} Because singularity {{{ 397 }}} is thus mingled into this relationship of the wife, its ethical life is not pure; yet to the extent that this ethical life is pure, singularity becomes _indifferent_, and the wife then goes without that moment in which she would cognize herself as _this_ self in the other. — For the sister, however, the brother is simply the calm essence of like rank, and the recognition she has in him stays pure, unmixed with any natural relation; the indifference of singularity and its ethical contingency are accordingly absent from this relationship; instead, the moment of the _singular self_ that recognizes and is recognized is permitted here to make good its right, for it is joined to the equilibrium of the blood and to a relation without desire. For the sister, therefore, a brother's loss admits of no replacement, and her duty toward him stands highest.

At the same time this relationship marks the limit where the family, enclosed within itself, dissolves and passes outside itself. In the brother lies the side on which the family's spirit becomes an individuality that turns toward what is other, making the transition into the consciousness of universality. Leaving behind the family's _immediate, elemental_ and hence properly _negative_ ethical life, the brother sets out to win and to bring forth ethical life that is actual and conscious of itself.

Out of the divine law, within whose sphere he had lived, he crosses over to the human law. The sister, on the other hand, becomes what the wife remains: the one who presides over the house and keeps the divine {{{ 398 }}} law. So it is that the two sexes get beyond their natural essence and step forward in their ethical significance, as diversities which share out between them the two differences that the ethical substance confers on itself. If the ethical world's two _universal_ essences find their determinate _individuality_ in self-consciousness that is _naturally_ differentiated, the reason is that ethical spirit unites substance with self-consciousness _immediately_; — an _immediacy_ which, taken on the side of reality and of difference, thus shows up at once as a natural difference given in existence. — This is the very side that showed up, in the shape of individuality real to itself, within the concept of spiritual essence, under the title of _originally determinate nature_. Here that moment sheds the indeterminateness which still clings to it there, and sheds the contingent diversity of dispositions and capacities. What we now have is the two sexes in determinate opposition, their naturalness at once taking on the significance of their ethical determination.

Sexual difference, however, together with the difference of ethical content, stays inside the substance's unity, and the movement of that difference is precisely the substance's abiding becoming. The family spirit dispatches the man out into the communal being, where he finds the essence that is conscious of itself; and if the family thereby has in him its universal substance and its subsistence, conversely the communal being {{{ 399 }}} owes the formal element of its actuality to the family, and its force and corroboration to the divine law. {{ 249 }} Neither of the two exists alone in and for itself; in its living movement the human law issues from the divine, what is valid on earth from what is subterranean, the conscious from what lacks consciousness, mediation from immediacy, and it returns just as surely to the point from which it issued. The subterranean power, for its part, possesses its _actuality_ upon the earth; by means of consciousness it turns into existence and into activity.

So the universal ethical essences are, on one hand, substance in its universality and, on the other, substance as singular consciousness; their universal actuality lies in the people and in the family, while the man and the woman furnish their natural self and the individuality that carries them into activity. Within this content of the ethical world the purposes set by the earlier, substanceless shapes of consciousness stand fulfilled before us; what reason apprehended merely as object has turned into self-consciousness, and what self-consciousness held merely within itself is on hand as true actuality. — That which observation knew as something _found on hand_, in which the self would have no share, is here custom found on hand, yet an actuality that is at once the work and the deed of the one who finds it. — The singular, in search of the pleasure _of enjoying his singularity_, finds it in the family, while the necessity in which such pleasure perishes is nothing other than his self-consciousness {{{ 400 }}} as citizen of his people; — or it is this: knowing the _law of the heart_ to be the law of every heart, knowing the consciousness of the _self_ to be the universal order that is acknowledged; — it is _virtue_, which enjoys what its own sacrifice has borne as fruit; virtue accomplishes what it aims at, namely to lift the essence out into actual presence, and its enjoyment is this universal life. — Finally, consciousness of _the matter at hand_ is satisfied in the real substance, which positively contains and preserves the abstract moments of that empty category. In the ethical powers it possesses a genuine content, which has taken the place of those substanceless commandments that sound reason wished to hand down and to know; — and along with it a contentful standard, determined in its own self, by which to test, not the laws, but rather what gets done.

The whole is a calm equilibrium of all its parts, each part a spirit at home there, a spirit that looks for its satisfaction in no beyond but possesses it within, for the very reason that it stands within this same equilibrium with the whole. — To be sure, such an equilibrium can be a living one only in that inequality arises within it and is led back to equality by _justice_. But justice is neither an alien essence located in a beyond, nor is it that unworthy actuality of mutual malice, betrayal, ingratitude {{{ 401 }}} and so on, which after the manner of thoughtless chance would execute judgment as an uncomprehended interconnection and as unconscious doing and refraining; rather, as the justice of _human_ right, bringing back into the universal that being-for-itself which steps out of equilibrium, the self-subsistence of the estates and of individuals, justice is the people's government, which is the individuality of the universal essence present to itself, and the very self-conscious will of all. {{ 250 }} — But the justice that brings back to equilibrium the universal which has become overpowering against the singular is likewise the simple spirit of the one who has suffered wrong; — not decomposed into him who suffered it and an essence lying beyond; he himself is the subterranean power, and it is _his_ Erinys that pursues the revenge; for his individuality, his blood, goes on living in the house; his substance has an actuality that lasts. In the realm of ethical life the only wrong that can be inflicted on the singular is that something purely and simply _happens_ to him. The power that perpetrates this wrong upon consciousness, making it into a mere thing, is nature; the universality at work is not that of the _communal being_ but the _abstract_ universality of _being_; and in undoing the wrong suffered, singularity turns not against the former, having suffered nothing from it, but against the latter. The consciousness of the individual's blood undoes this wrong, {{{ 402 }}} as we have seen, in such a way that what has merely _happened_ becomes rather a _work_, so that _being_, the _last_ thing, may also be something _willed_ and thereby gratifying.

In this way the ethical realm is, in its _subsistence_, an unblemished world defiled by no discord. Its movement, equally, is a calm becoming in which the one power of that realm turns into the other, each of them sustaining and producing the other. We do indeed see it divide into two essences along with their actuality; their opposition, however, is rather the corroboration of the one by way of the other, and at the point where they touch immediately as actual, their middle and element consists in their immediately interpenetrating each other. The one extreme, universal spirit that is conscious of itself, is closed together, through the _individuality_ of the _man_, with its other extreme, with its force and its element, that is, with _unconscious_ spirit. Conversely, it is in the woman that the _divine_ law has its individualization, or that the _unconscious_ spirit of the singular has its existence, and through her as the _middle_ that spirit climbs up out of its unactuality into actuality, out of what does not know and is not known into the realm of consciousness. {{ 251 }} What man and woman make up in their union is the active middle of the whole and the element which, split in two into those extremes of divine and of human law, is at the same time the immediate uniting of them, an element which welds those first two syllogisms into one and the same syllogism {{{ 403 }}} and which takes the opposed movement — that of actuality descending into unactuality, the human law, which organizes itself into self-subsistent members, going down toward the peril and the corroboration of death; — and that of the subterranean law rising to the actuality of the day and to conscious existence, — the first falling to the man, the second to the woman — and unites it into One.
