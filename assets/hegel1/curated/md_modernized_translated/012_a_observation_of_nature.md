## a. Observation of Nature

{{ 139 }} Should thoughtless consciousness pronounce observation and experience to be the source of truth, its words may indeed sound as if everything turned on nothing but taste, smell, touch, hearing and sight; so eager is it to commend taste, smell, and so on, that it neglects to add that it has in fact, and quite as essentially, already determined for itself the object of such sensing, and that this determination weighs with it at least as much as that sensing does. It will also concede at once that it is not simply perceiving as such that concerns it, and it will not let the perception that this penknife is lying next to this snuffbox count as an observation. What is perceived must at least stand for _a universal_, not for _a sensuous this_.

{{{ 178 }}} Thus far this universal is only _what remains the same as itself;_ its movement, nothing but the unvarying return of one and the same doing. To the extent that consciousness meets with nothing in the object but _universality_ or the _abstract mine_, it has to assume the object's proper movement onto _itself_; not yet being the understanding of that object, it has at least to be its memory, giving universal expression to what, in actuality, is on hand only in singular fashion. Such shallow extraction from singularity, together with a no less shallow form of universality that merely receives the sensuous without the sensuous having itself become universal, in short the _describing_ of things, still lacks the movement within the object itself; the movement lies instead solely in the describing. Once described, the object has thereby forfeited all interest; with one of them finished, another must be taken in hand and the searching must go on without end, so that the describing does not run dry. Once new _whole_ things become hard to come by, recourse must be had to the ones already in hand, dividing them up further, laying them apart, and ferreting out yet more sides of thinghood in them. This restless, unquiet instinct can never run short of material; to find a new and distinguished genus, or indeed a new planet, which, though it is an individual, still has the nature of a universal {{{ 179 }}} belonging to it, can fall only to the lucky few. {{ 140 }} But the boundaries of what, like the elephant, the oak, gold, is _distinguished_, of what is _genus_ and _species_, pass through many gradations into the endless _particularization_ of the chaotic animals and plants, of the kinds of rock, or of the metals, earths and so on that must first be produced by force and art. In that realm where the universal is indeterminate, a realm in which particularization draws close once more to _singularization_ and here and there sinks entirely back into it, an inexhaustible supply stands open for observing and describing. Yet here, at the boundary of the universal, where a field defying all survey opens before it, what it may rather have come upon is not an immeasurable wealth but only the barrier set by nature and by its own doing; knowing whether that which appears to be in itself is not instead a contingency is now beyond it; and anything carrying the mark of a muddled or unripe formation, feeble and hardly working its way out of elemental indeterminateness, cannot claim even to be described.

Even if the things alone appear to be what such seeking and describing is after, what we actually see is that it does not run its course along _sensuous perceiving_; instead it attaches more weight to whatever it is by which things are _cognized_ than to the rest of the compass of sensuous properties, properties the {{{ 180 }}} thing itself certainly cannot spare, though consciousness relieves itself of them. By drawing this distinction between the _essential_ and the _inessential_, the concept lifts itself clear of sensuous dispersion, and cognition thereby avows that its concern is at least as essentially _with itself_ as it is with the things. Faced with this doubled essentiality it begins to waver over whether that which _cognition_ finds essential and necessary belongs likewise to _the things_. In one respect the _marks_ are to serve cognition alone, as the means by which it tells things apart; in another respect, though, what is to be cognized is not the inessential in things but that whereby they _tear themselves free_ of being's universal continuity as such, _sever_ themselves from the other, and are _for themselves_. Not only are the marks to stand in an essential relation to cognition; they are also to be the things' essential determinatenesses, and the artificial system is to accord with nature's own system and give expression to nothing but that. This follows necessarily from the concept of reason, and reason's instinct — for in this observing reason behaves merely as instinct — has in its systems indeed attained such unity, namely wherever the constitution of its own objects is such that they carry an essentiality or a _being-for-itself_ about them and are not simply the accident of this _moment_ or this _Here_. For animals, say, the distinguishing marks {{{ 181 }}} are drawn from claws and teeth; for in fact cognition is not alone in using them to _distinguish_ one animal from another; the animal itself thereby _sets_ itself apart; by these weapons it maintains itself _for itself_ and in separation from the universal. {{ 141 }} The plant, by contrast, does not attain being-_for-itself_ but only touches the boundary of individuality; at this boundary, where it displays the shine of a _splitting-in-two_ into sexes, it has therefore been taken up and distinguished. But what stands lower down can no longer distinguish itself from another and is instead lost as soon as it enters into opposition. _Being at rest_ and _being in relation_ come into conflict with each other; in the latter the thing is something other than it is according to the former, whereas the individual is precisely this, to preserve itself in relation to another. Whatever is incapable of this, however, and _chemically_ becomes something other than what it _empirically_ is, throws cognition into confusion and lands it in that same conflict about which of the two sides to cleave to, seeing that the thing is itself nothing that stays constant and that in it the sides come apart.

In such systems of the universal that remains the same as itself, then, this universal has the significance of being what remains the same as itself in cognition just as much as in the things themselves. Yet this spreading out of _the determinatenesses that stay the same_, each of which {{{ 182 }}} quietly traces the series of its own advance and is allowed room to hold good for itself, essentially passes over no less into its opposite, into the confusion of those very determinatenesses; for what the mark is, the universal determinateness, is a unity of opposites, of the determinate together with what is universal in itself; hence it must step apart into that opposition. If on one side, then, determinateness gets the better of that universal wherein its essence lies, on the other side the universal preserves its mastery over determinateness just as well, pressing it out to its limit and there blending its differences and essentialities together. Observing, which had kept them tidily apart and supposed it possessed something firm in them, watches one principle overreach the others, watches transitions and confusions take shape, and watches what it had first taken as utterly separate become joined here, and what it had reckoned together become separated; so that this holding fast to a calm being that remains the same as itself must, in its most universal determinations of all, for instance as to what essential marks belong to the animal or to the plant, find itself mocked by instances that strip it of every determination, reduce to silence the universality it had risen to, and throw it back upon thoughtless observing and describing.

Observing of this kind, which confines itself to the simple or curbs sensuous dispersion by means of the universal, {{{ 183 }}} accordingly comes upon _the confusion of its own principle_ in its object, since by its very nature the determinate is bound to lose itself in what is opposed to it; reason must for that reason press on from the _sluggish_ determinateness, which had the semblance of permanence, to the observing of that determinateness as it truly is, namely _as standing in relation to its opposite_. {{ 142 }} The so-called essential marks are _resting_ determinatenesses which, expressed and apprehended as _simple_ in the way they are, fail to exhibit what their nature really consists in, namely being vanishing _moments_ of the movement that withdraws into itself. Now that the instinct of reason sets about seeking out determinateness in keeping with its nature, essentially not to be for itself but to go over into what is opposed to it, it looks for the _law_ of determinateness and for its _concept_; it looks for these, admittedly, as an actuality that equally _is_, yet that actuality is in truth going to disappear for it, while the law's sides turn into sheer moments, or abstractions, with the result that the law steps forth within the nature of a concept that has annihilated in itself the indifferent subsistence of sensuous actuality.

Observing consciousness locates the _truth of the law_ in _experience_, that is, in the mode whereby _sensuous being is for it_; the law's truth does not lie in and for itself. Should the law, however, fail to possess its truth in the concept, it is then something contingent {{{ 184 }}} rather than a necessity, indeed not a law at all. Yet that it essentially is as concept not only fails to conflict with its being present for observation; rather, for that very reason it has necessary _existence_ and is there for observation. What is universal in the _sense of reason's universality_ is universal too in the sense that observation attaches to it, namely that _for_ consciousness it presents itself as what is present and actual, or that the concept exhibits itself after the manner of thinghood and sensuous being; — yet without thereby forfeiting its nature and having sunk into sluggish subsistence or indifferent succession. What is universally valid also holds universally; what _ought to be, is_ in fact also, and what merely _ought_ to be without _being_ has no truth. To this the instinct of reason rightly holds fast for its part, and refuses to be led astray by the thought-things that merely _ought_ to be and are supposed to have truth as an _ought_ even though they are met with in no experience — as little by hypotheses as by all the other invisibilities of a perennial ought; for reason is precisely this certainty of having reality, and whatever is not there for consciousness as an essence that is a self, that is, whatever does not appear, is for it nothing at all.

That the truth of the law is essentially _reality_ does indeed become, for this consciousness that stays with observing, {{{ 185 }}} once more an _opposition_ to the concept and to what is universal in itself; or something such as its law is is for it not an essence of reason; it supposes that in this it receives something _alien_. {{ 143 }} Yet it refutes this supposition of its own by what it does, since it does not itself take the law's universality in the sense that _all singular_ sensuous things would have to have shown it the appearance of the law before it could assert the law's truth. For stones to fall when lifted from the earth and let go, it by no means demands that the experiment be carried out on every stone; it may indeed say that a great many at least must have been tried, and that from these one could then draw a conclusion about the remainder, with the highest probability or with full warrant _by analogy_. Yet analogy not only gives no full right; by its very nature it refutes itself so often that, to infer by analogy itself, analogy rather permits no inference to be drawn at all. The _probability_ to which its result would reduce loses, over against _truth_, every difference between lesser and greater probability; however great it may be, it is nothing against truth. In fact, though, the instinct of reason accepts such laws as _truth_, and it is only with respect to their necessity, which it does not cognize, that it lapses into this {{{ 186 }}} distinction and demotes the truth of the matter itself to probability, so as to designate the defective mode in which truth is on hand for a consciousness that has not yet reached insight into the pure concept; universality, after all, is on hand merely as _simple, immediate_ universality. Yet it is on account of that same universality that the law holds truth for consciousness; that the stone falls is true for consciousness because for it the stone is _heavy_, that is, because in virtue of its heaviness the stone bears _in and for itself_ the essential relation _to the earth_, a relation that expresses itself as falling. So in experience it has the law's _being_, but equally has the law as _concept_, and the law is true for it only because _both circumstances_ hold together; the law counts as a law on the ground that it exhibits itself in appearance while being in its own self concept.

Since the law is at once _concept in itself_, the instinct of reason belonging to this consciousness necessarily aims, albeit unaware that this is its own aim, at _purifying into the concept_ both the law and the law's moments. It conducts experiments on the law. In its first appearing the law presents itself impurely, shrouded in singular sensuous being, the concept that makes up its nature lying sunk in empirical material. What the instinct of reason aims at in its experiments is to find out what ensues under these circumstances and those. By this the law {{{ 187 }}} seems only to be dipped all the more into sensuous being; yet it is rather sensuous being that gets lost in the process. The inner significance of such research is to find _pure conditions of the_ law; and that says neither more nor less, however much the consciousness which so expresses itself may fancy it is saying something else, than that the law is to be raised entirely into the shape of the concept and that every bond of _its moments_ to _determinate being is to be erased_. {{ 144 }} Take negative electricity, which announces itself at first perhaps as _resinous_ electricity, as the positive announces itself as _vitreous electricity_: through the experiments it forfeits that significance altogether and becomes electricity that is simply _positive_ and simply _negative_, with neither of the two belonging any more to some particular sort of things; and one can no longer say that some bodies are positively electric and others negatively so. In the same way the relation of acid and base and their movement against each other makes up a law in which these opposites appear as bodies. These separated-off things, however, possess no actuality; the violence by which they are torn apart is powerless to keep them from re-entering a process straightaway, for this relation is all that they are. Unlike a tooth or a claw, they cannot persist for themselves and be pointed out in that fashion. Their essence being just this, to go over without mediation into a neutral product, their _being_ is thereby made into a being sublated in itself, that is, into a universal one, {{{ 188 }}} and acid and base possess truth solely as _universals_. Hence, just as glass and resin admit of being electric positively no less than negatively, acid and base are not tied down as a property to one _actuality_ or another; every thing is instead acid or basic only _relatively_; and whatever looks to be a decided base or acid acquires, in the so-called synsomaties, the reverse significance with respect to something else. — The upshot of the experiments thus sublates the moments, or inspiritings, in their character as properties of determinate things, and it liberates the predicates from their subjects. Such predicates, taken as they are in truth, turn up only as universals; for the sake of this self-subsistence they accordingly acquire the name of _matters_, being neither bodies nor properties. And one takes good care not to call oxygen and so on, positive and negative electricity, heat and so on, bodies.

_Matter_, on the other hand, is no _thing that is_, but rather being taken as _universal_, being after the manner of the concept. Reason, still instinct as it is, makes this distinction correctly, yet without the consciousness that when it tries the law out upon everything sensuous in being, it is precisely there sublating the law's merely sensuous being, and that when it apprehends the law's moments as _matters_, their essentiality has become universal for it and is pronounced in that very expression as a sensuous that is non-sensuous, as a being both bodiless and objective.

{{{ 189 }}} We must now see how its result turns out for it, and which new shape of its observing thereby comes on the scene. As the truth of this experimenting consciousness we see the pure law that frees itself from sensuous being; we see it as _concept_, which is present in sensuous being yet moves within it self-subsistently and unbound, submerged in it yet free of it and a _simple_ concept. {{ 145 }} What in truth is the _result_ and the _essence_ now comes on the scene for this consciousness in its own right, yet as an _object_, and, precisely because for consciousness it is not a _result_ at all and lacks any bearing on the movement that preceded, it comes on the scene as an object of a _particular kind_, its relation to which is another kind of observing.

The _organic_ is such an object, one that carries the process about it in the _simplicity_ of the concept. It is that absolute fluidity wherein whatever determinateness would make it merely something _for another_ has been dissolved. If the inorganic thing has determinateness for its essence, and for that reason constitutes the full complement of the concept's moments only in company with some other thing, and is therefore lost once it enters into movement, in the organic essence, by contrast, every determinateness through which it stands open to another is tied beneath the simple unity of the organism; not one of them steps forward as essential in a manner that would relate freely to {{{ 190 }}} another; and so the organic maintains itself in its very relating.

As follows from this determination, the _sides_ of the _law_ that the instinct of reason here sets out to observe are, to begin with, _organic_ nature together with _inorganic_ nature, each as related to the other. The latter is, for organic nature, precisely that freedom of _untied_ determinatenesses which stands opposed to organic nature's _simple concept_, determinatenesses in which individual nature is _at once dissolved_, and from whose continuity it _at once_ marks itself off so as to be _for itself_. Universal elements of this sort are air, water, earth, zones and climate; they constitute the indeterminate simple essence of individualities, within which those individualities are at once reflected into themselves. Individuality is not simply in and for itself, nor is the elemental; rather, within the self-subsistent freedom in which the two come forward against one another for observation, they relate themselves at the same time as _essential relations_, though in such fashion that what predominates is the self-subsistence and mutual indifference of both, which passes over into abstraction only in part. Law is present here, then, in the guise of an element's relation to the formation of the organic, an organic that at one moment has elemental being confronting it and at another exhibits that being in its own organic reflection. Yet _laws_ of this sort, that animals belonging to the air are of the constitution of birds, those belonging {{{ 191 }}} to water of the constitution of fish, that northern animals have a thickly haired pelt, and so forth, immediately betray a poverty unequal to the manifoldness of the organic. {{ 146 }} Besides the fact that organic freedom knows how to withdraw its forms from such determinations once more, and necessarily presents exceptions everywhere to laws or rules of that kind, whatever one may care to call them, even in the very instances that do fall under them the determination stays so superficial that the expression of their necessity cannot be otherwise either and gets no further than _great influence_, with no one knowing what actually pertains to such influence and what does not. Such relations of the organic to the elemental are therefore not in truth to be called _laws_, since on the one hand, as was recalled, a relation of this kind by no means exhausts, by its content, the compass of the organic, while on the other hand the relation's own moments stay indifferent toward one another and give voice to no necessity. The _concept_ of base lies within the concept of acid, as negative electricity lies within the concept of positive electricity; but however often a thickly haired pelt may be _met with_ alongside the north, or the build of fish alongside water, or the build of birds alongside air, the concept of the north contains no concept of thick hair, that of the sea none of the build of fish, {{{ 192 }}} that of the air none of the build of birds. Owing to this freedom of both sides toward each other, there _are_ also land animals bearing the essential characters of a bird, or of the fish, and so on. Because necessity admits of no comprehension as anything inner to the essence, it likewise ceases to possess sensuous existence and is no longer observable in actuality; it has instead _stepped out_ of actuality. Failing thus to be found in the real essence itself, necessity is that which goes by the name of teleological relation, a relation _external_ to the terms it relates and hence the very reverse of a law. It is thought entirely freed from necessary nature, thought that abandons nature and moves for itself above it.

If the relation of the organic to elemental nature touched on above does not express the organic's essence, that essence is by contrast contained in the _concept of purpose_. For this observing consciousness, to be sure, the concept of purpose is not the organic's own _essence_ but falls outside it, and is then only that external, _teleological_ relation. Yet on the determination of the organic given a moment ago, the organic is really the real purpose itself; for since in its relation to another it is _itself_ that it _keeps in being_, it is just that natural essence wherein nature bends back into the concept, drawing into one the moments that necessity had laid apart, those of a cause {{{ 193 }}} and an effect, of an agent and a patient; so that what comes forward here is not merely a _result_ of necessity; rather, since it has withdrawn into itself, what is last, the result, is equally the _first_ that begins the movement, and is its own _purpose_, which it brings to actuality. The organic does not bring something forth; it _only preserves itself_, or what is brought forth is already just as much present as it is brought forth.

{{ 147 }} We must look more closely at this determination, in its being in itself no less than in its being for the instinct of reason, so as to see how that instinct finds itself therein and yet fails to recognize itself in its own find. The concept of purpose, then, to which observing reason lifts itself, being reason's _conscious concept_, is on hand quite as much as something _actual_; nor is it a mere _external relation_ of that actual, but the actual's _essence_. To say that this actual, itself a purpose, relates purposively to another is to say that its relating is contingent _in respect of what the two immediately are;_ immediately, the two are self-subsistent and indifferent toward each other. The essence of their relating is nevertheless other than what they seem thus to be, and their doing carries a different sense from the one it has _immediately_ for sensuous perceiving; necessity lies hidden in what takes place and comes to light only _at the end_, though in such manner that the end itself reveals necessity to have been the first as well. {{{ 194 }}} And the end demonstrates this priority of its own by the circumstance that the alteration which the doing has undertaken yields nothing other than what already was. Or, beginning from the first, we find that at its end, in the result of its doing, it merely returns to itself; and just thereby it proves to be something having _itself_ for its end, something therefore that as first has already returned into itself, something that is _in and for itself_. What it attains through the movement of its doing is thus _itself;_ and that it attains only itself is its _self-feeling_. Herewith a difference is indeed on hand, the difference of _what it is_ from _what it seeks_, yet this amounts only to the _semblance of a difference_, and by this it is concept in its own self.

But _self-consciousness_ is constituted in just this way, to distinguish itself from itself in a manner in which at the same time no difference comes out. Observing organic nature, it accordingly comes upon nothing but this essence; it comes upon its own self as a thing, _as a life_, while still drawing a difference between what it is itself and what it has found, a difference that is none. As the animal's instinct hunts for fodder and consumes it, thereby bringing forth nothing but itself, the instinct of reason likewise comes upon nothing but reason itself in its own hunting. The animal ends with self-feeling. The instinct of reason, on the contrary, is {{{ 195 }}} self-consciousness as well; but being merely instinct, it is placed off to the side over against consciousness and has its opposite in consciousness. Its satisfaction is therefore split in two by this opposite; it does indeed find itself, namely the _purpose_, and likewise finds this purpose as a _thing_. But in the first place the purpose falls for it _outside the thing_ that presents itself as purpose. Secondly, this purpose qua purpose is at once _objective_, and hence it too does not fall for the instinct within itself as consciousness, but into some other understanding.

Looked at more closely, this determination lies no less within the thing's own concept, namely that the thing is _purpose in its own self_. {{ 148 }} For it maintains _itself;_ that is, at the same time, concealing necessity and exhibiting it under the form of a _contingent_ relation belongs to the thing's nature; for its freedom, its _being-for-itself_, consists precisely in this, that it relates itself indifferently toward what is necessary to it; and so it exhibits itself as something whose concept would fall outside its being. In the same way reason has the necessity of intuiting its own concept as falling outside it, and hence as a _thing_, as something toward which it is _indifferent_ and which reciprocally is indifferent toward reason and toward its own concept. As instinct, reason too halts within this _being_, this _indifference_, and the thing giving expression to the concept remains for reason distinct from that concept, the concept distinct {{{ 196 }}} from the thing. For reason, accordingly, the organic thing is _purpose_ in its own self only in the sense that the necessity presenting itself as concealed within the thing's doing, given that in that doing the agent relates itself as something indifferent and being for itself, falls outside the organic itself. — But since the organic as purpose in its own self cannot relate itself otherwise than as such, the fact that it is purpose in its own self is also apparent and sensuously present, and it is observed as such. The organic shows itself as something _preserving_ itself and _returning_ into itself and _having returned_. But in this being the observing consciousness does not cognize the concept of purpose, or does not cognize that the concept of purpose exists not somewhere else in an understanding but precisely here, and is as a thing. It draws a difference, one that is none, between the concept of purpose on the one hand and, on the other, being-for-itself together with self-preservation. That it is none is not there for it; what is there for it is rather a doing that appears contingent and indifferent toward what comes about through it, and the unity that nevertheless ties the two together, — that doing and this purpose, falls apart for it.

What on this view belongs to the organic itself is the doing that lies in the middle between its first and its last, insofar as that doing has the character of singularity in it. But doing insofar as it bears the character of universality, and {{{ 197 }}} with the agent set equal to whatever is thereby brought forth, purposive doing as such, would not belong to the organic. That singular doing, which is only a means, falls through its singularity under the determination of a thoroughly singular or contingent necessity. Whatever the organic does to maintain itself as an individual, or itself as genus, is therefore, by this immediate content, wholly lawless, since the universal and the concept fall outside it. Its doing would on that showing be an empty efficacy, contentless in its own self; nor would it so much as amount to the efficacy of a machine, since a machine does have a purpose, and its efficacy has thereby a determinate content. So forsaken by the universal, its activity would be that merely of something that is, as something that _is_, that is to say an activity not reflected into itself at the same time, such as the activity of an acid or of a base; an efficacy incapable of detaching itself from its immediate existence, and incapable too of surrendering that existence, which is forfeited in relating to its opposite, while yet maintaining itself. {{ 149 }} The being whose efficacy is under consideration here, however, is posited as a thing _maintaining itself_ throughout its relation to what is opposed to it; _activity_ as such amounts to nothing more than the pure, essenceless form of that being's being-for-itself, and the substance of this activity, which is not mere determinate being but the universal, its _purpose_, does not lie outside it; the activity is one that in its own self goes back into itself, not {{{ 198 }}} one directed back into itself by anything alien.

For the _observing_ consciousness, however, this unity of universality with activity is not there, for the reason that such unity is in essence the organic's inward movement and admits of apprehension only as concept; observing, on the other hand, looks for the moments under the form of _being_ and _abiding;_ and since the organic whole is in essence such that it does not have the moments in it that way and does not let them be found in it, consciousness in its view converts the opposition into one that suits that view.

What arises for it in this way is the organic essence as a relation between two moments that _are_ and are _fixed_, — an opposition of which the two sides appear to it, in one respect, to be given in observation, while in another respect, by their content, they give voice to the opposition between the organic _concept of purpose_ and _actuality_; but since the concept as such has been erased in them, they do so obscurely and shallowly, in a manner where thinking has slid down into representing. The former, then, we see roughly meant under the heading _inner_, the latter under _outer_, and their relation begets the law _that the outer is what expresses the inner_.

When this inner is considered more closely along with its opposite and their relation to each other, it emerges {{{ 199 }}} that, first, the two sides of the law no longer read as they did in earlier laws, where they appeared as self-subsistent _things_, each as a particular body, nor, second, in such a way that the universal should have its concrete existence somewhere else _outside what is_. Instead the organic essence is laid at the base undivided throughout, as the content of inner and of outer alike, one and the same for both; thereby the opposition is no more than a formal one, its real sides having the same _in-itself_ for their essence, while at once, inner and outer being also opposed reality and a _being_ that differs for observation, each of them seems to observation to possess a content peculiar to itself. Being the very same substance, the very same organic unity, this peculiar content can in truth amount to no more than a differing form of that substance; and observing consciousness hints as much when it says that the outer amounts only to the _expression_ of the inner. {{ 150 }} — These same determinations of the relationship, namely the indifferent self-subsistence of the differing terms, and within it their unity wherein they vanish, are what we saw in the concept of purpose.

What _shape_ inner and outer have in their being is what must now be seen. No less than the outer as such, the inner as such is bound to possess an outer being and a shape, for it {{{ 200 }}} is object, or is itself posited as something that is and as on hand for observation.

As _inner_, organic substance is the _simple_ soul, that is, the pure _concept of purpose_, the _universal_ which in dividing itself stays universal fluidity all the same and hence shows up in its _being_ as the _doing_ or the _movement_ of an actuality that is _vanishing_; the _outer_, by contrast, set over against that inner which is, consists in the organic's _being at rest_. Law, being the way that inner bears on this outer, accordingly gives its content expression at one time by presenting universal _moments_ or _simple essentialities_, at another by presenting the actualized essentiality, that is, the _shape_. The first simple organic _properties_, if we may so name them, are _sensibility, irritability_ and _reproduction_. Now these properties, or at any rate the first two of them, do seem to bear not on the organism at large but on the animal organism alone. And indeed the vegetable organism gives expression merely to the organism's simple concept, one that _does not develop_ its moments; with respect to those properties, therefore, insofar as they are to be there for observation, we have to keep to the organism which exhibits their developed existence.

As for the properties themselves, they follow immediately from the concept of what is its own purpose. For _sensibility_ gives expression generally to the simple concept {{{ 201 }}} of organic reflection into itself, that is, to the universal fluidity of the organic; _irritability_, on the other hand, gives expression to organic elasticity, to relating itself within that reflection as at the same time _reacting_, and to the actualization opposed to the first quiescent _being-within-itself_, an actualization wherein that abstract being for itself amounts to a being _for another_. _Reproduction_, though, is the action of the _whole_ organism as reflected into itself, the organism's activity as purpose in itself or as _genus_, wherein the individual thrusts itself away from itself, reproducing in its generating either its own organic parts or the whole individual. {{ 151 }} Understood in the sense of _self-preservation as such_, reproduction gives expression to the formal concept of the organic, to sensibility; properly, however, it is the real organic concept, the _whole_ that returns into itself either as individual, by bringing forth the singular parts of itself, or as genus, by bringing forth individuals.

Their _other significance_, that of these organic elements taken as the _outer_, is the _shaped_ mode in which they are on hand as _actual_ and yet at once as _universal_ parts, or organic _systems_: sensibility, say, as the nervous system, irritability as the muscular system, reproduction as the viscera serving preservation of the individual and of the genus.

{{{ 202 }}} Laws proper to the organic thus have to do with how the organic moments stand to one another under their twofold meaning: to be, on the one hand, a _part_ of the organic _formation_, and on the other a _universal fluid_ determinateness running through every one of those systems. In the wording of such a law, then, a determinate _sensibility_, taken as a moment of the _whole_ organism, would be expressed by a nervous system formed in a determinate way, or else it would be bound up with a determinate _reproduction_ of the individual's organic parts, or with the whole's propagation, and so on. — The two sides of such a law can each be _observed_. The _outer_, by its concept, is _being for another_; sensibility, for example, possesses its immediately actualized mode within the sensible _system_; and taken as a _universal property_ it is, within its _expressions_, no less objective. The side termed the _inner_ possesses its _own outer_ side, distinct from what is termed the _outer_ within the whole.

So the two sides of an organic law would indeed be open to observation, only not laws of their relation; and observation falls short, not on the ground that _as observation_ it would be too shortsighted, and that one ought to have started out from the Idea rather than proceed empirically; for laws like that, if they were anything real, would in fact have to be actually on hand and therefore open to observation {{{ 203 }}}; but because the thought of laws of this sort turns out to possess no truth.

The relation that emerged for a law was this: that the universal organic _property_ turns itself into a thing within an organic _system_ and has in it its shaped imprint, so that the two would be the same essence, present now as a universal moment, now as a thing. But beyond that, the side of the inner is for itself likewise a relation among several sides, and so what first suggests itself is the thought of a law as a relation in which the universal organic activities, or properties, are referred to one another. Whether such a law is possible must be settled by the nature of a property of this kind. {{ 152 }} As a universal fluidity, however, it is, for one thing, not something bounded after the manner of a thing and holding itself within the difference of an existence that was supposed to make up its shape; sensibility instead passes out beyond the nervous system and on through all the other systems of the organism, — for another, it is a universal _moment_ that is essentially undivided and undetachable from reaction, or irritability, and from reproduction. For being reflection into itself, it simply carries reaction within it. Being merely reflected into itself is passivity, or dead being, not a sensibility, exactly as action, that being the same thing as reaction, fails to be irritability apart from a being-reflected-into-itself. It is reflection within action or reaction, {{{ 204 }}} together with action or reaction within reflection, that constitutes the very thing whose unity makes up the organic, a unity that means the same as organic reproduction. From this it follows that in every mode of actuality the same _magnitude_ of sensibility — if we look first at how sensibility and irritability stand to each other — must be on hand as of irritability, and that any organic appearance may be apprehended and determined, or explained if one prefers, just as much by the one as by the other. The very thing that one person takes, say, for high sensibility can with equal justice be viewed by another as high irritability, and as irritability at _the same level_. Once one calls them _factors_, and does not mean by that an empty word, just this much is thereby stated: that they are _moments_ of the concept, so that the real object whose essence this concept makes up carries them both alike, and that if the object is determined by the one as highly sensible, it is equally to be pronounced by the other as irritable to just that degree.

When they are drawn apart, as they must be, the distinction lies in the concept, and their opposition is _qualitative_. But if, over and above this genuine difference, they are further posited as differing in the mode of being, and for representation, as sides of the law might differ, then they show up in a _quantitative_ diversity. Their own qualitative {{{ 205 }}} opposition thus passes over into _magnitude_, and laws arise of the sort that sensibility and irritability, for instance, stand to each other inversely in respect of their magnitude, so that the growth of the one goes with the shrinking of the other; or, better, with magnitude itself taken straight off as the content, that something's magnitude grows as its smallness dwindles. — But give this law a determinate content, say in this fashion, that a hole's magnitude _grows_ the more what fills it _dwindles_, and the inverse ratio can just as readily be turned into a direct one and stated thus, that the hole's magnitude _grows_ in direct ratio to the amount removed; — a _tautological_ proposition, whether it be put as a direct or as an inverse ratio, one that in its own peculiar wording says nothing but this, that a magnitude grows as this magnitude grows. {{ 153 }} Just as the hole and what fills it and gets removed stand qualitatively opposed, while the real element in them, and the determinate magnitude of that element in both, is one and the same, and just as a growth in magnitude and a dwindling of smallness come to the same thing, so that their empty opposing of the two runs out into a tautology, so too the organic moments are equally undetachable, both in what is real in them and in the magnitude of that real element; the one dwindles only along with the other and grows only along with it, since either has any sense at all only so far as the {{{ 206 }}} other is on hand; — or rather, it makes no difference whether an organic appearance is taken as irritability or as sensibility, this quite generally to begin with, and no less when the talk turns to its magnitude. It makes just as little difference whether the growth of a hole is put as an enlarging of it as emptiness or as an enlarging of the fullness taken out of it. Or a number, _three_ for instance, stays just as large whether I take it as positive or as negative; and if I enlarge the three into four, then positive and negative alike have become four; — just as, in a magnet, the south pole has exactly the strength the north pole has, or as positive electricity, or an acid, has exactly the strength of its negative counterpart, or of the base upon which it acts. — A magnitude of that sort, like those three, or a magnet and so on, is an organic _existence_; it is what gets enlarged and lessened, and when it is enlarged, _both_ its factors are enlarged, just to the degree that _both_ poles of a magnet, or the two electricities, grow when a magnet and so on is strengthened. — That the two can just as little differ in respect of _intensity_ and _extension_, the one being unable to fall off in extension while growing instead in intensity, while the other conversely was to lessen its intensity and grow instead in extension, comes under the same concept of empty opposing; real intensity is quite as {{{ 207 }}} unqualifiedly great as extension, and the reverse.

As now becomes clear, what actually happens in this law-giving is that irritability and sensibility at first make up the determinate organic opposition; but this content slips away, and the opposition runs off into the formalities of magnitude's waxing and waning, or of differing intensity and extension; — an opposition that no longer touches the nature of sensibility and of irritability at all, and no longer gives them expression. Hence an empty game of law-giving like this is in no way tied to the organic moments; it can be played anywhere and with anything, and rests throughout on a want of acquaintance with the logical nature of these oppositions.

{{ 154 }} If finally reproduction, rather than sensibility and irritability, is set in relation to the one or the other of them, then the very occasion for this law-giving drops away; for reproduction does not stand to those moments in the opposition in which they stand to each other; and since this law-giving rests on that opposition, here too the semblance of its taking place drops away.

The law-giving we have just been examining takes the organism's differences in the sense of moments of its _concept_, and ought strictly to be an a priori law-giving. Yet {{{ 208 }}} essentially bound up with it is the thought that these differences have the sense of things _at hand_, and in any case a merely observing consciousness has to stick to nothing but their existence. Organic actuality does necessarily carry within it an opposition of the kind its concept states, one determinable as irritability and sensibility, and these two in turn do appear as different from reproduction. — That _externality_ in which the organic concept's moments come under consideration here is the inner's _own immediate_ externality, and not the _outer_ that counts as outer within the whole and is _shape_, the one to which the inner will later be considered in relation.

But take the opposition of the moments as it stands in existence, and sensibility, irritability, reproduction come down to the level of ordinary _properties_, universalities as mutually indifferent as specific gravity, color, hardness, and so on. In that sense one may well observe that one organic thing is more sensible, or more irritable, or of greater reproductive power than another; — likewise that the sensibility and so on of one thing differs in _kind_ from another's, that one responds to determinate stimuli otherwise than a second does, as a horse responds otherwise to oats than to hay, and a dog otherwise again to both, and so on, just as much as one may observe that a body is harder than {{{ 209 }}} some other, and so on. — Yet these sensuous properties, hardness, color, and so on, together with the appearances of receptivity to the stimulus of oats, of irritability toward burdens, or of how many young are borne and of what kind, once brought into relation with one another and compared, are essentially in conflict with any lawfulness. For their _sensuous being_ has its determinateness precisely in this, that they exist in complete indifference toward one another, and that they display nature's freedom released from the concept rather than the unity of a relation, that they display nature's unreasoning play back and forth on the ladder of contingent magnitude among the concept's moments rather than those moments themselves.

The _other_ side, that on which the organic concept's simple moments are set beside the moments of _formation_, would be the first to yield a law properly so called, one that would state the true _outer_ as an imprint of the _inner_. {{ 155 }} — Since those simple moments are pervading fluid properties, however, they do not possess in the organic thing any separated-out real expression such as what is called a single system of the shape. Or if the organism's abstract Idea finds its true expression in those three moments only because they are nothing fixed but merely moments belonging to the concept and to movement, then the organism as formation is by contrast not comprised within three such determinate systems {{{ 210 }}} as anatomy sets out separately. So far as systems of that sort are to be discovered in their actuality and legitimated by that discovery, it must also be recalled that anatomy exhibits not merely three such systems but a good many more. — And then, quite apart from this, the sensible _system_ must mean something altogether different from what goes by the name of _nervous system_, the irritable _system_ something other than what is called the _muscular system_, the reproductive _system_ something other than the _viscera_ of reproduction. Within the systems of _shape_ as such, what is apprehended of the organism is its abstract side of dead concrete existence; its moments, taken up in that way, are matter for anatomy and the cadaver, not for cognition and the living organism. As parts of that sort they have rather ceased _to be_, since they cease to be processes. Because the organism's _being_ is essentially universality, or reflection into its own self, the _being_ of its whole, and of its moments too, can have no subsistence in an anatomical system; the real expression, their externality, is rather on hand only as a movement that runs through the various parts of the formation, a movement in which whatever gets torn loose and fixed as a single system shows itself essentially to be a flowing moment, so that what may count as their reality is not the actuality anatomy comes upon, but only that actuality {{{ 211 }}} as process, wherein even the anatomical parts have any sense at all.

The upshot, then, is that the organic _inner_'s moments, taken by themselves, are incapable of furnishing sides of a law of being; for in such a law they would be predicated of an existence, held apart from one another, and it would have to be impossible to name any one of them indifferently in place of another; nor, when set on the one side, do they find their realization on the other in some fixed system; for this last is as little something possessing organic truth at all as it is what gives those inner moments expression. What is essential to the organic, since in itself it is the universal, is rather quite generally this: to have its moments in actuality just as universally, that is to say, as processes running throughout, and not to furnish in some isolated thing a picture of the universal.

{{ 156 }} In this way the _representation_ of a _law_ gets lost altogether where the organic is concerned. A law means to apprehend and state the opposition as sides at rest, and in them the determinateness that consists in how they relate to each other. The _inner_, the side to which the universality that appears belongs, and the _outer_, to which belong the parts of the shape at rest, were meant to constitute the mutually answering sides of the law, but held apart in this way they forfeit their organic sense; and just this is what the representation of law takes as its ground, {{{ 212 }}} that its two sides would have an indifferent subsistence that is for itself, and that the relation would be distributed over them as a doubled, mutually answering determinateness. Each side of the organic is rather, in its own self, this: to be simple universality with all determinations dissolved in it, and to be the movement of that dissolving.

What this law-giving amounts to will become fully clear from insight into how it differs from earlier forms. — For if we look back at the movement of perceiving, and at the understanding that within perceiving reflects into itself and so determines its object, then what this understanding has before it in its object is not the _relation_ of those abstract determinations, of the universal and the singular, of the essential and the external, but rather it is itself the transition, a transition that does not become objective for it. Here, by contrast, what is itself the _object_ is the organic unity, that is, precisely the relation of those opposites, and this relation is pure transition. In its simplicity this transition is immediately _universality_, and inasmuch as universality enters the difference whose relation is what the law is to state, the law's moments are objects for this consciousness _as universal_, and the law reads: the _outer_ is expression of the _inner_. Here the understanding has laid hold of _the thought_ of law itself, whereas before {{{ 213 }}} it merely sought laws in general, and law's moments floated before it as a determinate content, not as the thoughts of law. — As regards content, the laws to be won here are not to be laws that merely take purely _existent_ differences up quietly into the form of universality, but laws that carry, immediately in those differences, the concept's restlessness as well, and with it the necessity of the relation of the sides. Yet since this object, the organic unity, immediately unites infinite sublating, or the absolute negation of being, with being at rest, and since the moments are essentially nothing but _pure transition_, no such _existent_ sides emerge as a law requires.

{{ 157 }} To get such sides the understanding has to hold on to the other moment of the organic relationship, namely to the _being-reflected_ of organic existence into its own self. This being, however, is reflected into itself so completely that nothing is left to it by way of a determinateness over against another. _Immediate_ sensuous being is immediately at one with determinateness as such, and for that reason gives voice in itself to a qualitative difference, as blue does over against red, or the acidic over against the alkaline, and so on. Organic being that has returned into itself, by contrast, is entirely indifferent toward another; its existence is a simple universality, one refusing observation {{{ 214 }}} every lasting sensuous difference, or, what amounts to the same, showing its essential determinateness only as the _alternation of existent_ determinatenesses. The way, then, in which the difference gives itself expression as existent is just this, that it is an _indifferent_ difference, that is, expresses itself as _magnitude_. In this, however, the concept is wiped out and necessity has disappeared. — But the content and filling of this indifferent being, the alternation of sensuous determinations, gathered into the simplicity of one organic determination, then states at the same time that it precisely lacks that determinateness — the determinateness of an immediate property — and the qualitative falls into magnitude alone, as we saw above.

Granted, then, that the objective element apprehended as organic determinateness does carry the concept within itself, and is on that account something other than what stands before an understanding which behaves as merely perceiving when it apprehends the content of its laws, still that apprehending drops back wholly into the principle and the manner of the merely perceiving understanding, and for this reason: that what has been apprehended is pressed into service as moments of a _law_; for by this it acquires the character of a fixed determinateness, the shape of a property that is immediate, or of an appearance at rest, is drawn besides into the determining of magnitude, and the concept's nature is smothered. — The swapping of something merely perceived for {{{ 215 }}} what is reflected into itself, and of a determinateness that is merely sensuous for one that is organic, thus forfeits its value once more, and this because the understanding has not yet sublated law-giving.

To draw the comparison bearing on this swap by way of a few examples: what for perception is an animal with strong muscles gets determined, say, — as an animal organism possessing high irritability; or what for perception is a state of great weakness, — as a state of heightened sensibility, or, if one would rather, as an abnormal affection and indeed a potentiation of it (expressions that render the sensuous not into the concept but into a German-Latin patois). {{ 158 }} That the animal has strong muscles may equally be put by the understanding thus, that the animal is possessed of great _muscular force_, — as the great weakness is put as a slight _force_. Determination by irritability has this over determination as _force_, that the latter expresses the indeterminate reflection into itself while the former expresses the determinate one, for the muscle's _peculiar_ force is precisely irritability; — and it has this over determination as _strong muscles_, that, as already in force, reflection into itself is at the same time contained in it. Just as weakness, or slight force, that is, _organic passivity_, gets expressed by way of _sensibility_. But with this sensibility taken and pinned down in that way for itself, and further coupled with the {{{ 216 }}} determination of _magnitude_, and set as a greater or lesser sensibility against a greater or lesser irritability, each of them is dragged down entirely into the sensuous element and into the common form of a property, while what relates them is not the concept but on the contrary magnitude, into which the opposition now falls and turns into a thoughtless difference. If what was indeterminate in the expressions _force_ and _strength_ and _weakness_ has here been cleared away, what springs up now is a drifting about just as empty and indeterminate in the oppositions of a higher and a lower sensibility, of an irritability rising and falling in itself and against the other. Just as much as strength and weakness are wholly sensuous, thoughtless determinations, so too the greater or lesser sensibility, irritability, is a sensuous appearance thoughtlessly apprehended and just as thoughtlessly pronounced. The concept has not stepped into the place of those conceptless expressions; rather, strength and weakness have been filled out by a determination that, considered on its own, does rest on the concept and has the concept for its content, but that loses this origin and character entirely. — Owing, then, to the form of simplicity and of immediacy under which this content is turned into one side of a law, and owing to magnitude, which makes up the element of difference among determinations of this sort, the essence that originally was and was posited as concept keeps the {{{ 217 }}} manner of sensuous perceiving, and stays as remote from cognition as it was under determination by strength and weakness of force, or by immediate sensuous properties.

{{ 159 }} It still remains to consider _on its own account_ that which constitutes the _outer_ of the organic, and how within it the opposition of _its_ inner and outer is determined; just as the _inner_ of the whole was first considered in relation to its _own_ outer.

Considered for itself, the _outer_ is _formation_ at large, life's system as it articulates itself within the _element_ of _being_, and no less essentially the being of the organic essence _for an other_, — objective essence within its _being for itself_. — At first this _other_ appears as the organic essence's external inorganic nature. If the two of them are viewed with an eye to a law, then inorganic nature, as we saw above, cannot make up one side of a law over against the organic essence, since the latter is at once simply for itself and holds a universal and free relation toward it.

But determine the relationship of these two sides more closely on the organic shape itself, and the shape turns on the one side toward inorganic nature, while on the other side it is _for itself_ and is reflected into itself. What forms the middle joining life's _being for itself_ together with the _outer_ at large, or with _being-in-itself_, is the _actual_ organic essence. {{{ 218 }}} — The extreme of being-for-itself, however, is the inner as infinite One, which draws the very moments of the shape out of their subsistence and their connection with the outer back into itself, the contentless which gives itself its content in the shape and appears in it as its process. The organic has its absolute freedom within this extreme, as simple negativity or _pure singularity_, and by that freedom it is indifferent toward, and secured against, being for another and the determinateness of the shape's moments. This freedom is at once a freedom of the moments themselves, their possibility of appearing and being apprehended as _existing_ things, and just as they are freed from what is external, so within it they are freed from and indifferent toward one another as well, since this freedom's _simplicity_ is _being_, or their simple substance. One and the same life is this concept, or pure freedom, however manifold the play in which the shape, or being for another, may roam; this stream of life is indifferent to what sort of mills it drives. — The first thing to note now is that this concept is not to be apprehended here, as it was earlier in the consideration of the inner proper, in its form of _process_, or of the development of its moments, but rather in its form as _simple inner_, which makes up the purely universal side over against the _actual_ living essence, or as the _element_ of the _subsistence_ of the existent members of the shape; {{{ 219 }}} for it is the shape we are considering here, and in the shape the essence of life is the simplicity of subsistence. And then _being for another_, or the determinateness of the actual formation, once received into this simple universality that is its essence, is a determinateness equally simple, universal and nonsensuous, and can be none other than the one expressed as _number_. {{ 160 }} — It is the middle of the shape, tying indeterminate life to actual life, as simple as the one and as determinate as the other. Whatever in that first, in the _inner_, would be number, the outer would have to express after its own fashion as multiform actuality, mode of life, color, and so on, in general as the whole crowd of differences that unfold in appearance.

Compare the two sides of the organic whole — the one being the _inner_, the other the _outer_, each of them in turn having within itself an inner and an outer — with respect to the inner of each, and the inner of the first turned out to be the concept in the restlessness of _abstraction_, whereas the second has for its own inner the universality at rest, and in that the determinateness at rest as well, number. If the first, then, because in it the concept develops its moments, held out a deceptive promise of laws through the semblance of a necessity in their relation, the second renounces that at once, since number shows up as the determination of one side of its laws. {{{ 220 }}} Number, after all, is precisely the determinateness that is wholly quiescent, dead and indifferent, one in which all movement and relation has died out, and which has broken off the bridge to the living element of the drives, of the mode of life, and of the remaining sensuous existence.

Yet to consider the organic's _shape_ as such, and the inner as an inner belonging merely to the shape, is in truth to be considering the organic no longer. For the two sides that were to be brought into relation are merely posited as indifferent toward each other, and with that the reflection into itself which makes up the essence of the organic is sublated. What happens here is rather that the comparison attempted between inner and outer gets transferred onto inorganic nature; here the infinite concept is nothing but the _essence_ that lies hidden within, or falls outside into self-consciousness, and it no longer has the objective presence it had in the case of the organic. This relation of inner and outer is therefore still to be considered within its own proper sphere.

To begin with, that inner of the shape is _specific gravity_, the simple singularity belonging to an inorganic thing. As simple being it lends itself to observation quite as much as does the determinateness of number, the only one of which it is capable, or rather it is found by comparing observations, and in that way it appears to give the one side of the law {{{ 221 }}}. Shape, color, hardness, toughness, and an innumerable host of further properties would jointly make up the _outer_ side, and would have to express the inner's determinateness, number, so that each would have its counter-image in the other.

{{ 161 }} Since negativity is apprehended here not as the movement of the process but as a _pacified_ unity, or _simple being for itself_, it shows up rather as that by which the thing sets itself against the process, holding itself within itself and indifferent toward it. But since this simple being-for-itself is a quiescent indifference toward another, specific gravity comes on the scene as one _property beside_ others; and thereupon all necessary relation of it to this multiplicity, or all lawfulness, ceases. — As this simple inner, specific gravity carries no difference _in its own self_, or carries only the unessential one; for its _pure simplicity_ is precisely what sublates all essential differentiation. This unessential difference, _magnitude_, would therefore have to have its counter-image, or its _other_, on the other side, which is the multiplicity of properties, since only thereby is it a difference at all. If this multiplicity is itself gathered into the simplicity of the opposition and determined, say, as _cohesion_, so that cohesion is the _for-itself in otherness_ as specific gravity is _pure being-for-itself_, then this {{{ 222 }}} cohesion is in the first instance the determinateness posited purely in the concept over against that other determinateness, and the manner of law-giving would be the one gone through above where sensibility was related to irritability. — And then, further, cohesion, taken as the _concept_ of a being-for-itself in otherness, is only the _abstraction_ of the side standing opposite specific gravity, and as such it has no concrete existence. For being-for-itself in an otherness is the process wherein the inorganic would have to give expression to its being-for-itself in the form of a _self-preservation_ that would keep it from stepping out of the process as the moment of a product. But precisely this goes against its nature, which does not have purpose or universality in its own self. Its process is rather only the determinate way of relating in which its being-for-itself, its specific gravity, _sublates_ itself. But this determinate way of relating, in which its cohesion would consist according to cohesion's true concept, and the determinate magnitude belonging to its specific gravity, are concepts wholly indifferent to each other. If the kind of relating were left wholly out of account and one restricted oneself to the representation of magnitude, then this determination might perhaps be thought: that the greater specific weight, as a higher being-within-itself, would put up more resistance to entering the process than the lesser. But conversely, the freedom of being-for-itself corroborates itself only in the readiness with which it engages with everything and preserves itself amid {{{ 223 }}} this manifoldness. That intensity without extension of the relations is an abstraction void of content, for extension makes up the _existence_ of intensity. But the inorganic's self-preservation in its relation falls, as was recalled, outside the nature of that relation, since the inorganic does not carry within itself the principle of movement, or since its being is neither absolute negativity nor concept.

{{ 162 }} Take this other side of the inorganic, by contrast, not as process but as being at rest, and it is common cohesion, a sensuous property that is _simple_, one that has stepped over to one side against the moment of _otherness_ set free, a moment lying spread out in many indifferent properties and itself falling, like specific gravity, among them; the crowd of properties taken together then makes up the side other than it. In it, however, as in the others, _number_ is the sole determinateness, and number not merely fails to express a relation and transition of these properties into one another, but is essentially just this, to have no necessary relation and to present the annihilation of all lawfulness; for number is what expresses determinateness as something _unessential_. So that a series of bodies expressing difference as a difference in number among their specific gravities in no way runs parallel to a series of the difference of the other {{{ 224 }}} properties, even if, to make matters easier, only one of them or a few are taken. For in fact it could only be the whole bundle of them that would have to make up the other side in this parallel. For ordering this bundle within itself and combining it into a whole, the magnitude-determinatenesses of these various properties are on the one hand available to observation, but on the other hand their differences come in as qualitative ones. What in this heap would have to be designated as positive or negative and would cancel out reciprocally, in short the formula's inner configuration and exposition, which would be highly composite, would belong to the concept, and the concept is excluded precisely by the way in which the properties lie there and are to be taken up as _existent_; within this being none of them shows the character of a negative over against another, rather, the one _is_ every bit as much as the other, and neither does it indicate its place in the whole's ordering in any other way. — With a series running in parallel differences — the relation might be meant as rising on both sides at once, or as rising on the one and falling on the other — all that matters is the _final_ simple expression of this whole gathered together, which was to constitute the law's one side as against specific gravity; but that side, taken as an _existent result_, is nothing other than what has already {{{ 225 }}} been mentioned, namely a single property, such as common cohesion, alongside which the others, and among them specific gravity too, are indifferently present, and any other of them can with the same right, that is, with the same lack of right, be chosen as representative of the whole other side; the one as much as the other would only represent the essence, in plain German _stand in for it_, but would not be the matter at hand itself. {{ 163 }} So that any effort to hunt out series of bodies that would run along the simple parallel of two sides, and would state what the bodies essentially are according to a law of those sides, has to be taken for a thought ignorant both of its task and of the means by which the task was to be carried out.

Just now the relation of outer and inner, taken in the shape that ought to display itself for observation, was carried straightaway over into the inorganic sphere; what determination drags it there can now be stated more precisely, and from that a further form and relation of this relationship comes to light. For with the organic, whatever it is in the inorganic that appears to make inner and outer comparable in that way drops away altogether. In the inorganic the inner is something simple, an inner presenting itself to perception as an _existent_ property; what its determinateness comes to is {{{ 226 }}} therefore essentially magnitude, and as an existent property it shows itself indifferent to the outer, or to the many other sensuous properties. The being-for-itself of the organically living, however, does not stand off to one side against its outer in this way; rather, the principle of _otherness_ lies within it. Should we determine being-for-itself as _a simple relation to itself that maintains itself_, its otherness will be simple _negativity_, and the unity that is organic will be a unity of self-equal self-relating together with pure negativity. This unity, taken as unity, is what is inner in the organic; the inner is on this account universal in itself, or it is _genus_. But the genus is free over against its actuality in a different way than specific _gravity_ is free over against shape. The latter's freedom is an _existent_ freedom, or it amounts to gravity's stepping to one side as a particular property. Yet just because it is _existent_ freedom, it also amounts to no more than _One determinateness_, one belonging _essentially_ to this shape, or one through which this shape, _as essence_, is a determinate one. The genus, on the other hand, is free in a universal way, and is indifferent toward this shape or toward its actuality. In the organic, then, the _determinateness_ that the inorganic's _being-for-itself_ possesses _as such_ comes to stand _beneath its_ being-for-itself, whereas in the inorganic it comes to stand only beneath the latter's _being_; and so, even though in the inorganic it is at the same time no more than a _property_ {{{ 227 }}}, the dignity of _essence_ still falls to it, since as the simple negative it confronts existence, which is being for something else; and this simple negative, in its last singular determinateness, is a number. Being itself pure negativity, the organic is a singularity that wipes out within itself the fixed determinateness of number which attaches to _indifferent being_. Insofar as the organic does have about it the moment of indifferent being, and therein number, number can be taken only as a play upon it, not as the essence of its vitality.

{{ 164 }} Granted, now, that the principle of the process, pure negativity, does not lie outside the organic; granted too that the organic therefore does not carry it as a determinateness within its _essence_, singularity being rather universal in itself; still, in its moments, taken as themselves _abstract_ or _universal_, this pure singularity attains neither development nor actuality in the organic. Instead, this expression steps outside that universality which falls back into _inwardness_, positioning itself between actuality or shape, that is, singularity in the course of developing, and the organic universal, or the genus, the _determinate_ universal, the _species_. Nothing but the worked-out movement of a process, one that plays itself out upon the _parts of the existent shape_, makes up the concrete existence at which the negativity of the universal, that is, of the genus, arrives. Suppose the genus, as a simplicity at {{{ 228 }}} rest, had the differentiated parts within it, so that its _simple negativity_ would as such be at the same time movement, a movement running through parts that are just as simple and, immediately in them, universal, parts which as such moments would here be actual; then the organic genus would be consciousness. As it is, though, the _simple determinateness_, being the determinateness of the species, is there in the genus spiritlessly; actuality takes its start from that determinateness, or rather what enters actuality is not genus as such, that is, not thought at all. As something actually organic, the genus has only a representative to stand in for it. This representative, however, namely number, which seems to mark the transition out of the genus into individual formation and to hand observation the two sides of necessity, once as simple determinateness and the other time as the shape developed and born out into manifoldness, marks rather the indifference and mutual freedom of the universal and the singular, the singular being surrendered by the genus to the inessential difference of magnitude while, as something living, it proves just as free of that difference itself. True universality, in the sense determined above, is nothing here but _inner essence_; taken as _determinateness of the species_ it amounts to formal universality, and against that formal universality true universality moves over to the side of singularity, a singularity that is thereby living and that, by means of its _inner, overrides its {{{ 229 }}} determinateness as species_. Yet this singularity fails to be at once a universal individual as well, that is, one in which universality would have outer actuality too; that individual lies instead outside the organically living. This _universal_ individual, however, in the way it _immediately_ is the individual of the natural formations, is not consciousness itself; were it supposed to be consciousness, its existence as _a ***singular*** organic and living individual_ could not be allowed to fall outside it.

We therefore see a syllogism in which the one extreme is _universal life as universal_, or as genus, while the other extreme is _the same life as singular_, or as universal individual; the middle, however, is put together out of both, the first appearing to dispatch itself into the middle in the form of _determinate_ universality, or as _species_, the second in the form of _proper_, that is, singular _singularity_. {{ 165 }} — And since this syllogism belongs on the whole to the side of _formation_, what is distinguished as inorganic nature is comprised under it as well.

Now inasmuch as universal life, being _the simple essence of the genus_, develops for its own part the differences of the concept and must lay them out in a series of simple determinatenesses, that series constitutes a system of differences posited as indifferent, or _a numerical series_. If earlier the organic, under the form of singularity, was placed over against this inessential difference, a difference that neither gives voice to nor holds within it their {{{ 230 }}} living nature — and if the very same must be said of the inorganic, with regard to its whole existence as developed in the multitude of its properties, — then it is by now the universal individual that has to be regarded not simply as free of each articulation of the genus, but as the genus's power as well. The genus, which breaks itself up into species by the _universal determinateness_ of number, or which may equally take singular determinatenesses of its existence, for example figure, color, and so on, as the ground of its division, suffers violence in this quiet business at the hands of the universal individual, that is, of _the earth_, which, being universal negativity, brings to bear against the genus's systematizing those differences that it has in itself, differences whose nature, on account of the substance they belong to, is other than the nature of the genus's own. This doing of the genus becomes a wholly restricted business, one it is permitted to carry on only within those mighty elements, and one that their unbridled violence interrupts on all sides, leaving it full of gaps and stunted.

The upshot is that in shaped existence what can come to observation is reason only _as life in general_, a life which, however, in its differentiating does not actually have in its own self any rational seriation or articulation, and which is no system of shapes with a ground in itself. — Were {{{ 231 }}} the middle in the syllogism of organic formation, the middle into which the species and its actuality as singular individuality fall, to have within it both extremes, inner universality and universal individuality, then this middle would carry in _the movement_ of its actuality universality's expression and nature, and would be the development that systematizes itself. _Consciousness_, in this way, standing between universal spirit and its own singularity or sensuous consciousness, has for its middle the system of the formations of consciousness, spirit's life as it orders itself into a whole, — the very system under consideration here, which possesses its objective existence as world history. {{ 166 }} Organic nature, though, has no history at all; from its universal, life, it sinks straight down into the singularity of existence, and the moments united in this actuality, simple determinateness and singular vitality, bring becoming forth only as the contingent movement wherein each is at work on its own portion while the whole is maintained; but this stirring is, _for itself_, held within its own point alone, since the whole is not there in it, and the whole is not there in it because here it does not exist as a whole _for itself_.

So, over and above the fact that observing reason in organic nature comes only to the intuition of itself as universal life in general, {{{ 232 }}} the intuition of that life's development and realization is granted to it only according to systems distinguished in a quite general way, systems whose determination has its essence not in what is organic as such, but rather in the universal individual; and _within_ these differences of the earth, according to seriations that the genus attempts.

Since, then, the _universality of organic life_ in its actuality lets itself sink straight into the extreme _of singularity_, dispensing with the genuine mediation that is for itself, observing consciousness has nothing before it but _meaning_ as a thing; and should reason be able to muster the idle interest of observing this meaning, it is confined to describing and rehearsing nature's opinions and stray notions; this spiritless freedom of meaning will, to be sure, everywhere hold out rudiments of laws, traces of necessity, hints at order and seriation, witty and plausible-looking relations. But where the organic is related to the existent differences of the inorganic, that is, the elements, the zones and the climates, observation, so far as law and necessity go, gets no further than _great influence_. The same holds on the other side, where individuality carries not the earth's significance but that of the _One immanent_ in organic life, an One that, united immediately with the universal, does indeed make up the genus {{{ 233 }}}, though its simple unity determines itself for that very reason only as number and hence leaves qualitative appearance free, — observation cannot get beyond _polite remarks, interesting relations, an obliging friendliness toward the concept_. But polite _remarks_ are no _knowledge of necessity_, the _interesting_ relations get no further than the _interest_, and interest is as yet nothing more than opinion about reason; and the _friendliness_ of the individual, the friendliness with which it alludes to a concept, is a childlike friendliness that turns childish once it wants, or is meant, to have validity in and for itself.
