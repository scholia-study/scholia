## {{{ 22 }}} I. Sense-Certainty; or the This and Meaning

{{ 63 }} Whatever knowledge comes first, that is, whatever is our object immediately, can only be a knowledge that is itself immediate: _knowledge_ of the _immediate_, of _what has being_. Our own bearing has to be equally _immediate_ or _receptive_: we are to alter nothing in it as it offers itself, and to keep comprehension away from our apprehending.

The concrete content of _sense-certainty_ immediately makes it look like the _richest_ cognition, indeed like a cognition of infinite wealth — a wealth for which no boundary can be found, whether we go _out_ into the space and time in which it spreads itself, or whether we take a piece from this abundance and _go into_ it by dividing it. Besides this, it appears as the _truest_, for it has so far discarded nothing of the object, but has it before itself in its full completeness. Yet this _certainty_ in fact puts itself forward as the {{{ 23 }}} most abstract and poorest _truth_. About what it knows, its whole statement runs: it _is_; the _being_ of the matter is all that its truth holds; consciousness, on its side, figures in this certainty as nothing but a pure _I_, and _I_ am there as nothing but a pure _this one_, the object as nothing but a pure _this_. I, _this one_, am _certain_ of _this_ matter, and not because _I_ as consciousness developed myself in the process and set thought moving in manifold ways. Nor because _the matter_ of which I am certain would have, in virtue of a host of differing characteristics, a rich relation within itself, or a manifold bearing upon others. Both of those points fall outside the truth of sense-certainty; within it, the significance of a manifold mediation attaches neither to I nor to the matter; to I there attaches no significance of a manifold representing or thinking, nor to the matter any significance of manifold characteristics; the matter simply _is_, and it _is_ for no other reason than that it _is_; it _is_, and precisely this is the essential thing for sensuous knowledge, while its _truth_ is constituted by this pure _being_, by this simple immediacy. In the same way, certainty taken as _immediate relation_ is pure relation: consciousness is _I_ and nothing further, a pure _this one_; the _singular_ one knows what is a pure this, or _the singular_.

{{ 64 }} Yet a good deal else plays alongside the _pure being_ that makes up this certainty's essence and that it announces as its truth, {{{ 24 }}} as we can see if we watch. A sense-certainty that is actual is not this pure immediacy alone, but rather an _example_ of it. Among the countless differences that turn up here we find everywhere the one main disparity: namely that in sense-certainty there drop out of pure being, right away, the two _Thises_ already named, a _This one_ as _I_ and a _This as object_. Let _us_ reflect on this difference, and what emerges is that the one no more than the other stands in sense-certainty _immediately_ alone: each stands there at the same time as something _mediated_; I possess the certainty _through_ another, namely the matter, and the matter is in the certainty just as much _through_ another, that is, through I.

This difference between essence and example, between immediacy and mediation, is not one that we alone draw; sense-certainty exhibits it in its own right, and what must be taken up is the difference in the shape it wears there, not in the shape we have just now given it. Within it, one side gets posited as the simple, immediately existing side, that is, as the essence, namely _the object_; the other side gets posited as the unessential and mediated one, which within it is not _in itself_ but only through another, namely I, _a knowledge_ whose knowing of the object rests solely on the fact that _it_ is, and which is able to be, or equally not to be. The object, for its part, _is_, the true and the essence; it _is_, and whether it gets known or not makes no difference; it stays even when it goes unknown; knowledge, {{{ 25 }}} on the other hand, is not, unless the object is.

The object, then, calls for examination as to whether it really is, within sense-certainty itself, the essence sense-certainty passes it off as, and whether this concept of it, that of being essence, squares with the way it is on hand there. To that end our task is not to reflect on the object and ponder what it might in truth be, but merely to look at it as sense-certainty has it within itself.

_It_ is therefore itself the one we must question: _What is the This?_ Suppose we take it in the doubled shape that its being wears, as the _Now_ and as the _Here_; then the dialectic it carries within itself will assume a form no less intelligible than the This itself is. And so, asked _what is the Now?_, we answer, say: _The Now is the night_. To put the truth of this sense-certainty to the test, one simple experiment is enough. So we set this truth down in writing; writing a truth out cannot cost it anything, and neither can our storing it away. Looking at the written-down truth again _Now, this midday_, we will find ourselves obliged to grant that it has gone stale.

{{ 65 }} The Now that is night gets _preserved_, that is, gets handled as the thing it was passed off as, as something _that has being_; and yet what it turns out to be is something without being. The _Now_ {{{ 26 }}} itself does maintain itself, yet as one that is not night; equally, over against the day which it now is, it maintains itself as one that is not day either, or as something _negative_ in general. This self-maintaining Now is therefore not an immediate one but a mediated one, for as something that endures and maintains itself it is determined _by this_, that another, namely day and night, is not. Through all this it remains just as simple as before, _Now_, and in that simplicity indifferent toward whatever else keeps playing about it; night and day are not what its being consists in, and just as much it is also day and night; this otherness of its own leaves it wholly unaffected. A simple item of that kind, one which is by way of negation, which is not this and not that, a _not-this_, and which is likewise indifferent as to being this or that, we call a _universal_; the universal, then, is what in fact holds as the true in sense-certainty.

The sensuous, too, is something we _utter_ as a universal; what we say is _This_, meaning the _universal This_; or _it is_, meaning _being in general_. Admittedly we do not _represent_ the universal This to ourselves in doing so, or being in general, and yet the universal is what we _utter_; or rather, we simply do not speak in the way we _mean_ things in this sense-certainty. Language, however, as we see, is the truer of the two; in it we ourselves refute our own _opinion_ straight off, and since the universal is what is true in sense-certainty, and {{{ 27 }}} language expresses only this truth, it is not at all possible for us ever to say a sensuous being that we _mean_.

Matters will stand the same way with the This in its other form, with _the Here_. _The Here_ is, for example, the _tree_. I turn myself around, and this truth has vanished, having inverted itself into its opposite: _the Here is no tree at all_, but rather _a house_. What does not vanish is the _Here_ itself; rather _it is_ what abides through the vanishing of house, tree, and so on, indifferent as to being house or tree. The _This_, then, shows itself once more to be _mediated simplicity_, or _universality_.

For this sense-certainty, which on its own shows the universal to be its object's truth, what stays behind as its essence is thus _pure being_, though not immediate being, but rather a being for which negation and mediation are indispensable; hence not the being that we _mean by being_, but _being_ carrying the _determination_ that it is abstraction, the purely universal; and what alone remains standing over against this empty, indifferent Now and Here is _our opinion_, for which the true in sense-certainty is not the universal.

{{ 66 }} Set the relationship in which _knowledge_ and the _object_ first stepped forward beside the relationship the two occupy in this result, {{{ 28 }}} and the relationship has inverted itself. What was to have been the essential side, the object, is now the unessential side of sense-certainty, for the universal that it has become is no longer of the kind in which the object was to be essential for that certainty; sense-certainty is instead on hand now in the opposite one, in the knowledge that formerly ranked as the unessential one. Its truth resides in the object taken as _my_ object, that is, in _meaning_: the object is because _I_ know of it. Sense-certainty has indeed been driven out of the object, yet not thereby sublated, only forced back into the I; what experience has to show about this reality of its own is what we now have to see.

So the force of its truth now rests with the _I_, with how immediately my _seeing, hearing_, and the rest go on; what stops the vanishing of the singular Now and Here we mean is that _I_ hold them fast. _The Now is day_, and this because I see it; _the Here a tree_, for that same reason. In this relationship, though, sense-certainty undergoes the same dialectic on itself as it did in the earlier one. _I, this one_, see the tree, and _I assert of the tree that it is the Here_; but the house is what _another I_ sees, and his assertion is that the Here is no tree but a house. Both truths carry the same credentials, namely the immediacy of seeing, together with the assuredness and assurance {{{ 29 }}} of both parties about their knowledge; but the one vanishes in the other.

What survives all this is _I_ as something _universal_, and its seeing is not the seeing of a tree, nor the seeing of this house, but a simple seeing, one that the negation of this house and the rest has mediated, and in that very mediation just as simple, just as indifferent toward whatever else plays about it, toward the house, toward the tree. I is nothing but a universal, on a par with _Now, Here, or This_ in general; true enough, what I mean is a _singular I_, but saying what I mean is beyond me with Now and Here, and with I it is no less beyond me. In saying _this Here, Now_ or a _singular_, what I say is _***all*** thises, all Heres, Nows, singulars_; and in the same way, in saying _I, this singular_ I, my utterance is quite generally _***all***_ I's; each one is what I say, namely _I, this one, this singular one_. Where science has this demand laid before it as the touchstone it could simply never survive, the demand that a so-called _this thing_ or _a this human being_ be deduced, constructed, found à priori, or however one cares to put it, it is only fair that the demand itself _say_ what _this_ thing, or what _this_ I, it means; but that is impossible to say.

What sense-certainty learns by experience, then, is that its essence lies in the object as little as in the I, and that immediacy belongs to the one as little as to the other, since in both of them what I mean amounts rather to something unessential, {{{ 30 }}} while object and I alike are universals, and the Now, the Here, the I that I mean keep no subsistence within them, nor _are_ they at all. {{ 67 }} This brings us to positing sense-certainty in its _whole_ extent as its own _essence_, rather than merely one moment of it, which is what happened in the two previous cases, where the reality of sense-certainty was supposed to lie first in the object standing opposed to the I, and then in the I itself. It is therefore only the _whole_ of sense-certainty itself that holds fast within itself to _immediacy_, and thereby shuts out from itself all the opposition that occurred previously.

None of this concerns that pure immediacy any longer: not the Here's otherness as tree, an otherness that goes over into a Here which is not-tree; not the Now's otherness as day, going over into a Now which is night; nor any other I with something else for its object. Its truth keeps itself as a relation that stays equal to itself, one that makes no distinction of essentiality and unessentiality where the I and the object are concerned, and which therefore admits no difference at all into itself. So I, this one, assert the Here as tree, and I do not turn around, which would turn the Here into a not-tree for me; nor do I pay any heed when some other I sees the Here as not-tree, or when I myself, on some other occasion, take the Here for not-tree and the Now for not-day; instead I am pure intuiting; {{{ 31 }}} for my own part I stick to it that the Now is day, or else to this, that the Here is tree; and I do not set the Here and the Now side by side with each other either, but hold to _one single_ immediate relation: the Now is day.

Since this certainty now refuses to step forward whenever we point it toward a Now that happens to be night, or toward an I for whom it is night, we go over to it ourselves and have it point out to us the Now that it asserts. We have to let it _point_ the thing out, since what is true in this immediate relation is what is true of _this_ I here, the one who confines himself to a single _Now_ or a single _Here_. Should we take this truth up only _afterwards_, or place ourselves at a _distance_ from it, then it would carry no significance whatever, since we would then be sublating an immediacy that belongs to it essentially. We have to step, therefore, into the very same point of time or of space, have the truth pointed out to us, that is, let ourselves be turned into that same this I, the one that does the knowing with certainty. So let us see how the immediate is constituted that gets pointed out to us.

The _Now_ gets pointed out; _this Now. Now;_ by the time it is pointed out it has already stopped being; the _Now_ which _is_ differs from the one pointed out, and we see this to be just what the Now amounts to: in being, to be already no more. As pointed out to us, the Now is something _that has been_; therein lies its truth; the truth of being is not what it has. Even so, this much holds true, that it has {{{ 32 }}} been. Yet whatever _has been_ is indeed _no essence at all; it ***is*** not_, and being was the point at issue.

{{ 68 }} What we see in this pointing-out is thus nothing but a movement, running its course as follows: 1) I point the Now out; it stands asserted as the true; but I point it out as something that has been, as something sublated, and so I sublate the first truth; and 2) now, as the second truth, my assertion is that it _has been_, that it stands sublated. 3) But what has been has no being; the second truth, its having-been or its being-sublated, I now sublate in turn, negating thereby the Now's negation, and so coming back to the first assertion: that _Now_ is. The Now, together with the pointing-out of it, is accordingly so constituted that an immediate simple is what neither of them is; each is instead a movement with various moments in it: _this_ gets posited, but what gets posited is rather _another_, or the this gets sublated; and that _otherness_, that sublating of the first, is itself _sublated once more_, whereby a return to the first comes about. But this first, once reflected into itself, is not quite what it was at the outset, namely something _immediate_; it is precisely _what stands reflected into itself_, or a _simple_ that stays what it is throughout an otherness: a Now that amounts to absolutely many Nows; and there we have the true Now, the Now as a single day holding many Nows within it, hours; and such a Now, an hour, is likewise so many minutes, and these Nows {{{ 33 }}} in turn many more Nows, and so on. — So the _pointing-out_ is itself the movement that expresses what the Now in truth is: a result, that is, a plurality of Nows gathered into one; and the pointing-out is the experiencing of the Now as something _universal_.

The _Here that gets pointed out_, the one I hold fast, is in the same way a _this_ Here which in truth turns out to be _not this_ Here, but a front and a back, an above and a below, a right and a left. And the above, taken by itself, is in the same way this manifold otherness within above, below, and so on. The Here that was to be pointed out disappears into other Heres, and these disappear in their turn; what gets pointed out, held fast, and left standing is a _negative This_, one that only _is_ on condition that the _Heres_ are taken in the way they are meant to be taken, only to sublate themselves in that very taking; it is a simple complexion of many Heres. The point would be the Here that is meant; but the point _is_ not; rather, when it gets pointed out as something with being, the pointing-out proves to be no immediate knowledge at all, but a movement that runs from the meant Here by way of many Heres to the universal Here, and just as the day is one simple plurality of Nows, so this universal Here is one simple plurality of Heres.

Clearly the dialectic of sense-certainty amounts to nothing beyond the simple history of its own movement, its own experience, and sense-certainty itself amounts to nothing beyond just this history. {{ 69 }} That is also why natural consciousness {{{ 34 }}} keeps advancing of its own accord to this result, to what in sense-certainty is the true, and undergoes the experience of it; only it just as constantly forgets it again and starts the movement over from the beginning. It must therefore be a matter for astonishment that, against this experience, the claim is set up, as universal experience, as a philosophical assertion too, and even as a result of skepticism, that external things, in their reality or their being as _these_, as sensuous things, possess absolute truth for consciousness; an assertion of that kind has no idea what it is saying, no idea that it says the reverse of what it intends. For consciousness, the truth of the sensuous _This_ is supposed to amount to universal experience; but universal experience is rather the opposite; every consciousness itself sublates such a truth again, for instance _the Here is a tree_ or _the Now is midday_, and pronounces the contrary: the Here is _not_ a tree, it is _rather_ a house; and whatever in this assertion, the one sublating the first, is once again just such an assertion about a sensuous This, that too it goes on sublating in the same way; so that throughout sense-certainty what really gets experienced is nothing but what we have seen, namely _this_ as a _universal_, the contrary of what that assertion assures us is universal experience. — Given this appeal to universal experience, it may be permissible to anticipate a regard for the practical side. {{{ 35 }}} With the practical in view, those who assert that truth and that certainty about the reality of sensuous objects can be told that they belong back in the lowest class of the school of wisdom, the old Eleusinian mysteries of Ceres and of Bacchus, where the secret of eating bread and of drinking wine still awaits their learning; for anyone initiated into those secrets reaches not merely doubt about the being of sensuous things but despair over it, and in dealing with them he partly accomplishes their nullity himself, partly watches it accomplished. The animals are not excluded from this wisdom either; on the contrary, they show themselves initiated into it most deeply of all, for they do not stop short before sensuous things as things that are in themselves; despairing of that reality and wholly certain of its nullity, they help themselves without further ado and devour them; and the whole of nature, along with them, celebrates these revealed mysteries, which teach what the truth of sensuous things is.

Those, however, who set up such an assertion also say, in keeping with the remarks just made, the immediate opposite of what they mean; and this appearance is perhaps the one best able to provoke reflection on the nature of sense-certainty. What they talk about is the existence of _external_ objects, determinable still more precisely as _actual_ things that are absolutely _singular, entirely personal_, {{{ 36 }}} _individual_, no one of which has anything absolutely like it any longer; and this existence, they hold, carries absolute certainty and truth. {{ 70 }} What they mean is _this_ piece of paper, the one I am writing _this_ on, or rather have written on already; but what they mean, they do not say. Suppose they really did want to _say_ this piece of paper that they mean, and suppose the wanting to _say_ it were serious: it cannot be done, since the sensuous This that is meant lies _beyond the reach_ of language, and language belongs to consciousness, to what is in itself universal. The paper would therefore molder away under any actual attempt to say it; those who had started its description could not bring it to completion, but would have to hand it over to others, who would in the end have to concede of their own accord that they were speaking of a thing which _is_ not. No doubt, then, what they mean is _this_ piece of paper, which here is quite another one than the paper above; but what they speak of are actual _things, external ones_ or _sensuous objects, absolutely singular_ essences, and so on, which is to say that of these they utter only the _universal_; and hence whatever gets called the unsayable is nothing else than what is untrue, irrational, merely meant. — Where of something nothing more gets said than this, that it is _an actual thing_, that it is an _external object_, then it stands uttered merely as what is most universal of all, and thereby its _equality_ with everything is what gets expressed rather than its differentiatedness. If I say a _singular thing_, I say it just as much as something wholly _universal_, for all {{{ 37 }}} things are a singular thing; and in the same way _this_ thing is anything one likes. Designated with more precision, as _this piece of paper_, it makes no difference: _all_ paper, _every_ sheet of it, is _a this_ piece of paper, so that all along I have said nothing but the universal. But suppose I try to come to the aid of speech, which has the divine nature of inverting what is meant on the spot, of turning it into something else, and so of never letting it _reach the word_ at all, by _pointing out_ this piece of paper: then I undergo an experience of what in fact the truth of sense-certainty is; I point the paper out as a _Here_ that is a Here belonging to other Heres, or, taken on its own, a _simple togetherness_ of many _Heres_, that is, a universal; so I take it up as it truly is, and rather than knowing something immediate, _I perceive_.
