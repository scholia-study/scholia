## c. Conscience, the Beautiful Soul, Evil and its Forgiveness

The antinomy of the moral worldview, namely, that moral consciousness both exists and does not exist, — or that duty's validity is a beyond of consciousness while conversely occurring nowhere but within it, had been condensed into the representation in which non-moral consciousness is supposed to be valid as moral, its contingent knowledge and willing to be accepted as fully weighty, and happiness to be allotted to it out of grace. {{ 341 }} Moral self-consciousness did not take this self-contradicting representation upon itself, but displaced it into an essence other than itself. But when it posits beyond itself what it is compelled to think as necessary, this too is contradiction, a contradiction of form, just as the earlier one was a contradiction of {{{ 582 }}} content. Yet since in itself the very thing that appears to be contradictory, the thing in whose separation and re-dissolution the moral worldview keeps knocking about, is one and the same, namely pure duty, which as _pure knowledge_ amounts to nothing but the _self_ of consciousness, while that self amounts to _being_ and _actuality_; — and since, in the same way, whatever is supposed to have its place beyond _actual_ consciousness amounts to nothing but pure thinking and thus really to the self: therefore self-consciousness, _for us_ or _in itself_, withdraws into itself and recognizes as its own self that essence in which _pure knowledge_ and _pure duty_ are at the same time what is _actual_. For itself it is that which, in its contingency, possesses full validity, that which knows its immediate singularity to be pure knowledge and acting, to be the true actuality and harmony.

This _self of conscience_, spirit that is immediately certain of itself as the absolute truth and as being, is the _third self_, the one that the third world of spirit has yielded for us, and a brief comparison with the preceding selves is in order. The totality, or actuality, that exhibits itself as the ethical world's truth is the self of the _person;_ that self's existence is _being-recognized_. Since the person is a self drained of substance, this its existence is equally an abstract actuality; the person _is valid_, and immediately so; the self is a point at rest immediately within the element of its being; nothing severs it from its universality, so that the two neither {{{ 583 }}} move nor stand in relation to each other, the universal within it lacks all differentiation, forming no content for the self, while the self likewise receives no fulfillment from itself. — The _second self_ is the culture-world that has arrived at its truth, or the spirit of splitting-in-two restored to itself — absolute freedom. It is in this self that the first immediate unity of singularity and universality breaks apart; the universal, which equally persists as purely spiritual essence, as being-recognized, or as universal willing and knowledge, is the _object_, is the content belonging to the self, and is the self's universal actuality. Yet this universal lacks the form of an existence set free from the self; hence within this self it arrives at no fulfillment, at no positive content, at no world. Moral self-consciousness does, to be sure, let its universality go free, so that the universality turns into a nature in its own right, while equally holding it fast within itself as something sublated. But this is merely the dissembling play of alternation between these two determinations. {{ 342 }} Only as conscience does it possess, in its _self-certainty_, the _content_ for what was hitherto empty duty, and likewise for empty right and the empty universal will; and since this self-certainty is equally what is _immediate_, it is existence itself.

Once it has reached this its truth, moral self-consciousness accordingly leaves behind, or rather sublates within itself, the division from which dissemblance sprang: the division between the _in-itself_ and the _self_, between pure duty as the pure _purpose_ {{{ 584 }}} and _actuality_ as a nature and sensuousness set over against that pure purpose. Having in this way gone back into itself, it is _concrete_ moral spirit, which does not, in the consciousness of pure duty, furnish itself with an empty standard that would stand over against actual consciousness; instead, both pure duty and the nature set against it are sublated moments; in immediate unity it is a _self-actualizing moral_ essence, and the action is immediately a moral shape that is _concrete_.

A case calling for action lies before us; for the knowing consciousness this case is an actuality of an objective kind. This consciousness, as conscience, knows the case in an immediate concrete manner, and the case at the same time is only as consciousness knows it. Knowledge counts as contingent to the extent that it differs from the object; but spirit certain of itself no longer performs such contingent knowing, no longer fabricates thoughts inside itself from which actuality would diverge; rather, once the division between the _in-itself_ and the _self_ has been sublated, the case stands immediately within the sensuous _certainty_ of knowledge just as it stands _in itself_, and it stands _in itself_ in no other way than as it stands within this knowledge. — On this basis, action understood as actualization is the will's pure form: the bare reversal whereby actuality as a case that merely _is_ turns into an actuality that has been _done_, and the bare mode of _objective_ knowledge turns into the mode of knowing {{{ 585 }}} _actuality_ as something consciousness has produced. Sense-certainty gets taken up into spirit's in-itself immediately, or rather gets reversed into it, and this reversal too is simple and unmediated, a passage through the pure concept that alters nothing in the content, which is determined by the interest of the consciousness knowing it. — Conscience, moreover, does not parcel out the circumstances of the case among distinct duties. It does not conduct itself as a _positive universal medium_ within which the many duties would each on its own retain an immovable substantiality, with the result _either_ that acting would be impossible altogether, since every concrete case harbors opposition as such, and as a moral case harbors the opposition of duties, so that in settling how to act one side, one duty, would invariably be _violated_; — _or_ that, once action does occur, one of the opposed duties really does suffer violation. {{ 343 }} Conscience is instead that negative One, the absolute self which wipes out these various moral substances; its acting in accordance with duty is simple, fulfilling no particular duty but rather knowing and doing what is concretely right. With conscience, then, moral _acting_ as acting emerges for the first time, and it is into this that the deedless consciousness of morality preceding it has passed over. — A differentiating consciousness may take the deed's concrete shape apart into various properties, that is, here {{{ 586 }}} into various moral relations, and may then declare each of them absolutely valid, as it must if each is to be a duty, or else may set them side by side and test them. Within conscience's simple moral action the duties lie so thoroughly buried that every one of these singular essences immediately suffers _damage_, and the testing that shakes at duty finds no place at all in conscience's unwavering certainty.

Equally absent from conscience is that oscillating uncertainty of a consciousness which at one moment locates so-called pure morality outside itself, in another holy essence, and takes itself to be the unholy one, while at the next moment it locates moral purity within itself and assigns the linking of the sensuous to the moral to that other essence.

It renounces all these posturings and dissemblings of the moral worldview by renouncing the consciousness that grasps duty and actuality as contradictory. According to the latter, I act morally insofar as I am _conscious_ of accomplishing only pure duty and not _anything else_, which in fact means: insofar as I _do not_ act. When I really do act, however, awareness of an _other_ accompanies me, of an _actuality_ already at hand and of one I intend to bring forth, and I hold a _determinate_ purpose and discharge a _determinate_ duty; {{{ 587 }}} something _other_ than the pure duty that alone was to be intended is present in it. — Conscience, by contrast, is precisely the consciousness that, when moral consciousness pronounces that the essence of its acting is _pure duty_, this pure purpose dissembles the matter; for the matter itself is this: that pure duty has its standing in pure thinking's empty abstraction and acquires reality and content only on some determinate actuality, an actuality which is the actuality of consciousness itself, consciousness taken not as a thing of thought but as a singular. _For its own part_, conscience possesses its truth in the _immediate certainty_ it has of itself. The essence is this concrete certainty of itself, certainty that is _immediate_; viewed according to the opposition of consciousness, one's own immediate _singularity_ makes up the content of moral doing, while the _form_ of that doing is just this self as pure movement, namely as _knowledge_ or as _one's own conviction_.

{{ 344 }} Looked at more closely in its unity and in what the moments signify: moral consciousness grasped itself merely as the _in-itself_ or _essence_, whereas as conscience it grasps its own _self_, its being-_for-itself_. — The moral worldview's contradiction _dissolves_; that is to say, the difference underlying it turns out to be no difference, and it collapses into pure negativity; and pure negativity is nothing but the _self_, a simple _self_ {{{ 588 }}} that is _pure_ knowledge just as much as it is knowledge of itself as _this singular_ consciousness. Hence what fills the previously empty essence is this self, since the self is what is _actual_, and being actual no longer signifies being a nature foreign to the essence, self-subsistent in laws of its own. Being the negative, it is the pure essence's _difference_, a content, and indeed one valid in and for itself.

Further, since this self is pure knowledge equal to itself, it is the _simply universal_, so that _duty_ is precisely this knowledge taken _as its own_ knowledge, as conviction. Duty has ceased to be the universal that confronts the self; it is known to possess no validity in such separatedness; the law now subsists for the self's sake, and not the reverse, the self existing for the law's sake. But for that very reason law and duty have not only the significance of _being-for-itself_ but also of _being-in-itself_, for this knowledge, by virtue of its self-equality, is precisely the _in-itself_. This _in-itself_ also separates itself, within consciousness, from that immediate unity with being-for-itself; stepping over against it in this way, it is _being, being for another_. — Duty, precisely as duty deserted by the self, now comes to be known as a mere _moment_; from signifying _absolute essence_ it has sunk to a being that is not self, not _for itself_, and so to _being for another_. Yet just for that reason this _being for another_ stays an essential moment, since it is the self as consciousness that makes up the opposition between being-for-itself and {{{ 589 }}} being for another, and since duty now has in it something immediately _actual_ and is no longer merely abstract pure consciousness.

This _being for another_, accordingly, is substance which has being _in itself_ and is differentiated from the self. Pure duty, or the _abstract in-itself_, has not been surrendered by conscience; on the contrary, it is the essential moment of relating oneself to others as _universality_. It constitutes what self-consciousnesses share in common, and this shared element is the substance wherein the deed obtains _subsistence_ and _actuality_; the moment of _being recognized_ by the others. This moment of being-recognized, of a _pure consciousness_ that _is there_, is lacking to moral self-consciousness, which for that reason neither acts at all nor actualizes anything. {{ 345 }} Its _in-itself_ is for it either the abstract _non-actual_ essence or _being_ as an _actuality_ that is not spiritual. The _existing actuality_ belonging to conscience, however, is one that is _self_, that is, existence aware of itself, the spiritual element in which recognition takes place. Doing therefore amounts merely to carrying its _singular_ content over into the _objective_ element, where that content is universal and recognized, and it is just this being recognized that turns the action into actuality. What makes the action recognized and thereby actual is that existing actuality ties immediately into conviction or knowledge, or that the knowledge one has of one's purpose is itself, immediately, the element of existence, universal recognizing. For {{{ 590 }}} duty, the action's _essence_, has its standing in the _conviction_ conscience holds regarding it; that conviction is precisely the _in-itself_ itself; it is self-_consciousness_ that is _universal in itself_, or _being-recognized_, and with that, actuality. Whatever gets done out of the conviction of duty is thus, immediately, something possessing subsistence and existence. There is thus no longer any question here of the good intention failing to come about, or of things going badly for the good person; rather, what is known as duty carries itself out and comes to actuality, because what is dutiful is precisely the universal of all self-consciousnesses, what is recognized and hence what has being. Taken separately and on its own, though, with the self's content left out, this duty is _being for another_, the transparent, which has only the significance of an essentiality wholly devoid of content.

If we look back to the sphere with which _spiritual reality_ first entered at all, the concept there was that the expressing of individuality is what _is in and for itself_. But the shape giving that concept immediate expression was the _honest consciousness_, which knocked about with the _abstract matter at hand_. There this _matter at hand_ was a _predicate;_ only in conscience does it become _subject_, a subject that has posited within itself every moment of consciousness, and one for which all those moments (substantiality as such, outward existence, and the essence of thinking) lie enclosed in this certainty it has of itself. Substantiality as such belongs to the _matter at hand_ in ethical life, outward existence in {{{ 591 }}} culture, and thinking's self-knowing essentiality in morality; in conscience the matter at hand is the _subject_ knowing these moments within its own self. Whereas the honest consciousness only ever seizes _the empty matter at hand_, conscience by contrast wins it in its fulfillment, which conscience gives to it through itself. Conscience is this power in virtue of knowing consciousness's moments to be _moments_ and, as their negative essence, dominating them.

{{ 346 }} If we consider conscience with respect to the several determinations of the opposition that shows up in acting, and consider its awareness of what those determinations are, then it stands first of all as a _knower_ toward the _actuality_ of the _case_ in which action is to be taken. To the extent that the moment of _universality_ attaches to this knowledge, the knowledge required for conscientious acting includes embracing without any restriction the actuality that lies before it, and hence knowing the circumstances of the case precisely and weighing them. But since this knowledge is _acquainted_ with universality as a mere _moment_, it is a knowledge of these circumstances that is conscious of not encompassing them, or of not being conscientious in this regard. Knowledge's genuinely universal and pure relation would be a relation to something not _opposed_ to it, to itself; but _acting_, by way of the opposition essential to it, is related to something negative over against consciousness, to an _actuality that has being in itself_. Over against the simplicity of {{{ 592 }}} pure consciousness, the absolutely _other_, or manifoldness _in itself_, this actuality is an absolute multiplicity of circumstances that divides and spreads itself out infinitely: backward into its conditions, sideways into its juxtapositions, forward into its consequences. — Conscientious consciousness is aware of this nature of the matter and of its own relation to it, and it knows that its acquaintance with the case in which it acts falls short of the universality demanded, and that its pretension to such a conscientious weighing of all circumstances is null. Yet this acquaintance with and consideration of all circumstances is not entirely absent; it is there only as a _moment_, as something that is only for _others_; and its incomplete knowledge, because it is _its own_ knowledge, counts for it as sufficient and complete knowledge.

Matters stand no differently for the universality of the _essence_, that is, for pure consciousness's determining of the content. — As it moves toward action, conscience is related to the many sides of the case. The case breaks up into those sides, and pure consciousness's relation to the case breaks up along with it, so that the case's manifoldness amounts to a manifoldness of _duties_. — That it must choose among these and decide is something conscience knows, since not one of them is absolute in its determinateness or in its content, only _pure duty_ is. But within its reality this abstraction has come to signify the I conscious of itself. As conscience, {{{ 593 }}} the spirit certain of itself rests within itself, and its _real_ universality, or its duty, has its seat in the pure _conviction_ it has of duty. Taken as such, this _pure_ conviction is quite as empty as pure _duty_ itself, being pure in the sense that nothing inside it, no determinate content, counts as duty. Action, however, is called for; the individual has to _determine_ it; and the spirit certain of itself, within which the in-itself has come to signify the self-conscious I, knows that determination and content are to be had in the immediate _certainty_ it has of itself. {{ 347 }} This certainty, in the role of determination and content, is _natural_ consciousness, which is to say, the drives and inclinations. — No content whatever does conscience acknowledge as absolute for it, since conscience is the absolute negativity of all that is determinate. Determining comes _out of itself_; yet determinateness as such falls within the self's own circle, and that circle is what one calls sensuousness; for having a content drawn from the immediate certainty it has of itself, nothing lies ready to hand except sensuousness. — Whatever, in earlier shapes, presented itself as good or as bad, as law and as right, is an _other_ over against immediate self-certainty; it is a _universal_ which is now a being for another; or, seen from another side, an object that mediates consciousness with itself, steps in between consciousness and its own truth, and, far from being consciousness's immediacy, rather cuts it off from itself. — For conscience, though, certainty of itself is the pure immediate {{{ 594 }}} truth; and this truth is accordingly its immediate self-certainty represented as _content_, which is to say, quite generally, the arbitrary will of the singular and the contingency of its unconscious natural being.

This content is at the same time valid as moral _essentiality_, as _duty_. For pure duty, as the testing of laws already made plain, remains utterly indifferent to any content whatever and puts up with any content whatever. Here it also carries the essential form of _being-for-itself_, and individual conviction in this form amounts to nothing but the awareness that pure duty is empty, that duty is only a moment, its substantiality a predicate whose subject lies in the individual, whose arbitrary will supplies duty with content, can attach any content to this form, and can pin its conscientiousness to that content. — Someone increases his property in a certain manner; it is a duty for each to see to his own preservation and that of his family, and no less to the _possibility_ of proving useful to his fellows and of doing good to those who need help. The individual is aware that this is a duty, since such content lies immediately within the certainty he has of himself; he sees further that in the present case he is discharging this duty. Others may take that certain manner for fraud; _they_ fasten on other sides of the concrete case, while _he_ holds {{{ 595 }}} onto this side by being aware of the increase of property as pure duty. — So it is that what others name violence and wrong discharges the duty of asserting one's self-subsistence against them; what they name cowardice, — the duty of holding onto one's life and onto the possibility of usefulness to one's fellows; whereas what they name bravery in fact violates both duties. {{ 348 }} Cowardice, though, dare not be so inept as to overlook that keeping one's life and the possibility of usefulness to others are duties, so inept as to lack _conviction_ about the dutifulness of its dealings, or so inept as to overlook that what accords with duty resides in _knowing_; otherwise it would commit the ineptitude of being immoral. Since morality resides in the awareness of having discharged duty, the acting called cowardice will be no more devoid of this awareness than the acting called bravery; the abstraction that goes by the name of duty admits of this content as it admits of any, so cowardice knows what it does as duty, and inasmuch as it knows this and inasmuch as conviction of duty is itself what accords with duty, it is recognized by the others; the action is thereby valid and has actual existence.

Against this freedom, which deposits whatever content it pleases in the passive universal medium of pure duty and pure knowledge, one content as readily as another, nothing is gained by insisting that a different content {{{ 596 }}} ought to have been deposited there; for whichever it might be, each carries upon it the _blemish of determinateness_, a determinateness that pure knowledge has left behind and can spurn as readily as it can take any up. In being determinate, every content ranks alongside every other, even where it appears to bear the very character of having the particular sublated within it. One might suppose that, because in the actual case duty splits in two into _opposition_ generally and thereby into the opposition of _singularity_ and _universality_, that duty whose content is the universal itself immediately bears upon it the nature of pure duty, so that form and content come to correspond entirely; so that, for example, action for the universal good would deserve preference over action for the individual good. But this universal duty is precisely that which is _at hand_ as substance existing in and for itself, as right and law, and which is valid _independently_ of the knowledge and conviction as well as of the immediate interest of the singular; it is therefore exactly that against whose _form_ morality as such is directed. As for its _content_, that too is _determinate_, to the extent that the universal good stands _opposed_ to the singular good; its law is accordingly a law that conscience knows itself to be flatly free of, granting itself absolute warrant to add to it and take from it, to leave it undone as much as to carry it out. — Furthermore, that distinction {{{ 597 }}} between duty toward the singular and duty toward the universal is, by the very nature of opposition as such, nothing fixed. {{ 349 }} On the contrary, what the singular does for himself also benefits the universal; the more he has provided for himself, the greater is not only his _possibility of being useful to others_; rather, his _actuality_ itself consists in nothing but being and living in connection with others; his singular enjoyment essentially has the significance of thereby giving up what is his to others and helping them to acquire their own enjoyment. In fulfilling the duty toward the singular, and hence toward himself, the duty toward the universal is fulfilled as well. — The _weighing_ and _comparing_ of duties that would enter here would come down to calculating the advantage the universal might derive from an action; but by this route morality falls partly to the necessary _contingency_ of _insight_, and partly conscience has its very essence in _cutting off_ such _calculating_ and weighing and in deciding out of itself without grounds of that kind.

Acting and sustaining itself in this manner, conscience holds together the _in-itself_ and _being-for-itself_, holds together pure thinking and individuality, and is the spirit certain of itself, a spirit whose truth resides in it, in its self, in its knowledge, and there as knowledge of duty. It sustains itself precisely in this: that everything _positive_ in the action, {{{ 598 }}} duty's content no less than duty's form and the knowing of duty, falls to the self, to the self's certainty of itself, whereas whatever would set itself against the self as _an in-itself of its own_ is valid only as untrue, only as sublated, only as a moment. Hence it is not _universal knowledge_ as such that counts, but _its own acquaintance_ with the circumstances. Into duty, as the universal _being-in-itself_, it inserts the content that it takes from its natural individuality, for that content is the one present in it; through the universal medium in which this content stands, it becomes the _duty_ that conscience exercises, and thereby empty pure duty is posited as something sublated, as a moment; this content is duty's sublated emptiness, or its fulfillment. — Yet conscience is just as much free of every content whatever; it absolves itself of any determinate duty that claims to be valid as law; drawing on the force of its self-certainty, it wields the majesty of an absolute autarky that binds and looses. — Such _self-determination_ is therefore immediately what simply accords with duty; duty is knowledge itself; and this simple selfhood is the in-itself, since the _in-itself_ means pure self-equality, which is present in this consciousness. —

Immediately, this pure knowledge is _being for another;_ for being pure self-equality, it is _immediacy_, or being. Yet at the same time this being is the pure universal, the selfhood belonging to all; in other words, acting is recognized and for that reason actual. {{{ 599 }}} This being is the element by which conscience stands immediately in a relation of equality with all self-consciousnesses; and what that relation signifies is not the selfless law but the self of conscience.

{{ 350 }} But an inequality seems to attach to conscience in that what it does as right is at the same time _being for another_. The duty it carries out is a _determinate_ content; granted, this content is consciousness's _self_, and therein consciousness's _knowledge_ of itself, its _equality_ with itself. But once carried out, once its place is in the universal medium _of being_, this equality has ceased to be _knowledge_, has ceased to be that differentiating which sublates its differences just as immediately; in _being_, rather, the difference is posited as something that subsists, and the action is a _determinate_ one, unequal to the element of everyone's self-consciousness and therefore not necessarily recognized. Each of the two sides, conscience in its acting and the universal consciousness that recognizes this action as duty, is equally _free_ of the determinateness of the doing. Because of this freedom, the relation within the common medium of connection is rather a relationship of complete inequality, and consequently the consciousness for which the action exists finds itself in complete uncertainty about the acting spirit that is certain of itself. It acts, it posits a determinateness as something that is; the others hold to this _being_ as to its {{{ 600 }}} truth and are in this respect certain of it; in that being it has expressed _what_ counts for it as duty. Yet it is free of any _determinate_ duty; it is already out of the place where they suppose it to be actual, and this medium of being itself, along with duty as existing _in itself_, counts for it only as a moment. What it thus sets up before them, it also dissembles again, or rather has immediately dissembled. For its _actuality_ is for it not this duty and determination put on display, but the actuality it possesses in absolute self-certainty.

Whether this conscience is morally good or whether it is evil is thus beyond them, or rather, not only are they unable to know it, they are bound to regard it as evil. For its freedom from the _determinateness_ of duty and from duty as existing _in itself_ is theirs no less. What it sets up before them they know how to dissemble in their turn; it is something through which only the _self_ of another is expressed, not their own; they not only know themselves to be free of it, but must dissolve it in their own consciousness, must annihilate it by judging and explaining, in order to preserve their own self.

The action of conscience, however, is more than this _determination_ of being that the pure self has deserted. Anything that is to be valid and recognized as duty is so only through knowledge of it as duty and conviction of it as duty, through {{{ 601 }}} knowing one's own self within the deed. Should the deed no longer carry this self in it, it stops being the one thing that constitutes its essence. Its existence, once deserted by this consciousness, would be an actuality of the ordinary sort, and the action would strike us as the satisfying of one's pleasure and desire. {{ 351 }} What is to _be there_ counts here as essentiality solely because it is _known_ as individuality that expresses itself; and this _being-known_ is what gets recognized and what is to have _existence as such_.

The self enters existence _as self_; spirit that is certain of itself exists for others precisely as such; its _immediate_ action is not what is valid and actual; what gets recognized is neither the _determinate_ nor what has _being-in-itself_, but only the _self_ that knows itself, taken as such. Universal self-consciousness is the element of subsistence; whatever steps into that element cannot be the action's _effect_, since the effect does not last there and acquires no permanence; self-consciousness alone is what gets recognized and wins actuality.

We thus see _language_ once again as the existence of spirit. It is self-consciousness existing _for others_, which is immediately _at hand as such_ and, as _this_ one, is universal. Language is the self that detaches itself from itself, a self that turns objective for itself as pure I — I, that in this objectivity keeps itself as _this_ self just as it flows together immediately with the others and is _their_ {{{ 602 }}} self-consciousness; it hears itself just as it is heard by the others, and this hearing is precisely _existence that has become self_.

What language has gained here as content is no longer the inverted, inverting, torn-apart self of the world of culture; it is spirit that has gone back into itself, certain of itself and, within its self, certain of its truth or of its recognizing, and recognized as this knowledge. Ethical spirit speaks in law and in the simple command, and in lament, which is more a tear over necessity; moral consciousness, by contrast, remains _mute_, locked up inside itself, for in it the self does not yet have existence, and existence and the _self_ still stand in a merely external relation to one another. Language emerges only as the middle between self-subsistent and mutually recognized self-consciousnesses; the _existing self_ is immediately a being-recognized that is universal, manifold, and simple within this multiplicity. Conscience's language has for its content _the self that knows itself to be essence_. This alone is what language expresses, and this expressing is the true actuality of the doing and the validity of the action. Consciousness expresses its _conviction_; it is this conviction alone in which the action is duty; and the action _counts_ as duty solely because the conviction is _expressed_. For universal self-consciousness is free of the _merely existing determinate_ action; _that action_ as _existence_ {{{ 603 }}} counts for nothing with it, but rather the _conviction_ that the action is duty; and this conviction is actual in language. {{ 352 }} — To actualize the action means here not carrying its content over from the form of _purpose_ or of _being-for-itself_ into that of _abstract_ actuality, but carrying it over from the form of immediate _certainty_ of oneself, which knows its knowledge or being-for-itself to be the essence, into that of an _assurance_ that consciousness has been convinced of duty and, as conscience, knows duty _out of itself_; so the assurance amounts to assuring that what consciousness is convinced of is that its own conviction is the essence.

Whether the assurance of acting from conviction of duty is _true_, whether what gets done _really_ is _duty_ — such questions or doubts carry no weight against conscience. — The question whether the _assurance is true_ would presuppose that the inner intention diverges from the one professed, that is, that the singular self's willing can come apart from duty, from what universal and pure consciousness wills; the latter would have been placed into the words spoken, while the former would in truth drive the action. But this very difference between universal consciousness and the singular self is the one that has sublated itself, and conscience is that sublating. Law and duty are the immediate knowledge of the self certain of itself; its intention is right by the mere fact of being its intention; all that is demanded {{{ 604 }}} is that it know this, and that it state its conviction that its knowing and willing is what is right. Uttering this assurance sublates in itself the form of the self's particularity; in that utterance the self recognizes the _necessary universality of the self_; by naming itself _conscience_, it names itself pure self-knowing and pure abstract willing, that is, it names itself a knowing and willing that is universal, that recognizes the others and is _equal_ to them, since they too are just this pure self-knowing and willing, and that is for this reason recognized by them in turn. What is right has its essence in the willing that belongs to the self certain of itself, in the knowledge that essence is the self. — Anyone who says that he acts thus out of conscience speaks truly, for his conscience is the self that knows and wills. But he must essentially _say_ so, for this self has at the same time to be a _universal_ self. It is not universal in the action's _content_, since that content, on account of its _determinateness_, is in itself a matter of indifference: universality lies rather in the action's form; it is this form that is to be posited as actual; the form is the _self_, which as such is actual in language, declares itself to be the true, and precisely therein recognizes all selves and is recognized by them.

Conscience, therefore, raised in majesty above any determinate law and above every content that duty might have, puts into its knowledge and its willing whatever content it likes; it is moral genius, {{{ 605 }}} for which the inward voice of its own immediate knowledge counts as the voice of God, and since existence too is known just as immediately in that same knowledge, it is the divine power of creation, whose very concept holds livingness in it. {{ 353 }} It is likewise worship carried on within its own self; for its acting is the intuiting of this divinity of its own.

Worship of this solitary kind is essentially, and at the same time, worship belonging to a _community_, and the pure inward _knowing_ and hearing of oneself passes over into the moment of _consciousness_. Its _objective_ existence lies in the intuiting of itself, and this objective element consists in the speaking-out of its knowledge and willing as something _universal_. By speaking out in this way the self acquires validity and the action becomes the deed that executes it. Universal self-consciousness is the actuality and the subsistence of what it does; the speaking-out of conscience, however, posits its own certainty as a pure self and thereby as a universal one; the others allow the action to be valid for the sake of this speech, wherein the self stands expressed and recognized as the essence. So the mutual assurance of one another's conscientiousness and good intentions is what constitutes the spirit of their bond and its substance, along with the delight taken in this reciprocal purity and the refreshment found in the splendor of such knowing and speaking, of nursing and tending such excellence. — To the extent that this conscience still keeps its _abstract_ consciousness {{{ 606 }}} apart from its _self-consciousness_, its life remains _hidden_ in God; God is indeed present _immediately_ to its spirit, its heart, its self; but the revealed side, that is, its actual consciousness together with the mediating movement belonging to it, is for conscience something other than that concealed interior, other than the immediacy with which the essence is present. Yet with the completion of conscience the distinction between the abstract consciousness and the self-consciousness sublates itself. It knows that the _abstract_ consciousness is nothing but _this self_, this being-for-itself certain of its own self, and that within the _immediacy_ with which the self is _related_ to the in-itself, which, once placed beyond the self, amounts to the abstract essence and to what stays concealed from it, precisely the _difference is sublated_. For a relation counts as a _mediating_ one where the terms it relates are not identical but rather an _other_ for one another, becoming one only within a third; the _immediate_ relation, by contrast, in fact signifies nothing but unity. Consciousness, having risen above the thoughtlessness of still counting these differences, which are no differences at all, as differences, knows the immediacy with which the essence is present in it as the unity of the essence with its own self, knows its self accordingly as the living in-itself, and knows this knowledge of its own as religion, which, being knowledge intuited or knowledge that exists, is what the community speaks about its own spirit.

{{{ 607 }}} What we see here, accordingly, is self-consciousness withdrawn into its innermost depth, a depth for which every externality as such disappears — withdrawn into the intuition of the I = I, where this I amounts to all essentiality and all existence. {{ 354 }} Within this concept of its own self it goes under, since it has been driven up to the point where its extremes meet, and driven there in such a way that the differentiated moments that make it real, or still make it _consciousness_, are not for us merely these pure extremes; rather, what it is for its own part, and what counts to it as the _in-itself_ and what counts to it as _existence_, have evaporated into abstractions that retain no hold, no substance, for this consciousness itself; and whatever counted hitherto as essence for consciousness has withdrawn into those abstractions. — Refined to a purity like this, consciousness is the poorest shape it has, and the poverty that constitutes all it owns is itself a disappearing; this absolute _certainty_, the certainty into which substance has dissolved, is the absolute _untruth_, which collapses in upon itself; it is absolute _self-consciousness_, and in it _consciousness_ goes under.

If we consider this going-under within its own self, then for consciousness the _substance_ that has being-_in-itself_ is _knowledge_ as _its_ knowledge. Being consciousness, it stands divided in an opposition of itself against the object that counts for it as the essence; but this very object is the wholly transparent one, it is _its own self_, and consciousness on its part is nothing more than knowledge of itself. Every form of life, and every spiritual essentiality, has receded into this {{{ 608 }}} self and forfeited its difference from the I itself. Hence the moments of consciousness are those extreme abstractions, and none of them stands fast; each rather loses itself in the other and brings the other forth. It is the unhappy consciousness's alternation with itself, though here the alternation takes place for that consciousness itself and inside it, and it is aware of being that concept of reason which the earlier consciousness was only _in itself_. So the absolute certainty of its own self, precisely as consciousness, immediately turns round into a sounding-forth, into objectivity of its being-for-itself; yet this world it has created is its own _speech_, a speech it has heard just as immediately, and only the echo of it returns to it. This returning, accordingly, does not carry the sense that it is there _in_ and _for itself_; for the essence is for it no _in-itself_ but rather itself; and just as little does it possess _existence_, since the objective never gets so far as to be a negative of the actual self, any more than that self gets so far as to be actual. What it lacks is the force of externalization, the force to turn itself into a thing and to bear up under being. It lives in fear of soiling the splendor of its inner life by acting and by existing, and so, to keep its heart pure, it shuns any contact with actuality and persists in the stubborn powerlessness that will not renounce the self it has honed to the last abstraction, will not confer substantiality on itself, will not convert its thinking into being nor commit itself to the absolute difference. {{ 355 }} The hollow object {{{ 609 }}} that it produces for itself therefore fills it now with the consciousness of emptiness; its doing is a yearning that merely loses itself in the course of becoming an essenceless object, and that, falling back beyond this loss and into itself, finds itself only as something that has been lost; — an unhappy, so-called _beautiful soul_ amid this transparent purity of its moments, it flickers out inwardly and fades like a shapeless vapor dissolving into air.

This silent confluence of the marrowless essentialities of a life turned to vapor has yet to be grasped under the other meaning that belongs to conscience's _actuality_ and under the _appearance_ that its movement takes, with conscience regarded now as something that acts — Above, the _objective_ moment within this consciousness determined itself to be universal consciousness; the knowledge that knows its own self is, as _this_ self, marked off from other selves; language, in which all reciprocally recognize one another as acting conscientiously, this universal _equality_, breaks up into the inequality of the singular being-for-itself, since each consciousness is just as absolutely reflected out of its universality and back into itself; the opposition of singularity to the other singulars and to the universal thereby sets in of necessity, and it is this relationship together with its movement that we have to consider. — Or, put the other way, this universality and duty carry the flatly opposite meaning of a determinate _singularity_ that exempts itself {{{ 610 }}} from the universal, one for which pure duty is nothing but universality that has risen to the _surface_ and been turned outward; duty resides in words alone and is valid as a being for another. Conscience, aimed at first only _negatively_ against duty in the shape of _this determinate duty on hand_, knows itself free of it; but once it fills the empty duty with a _determinate_ content drawn _out of its own self_, it gains the positive consciousness that it is qua _this_ self that it makes the content; its pure self, being empty knowledge, is what has neither content nor determination; the content it hands over to that self is taken from its own self _as this_ determinate one, from itself as natural individuality, and while it talks about how conscientious its acting is, it is indeed aware of its pure self, yet in the _purpose_ of its acting, taken as actual content, it is aware of itself as this particular singular and aware of the opposition of what it is for its own part to what it is for others, the opposition between universality, or duty, and its own being-reflected out of that universality.

If the opposition that conscience enters upon as _acting_ thus finds expression in conscience's interior, that same opposition is at once an outward inequality in the element of existence, the inequality of its particular singularity over against another singular. {{ 356 }} — Its particularity lies in this: the pair of moments that make up its consciousness, namely the self and the in-itself, are valid within it at _unequal worth_, and valid with the determination that certainty of its own {{{ 611 }}} self is the essence, _as against the in-itself_ or the _universal_, which is valid merely as a moment. Set over against this inward determination, then, is the element of existence, that is, universal consciousness, to which on the contrary universality, duty, is the essence, while singularity, which is for itself over against the universal, is valid only as a sublated moment. The first consciousness, in the eyes of this fastening upon duty, counts as _evil_, since it is the inequality of its _being-within-itself_ with the universal, and since it at the same time proclaims what it does to be equality with itself, to be duty and conscientiousness, it counts as _hypocrisy_.

The _movement_ of this opposition is at first the formal establishing of an equality between what evil is in itself and what it declares itself to be; it has to come to light that evil is evil, so that its existence matches its essence, and _hypocrisy_ has to be _unmasked_. — The bringing back of the inequality present in hypocrisy into equality is not already achieved by the familiar claim that hypocrisy proves its respect for duty and virtue in the very act of assuming their _semblance_ and wearing it as a mask before its own consciousness no less than before that of others; a recognition of the opposite, so it is said, in which equality and agreement are implicitly contained. — Yet hypocrisy has equally reflected itself out of this recognition made in speech {{{ 612 }}} and back into itself, and in using what has being-_in-itself_ merely as a _being for another_ it rather contains its own contempt for that in-itself and exhibits its essencelessness before everyone. For whatever permits itself to be put to use as an external tool proves to be a thing possessing no weight of its own within it.

Nor is this equality brought about by the one-sided persistence of the evil consciousness in itself, any more than by the universal consciousness's judgment. — Should the former deny itself before the consciousness of duty, and maintain that what the latter pronounces wickedness, absolute inequality with the universal, is in truth an acting that follows inner law and conscience, then its inequality with the other still remains within such a one-sided assurance of equality, since the other neither believes nor recognizes it. — Or again, since one-sided persistence in _one_ extreme dissolves itself, evil would by that route admit to being evil, but in the very admission it would _immediately_ sublate itself and would be neither hypocrisy nor an unmasking of itself as such. It does in fact admit to being evil by maintaining that, set against the recognized universal, it acts in accordance with _its own_ inner law and conscience. {{ 357 }} For were such a law and conscience not the law belonging to its _singularity_ and _arbitrary choice_, it would not be anything inner and its own, but would be what is universally recognized. Anyone who says, therefore, that he goes by _his own_ law and conscience {{{ 613 }}} in acting against others is in fact saying that he mistreats them. But _actual_ conscience is not this persistence in a knowledge and a will opposed to the universal; on the contrary, the universal is the element in which it has its _existence_, and its language pronounces what it does to be the _recognized_ duty.

Just as little does the universal consciousness, by persisting in its judgment, unmask and dissolve hypocrisy. — When it cries out against hypocrisy that it is bad, base, and so on, what such a judgment invokes is _its_ law, exactly as the _evil_ consciousness invokes _its own_. For the former steps forth over against the latter, and steps forth thereby as a particular law. So it holds no advantage over the other; it rather legitimates the other, and this zeal accomplishes exactly the reverse of what it supposes itself to be doing — namely, it displays what it calls true duty, what is supposed to be _universally_ recognized, as something _not recognized_, and so grants the other an equal right of being-for-itself.

This judgment, though, carries another side along with it, and from that side it becomes the opening toward a dissolution of the opposition on hand. — The consciousness _of the universal_ does not relate to the first as something _actual_ and _acting_, since the first is rather the actual one — it relates to it instead as the party that is not caught up in the opposition of singularity and universality which acting {{{ 614 }}} brings on. It stays within the universality of _thought_, relates as something _apprehending_, and its first action amounts to nothing more than judgment. — By this judgment it now sets itself, as was just noted, _beside_ the first, and the first, _by way of this equality_, arrives at the intuition of its own self within this other consciousness. Duty's consciousness, after all, relates in an _apprehending, passive_ way; yet this very fact puts it at odds with itself qua absolute will of duty, at odds with itself qua that which determines flatly out of its own self. It can well afford to keep itself in purity, since it _does not act_; it is that hypocrisy which wants judging taken for an _actual_ deed, and which proves its uprightness not by acting but by pronouncing excellent sentiments. It is therefore constituted just like the one reproached for putting duty into its talk and nowhere else. In both, the side of actuality stands equally distinct from the talk: in the one case through the action's _self-serving purpose_, in the other through the _absence of any acting at all_, an acting whose necessity lies in the very speaking about duty, for duty without a deed carries no meaning whatever.

{{ 358 }} But judging must also be regarded as a positive action on the part of thought, and it carries a positive content; from this side the contradiction lodged in the apprehending consciousness, along with that consciousness's equality with the first, grows still {{{ 615 }}} more complete. — The acting consciousness pronounces this determinate doing of its own to be duty, and the judging consciousness has no way to deny it that; for duty is itself the contentless form open to any content whatever, — or else the concrete action, differentiated in itself by its many-sidedness, bears upon it the universal side, the side taken for duty, quite as much as it bears the particular side that constitutes the individual's share and interest. Now the judging consciousness does not remain standing at that side of duty, nor at the agent's knowledge that this is his duty, the relationship and the station of his actuality. It fastens instead on the other side, plays the action over into the interior, and explains it out of an _intention_ distinct from the action itself and out of a self-serving _motive_. Just as every action admits of being considered for its conformity to duty, so it admits of this other consideration, that of _particularity_; for qua action it is the individual's actuality. — This judging accordingly lifts the action clear of its existence and reflects it back into the interior, that is, into the form of the agent's own particularity. — Should the action be attended by fame, judging knows this interior to be a _craving_ for fame; — should the action be on the whole suited to the individual's station without exceeding it, and be so constituted that individuality does not carry that station hung upon it as an external determination but fills this universality out through {{{ 616 }}} itself and precisely thereby shows itself fit for something higher, then judgment knows the action's interior to be an appetite for honor, and so on. Since in action generally the agent attains the intuition of _its own self_ in objectivity, or the feeling of itself in its existence and hence enjoyment, judgment knows the interior to be a drive toward the agent's own happiness, even were that happiness to consist in nothing more than inner moral vanity, in the enjoyment felt at the consciousness of one's own excellence, and in the foretaste that the hope of a happiness to come affords. — No action can withdraw itself from such judging, since the pure purpose of duty done for duty's own sake is what is unactual; its actuality lies in the doing of individuality, and by that fact the action bears upon it the side of particularity. — For the valet there exists no hero; not, however, because the one is no hero, but because the other is — the valet, and the hero has dealings with him not as a hero but as one who eats, drinks, puts on his clothes, in short in the singularity of need and representation. {{ 359 }} So too, for judging there exists no action where it could not set the side of the individuality's singularity against the universal side of the action and play morality's valet toward the agent.

With this the judging consciousness is itself _base_, for it divides the action and both produces and holds fast to the action's {{{ 617 }}} inequality with itself. It is furthermore _hypocrisy_, because it gives out such judging not for _another manner_ of being evil but for the _right consciousness_ of the action, because in this unactuality of its own and in this vanity of knowing well and knowing better it raises itself above the deeds it has torn down, and because it would have its deedless talk taken for an excellent _actuality_. — Making itself equal in this way to the acting consciousness it judges, it is cognized by that consciousness as one and the same with itself. The acting consciousness finds that the other not only apprehends it as something alien and unequal to itself, but that the other is rather, by its own constitution, equal to it. Intuiting this equality and _uttering it, it confesses_ itself to the other, and it expects likewise that the other, having in fact placed itself on a par with it, will answer its _speech_ in turn, will utter its own equality in that speech, and that the existence of recognition will thereby come on the scene. Its confession is not a debasement, a humiliation, a casting-away of itself in relation to the other; for this uttering is not the one-sided act whereby it would posit its _inequality_ with the other; rather, it speaks out solely on account of having intuited _the equality_ of the other with itself, it utters _their equality_ from its own side in its confession, and it utters it because language is spirit's _existence_ as an immediate self; so it expects that the other will contribute its share to this existence.

{{{ 618 }}} Yet upon evil's admission, _It is I_, this answering confession of like kind does not follow. That is not how the judging meant it; on the contrary! It pushes this community away from itself, and it is the hard heart, existing for itself and refusing continuity with the other. — With that the scene turns around. The one that had confessed sees itself thrust back and sees the other in the wrong, the other which refuses to let its interior step out into the existence of speech, and which sets against evil the beauty of its soul, and against the confession the stiff neck of a character that stays equal to itself, together with the muteness of holding itself within itself and not throwing itself away upon another. Here the supreme revolt of the spirit certain of itself is posited; for it beholds itself in the other as this _simple knowledge of the self_, and beholds it so that even the outward shape of this other is not, as wealth was, the essenceless, not a thing; rather, what is held up against it is thought, knowledge itself; it is that pure _knowledge_ in its absolutely fluid continuity which refuses to set up communication with the confessing one — with the very one that in its confession had already renounced _separated being-for-itself_ and had posited itself as sublated particularity and thereby as continuity with the other, as something universal. {{ 360 }} The other, however, holds in reserve _for its own part_ its uncommunicating being-for-itself; and in the confessing consciousness it holds fast to {{{ 619 }}} that very same thing, which the confessing consciousness has nonetheless already cast off. It thereby exhibits itself as the consciousness abandoned by spirit and denying spirit, for it fails to cognize that spirit, in absolute certainty of its own self, is master over every deed and every actuality and can throw them off and undo them. At the same time it fails to cognize the contradiction it commits when it will not let the throwing-off that has taken place in _speech_ count as the true throwing-off, while it has the certainty of its own spirit not in any actual action but in its interior, and the existence of that interior in the _speech_ of its judgment. So it is itself what checks the other's return out of the deed into speech's spiritual existence and into the equality of spirit, and by this hardness it produces the inequality that is still on hand.

Insofar, then, as the spirit certain of itself, as beautiful soul, lacks the force to externalize the knowledge of itself that it keeps within, it can reach no equality with the consciousness it thrust back, and hence no intuited unity of its own self in the other, no existence at all; equality accordingly comes about only negatively, as a spiritless being. The beautiful soul, void of actuality, standing in the contradiction of its pure self against that self's necessity to externalize itself into being and to swing over into actuality, standing in the _immediacy_ of this opposition held fast — an immediacy that alone is the middle and the reconciliation {{{ 620 }}} of an opposition heightened to its pure abstraction, and that is pure being or empty nothing — is therefore, as consciousness of this contradiction while its immediacy stays unreconciled, unhinged into madness and melts away in a consumption of longing. With this it does in fact surrender the hard clinging to _its being-for-itself_, but what it brings forth is merely the spiritless unity of being.

The true equalization, namely the _self-conscious_ and _existent_ one, is already contained in what precedes, as regards its necessity. Breaking the hard heart and raising it to universality is the very movement expressed in the consciousness that confessed itself. The wounds of spirit heal, and no scars are left behind; the deed is not the imperishable thing but is withdrawn by spirit back into itself, and the side of singularity present in the deed, whether as intention or as the deed's existent negativity and limit, is what immediately disappears. {{ 361 }} The _self_ that actualizes, the form of its action, is nothing but a _moment_ of the whole, and the same holds of the knowledge that determines by judgment and fixes the difference between the singular side and the universal side of acting. That evil consciousness posits this externalization of itself, that is, posits itself as a moment, having been drawn forth into the existence of confession by the intuition of its own self in the other. This other, however, must have its one-sided, unrecognized judgment broken, just as the first had to have broken its unrecognized, one-sided existence of particular being-for-itself; {{{ 621 }}} and just as the first exhibits spirit's power over its actuality, so this one exhibits that power over its determinate concept.

This other, however, renounces the dividing thought and the hardness of the being-for-itself that clings to it, and does so because in fact it intuits its own self in the first. The first, which throws its actuality away and makes itself into a _sublated This_, thereby in fact presents itself as something universal; out of its external actuality it returns into itself as essence; and so the universal consciousness cognizes its own self in it. — The forgiveness that it grants the first is its renouncing of itself, of its _unactual_ essence, which it sets equal to that other, to what was _actual_ acting, and it recognizes as good the consciousness that was named evil on account of the determination acting received in thought, or rather it lets go of this difference of determinate thought together with its own determining judgment that is for itself, exactly as the other relinquishes the determining of the action that is for itself. — The word of reconciliation is _existent_ spirit, spirit which intuits the pure knowledge of itself as _universal_ essence within its opposite, within the pure knowledge of itself as the _singularity_ that is absolutely within itself — a reciprocal recognizing that is _absolute spirit_.

It steps into existence only at that apex where its pure knowledge of its own self is opposition and alternation with itself. Since it knows that its {{{ 622 }}} _pure knowledge_ is the abstract _essence_, it is this knowing duty standing in absolute opposition to the knowledge that knows itself, as the absolute _singularity_ of the self, to be the essence. That first side is the universal in its pure continuity, and it knows the singularity that knows itself to be essence as what is in itself null, as _evil_. This second side, however, is absolute discreteness, which knows itself in its pure One to be absolute and knows that universal to be the unactual, which is only _for others_. Both sides are refined to this purity, in which no selfless existence, nothing negative of consciousness, still attaches to them; instead that _duty_ is the character, remaining equal to itself, of its knowledge of its own self, and this evil, for its part, has in its _being-within-itself_ its purpose, and in what it says its actuality; the content of what it says is the substance of its subsistence; it is the assurance of spirit's certainty within itself. {{ 362 }} — Neither of these two spirits certain of themselves has any purpose beyond its pure self, nor any reality and existence beyond just this pure self. But they are still distinct, and the distinctness is the absolute one, since it is posited in this element of the pure concept. And it is so not only for us, but for the very concepts that stand in this opposition. These concepts are, to be sure, _determinate_ over against one another, yet at the same time universal in themselves, so that they fill out the whole compass of the self, and this self possesses no content other than this determinateness of its own, {{{ 623 }}} a determinateness that neither reaches past the self nor is narrower than it; for the one of them, the absolutely universal, is just as much the pure knowing of its own self as is the other, the absolute discreteness of singularity, and both are nothing other than this pure knowing of themselves. Both determinacies are therefore the knowing pure concepts, whose determinateness is itself immediately knowledge, or whose _relationship_ and opposition is the I itself. In this way they are, _for one another_, these flatly opposed terms; it is the completely _inner_ that has in this way come to face itself and stepped into existence; they constitute the _pure knowledge_ that is posited as _consciousness_ through this opposition. But it is not yet _self-consciousness_. It has this actualization in the movement of this opposition. For this opposition is rather itself that _indiscrete continuity_, that _equality_, of the I = I; and each of them, _for itself_, sublates itself in its own case precisely through the contradiction of its pure universality, which at once still resists its equality with the other and cuts itself off from it. Through this externalization the knowledge split in two in its _existence_ goes back to the unity of the _self_; this is the _actual_ I, the universal knowing of _itself_ within its _absolute opposite_, within the knowledge that has being-_within-itself_, a knowledge which, because its separated being-within-itself is pure, is itself the completely universal one. The reconciling _***Yes***_, in which both I's let their opposed _existence_ {{{ 624 }}} go, is the _existence_ of the _I_ expanded into a twofoldness, an I that stays equal to itself therein and, in its complete externalization and its opposite, has the certainty of its own self; — it is God appearing in the midst of those who know themselves as pure knowledge.
