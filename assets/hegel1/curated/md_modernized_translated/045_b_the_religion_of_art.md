## {{{ 651 }}} B. The Religion of Art

{{ 376 }} Spirit has lifted the shape in which it is for its own consciousness into the very form of consciousness, and it produces such a shape for itself. The master builder has given up _synthetic_ labor, the _mingling_ of the disparate forms of thought and of what is natural; once the shape has acquired the form belonging to self-conscious activity, he has thereby become a worker in spirit.

Suppose we ask which _actual_ spirit it is that has, in the religion of art, the consciousness of its absolute essence: what then emerges is that this spirit is the _ethical_ one, or the _true_ spirit. It is not simply the universal substance of all singulars; rather, because for actual consciousness that substance bears the shape of consciousness, the sense of this is that the substance, which possesses individualization, is known by them as their own essence and work. So for them it is not the light-essence, in the unity of which the being-for-itself belonging to self-consciousness is preserved only negatively, only as something that vanishes, {{{ 652 }}} and which intuits the master of its actuality, — nor is the substance the ceaseless self-devouring of peoples who hate one another, — nor their subjugation into castes which, taken together, constitute the semblance of an organization of a whole brought to completion, though one lacking the universal freedom of the individuals. Rather, it is the free people in which custom constitutes the substance of all, and whose actuality and existence everyone, and each singular individual, knows as his own will and his own deed.

The religion of ethical spirit, however, is that spirit's raising of itself above its own actuality, a going back _out of its truth_ into pure _knowledge of itself_. Since it is in immediate unity with its own substance that the ethical people lives, and since the principle of the pure singularity of self-consciousness is not present in it, its religion appears in its consummation only in the _parting_ from its _subsistence_. For the _actuality_ of ethical substance rests partly on its calm _immutability_ over against the absolute movement of self-consciousness, and thus on this: that self-consciousness has not yet, out of its calm custom and its firm trust, gone into itself; — partly on the organization of that substance into a multiplicity of rights and of duties, as well as on the apportionment into the masses of the estates and of their particular doing, which works together into the whole; — and thus on this: that the singular individual is satisfied with the restriction of his existence and has yet to seize the unrestricted {{{ 653 }}} thought of his free self. {{ 377 }} But that calm _immediate_ trust in the substance goes back into trust _in itself_ and into the _certainty of itself_, while the multiplicity of rights and of duties, along with restricted doing, constitutes for the ethical the very same dialectical movement that the multiplicity of things and of their determinations constitutes, — a movement that finds its rest and its fixity nowhere but in the simplicity of self-certain spirit. — The consummation of ethical life into free self-consciousness and the destiny of the ethical world is therefore individuality that has gone into itself, the absolute frivolity of ethical spirit, which has dissolved in itself every fixed distinction of its subsistence and the masses of its organic articulation, and which, wholly sure of itself, has attained an unrestricted joyfulness and the freest enjoyment of its own self. This simple certainty that spirit has within itself is what is ambiguous, in that it is to be calm subsistence and firm truth, — as well as absolute unrest and the perishing of ethical life. It veers over, however, to the second of these, since the truth belonging to ethical spirit is still only this substantial essence and this trust, within which the self has no knowledge of itself as free singularity, and which therefore founders in that inwardness, or in the self's becoming free. Since trust has thus been broken and the substance of the people buckled inwardly, spirit, which had been the middle between extremes that have no subsistence, has now {{{ 654 }}} come forth into the extreme of a self-consciousness that seizes itself as essence. This latter is spirit certain within itself, which grieves over the loss of its world and which now brings forth its essence, raised above actuality, out of the purity of the self.

It is in such an epoch that absolute art steps forth; earlier, art is that instinct-like laboring which, submerged in existence, labors outward from existence and back into it, which does not find its substance in free ethical life, and which therefore does not have free spiritual activity for its laboring self either. Later, spirit has moved past art in order to gain a higher presentation of itself; — that is, to be not merely the _substance_ born from the self, but, in its presentation as object, to be _this self_; not merely to bring itself to birth out of its concept, but to have as its shape that concept itself, so that the concept and the artwork produced know one another mutually to be one and the same.

Now that ethical substance has taken itself back, out of its existence, into its pure self-consciousness, this latter is the side of the concept, or of _activity_, by means of which spirit produces itself as object. That side is pure form, because in ethical obedience and service the singular individual has worked away from himself all unconscious existence and all fixed determination, in the same way that the substance has itself turned into this fluid essence. Such a form is the night in which betrayal came upon the substance and it made itself into subject; and it is from {{{ 655 }}} that night of the pure certainty of itself that ethical spirit is resurrected, as the shape set free from nature and from its own immediate existence.

{{ 378 }} The _existence_ of the pure concept, the concept into which spirit has escaped from its body, is an individual whom spirit chooses for itself as the vessel of its pain. Spirit is present in this individual as the individual's universal and as its power, at whose hands it suffers violence, — as its pathos, and in giving itself over to that pathos the individual's self-consciousness forfeits its freedom. But the pure self of the individual, as the negative power, overcomes that positive power of universality. This pure activity, aware of the force it cannot lose, struggles with the unshaped essence; and in becoming master over it, the activity has turned pathos into its material and has furnished itself with its own content, and this unity emerges as a work, universal spirit individualized and represented.
