## {{{ 645 }}} c. The Master Builder

Spirit therefore appears here as the _master builder_, and its doing, by which it brings itself forth as an object while not yet having grasped the thought of what it itself is, is an instinct-like laboring, in the way bees build their cells.

The first form, since it is the immediate form, is the abstract one belonging to the understanding, and the work, in its own self, has not yet been filled by spirit. The crystals of the pyramids and obelisks, simple combinations of straight lines, with flat surfaces and equal proportions of the parts, forms in which the incommensurability of the round has been wiped out, are the works of this master builder of strict form. Owing to the mere intelligibility that the form has, the form is not, in its own self, its own meaning, and it is not the spiritual self. The works therefore either merely receive spirit into themselves, as a spirit that is alien and has departed, one that has abandoned its living interpenetration with actuality and, itself dead, takes up lodging in these crystals which are destitute of life; — or else they relate themselves outwardly to spirit as to something which is itself outward and is not there as spirit — as to the rising light which throws its meaning upon them.

The separation from which the laboring spirit sets out, the separation of _being-in-itself_, which becomes the material that it {{{ 646 }}} works up, — from _being-for-itself_, which is _the side_ of the laboring self-consciousness, has become objective for it in its work. Its further exertion must aim at sublating this separation of soul and body, at clothing and shaping the former in its own self, while ensouling the latter. As the two sides are brought closer to each other, they nonetheless keep, over against each other, the determinateness of the represented spirit and of the shell that surrounds it; its unity with itself contains this opposition of singularity and universality. {{ 374 }} When the work approaches itself in its sides, this at the same time brings about the other result as well, namely that it comes nearer to the laboring self-consciousness, and that this self-consciousness attains, within the work, a knowing of what it in and for itself is. In this manner, however, it constitutes as yet only the abstract side of the _activity_ of spirit, an activity that knows its content not yet in its own self but on its work, a work that is a thing. The master builder himself, spirit in its wholeness, has not yet appeared; he remains the essence that is still inward and concealed, an essence whose wholeness is on hand only in decomposed form, split into the active self-consciousness and into the object it has brought forth.

The surrounding dwelling, then, the external actuality which so far has only been raised into the abstract form of the understanding, the master builder works out into a more ensouled form. For this he makes use of plant life, which no longer counts as sacred, the way it did for the earlier powerless {{{ 647 }}} pantheism, but which he, grasping himself as the essence that is for itself, takes as something serviceable and demotes to outer surface and ornamentation. Yet it is not employed without alteration; rather, the one who labors at the self-conscious form simultaneously wipes out the transitoriness that the immediate existence of this life has about it, and draws its organic forms nearer to the stricter and more universal forms belonging to thought. The organic form, which when left free proliferates in particularity, is for its part yoked under the form of thought, while on the other side it raises these straight-lined and level shapes into a more ensouled roundedness — a blending that becomes the root of free architecture.

This dwelling, the side of the _universal element_ or of the inorganic nature of spirit, now encloses within itself a shape of _singularity_ as well, one that brings nearer to actuality the spirit previously severed from existence, inward or outward to it, and that thereby renders the work more equal to the active self-consciousness. The laborer reaches first for the form of _being-for-itself_ as such, for the _animal shape_. That animal life is no longer where his immediate consciousness of himself lies, he demonstrates by constituting himself, over against that life, as the force that brings it forth and by knowing himself within it as within _his_ work; through which the animal shape becomes at once something sublated and the hieroglyph of another meaning, of a {{{ 648 }}} thought. For this reason the laborer no longer employs it alone and in its entirety; he mixes it, rather, with the shape of thought, the human one. The work, however, still has no shape and no existence wherein the self is there as self; — it still lacks this, that it should pronounce in its own self that it encloses an inner meaning within itself; what it lacks is language, the element wherein the fulfilling sense is itself on hand. {{ 375 }} The work, then, even where it has cleansed itself completely of the animal and carries on it nothing but the shape of self-consciousness, is a shape that is as yet toneless and needs the ray of the sun as it rises before it can have a tone, a tone that, being engendered by light, is again mere sound and not language, and exhibits only an outer self, not the inner one.

Over against this outer self of the shape stands the other shape, which indicates that it has an _inner_ within it. Nature, in going back into its essence, reduces its living manifoldness, which singularizes itself and grows confused in its own movement, to an inessential shell that is the _covering of the inner_; and this inner, to begin with, is still the simple darkness, what is unmoved, the formless black stone.

Each of the two presentations holds _inwardness_ and _existence_ within it — the two moments belonging to spirit; and each holds both of them at the same time in an opposed relationship, the self as inner {{{ 649 }}} just as much as outer. Both must be brought into unity. — In the statue formed in human likeness the soul has not yet emerged from the inner, it is not yet language, that existence which in its own self is inward, — and the many-formed existence has an inner that is still toneless, one that draws no distinctions within itself and remains cut off from its outer side, to which all the distinctions belong. — The master builder consequently brings the two together by mixing the natural shape with the self-conscious one, and these ambiguous essences, enigmas to themselves, pairing what is conscious in its struggle with what is unconscious, the simple inner with the outer of many shapes, the obscurity of thought with the clarity of expression, break out into the language of a deep wisdom that is hard to understand.

In this work the instinct-like labor comes to an end, the labor that produced, over against self-consciousness, the unconscious work; for in it the activity of the master builder, which constitutes self-consciousness, is met by an inner that is just as self-conscious and pronounces itself. In doing this he has worked his way up to the splitting-in-two of his consciousness, the point at which spirit meets spirit. Where self-conscious spirit is thus at one with itself, insofar as it is to itself both shape and object of its consciousness, the mixtures it had entered into with the unconscious manner of the immediate shape of nature are accordingly purged away. These monsters in shape, {{{ 650 }}} speech, and deed dissolve into spiritual formation, — into an outer that has gone into itself, — into an inner that, out of itself and upon its own self, expresses itself; into thought, an existence that gives birth to itself, that keeps its shape in conformity with itself, and that is clear. Spirit is _artist_.
