## {{{ 129 }}} B. Freedom of Self-Consciousness; Stoicism, Skepticism, and the Unhappy Consciousness

In the self-subsistent self-consciousness the essence is, for one thing, merely the pure abstraction _of the I_, while for another, once that abstraction elaborates itself and supplies itself with differences, the act of differentiating fails to become for it an objective essence that has being-_in-itself_; so the I this self-consciousness arrives at is not one that genuinely differentiates itself in its own simplicity, nor one that stays equal to itself in that absolute act of differentiating. The consciousness that has been pressed back into itself, by contrast, comes to be an object for itself in shaping, namely as the form of the things it shapes, and in the master it looks upon being-for-itself as consciousness at the same time. For the serving consciousness as such, however, these two moments fall apart — _itself_ as a self-subsistent object, and this object as a consciousness, and with that its own essence. Since, however, the _form_ and _being-for-itself_ amount, _for us_ or _in itself_, to the same, and since within the {{{ 130 }}} concept of self-subsistent consciousness it is consciousness that constitutes being-_in-itself_, the side of being-_in-itself_, or of _thinghood_, which took on form in labor, is no substance apart from consciousness, and we have arrived at a new shape of self-consciousness: a consciousness that is the essence to itself as infinity, or as consciousness's pure movement; one that _thinks_, that is to say, one that is free self-consciousness. For _to think_ means being an object to oneself not as the _abstract I_ but as an I that carries at once the significance of being-_in-itself_, or relating to the objective essence so that this essence carries the significance of the _being-for-itself_ belonging to that consciousness for which it is. — For _thinking_, the object's movement takes place not in representations or in shapes but in _concepts_, that is to say, in a being-in-itself that is differentiated yet is, immediately, for consciousness nothing differentiated from consciousness. {{ 117 }} What is _represented, shaped, existent_, taken as such, bears the form of being other than consciousness; a concept, though, is at the same time something _existent_, — and this difference, so far as it belongs to the concept itself, makes up the concept's determinate content, — but because that content is at once a content grasped in a concept, consciousness stays _immediately_ conscious of being one with this determinate, differentiated existent; matters do not stand as with representation, where consciousness has first to recall separately that the representation is _its own_; the concept, rather, is straightaway _my_ {{{ 131 }}} concept. Within thinking I _am_ _free_, since I do not stand within another but stay purely alongside myself, and since the object that counts as the essence for me is, in unsevered unity, my being-for-myself; and the movement I make in concepts is a movement inside myself. — In determining this shape of self-consciousness, though, one point must be kept firmly in view: that the shape is _thinking_ consciousness _as such_, or that what stands before it is _being-in-itself_ together with _being-for-itself_ in _immediate_ unity. Consciousness that is like-named with itself, and that repels itself away from itself, becomes for itself an _element having being-in-itself;_ but so far it is this element to itself only as universal essence in general, and not yet as that objective essence within the unfolding and the movement of its own manifold being.

This freedom of self-consciousness, as is well known, has been called _Stoicism_, in that it came forward in the history of spirit as an appearance conscious of itself. Its principle is that consciousness is a thinking essence, and that something has essentiality for it, or is true and good for it, only insofar as consciousness relates itself therein as a thinking essence.

The manifold expansion of life, differentiating itself within itself, its singularization and entanglement, is the object against which desire and labor are active. This manifold doing has now contracted into the simple differentiation {{{ 132 }}} that lies in the pure movement of thinking. Essentiality no longer belongs to the difference that establishes itself in the guise of a _determinate thing_, or of _consciousness of a determinate natural existence_, of a feeling, or of _desire_ and of _purpose for that desire_, whether it be posited through one's _own_ consciousness or through _an alien consciousness_; essentiality belongs solely to the difference that is a _thought_ one, immediately not differentiated from Me. This consciousness therefore stands negatively toward the relationship of mastery and servitude; what it does is to find its truth neither in the servant, when it holds mastery, nor, as servant, in the master's will and in serving him, but to be free in fetters exactly as on the throne, free throughout every dependence of its singular existence, and to preserve in itself that lifelessness which perpetually withdraws, out of the movement of existence, out of acting as much as out of suffering, _into thought's simple essentiality_. {{ 118 }} Stubbornness is a freedom that attaches itself to a singularity and keeps its stand _inside_ servitude, whereas Stoicism is the freedom that steps immediately and always out of servitude and back into thought's _pure universality_; as a universal form taken by the world-spirit it could make its appearance only at a time of universal fear and servitude, though also of a universal culture that had heightened formative activity into thinking.

{{{ 133 }}} Now granted that what counts as the essence for this self-consciousness is neither another over against it nor yet the I's pure abstraction, but an I that has otherness within it, yet has it as a thought difference, so that within its otherness the I has immediately gone back into itself; even so, that essence of its remains at the same time merely an _abstract_ essence. Toward natural existence this freedom of self-consciousness is _indifferent_, and has for that reason _let natural existence go free in equal measure_, so that the _reflection_ here is a _doubled_ one. Freedom within thought possesses as its truth nothing but _pure thought_, a truth that lacks the fulfillment of life; hence it is likewise no more than the concept of freedom, and not living freedom itself; for so far its essence is only _thinking_ at large, the form as such, which has turned back into itself away from the self-subsistence of things. But if individuality, as acting, were to exhibit itself as living, or, as thinking, were to take in the living world under the form of a thought-system, then _thought itself_ would have to contain, for the former unfolding, a _content_ of what is good, and for the latter, of what is true; so that _throughout_, in whatever _is for consciousness_, no ingredient would occur besides the concept that constitutes the essence. Yet in the very way the concept here severs itself, as _abstraction_, from the manifoldness of things, it holds _no content of its own_, but only _a given content_. Consciousness does indeed wipe out the content as an alien _being_ when it thinks that content; but the concept {{{ 134 }}} is a _determinate_ concept, and just this _determinateness_ of the concept is the alien element carried within it. Stoicism was accordingly thrown into difficulty once it was asked, as the phrase ran, after the _criterion_ of truth in general, that is, after a _content_ belonging to _thought itself_, properly speaking. Asked _what_ the good and the true are, it answered once again with _contentless_ thinking itself; rationality is where the true and the good are supposed to reside. But this self-equality of thinking is once more only the pure form in which nothing determines itself; the universal words about the true and the good, about wisdom and virtue, at which it must come to a halt, are therefore no doubt in a general way uplifting, but since in fact they can never arrive at any expansion of content, they soon begin to be tedious.

This thinking consciousness, in the way it has determined itself, namely as abstract freedom, therefore amounts to no more than an unfinished negation of otherness; having merely _drawn back_ into itself out of existence, it has not carried itself out upon existence as the absolute negation of it. {{ 119 }} Content is valid for it, to be sure, only as thought, yet along with that as _determinate_ thought, and so determinateness as such is valid for it too.

_Skepticism_ realizes what Stoicism supplies only the concept of, — and it is the experience, in actuality, of what freedom of thought {{{ 135 }}} amounts to; such freedom is _in itself_ the negative, and has to exhibit itself accordingly. Once self-consciousness has reflected itself into the simple thought of itself, self-subsistent existence, or enduring determinateness, has in fact fallen out of infinity and confronts that freedom; in skepticism this other's complete unessentiality and non-self-subsistence now arises _for consciousness_; thought turns into the thinking that wholly annihilates the being of the _manifoldly determined_ world, and free self-consciousness's negativity turns, upon this manifold formation of life, into real negativity. — Clearly, then, Stoicism answers to the _concept_ of that _self-subsistent_ consciousness which appeared as the relationship of mastery and servitude, while skepticism answers to the _realization_ of that same consciousness, namely to the negative directedness upon otherness, to desire and to labor. But if desire and labor proved unable to execute the negation for self-consciousness, this polemical directedness against the manifold self-subsistence of things will succeed instead, since what turns upon those things is a free self-consciousness already completed within itself; more precisely, since it has _thinking_, or infinity, within it, and since therein the self-subsistent things, taken in their difference, count for it as nothing but magnitudes in the course of vanishing. Differences that within self-consciousness's pure thinking of itself amount only to the abstraction {{{ 136 }}} of difference here become _every_ difference, and differentiated being in its entirety becomes one difference within self-consciousness.

With this, the _doing_ of _skepticism_ in general, and its _manner_, has been determined. It points out that _dialectical movement_ which sense-certainty is, and perception, and the understanding; and it points out likewise the unessentiality of whatever passes for something _determinate_ within the relationship of ruling and serving, and of whatever so passes for abstract thinking itself. That relationship comprises at the same time a _determinate manner_ within which ethical laws, too, occur as commands issued by mastery; the determinations in abstract thinking, on the other hand, are concepts of science, into which contentless thinking spreads out, hanging the concept, in a way that is in fact merely external, onto the being that stands self-subsistent over against it and makes up its content, and holding as valid only _determinate_ concepts, granted that these are pure abstractions as well.

As negative movement in the way it immediately _is_, the _dialectical_ appears to consciousness at first as something to which consciousness has been abandoned, something that is not there through consciousness itself. {{ 120 }} As _skepticism_, by contrast, that movement is a moment of self-consciousness, and it is not something that merely _befalls_ self-consciousness, that its true and its real vanish without its knowing how; rather, in the certainty of its freedom, self-consciousness itself lets this other, which passes itself off as real, {{{ 137 }}} vanish; not the objective alone, taken as such, but its own relating to the objective, wherein the objective is valid and is made valid as objective, and hence its _perceiving_ as well, together with its _securing_ of what stands in danger of being lost, its _sophistry_, and the truth it has _determined out of itself_ and _fixed;_ by which self-conscious negation it procures _the certainty of its freedom_ _for its own self_, brings forth the experience of that certainty, and so raises it to _truth_. The determinate is what vanishes, that is, the difference which, in whatever manner and from whatever quarter, sets itself up as fixed and unchangeable. Nothing enduring belongs to it, and it _must_ vanish for thinking, since precisely this is what the differentiated is: not being _in its own self_, but holding its essentiality in another alone; thinking, however, is the insight into that nature which the differentiated has, it is the negative essence taken as simple.

Amid the changing of everything that would make itself fixed for it, skeptical self-consciousness thus undergoes the experience of its own freedom as something it gives to itself and preserves by itself; to itself it is that ataraxy of thinking that thinks itself, the unchangeable and _genuine certainty of itself_. This certainty does not emerge out of anything alien whose manifold development would have collapsed together within it, emerging as a result with its own becoming left behind it; on the contrary, what constitutes the _absolute dialectical unrest_ is consciousness itself, {{{ 138 }}} this mixture of sensuous and thought representations whose differences coincide, and whose _equality_ likewise, — for equality is itself the _determinateness_ over against the _unequal_ — dissolves once more. Yet precisely in this, rather than being a self-equal consciousness, this consciousness is in fact nothing but a wholly contingent confusion, the vertigo of a disorder that keeps generating itself. _It is this for its own self;_ for it is itself what maintains this self-moving confusion and brings it forth. It owns up to this as well: it owns up to being an entirely _contingent, singular_ consciousness, — a consciousness that is _empirical_, that takes its lead from what possesses no reality for it, that obeys what for it is no essence, that does and brings into actuality what for it holds no truth. But just as in this manner it is valid to itself as a _singular, contingent_ and in truth animal life, and as a _lost_ self-consciousness, so conversely it makes itself once more into the _universal, self-equal_ one; for negativity of every singularity and of every difference is what it is. Out of this self-equality, or rather within it, it drops back into that contingency and confusion, for this self-moving negativity has dealings with nothing but the singular and knocks about among contingent things. {{ 121 }} So this consciousness is that unconscious drivel of passing back and forth from the one extreme, self-equal self-consciousness, over to the other, the {{{ 139 }}} contingent, confused, and confusing consciousness. These two thoughts of itself it does not itself bring together; _on the one occasion_ it cognizes its freedom as an elevation above all confusion and all contingency of existence, and _on the other occasion_ it just as much owns up to being a relapse into _unessentiality_ and a drifting about within it. In its thinking it lets the unessential content vanish, but just therein it is a consciousness of something unessential; it utters absolute _vanishing_, and yet the _uttering ***is***_, and what has been uttered as vanishing is this consciousness itself; it utters the nullity of seeing, of hearing, and so on, and it _sees, hears_, and so on, _itself;_ it utters the nullity of the ethical essentialities, and itself makes them into the powers of its acting. What it does and what it says stand perpetually in contradiction, and it likewise carries within itself the doubled and contradictory consciousness of unchangeableness together with equality, and of utter contingency together with inequality with itself. But it keeps this contradiction of its own apart; and it relates to that contradiction as it does within its purely negative movement generally. Point out _equality_ to it, and it will point out _inequality_; and when that inequality, which it has just uttered, is now held up before it, it passes over to pointing out _equality_; its chatter is in fact the wrangling of stubborn boys, {{{ 140 }}} where the first says A whenever the second says B, and says B again whenever the second says A, and where, at the price of contradicting _themselves_, they buy the pleasure of staying in contradiction _with each other_.

Within skepticism consciousness comes to know itself, in truth, as a consciousness that contradicts itself inwardly; from this experience a _new shape_ proceeds, one that draws together the two thoughts which skepticism holds apart. Skepticism's thoughtlessness regarding itself has to vanish, since what carries these two modes within it is in truth _one_ consciousness. This new shape is thereby one that _for itself_ has a doubled consciousness of itself: consciousness of itself as what liberates itself, as unchangeable and self-equal, and consciousness of itself as what absolutely confuses and inverts itself, — and that is the consciousness it has of this contradiction of its own. — Under Stoicism self-consciousness amounts to the simple freedom of itself; under skepticism that freedom realizes itself, annihilates the other side, determinate existence, but rather doubles _itself_, and is now twofold to itself. By this means the doubling that formerly spread itself over two singular individuals, over master and over servant, has entered into one; self-consciousness's doubling within itself, which belongs essentially to the concept of spirit, is thereby at hand, though not yet the unity of that doubling, and _the unhappy consciousness_ amounts to the consciousness it has of itself {{{ 141 }}} as a doubled essence that merely contradicts itself.

{{ 122 }} This _unhappy, inwardly divided_ consciousness must therefore, because this contradiction of its essence is for itself _one_ consciousness, always have the other consciousness also within the one, and so must be immediately driven out again from each, just when it supposes it has attained the victory and the repose of unity. Its true return into itself, however, or its reconciliation with itself, will exhibit the concept of spirit that has come alive and stepped into existence, since it already carries this within it, that as one undivided consciousness it is a doubled one; it itself _is_ the beholding of one self-consciousness within another, and it itself _is_ the two of them, and the unity of the two counts for it as the essence as well, yet it _for itself_ is not yet this essence itself, not yet the two in their unity.

Being to begin with no more than the _immediate unity_ of the two, while for it the two are not the same but opposed, it takes the one, namely the simple unchangeable, for the _essence;_ the other, however, the manifold changeable, it takes for the _unessential_. _For it_ the two are essences alien to one another; being itself the consciousness of this contradiction, it ranges itself alongside the changeable consciousness and is to itself the unessential one; but qua consciousness of unchangeableness, that is, of the essence in its simplicity, it must at once aim {{{ 142 }}} at liberating itself from the unessential, which is to say, at liberating itself from itself. For although _for itself_ it is only the changeable, the unchangeable being something alien to it, _it is itself_ simple and hence unchangeable consciousness, conscious of that consciousness as _its own_ essence, yet in such a way that _it itself_ is once again, for itself, not this essence. The standing it grants to the two cannot, then, be an indifference of the one toward the other, that is, not an indifference of itself toward the unchangeable; rather, immediately, it is both of them itself, and _the relation of the two_ is for it a relation of essence to unessence, so that the latter is what must be sublated; but since the two are for it equally essential and equally contradictory, it is only that contradictory movement wherein one opposite finds no rest within its opposite, but instead generates itself there anew precisely as opposite.

There is thus at hand a struggle against an enemy over whom victory is rather a defeat, where to have attained the one is rather the loss of it in its opposite. The consciousness of life, of its existence and doing, is only pain over this existence and doing, for in it consciousness has only the consciousness of its opposite as the essence, and of its own nullity. Out of this it passes over into the elevation toward the unchangeable. {{ 123 }} But that elevation is itself this very consciousness; hence it is immediately a consciousness {{{ 143 }}} of the opposite, that is, of itself as singularity. The unchangeable that steps into consciousness is for just that reason touched at the same time by singularity, and is present only in company with it; far from singularity having been wiped out within the consciousness of the unchangeable, it does nothing there but keep coming forth.

Within this movement, however, what it experiences is precisely this _coming-forth of singularity ***in*** the unchangeable_, together with that _of the unchangeable ***in*** singularity_. There arises _for it_ singularity _in general in_ the unchangeable essence, and along with that _its own_ singularity there. For the truth of this movement is precisely the _being-one_ of this doubled consciousness. _This unity comes to be for it_, but _at first_ as itself one _in which the diversity_ of the two is still what dominates. Thereby a threefold manner in which singularity is bound up with the unchangeable is at hand for it; _on the first occasion_ it comes forth to itself once more as standing in opposition to the unchangeable essence, and it finds itself flung back to where the struggle began, a struggle that stays the element of the relationship throughout. _On the second occasion_, however, the _unchangeable_ itself has _within it_ the _singularity_ for consciousness; so that singularity is a shape of the unchangeable, to which the whole manner of existence thereby passes over. _On the third occasion_, it finds _itself_ as this singular one within the unchangeable. The _first_ unchangeable counts for it merely as the _alien_ essence that passes sentence upon singularity; {{{ 144 }}} since the _second_ is a _shape_ of _singularity_ such as consciousness itself is, consciousness becomes, _thirdly_, spirit, takes joy in finding itself there, and grows conscious that its singularity stands reconciled with the universal.

What presents itself here as the manner and relation of the unchangeable emerged as the _experience_ which the divided self-consciousness undergoes in its unhappiness. Admittedly this experience is not a movement that is _one-sidedly its own_, for it is itself unchangeable consciousness, and the latter is therewith singular consciousness as well, so that the movement belongs no less to the unchangeable consciousness, which enters into it quite as much as the other does; for the movement runs through these moments: being at first unchangeable over against the singular in general, then being itself singular over against the other singular, and finally being one with it. But this consideration, so far as it belongs to us, comes too early here, for what has so far arisen for us is only unchangeableness taken as consciousness's unchangeableness, which on that account is not the true one but is still saddled with an opposition, and not the unchangeable taken _in and for itself_; so we do not know how the latter will relate. What has emerged here is only this, that to the consciousness which is our object here these indicated determinations appear in the unchangeable.

{{{ 145 }}} For this reason the unchangeable _consciousness_ too retains, even in its own formation, the character and the basis of being divided and of being-for-itself over against the singular consciousness. {{ 124 }} For the singular consciousness, then, that the unchangeable takes on the shape of singularity is simply an _occurrence_; and in the same way it merely _finds_ itself standing opposed to the unchangeable, so that it holds this relationship _through nature_; that in the end it _finds itself_ within the unchangeable appears to it in part, to be sure, as produced through itself, or as taking place because it is itself singular; yet one part of this unity appears as belonging to the unchangeable, both as regards how the unity arose and in so far as it is; and the opposition persists inside this unity itself. In point of fact the _formation_ of the unchangeable has not merely left the moment of the beyond standing but has fortified it still further; for while on one side the shape of singular actuality seems to have brought that moment nearer to consciousness, on the other side it now confronts consciousness as an opaque sensuous _One_, with all the brittleness that belongs to something _actual_; the hope of coming to be one with it is bound to stay hope, which is to say, to stay without fulfillment and without presence, since what stands between the hope and its fulfillment is precisely that absolute contingency, or unmoving indifference, residing in the formation itself, in what grounds the hope. Through the nature of the _existent One_, through the actuality it {{{ 146 }}} has put on, it necessarily comes about that it has vanished in time, and has been somewhere in space and far away, and simply remains far away.

Whereas at first the mere concept of the divided consciousness determined itself as aiming at the sublation of itself as singular and at becoming unchangeable consciousness, its striving now has this determination, that it rather sublates its relation to the pure _unshaped_ unchangeable and gives itself only the relation to _the shaped unchangeable_. For what now counts for it as _essence_ and as _object_ is the _being-one_ of the singular with the unchangeable, whereas within the concept the essential object had been the shapeless, abstract unchangeable alone; and the relationship constituted by that absolute dividedness of the concept is now the thing from which it must turn away. Its relation to the shaped unchangeable, at first an external one that takes that unchangeable as something alien and actual, it has to raise into an absolute becoming-one.

The movement by which the unessential consciousness strives after this being-one is itself _threefold_, answering to the threefold relationship it will bear to its shaped beyond; first as _pure consciousness;_ secondly as a _singular essence_ relating to _actuality_ as desire and labor; and thirdly as _consciousness of its being-for-itself_. — In what way these three modes of its being stand and are determined within that universal relationship {{{ 147 }}} is what must now be seen.

Taking it first, then, as _pure consciousness_: the shaped unchangeable, in being for pure consciousness, seems to get posited just as it is in and for its own self. {{ 125 }} Only what it is in and for its own self has not yet arisen, as was already recalled. For it to be in consciousness just as it is in and for its own self would have to proceed from it rather than from consciousness; as it stands, though, this presence of its is so far at hand only one-sidedly, through consciousness, and for that very reason is neither complete nor genuine, but stays weighed down with incompleteness, or with an opposition.

Although the unhappy consciousness therefore lacks this presence, it has at the same time gone past pure thinking, so far as pure thinking is Stoicism's abstract thinking, which _looks away_ from _singularity_ altogether, and skepticism's merely _restless_ thinking, — which is in fact only singularity as the unconscious contradiction and its unresting movement; — it lies beyond the two of them, it brings pure thinking and singularity together and holds them so, and yet it has not risen to that thinking _for which_ consciousness's singularity has been reconciled with pure thinking itself. It stands rather at this middle point, where abstract thinking touches the singularity of consciousness {{{ 148 }}} qua singularity. It itself _is_ this touching; it is pure thinking and singularity in their unity; and _for it_ too there is this thinking singularity, or pure thinking, and the unchangeable itself essentially as singularity. But it is not _for it_ that this its object, the unchangeable, which for it essentially has the shape of singularity, is _it itself_, it itself which is the singularity of consciousness.

In this first mode, accordingly, the mode in which it stands before us as _pure consciousness_, it does not _relate_ _to its object_ by thinking; rather, since it is indeed _in itself_ pure thinking singularity and its object is just that too, while _their relation to one another is not itself pure thinking_, it goes, so to speak, no further than _up to_ thinking, and is _devotion_. Its thinking as such remains the shapeless droning of a peal of bells or a warm suffusion of mist, a musical thinking that never arrives at the concept, which would be the only immanent objective mode. Its object does indeed come to be for this infinite pure inner feeling; but it makes its entry so as not to enter as something grasped in a concept, and therefore enters as something alien. What is thereby at hand is the inner movement belonging to the _pure_ heart, a heart that _feels_ itself, but feels itself painfully as dividedness; the movement of an infinite _longing_ which carries the certainty that its own essence is just such a pure heart, pure _thinking_ that {{{ 149 }}} _thinks itself as singularity;_ and the certainty that, precisely because this object thinks itself as singularity, the longing is cognized and recognized by it. This essence, however, is at the same time the unattainable _beyond_, which flees in the very grasping, or rather has fled already. {{ 126 }} It has fled already; for it is on one side the unchangeable that thinks itself as singularity, so that consciousness reaches its own self in it immediately, reaches _itself_, but as _that which is opposed to the unchangeable;_ rather than grasping the essence it merely _feels_, and has dropped back into itself; unable, in reaching, to hold off itself as this opposed one, it has grasped, in place of the essence, only unessentiality. Just as on the one side, in striving to reach _itself in the essence_, it grasps only its own separated actuality, so on the other side it cannot grasp the other _as singular_, or as _actual_. Search where one will, it is not to be found, for it is meant to be precisely _a beyond_, the sort of thing that admits of no finding. Sought as something singular, it is not a _universal_, thought _singularity_, not a concept, but something _singular_ as object, or something _actual;_ an object of immediate sense-certainty; and precisely for that reason only something that has vanished. For consciousness, therefore, only the _grave_ of its life can come to presence. But because the grave is itself an _actuality_, and it runs counter to {{{ 150 }}} the nature of actuality to grant any enduring possession, this presence of the grave too is only the struggle of an effort that is bound to be lost. Yet once it has undergone the experience that _the grave_ belonging to its _actual_ unchangeable essence possesses _no actuality_, that the _vanished singularity_, qua vanished, is not the true singularity, it will abandon the search for the unchangeable singularity as something _actual_ and abandon holding fast to it as something vanished, and only thereby does it become capable of finding singularity as the most genuine, or as the universal.

To begin with, however, _the heart's return into itself_ must be understood thus, that the heart has itself as _singular actuality_. It is the _pure heart_ which has found itself, _for us_ or _in itself_, and is sated within itself, for although _for it_ the essence severs itself from it in its feeling, in itself that feeling is _self_-feeling: what it has felt is the object of its own pure feeling, and that object is the heart itself; so it comes forward out of this as self-feeling, or as something actual having being for itself. Within this return into itself there has come to be, for us, its _second relationship_, that of desire and labor, which corroborates for consciousness the inner certainty of itself that it has attained for us, doing so by sublating and enjoying the alien essence, namely that essence in the form of self-subsistent things. The unhappy consciousness, though, _finds_ itself merely as _desiring_ {{{ 151 }}} and _laboring_, and it is not at hand for it that what underlies finding itself in this way is the inner certainty it has of itself, and that what it feels as the essence is this self-feeling. {{ 127 }} Since it does not possess that certainty _for its own self_, its inner being remains rather a broken certainty of itself; the corroboration it would obtain through labor and enjoyment is for that reason equally a _broken_ one; or rather it must itself annihilate this corroboration, so that within it it does find corroboration, yet only corroboration of what it is for itself, namely of its dividedness.

For this consciousness the actuality against which desire and labor turn is no longer something _null in itself_, to be merely sublated and consumed by it, but something of the same sort as consciousness itself, an _actuality broken in two_, which on one side alone is null in itself while on the other side is also a hallowed world; it constitutes a shape of the unchangeable, since the unchangeable has preserved singularity within itself, and since, being universal qua unchangeable, its singularity carries the significance of all actuality whatsoever.

If consciousness were for itself a self-subsistent consciousness, and if actuality were for it null in and for itself, then in labor and in enjoyment it would arrive at the feeling of its self-subsistence, in that it would itself be the one which sublated actuality {{{ 152 }}}. Yet since actuality is for it a shape of the unchangeable, it is unable to sublate that actuality through itself. Rather, although it does arrive at the annihilation of actuality and at enjoyment, this happens for it essentially through the unchangeable itself _surrendering_ its own shape and _handing it over_ to consciousness for enjoyment. — Here consciousness on its own part steps forward _likewise_ as something actual, but equally as inwardly broken, and this dividedness exhibits itself in its laboring and enjoying as a breaking apart into a _relationship to actuality_, that is, into _being-for-itself_, and into a _being-in-itself_. The former relationship to actuality is _altering_, or _doing_, the being-for-itself that belongs to the _singular_ consciousness as such. But therein it is also _in itself;_ this side belongs to the unchangeable beyond; the side in question consists of capacities and forces, an alien gift that the unchangeable likewise leaves to consciousness for its use.

In its doing, accordingly, consciousness stands at first within the relationship of two extremes; on one side it stands as the active here below, and facing it stands passive actuality, the two of them related to one another and yet the two of them also withdrawn into the unchangeable and holding fast to themselves. From either side, therefore, only a surface comes loose toward the other, a surface that enters the play of movement directed against the other side. — The active extreme sublates the extreme of actuality; actuality {{{ 153 }}} on its side, however, admits of being sublated only because its own unchangeable essence sublates it, repels it away from itself, and surrenders what has been repelled to the activity. {{ 128 }} Active force makes its appearance as _the power_ within which actuality dissolves; but for that reason, for this consciousness, whose _in-itself_ or essence is another over against it, this power, in the guise of which it steps forward in its activity, is the beyond of its own self. So instead of returning into itself out of its doing and having corroborated itself for its own self, it reflects this movement of doing back into the other extreme, which is thereby exhibited as purely universal, as the absolute power out of which the movement issued in every direction, and which is said to be the essence alike of the extremes as they first stepped forward, now breaking up, and of the alternation itself.

The unchangeable consciousness _makes renunciation_ of its shape and _surrenders_ it, whereas the singular consciousness _renders thanks_, that is, _refuses_ itself the satisfaction of the consciousness of its _self-subsistence_ and consigns the essence of the doing away from itself over to the beyond; and through these two moments in which both parties _reciprocally_ _give themselves up_, consciousness does after all come to have its _unity_ with the unchangeable. But this unity is simultaneously afflicted with separation, broken within itself anew, and from it the opposition of the universal and the singular steps forth once more. For consciousness {{{ 154 }}} forswears the satisfaction of its self-feeling only _ostensibly_, whereas what it obtains is the _actual_ satisfaction of that feeling; after all, desire, work, and enjoyment are what _it has been_; as consciousness, _it_ is the one that has _willed, done_, and _enjoyed_. Its _giving of thanks_ is no different: in it consciousness acknowledges the essence in the other extreme and sublates itself, and yet this thanking is _its very own_ doing, one that offsets the doing of the other extreme and confronts the self-abandoning benefaction with a doing of _equal_ weight; if the other leaves it nothing but its _surface_, consciousness thanks _all the same_, and therein, by surrendering its own doing, that is, its _essence_, it accomplishes rather more than the other does, which merely sheds a surface of itself. So the entire movement bends back into the _extreme of singularity_, and it does so not merely in the course of actual desiring, working, and enjoying, but even within the thanking, where the reverse appears to be taking place. In this consciousness feels itself to be this singular, and the semblance of its renouncing does not take it in, for the truth of that renouncing is that it has surrendered nothing of itself; what has been brought about is merely a reflection redoubled into the two extremes, and what results is a cleavage repeated once more: on one side the counterposed consciousness of the _unchangeable_, on the other the consciousness of the willing, accomplishing, and enjoying that _stands over against_ it, and of the renouncing of self as such, or of _singularity that exists for itself_ in general.

{{{ 155 }}} What has now entered is the _third relationship_ belonging to the movement of this consciousness, a relationship issuing from the second inasmuch as consciousness has there put itself to the test, through its willing and accomplishing, and shown itself in truth to be self-subsistent. {{ 129 }} In the first relationship consciousness was merely the _concept_ of actual consciousness, that is, the _inward heart_, which in doing and enjoyment is not yet actual; the _second_ relationship is this actualization, in the form of outward doing and enjoying; but on returning from that, consciousness is one that has _experienced_ itself as actual and effective consciousness, or one for which its being _in and for itself_ is _true_. Here, however, the enemy has now been found out in his most proper shape. Within the struggle of the heart the singular consciousness exists merely as a musical and abstract moment; and where that essenceless being gets realized, namely in work and in enjoyment, consciousness can straightaway forget _itself_, while the conscious _ownness_ that has arisen in this actuality is beaten down by the recognition that thanks. Yet this beating down amounts, truly considered, to consciousness returning into itself, and indeed into itself as the actuality that is genuine for it.

In this third relationship the one extreme is that genuine actuality, and what the relationship consists in is this actuality's _relation_, as a nullity, to the universal essence; the movement of that relation still remains to be considered.

{{{ 156 }}} As regards, first, the opposed relation of consciousness, in which its _reality is immediately the null_ for it, its actual doing accordingly becomes a doing of nothing, and its enjoyment becomes the feeling of its unhappiness. With this, doing and enjoyment lose all _universal content and significance_, for through such content they would have a being in and for itself, and both withdraw into the singularity upon which consciousness is directed in order to sublate it. Consciousness is conscious of itself as _this actual singular_ in the animal functions. Instead of being performed without further ado, as something that is in and for itself null and can acquire no importance or essentiality for spirit, these functions, since they are the ones in which the enemy shows himself in his peculiar shape, are rather the object of earnest exertion and become precisely what matters most. But since this enemy engenders himself in his very defeat, and consciousness, in fixing him for itself, far from becoming free of him, always lingers with him and always sees itself defiled, while at the same time this content of its striving is, instead of something essential, the lowest thing, and instead of something universal, the most singular, we see only a personality confined to itself and to its petty doing, brooding over itself, as unhappy as it is impoverished.

But to both of these, the feeling of its unhappiness and the impoverishment of its doing, there attaches itself {{{ 157 }}} no less the consciousness of its own unity with the unchangeable. For it is the thought of the unchangeable that _mediates_ the annihilation which consciousness attempts immediately upon its actual being, and this occurs within this very _relation. The mediated_ relation is what makes up the essence of the negative movement wherein consciousness sets itself against its own singularity, a movement that is nonetheless positive as well, being a _relation in itself_, and that will bring this _unity_ of its into being for consciousness itself.

This mediated relation, accordingly, has the form of a syllogism: the singularity that initially fixes itself in opposition to the _in-itself_ is conjoined with that other extreme only by way of a third term. {{ 130 }} It is through this middle that the extreme of unchangeable consciousness exists for the unessential consciousness, and contained in that at the same time is this, that the latter likewise exists for the former only through this middle; the middle is accordingly one that presents each of the two extremes to the other and acts as the reciprocal servant of each before the other. The middle is a conscious essence in its own right, for it is a doing through which consciousness as such gets mediated; and what this doing has for its content is the extirpation that consciousness performs upon its own singularity.

In this extirpation, then, consciousness sets itself free from doing and from enjoyment as _its own;_ being the extreme that exists _for itself_, it pushes the essence of its _will_ away from itself and unloads onto the middle, onto the servant, the ownness {{{ 158 }}} and the freedom of the decision, and along with these the _guilt_ attaching to its doing. This mediator, standing in immediate relation to the unchangeable essence, serves by giving his _counsel_ as to what is right. The action, being the carrying out of a decision that is alien, ceases on the side of the doing or of the _will_ to be its own. But its _objective_ side still remains to the unessential consciousness, namely the _fruit_ of its work and the _enjoyment_. So it thrusts these away from itself as well, making renunciation of the _actuality_ that accrued to it in work and enjoyment just as it did of its will; renunciation of that actuality _in part_ as the achieved truth of its self-conscious _self-subsistence_ — inasmuch as it goes about representing and voicing something entirely foreign to it and senseless; — in part as _external property_ — inasmuch as it parts with a portion of what work had earned it in the way of possessions; in part renunciation of the _enjoyment_ it has had — inasmuch as by fasting and mortification it once again refuses it to itself altogether.

By these moments, the giving up of its own decision, then of property and enjoyment, and finally the positive moment of pursuing a business that it does not understand, consciousness genuinely and entirely strips itself of the consciousness of inner and of outer freedom, of actuality as its _being-for-itself;_ it possesses the certainty of having truly externalized its _I_, and of having made a _thing_ of its immediate self-consciousness, {{{ 159 }}} an objective being. — Only through this _actual_ sacrifice was consciousness able to corroborate its renunciation of itself; for it is there and nowhere else that the _fraud_ disappears which resides in the _inward_ recognition given by thanking with heart, disposition, and mouth, a recognition that does indeed shove all the power of being-for-itself off itself and credit it to a bestowal from above, but that in this very shoving-off retains the _outer_ ownness for itself in the possession which it does not surrender, while retaining the _inner_ ownness in the consciousness of a decision it has itself arrived at, and in the consciousness of a content of its own determining, which it has not exchanged for an alien content that fills it senselessly.

{{ 131 }} But in the sacrifice that has actually been carried out, just as consciousness has sublated the _doing_ as its own, so too, _in itself_, its _unhappiness_ has let go of it. That this letting-go has taken place _in itself_ is, however, something done by the syllogism's other extreme, the extreme that is the _in-itself-existing_ essence. Yet that sacrifice on the part of the unessential extreme was at once no one-sided doing; it held within itself the doing of the other as well. For the surrender of one's own will is negative on one side only, namely according to _its concept_ or _in itself_, while being positive at the same time: it posits the will as an _other_, and determinately as a will that is not singular but universal. For this consciousness {{{ 160 }}} the positive significance carried by the negatively posited singular will is nothing other than the will belonging to the other extreme, a will that accrues to it, precisely because it is another for it, not by its own agency but by way of the third, the mediator in the form of counsel. Hence _for it_ its will does indeed become the universal will, the will that is _in itself_, but _it itself_ is _not for itself_ this _in-itself;_ the giving up of its own will as _singular_ is not for it, according to the concept, the positive of the universal will. Likewise its giving up of possession and enjoyment has only the same negative significance, and the universal that comes to be for it thereby is not for it its _own doing_. This _unity_ of the objective and of being-for-itself, a unity residing in the _concept_ of doing and on that account becoming for consciousness the essence and _object_, — since this unity is not for consciousness the concept of its own doing, neither is it the case that the unity comes to be object _for it_ immediately and by its own agency; instead, consciousness has the mediating servant articulate for it this certainty, itself still broken, that only _in itself_ is its unhappiness the inverted, namely a doing self-satisfying in its own doing, or blessed enjoyment; that its impoverished doing is likewise _in itself_ the inverted, namely absolute doing; and that according to the concept, doing is doing at all only as the doing of the singular. But _for it_ itself, doing, and its actual doing, remains an impoverished one, and its enjoyment remains pain, and their having-been-sublated, {{{ 161 }}} taken in the positive significance, remains a _beyond_. But in this object, wherein its doing and being as this _singular_ consciousness is being and doing _in itself_ for it, there has arisen for it the representation of _reason_, of the certainty of consciousness that in its singularity it is absolutely _in itself_, or is all reality.
