## {{{ 625 }}} VII. Religion

{{ 363 }} Among the formations dealt with hitherto, which are broadly told apart into _consciousness, self-consciousness, reason_ and _spirit_, there did indeed also occur _religion_, as consciousness of the _absolute essence_ in general; but only as seen from the _standpoint of consciousness_, which for its part is conscious of the absolute essence; in those forms, though, neither the absolute essence taken _in and for itself_ nor spirit's self-consciousness came to light.

Even _consciousness_ already becomes, insofar as it is _understanding_, consciousness of what is _supersensible_ in objective existence, or of its _inner_. But whatever is supersensible and eternal, by whatever other name one gives it, is _selfless_; so far it is only the _universal_, which still falls well short of being spirit that has knowledge of itself as spirit. — Next, _self-consciousness_, whose completion lies in the shape taken by the _unhappy_ consciousness, was nothing but spirit's _pain_, struggling its way back out toward objectivity without ever arriving there. Hence the unity of the _singular_ self-consciousness with {{{ 626 }}} its unchangeable _essence_, a unity that self-consciousness brings itself to, remains a _beyond_ for it. — Neither _reason_ in its immediate existence, which for us issued from that pain, nor the peculiar shapes belonging to it has any religion, since the self-consciousness proper to them seeks and knows _itself_ in the _immediate_ present.

In the ethical world, by contrast, a religion did come into view, the _religion_ of the _underworld_; it consists in faith in the terrible, unknown night of _fate_, and faith in the Eumenides of _departed spirit_; — the first of these is pure negativity under the form of universality, the second is that same negativity under the form of singularity. In this latter form the absolute essence is indeed the _self_, and something _present_, as the self cannot but be; yet the _singular_ self is _this_ singular shade, which has the universality that is fate standing apart from it. It is, to be sure, a shade, a _sublated This_, and thus a universal self; but that negative significance has not yet turned over into this positive one, and so what the sublated _self_ still signifies at the same time, and immediately, is this particular and essenceless self. — Fate without the self, however, remains the unconscious night, which arrives neither at differentiation within itself nor at the clarity of self-knowing.

{{ 364 }} Such faith in the nullity of necessity and in the underworld turns into _faith_ in _heaven_, since the departed self has to make itself one with its universality, {{{ 627 }}} to unfold there what that universality holds, and so to become clear to itself. This _realm_ of faith, however, we saw unfold its content without the concept, within the element of thinking alone, and therefore we saw it go under in its fate, namely in the _religion_ of the _Enlightenment_. Here the supersensible beyond of the understanding re-establishes itself, yet in such a manner that self-consciousness rests satisfied on this side of things and knows the supersensible, that _empty_ beyond which is neither to be cognized nor to be feared, neither as self nor as power.

In the religion of morality what is finally reinstated is that the absolute essence has a positive content; it stands united, though, with the negativity of the Enlightenment. This content is a _being_ that is at the same time withdrawn into the self and stays shut up there, and a _differentiated content_ the parts of which are negated as immediately as they are put forward. But the fate into which this self-contradicting movement sinks away is the self that is conscious of being that fate, the fate of _essentiality_ and _actuality_.

Spirit knowing itself is, in religion, immediately its own pure _self-consciousness_. The shapes of spirit that have been examined — spirit as true, spirit as estranged from itself, spirit as certain of itself — make it up, taken together, in its _consciousness_, which, confronting its _world_, fails to cognize itself there. But in conscience spirit subjugates its objective world as such, {{{ 628 }}} and likewise its representation and its determinate concepts, and is now a self-consciousness that is at home with itself. Within this self-consciousness spirit, _represented as an object_, carries for itself the meaning of being universal spirit, which holds all essence and all actuality inside itself; but it is not there in the form of free actuality, nor in that of nature appearing as self-subsistent. Spirit does have _shape_, that is, the form of being, in that its consciousness has it as _object_; but since in religion this consciousness is posited under the essential determination of being _self_-consciousness, the shape is wholly transparent to itself; and the actuality that spirit contains lies enclosed within it, or sublated within it, exactly in the way it does when we say _all actuality_; it is actuality as _thought_, universal actuality.

Since, then, the determination of spirit's consciousness proper lacks, in religion, the form of free _otherness_, spirit's _existence_ stands distinguished from its _self-consciousness_, and its actuality proper falls outside religion; one and the same spirit does belong to both, yet spirit's consciousness does not span the two together, and religion shows up as one part of existence and of what is done and pursued, the other part being life in spirit's actual world. {{ 365 }} Now just as we know that spirit in its world and the spirit conscious of itself as spirit, that is, spirit in religion, are one and the same, so the completion of religion lies in this, that {{{ 629 }}} the two should become equal to each other, not merely that spirit's actuality be encompassed by religion, but conversely that spirit, as spirit conscious of itself, should become actual and should become the _object of its consciousness_. — To the extent that spirit in religion _represents_ itself to itself, it is of course consciousness, and the actuality shut up inside religion serves as the shape and clothing of spirit's representation. Within this representation, however, actuality is not granted its full right, which would be to be not merely clothing but self-subsistent free existence; and conversely, since it lacks completion in its own self, actuality is a _determinate_ shape, one that falls short of what it is meant to exhibit, namely spirit as conscious of itself. If spirit's shape were to express spirit itself, the shape would have to be nothing other than spirit, and spirit would have to have appeared to itself, or to be actual, just as it is in its essence. In that way alone would there also be attained what may look like a demand for the very opposite, namely that the _object_ of spirit's consciousness at the same time have the form of free actuality; but only that spirit which is an object to itself as absolute spirit is for itself an actuality just as free as it remains, within that actuality, conscious of itself.

When, to begin with, a distinction is drawn between self-consciousness and consciousness proper, between _religion_ and spirit in its world, that is, the _existence_ of spirit, this last consists in spirit as a whole, {{{ 630 }}} insofar as spirit's moments exhibit themselves as stepping apart, each on its own account. These moments, though, are the following: _consciousness_, _self-consciousness_, _reason_ and _spirit_ — the last of these being spirit in its immediacy, spirit not yet grown into the consciousness of spirit. Their _combined_ totality makes up spirit in its worldly existence as such; spirit as such holds the formations dealt with hitherto within the universal determinations, the moments just named. Religion presupposes the entire course these moments run through, and constitutes their _simple_ totality, that is, their absolute self. — Their course, incidentally, when related to religion, is not to be represented in temporal terms. Only spirit as a whole is in time, and shapes that are shapes of the whole of _spirit_ as such exhibit themselves in a sequence; for the whole alone possesses actuality proper, and therefore possesses the form of pure freedom over against another, a form that gives itself expression as time. But spirit's _moments_, consciousness, self-consciousness, reason and spirit, have, just because they are moments, no existence that differs from one another. — In the way that spirit was set off from its moments, so too, thirdly, the singularized determination of these moments must be set off from the moments themselves. {{ 366 }} For we saw each of those moments in turn differentiate itself within itself along a course of its own and take on differing shapes; as, for example, within consciousness, sense-certainty {{{ 631 }}} and perception were distinguished. These latter aspects step apart from one another in time, and they belong to a _particular whole_. — For spirit comes down out of its _universality_, by way of _determination_, into _singularity_. _Consciousness, self-consciousness_ and so on make up the determination, or the middle. _Singularity_, however, is constituted by the shapes of these moments; these therefore exhibit spirit in its singularity, that is, its _actuality_, and are distinguished in time, yet in such a way that the later one retains the earlier ones within itself.

If, then, religion is the completion of spirit, in which spirit's singular moments, consciousness, self-consciousness, reason and spirit, are grasped as _going back into their ground_ and as having _gone back_ into it, then taken together these moments make up the _existing actuality_ of spirit as a whole, a spirit that only _is_ as the movement of differentiating these aspects of itself and returning into itself out of them. What contains the becoming _of religion as such_ is the movement of the universal moments. Yet since each of these attributes has been exhibited not simply in the way it determines itself generally, but as it stands _in and for itself_, that is, in the way it runs through its own course within itself as a whole, what has arisen thereby is not only the becoming of religion _as such_; rather, those complete courses run by the _singular_ aspects contain at the same time the _determinatenesses of religion_ itself. The whole of spirit, the spirit of religion, is once again the movement {{{ 632 }}} of reaching, from out of its immediacy, the _knowledge_ of what it immediately, or _in itself_, is, and of bringing it about that the _shape_ in which it appears for its consciousness be perfectly equal to its essence, and that it intuit itself as it is — in this becoming, then, spirit is itself present in _determinate_ shapes, which make up the differences of this movement; and with that the determinate religion likewise has a _determinate actual_ spirit. If, therefore, the spirit that knows itself has, generally, consciousness, self-consciousness, reason and spirit belonging to it, then to the _determinate_ shapes of the self-knowing spirit there belong the _determinate_ forms that developed within each of these separately: within consciousness, within self-consciousness, within reason and within spirit. For its actual spirit, the _determinate_ shape of religion singles out, from the shapes of each of spirit's moments, the shape corresponding to it. This _one_ determinateness of religion runs through every aspect of religion's actual existence and imprints on them this shared stamp.

In this way the shapes that have emerged up to this point now order themselves otherwise than they appeared within their series, on which the necessary brief remark must first be made. {{ 367 }} — Within the series we considered, each moment, by deepening into itself, cultivated itself into a whole governed by its own peculiar principle; and the depth in which these moments, having no subsistence on their own account, had their substance was cognition, or {{{ 633 }}} spirit. This substance, however, has now stepped forth; it is the depth of the spirit certain of itself, and this depth does not allow the singular principle to isolate itself and turn itself into a whole on its own; instead, gathering all these moments into itself and holding them together, it advances within the whole wealth belonging to its actual spirit, and every particular moment of that spirit takes in and receives, in common with the rest, the same determinateness of the whole. — This spirit certain of itself, along with its movement, is their true actuality; it is the being-_in-and-for-itself_ that accrues to each singular one. — If, then, the single series so far, in its advance, marked the regressions within it by means of nodes, but continued on out of them once more into one single length, it has now been snapped, as it were, at those nodes, at the universal moments, and has broken up into many lines which, bound together into a single sheaf, at the same time join symmetrically, so that the identical differences into which each particular line shaped itself within its own bounds come together. — It is, besides, so plain of itself from the whole exposition how the coordination of the universal directions presented here is to be understood, that any remark to the effect that these differences must essentially be grasped only as moments of the becoming and not as parts becomes superfluous; {{{ 634 }}} in actual spirit these differences are attributes of its substance, whereas in religion they are rather only predicates of the subject. — Likewise, _in itself_ or _for us_, all the forms as such are indeed contained in spirit and in each of them; but as regards spirit's actuality, everything depends solely on which determinateness is there for spirit within its _consciousness_, on which determinateness it has used to express its self, or in what shape it has knowledge of its essence.

The difference drawn between _actual_ spirit and spirit as knowing itself to be spirit, or between spirit itself as consciousness and as self-consciousness, is sublated within the spirit that has knowledge of itself in accordance with its truth; in that spirit, consciousness and self-consciousness have been evened out. But since religion here is as yet only _immediate_, this difference has not yet gone back into spirit; nothing more than the _concept_ of religion is posited, and within that concept the essence is _self-consciousness_, which is all truth for itself and holds all actuality within that truth. This self-consciousness, taken as consciousness, has itself for its object; spirit, which so far knows itself only _immediately_, is therefore spirit to itself under the _form_ of _immediacy_, and the shape in which spirit appears to itself has the determinateness of _being_. {{ 368 }} This being, to be sure, is _filled_ neither with sensation nor with manifold material, nor with one-sided moments, purposes and determinations of any other kind, but with spirit, and is known by itself {{{ 635 }}} to be all truth and actuality. This _fulfillment_ is accordingly not equal to its _shape_, nor spirit as essence equal to its consciousness. Spirit becomes actual only as absolute spirit, in that it is to itself in its _truth_ as it also is in the _certainty of itself_, or in that the extremes into which spirit divides itself as consciousness stand for one another in spirit's own shape. The formation that spirit assumes as the object of its consciousness stays filled with spirit's certainty, filled with it as with substance; through this content there vanishes any prospect of the object sinking down to pure objectivity, to the form of the _negativity_ belonging to self-consciousness. Spirit's immediate unity with itself is the foundation, or pure consciousness, _within_ which consciousness steps apart. Shut up in this way within its pure self-consciousness, spirit has no existence, within religion, as the creator of _nature_ in general; what it brings forth in this movement are rather its own shapes as spirits, and these together make up the completeness of spirit's appearance, while the movement itself is the becoming of spirit's perfect actuality by way of the singular aspects of that actuality, that is, by way of its imperfect actualities.

Spirit's first actuality is the concept of religion itself, that is, religion in its _immediate_ form and hence as _natural religion_; within it spirit has knowledge of itself as its own object under a natural, immediate {{{ 636 }}} shape. The _second_, however, is necessarily this: that spirit should know itself under the shape of _sublated naturalness_, that is, of the _self_. It is therefore _the religion of art_; for it is through the _producing_ done by consciousness that the shape lifts itself into the form of the _self_, consciousness thereby intuiting its own doing, that is, the self, in its object. The _third_, finally, sublates what was one-sided in the first two; the _self_ is quite as much something _immediate_ as _the immediacy is self_. If spirit as such stands in the first under the form of consciousness, in the second — under that of self-consciousness, then in the third it stands under the form in which the two are united; it has the shape of what is _in_ and _for itself_; and since it is thus represented in the way it is in and for itself, what we have here is _revealed religion_. Although in revealed religion spirit does arrive at its true _shape_, it is precisely the _shape_ itself, together with _representation_, that remains the aspect not yet overcome, and out of it spirit has to pass over into the _concept_, so as to dissolve the form of objectivity entirely within the concept, within that which likewise encloses this its own opposite. Then spirit will have taken hold of its own concept, as we so far have only taken hold of it, and since its shape, the element in which it exists, is the concept, that shape is spirit itself.
