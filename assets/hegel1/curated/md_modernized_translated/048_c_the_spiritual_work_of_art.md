## c. The Spiritual Work of Art

The peoples' spirits, which reach awareness of their essence's shape in some particular animal, coalesce into One; and thus the several beautiful spirits of peoples are joined together into One pantheon, whose element and whose habitation is language. {{ 389 }} Within the actuality that a people's spirit possesses, pure self-intuition as _universal humanity_ takes this form: that such a spirit allies itself with the others with which nature makes it into One nation, for a shared undertaking, and that for this work it builds up a total people and with it a total heaven. Yet the universality that spirit reaches in its existence is only this first sort, the sort that has just set out from the individuality of the ethical, has not yet got beyond its own immediacy, and has not shaped One state out of these tribes. What grounds the ethical life of a people's actual spirit is partly the immediate trust that singular individuals place in the whole of their people, and partly the immediate share which everyone, regardless of the distinction of estates, takes in the resolves {{{ 677 }}} and deeds of the government. In this uniting, which at first is not into an abiding order but only for one commonly undertaken action, that freedom of all and each to have a share is _provisionally_ set to one side. Hence this first form of community amounts less to abstract thought's dominion, which would strip singular individuals of the self-conscious part they play in the whole's will and deed, than to a gathering of individualities.

This gathering of the peoples' spirits constitutes a circle of shapes that now embraces nature in its entirety along with the entire ethical world. They as well stand under the One's _supreme command_ more than under his _overlordship_. On their own account they constitute the universal substances of what, _in itself_, the _self-conscious_ essence both is and does. But this essence makes up the force, and at least to begin with the midpoint, about which those universal essences exert themselves, a midpoint that as yet appears to bind their several affairs together only by accident. Yet what already holds the ground for self-consciousness being the midpoint of those divine forces is the divine essence's own return into self-consciousness, a return that at first hides the essential unity beneath the form of an amicable outward relation between the two worlds.

The very universality that belongs to this content belongs necessarily to the form of consciousness too, {{{ 678 }}} the form in which the content makes its appearance. The cult's actual doing is no longer what is at work here; instead there is a doing raised not yet into the concept but only into _representation_, into that synthetic joining of self-conscious with external existence. _Language_, the existence of this representation, is the first language of all, the _epic_ taken as such, and it holds the universal content, if only as the world's _completeness_, and not as the _universality_ of _thought_. The _singer_ is the singular and actual one who, as this world's subject, generates it and carries it. What moves him as pathos is not nature's stupefying power but Mnemosyne: collectedness, an inwardness that has come to be, the recollection of the essence that was until now immediate. {{ 390 }} He is the organ that disappears into its own content; his own self is not what has validity, but his Muse, his universal song. In fact, though, what is present is the syllogism where the world of the gods, the extreme of universality, is knit through the middle of particularity with singularity, with the singer. That middle is the people as embodied in its heroes, who are singular human beings just as the singer is, but only _represented_ ones, and for that reason _universal_ ones as well, as the gods are, who form the free extreme of universality.

In this epic, then, what the cult brings about _in itself_, the divine's relation to the human, is quite generally _set forth_ before consciousness. Its content is an _action_ performed by the essence that is conscious of its own self. _Acting_ upsets the substance's repose {{{ 679 }}} and stirs the essence, so that the essence's simplicity is divided and opened out into a manifold world of forces both natural and ethical. That action wounds the quiet earth; it is the trench which, quickened by blood, summons up the spirits of the departed, and they, athirst for life, obtain it in the doing of self-consciousness. The business at which the general effort is directed acquires two sides: a _selflike_ side, being accomplished by a totality of actual peoples together with the individualities at their head, and a _universal_ side, being accomplished by their substantial powers. The _relation_ between the two, however, was determined a moment ago as the _synthetic_ linking together of universal and singular, in other words as _representing_. How this world is to be judged depends upon that determinateness. — Their relationship is thereby a blending that parcels out the unity of the doing inconsistently and needlessly tosses the action back and forth between the two sides. The universal powers carry the shape of individuality and with it the principle of acting; hence their working appears as a doing just as free, and proceeding just as entirely from them, as the doing of human beings does. One and the same deed, therefore, has been done by gods and by human beings alike. The earnestness of those powers is a laughable superabundance, since human beings are in fact the very force of acting individuality; — and the strain and {{{ 680 }}} labor of human beings is an equally pointless effort, since it is rather those powers that direct everything. — Mortals who live but a day, mortals who are nothing, are simultaneously the mighty _self_ that subjugates the universal essences, that injures the gods and that supplies them generally with actuality and with an interest in doing; conversely, these impotent universalities, which feed on human gifts and only through human beings get anything to do at all, are the natural essence and the stuff of all that happens, and are likewise the ethical matter and the pathos belonging to the doing. {{ 391 }} Although only individuality's free self brings their elemental natures into actuality and into a relationship set into activity, they are just as much the universal that withdraws from this linkage, that stays unlimited in its determination, that through the unconquerable elasticity of its unity wipes out the punctual character of the agent along with the agent's figurations, that keeps itself pure, and that dissolves everything individual in its own fluidity.

Just as they come to stand in this contradictory relation to the selflike nature that opposes them, so likewise does their universality fall into strife with its own determination and with that determination's relationship to others. Eternal and beautiful individuals, they rest in their own existence and are lifted clear of transience and of alien violence. — And yet they are also _determinate_ elements, _particular_ gods, {{{ 681 }}} and so they relate themselves to others. But this relationship to others, which by virtue of its opposition amounts to a quarrel with them, is a comic forgetting of their own eternal nature. — Determinateness has its roots in divine subsistence and possesses, within the bounds of that subsistence, the self-subsistence proper to a whole individuality; owing to this, their characters simultaneously forfeit the sharp edge of their own peculiarity and mingle in their ambiguity. — A purpose of activity, and the activity as well, inasmuch as both aim at another and so at a divine force that cannot be defeated, is a contingent and empty preening which likewise melts away and converts the seeming earnestness of the deed into a risk-free play, sure of itself, yielding neither result nor success. But if, within the nature that their divinity has, the negative, that is, the determinateness of that divinity, shows up merely as an inconsistency in their activity and as a contradiction of purpose with outcome, while that self-subsistent security in the face of the determinate keeps the upper hand, then for just that reason there steps opposite them the _pure force_ of the _negative_, and it steps opposite as their ultimate power, against which they are helpless. Over against mortals' _singular self_, which cannot withstand their power, they count as the universal and as the positive; and yet for that very reason the _universal self_ floats above them and above this entire world of representation, the world that owns the whole content, floating there as the _conceptless void_ of _necessity_ — an occurrence toward {{{ 682 }}} which they conduct themselves selflessly and in mourning, since natures thus _determinate_ cannot find themselves within such purity.

Such necessity, though, is the _unity of the concept_, to which the contradictory substantiality of singular moments stands subjected, a unity wherein the inconsistency and the contingency belonging to their doing are set in order and wherein the play of their actions gains earnestness and worth from those actions themselves. {{ 392 }} Loosed and on its own, the content of representation's world plays out its movement in the _middle_, gathered about the individuality of a hero who nonetheless feels his life broken in the midst of his force and beauty and who grieves as he looks ahead to an early death. For _singularity that is firm within itself and actual_ has been shut out to the extremity and split in two into its moments, moments that have not yet found one another and become united. The one singular, the _abstract_ and unactual one, is necessity, which takes no part in the life of the middle, and no more does the other, the _actual_ singular, the singer, who holds himself outside that life and goes under in his own representation. Both extremes have to come closer to the content: the one, necessity, must be filled out with the content; the other, the singer's language, must take part in it; and the content, which up to now was abandoned to itself, must gain within itself the certainty of the negative and its firm determination.

{{{ 683 }}} This higher language, _tragedy_, accordingly gathers the scattered moments of the essential and acting world into a tighter unity; the _substance_ of the divine steps apart into its shapes _in keeping with the nature of the concept_, and its _movement_ likewise accords with the concept. So far as form goes, language, by entering into the content, stops being narrative, and the content stops being a merely represented one. It is the hero himself who speaks, and what is performed sets before the listener, who is at once a spectator, human beings who are _self-conscious_, who _know_ their right and their purpose, along with the power and the will that their determinateness carries, and who know how to _say_ these. They are artists who do not, in the manner of the language that goes along with ordinary doing in actual life, pronounce unconsciously, naturally, and naively the _outward_ face of their resolve and their undertaking; instead they express the inner essence, prove the right of their acting, and the pathos they belong to they maintain with deliberation and pronounce with precision, in its universal individuality, free of contingent circumstances and of the particularity of personalities. The _existence_ of these characters consists, in the end, of _actual_ human beings, who put on the persons of the heroes and present them in speech that is actual and their own rather than narrated. Just as it is essential to the statue that human hands should have made it, so essential to his mask is the actor, — not as some outward condition that the contemplation of art would have to abstract from, — or, insofar as {{{ 684 }}} such abstraction really is to be made within that contemplation, what is being said thereby is precisely this, that art still does not have the true and proper self within it.

The _universal ground_ upon which these shapes, engendered out of the concept, carry on their movement is the consciousness of that first representing language and of its selfless, loosely dispersed content. {{ 393 }} It is the common people at large, whose wisdom finds words in the _chorus_ of _old age_; the people finds its representative in that chorus's want of strength, since the people itself amounts to nothing more than the positive, passive material set over against the individuality of the government. Going without the power of the negative, it cannot hold together or tame the riches and the motley plenitude of divine life; it lets that life scatter instead, and in its hymns of veneration it extols now this and now again another of the singular moments, each as a self-subsistent god. But where it feels the earnestness of the concept as that concept marches across these shapes and shatters them, and where it is given to witness how ill things turn out for the gods it has extolled once they hazard themselves on this ground where the concept holds sway, it does not itself become the negative power that intervenes by acting; it keeps rather to the selfless thought of that power, to a consciousness of _alien fate_, and it brings forward the empty wish for reassurance and the feeble speech of soothing. Given its _fear_ before the higher powers, substance's immediate {{{ 685 }}} arms, before the struggle they wage with one another, and before necessity's simple self, which grinds them down as it grinds down the living beings tied to them; — given its _compassion_ for those living beings, which it simultaneously knows to be one and the same with itself: what it has is only the idle horror at this movement, a regret just as helpless, and, at the end, the empty repose of submitting to a necessity whose work it grasps neither as the necessary action of character nor as the doing of the absolute essence within its own self.

Upon this spectating consciousness, as upon the indifferent ground of representing, spirit makes its entrance not in a scattered manifoldness but in the simple splitting-in-two of the concept, and its substance accordingly shows itself torn apart into two extreme powers. Simultaneously, these elemental _universal_ essences are self-conscious _individualities_ — heroes who invest their consciousness in one of these powers, who possess in it the determinateness of their character, and who constitute its activation and its actuality. — This universal individualizing descends, as was recalled, still further to the immediate actuality of existence proper, and it sets itself before a throng of spectators, for whom the chorus is their counter-image, or rather their own representation putting itself into words.

Both the content and the movement of the spirit that is here an object to itself have come under consideration already, as the ethical substance's nature and {{{ 686 }}} realization. Within its religion spirit gains consciousness about itself, or exhibits itself to its own consciousness in a purer form and a simpler configuration. {{ 394 }} If, then, the ethical substance split itself in two through its concept, so far as its _content_ goes, into the two powers determined as _divine_ and _human_, or as subterranean and upper right — the first being the _family_, the second the _power of the state_ — and if of these the first had the _feminine_ and the other the _masculine character_, then the circle of gods, formerly multiform and unsteady in its determinations, narrows itself down to these powers, which by this determination are brought nearer to individuality proper. For the whole's earlier scattering into the many abstract forces that appear substantialized amounts to the _dissolution_ of the _subject_, a subject that comprehends them merely as _moments_ within its own self; individuality is on that account no more than the surface form of those essences. Conversely, any difference among the _characters_ going beyond the one named must be reckoned to a personality that is contingent and in itself external.

The essence divides itself simultaneously with respect to its _form_, that is, with respect to _knowledge_. The _acting_ spirit confronts, as consciousness, the object upon which it is active, an object determined by that very fact as the _negative_ of the knower; through this the agent finds itself in the opposition between knowledge and non-knowledge. It draws its purpose from its character and knows that purpose {{{ 687 }}} as the ethical essentiality; but owing to the determinateness of its character it knows only the One power of the substance, while the other stays hidden from it. Present actuality is on that account one thing _in itself_ and something else for consciousness; in this relation the upper right and the lower right acquire the meaning of a power that knows and makes itself manifest to consciousness, and of a power that conceals itself and lurks in ambush. The One is the _side of light_, the oracle's god, who according to his natural moment sprang from the sun that lights up all things, who knows all and makes all manifest — _Phoebus_, and _Zeus_, his father. Yet the orders issued by this truth-speaking god, and his declarations of what _is_, prove rather to be deceptive. For in its concept such knowledge is immediately non-knowledge, since in acting _consciousness_ is, within its own self, precisely this opposition. The man who could unlock the riddling Sphinx herself, and the man who trusts with a child's trust, are for this reason dispatched to ruin by what the god manifests to them. This priestess, from whom the beautiful god speaks, is nothing other than those double-meaning sisters of fate who use their promises to drive men into crime and who, by the double tongue of what they had given out as a guarantee, cheat whoever trusted the manifest sense. {{ 395 }} This is why a consciousness purer than the second sort, which puts its faith in witches, and more deliberate {{{ 688 }}} and more thorough than the first sort, the sort that relies on the priestess and on the beautiful god, hesitates over revenge when the father's own spirit has made a revelation about the crime that murdered him, and stages still further proofs — on the ground that this revealing spirit might just as well be the devil.

Such mistrust has its grounds, for the knowing consciousness puts itself into the opposition of its own certainty to the objective essence. The ethical's right, that actuality is nothing _in itself_ over against the absolute law, comes to experience that its knowledge is one-sided, that its law is merely the law belonging to its own character, and that it laid hold of only the one power of the substance. The action is itself this inversion whereby what is _known_ turns into its _opposite_, into _being_; it is the sudden turning of the right of character and of knowledge into the right of the opposed one, to which the former is tied within the substance's essence — into the Erinys of the other, roused in hostility. Of the other's power and character, that is. This _lower_ right shares the throne with _Zeus_ and enjoys a standing equal to that of the manifest right and the knowing god.

It is to these three essences that acting individuality restricts the chorus's world of gods. The first is the _substance_, the power of the hearth, the spirit of family piety, and equally the universal power belonging to the state and to the government. {{{ 689 }}} Since it is to the substance as such that this difference belongs, the difference does not individualize itself for representation into two distinct shapes; in actuality it has instead the two persons of its characters. By contrast, the difference between knowledge and non-knowledge falls within _each_ of the _actual self-consciousnesses_ — distributing itself between two individual shapes only in abstraction, within the element of universality. For the hero's self has existence solely as a consciousness that is whole, and so it is essentially the _whole_ difference belonging to the form; its substance, though, is a determinate one, so that only one side of the content's difference belongs to it. Hence in _representation_ the two sides of consciousness, which possess in actuality no individuality of their own separately belonging to each, each acquire a particular shape of its own: one the shape of the god who makes manifest, the other the shape of the Erinys who keeps herself concealed. In part the two enjoy equal honor, and in part Zeus, the _shape_ of the _substance_, is the necessity that governs how the two stand in _relation_ to each other. Substance is that relation whereby knowledge exists for itself yet possesses its truth in the simple, whereby the difference through which actual consciousness comes to be possesses its ground in the inner essence that extinguishes that difference, and whereby the self-clear _assurance_ of _certainty_ possesses its confirmation in _oblivion_.

Consciousness unlocked this opposition by acting; acting according to the manifest knowledge, {{{ 690 }}} it undergoes that knowledge's deceit, and, devoted in respect of content to the one attribute of the substance, it violated the other and thereby granted the other a right against itself. In following the knowing god it laid hold rather of what was not manifest, and it pays the price for having trusted a knowledge whose ambiguity, since ambiguity is that knowledge's very nature, had to be at hand _for it_ too, together with a _warning_ about it. {{ 396 }} The priestess's raving, the witches' unhuman shape, the voice of the tree and of the bird, dreams, and so on are not modes in which truth makes its appearance; they are signs that warn of deception, of unreflectedness, of the singularity and contingency of this knowledge. Or, and this is the same thing, the opposed power that consciousness violates is at hand as a pronounced law and a valid right, whether the family's law or the state's; consciousness, however, followed its own knowledge and hid what was manifest from itself, whereas the truth of the powers of content and of consciousness that step forth against one another is the result that both have equal right and therefore, within the opposition which acting brings forth, equal wrong. The movement of the doing proves their unity in the reciprocal ruin that overtakes both powers and the self-conscious characters alike. The opposition's reconciliation with itself is _Lethe_ in the _underworld_, by way of death — or _Lethe_ in the _upper world_, by way of acquittal, not from guilt, which consciousness cannot disown, since it {{{ 691 }}} acted, but from the crime, together with the expiatory calm that acquittal brings. Both are _oblivion_, the having-vanished of the actuality and the doing that belonged to the substance's powers, to their individualities, and to those powers that belong to the abstract thought of good and of evil; for not one of them taken singly is the essence; the essence is rather the whole at rest within its own self, the unmoving unity of fate, the quiet existence and with it the inactivity and lifelessness of family and government, the equal honor and with it the indifferent unactuality of Apollo and of the Erinys, and the return of their inspiriting and their activity into the simple Zeus.

Heaven's depopulation is completed by this fate — the depopulation of that thoughtless blending of individuality with essence, — a blending through which the essence's doing appears as inconsistent, contingent, unworthy of the essence; for individuality, adhering to the essence only on the surface, is what is unessential. The expulsion of such essenceless representations, which philosophers of antiquity called for, thus begins already in tragedy at large, in that the concept governs the dividing of the substance, so that individuality here is what is essential, while the determinations count as the absolute characters. For that reason the self-consciousness that tragedy represents recognizes and acknowledges {{{ 692 }}} only One supreme power, and it knows this Zeus solely as the state's power or the hearth's, and, within the opposition belonging to knowledge, solely as the father of that knowledge of the _particular_ which is becoming a shape — and, further, as the Zeus of oath and Erinys, the Zeus of the _universal_, of an inwardness dwelling in concealment. {{ 397 }} Those moments, by contrast, which scatter further out of the concept into representation and which the chorus lets count one after another, are not the hero's pathos; for him they sink down to mere passion — to contingent, essenceless moments that the selfless chorus may well extol, but that are incapable of making up the heroes' character or of being pronounced and honored by them as their essence.

But the divine essence's own persons, and with them the characters of its substance, also converge into the simplicity of the unconscious. Over against self-consciousness, this necessity has as its determination that it is the negative power over every shape that steps forward, a power in which self-consciousness does not cognize itself but rather goes under. The self steps forward only as apportioned to the _characters_, not as the middle of the movement. Self-consciousness, however, which is the simple _certainty_ it has of itself, is in truth the negative power: it is the unity of Zeus, that is, the unity of the _substantial_ essence with _abstract_ necessity, the spiritual unity into which all things go back. Because actual self-consciousness is still held distinct from substance and from fate, it amounts _in part_ to that chorus, {{{ 693 }}} or better, to that spectating throng, whom this movement of divine life fills with fear as something _alien_, or in whom, when it comes close, that movement produces nothing beyond the emotion of a _compassion_ that does not act. In part, insofar as consciousness joins in the acting and belongs among the characters, this uniting is an external one, a _hypocrisy_, since the true uniting, that of the self, of fate, and of the substance, is not yet at hand; the hero who steps forward before the spectator breaks apart into his mask and his actor, into the person and the actual self.

The heroes' self-consciousness has to come out from behind its mask and exhibit how it knows that it is itself the fate of the chorus's gods, and the fate of the absolute powers themselves, and how it is no longer severed from the chorus, from universal consciousness.

_Comedy_, then, has to begin with this side: that the gods' fate is what actual self-consciousness exhibits itself to be. These elemental essences, being _universal_ moments, are no self and are not actual. Granted, they are furnished with the form of individuality, yet that form is merely imagined into them and does not belong to them in and for themselves; a moment so abstract does not serve the actual self as substance and content. The self, the subject, is therefore raised above such a moment as above a singular property; wearing this mask, it pronounces the irony of a mask that would like to be something {{{ 694 }}} in its own right. {{ 398 }} The preening of universal essentiality has been given away to the self; it shows itself captive within an actuality and drops the mask at the very point where it means to be something proper. Stepping forward here in its meaning as something actual, the self plays with the mask that it puts on for the once in order to be its person — but out of this semblance it emerges again just as quickly in a nakedness and an ordinariness of its own, and these show it to be no different from the self proper, the actor, and no different from the spectator either.

This universal dissolving of shaped essentiality as such within its individuality grows more serious in content, and with that more wanton and more bitter, in proportion as the content carries its more serious and more necessary meaning. Within the divine substance the meaning of natural essentiality and that of ethical essentiality are united. So far as the natural goes, actual self-consciousness already shows itself, in putting nature to use for its finery, its dwelling, and so on, and in feasting on its sacrifice, to be that fate which has had the secret betrayed to it of how things stand with nature's self-essentiality; through the mystery of bread and of wine it appropriates that self-essentiality along with what the inner essence means, and in comedy it is aware of the irony this meaning has generally. — Insofar, now, as this meaning holds ethical essentiality within it, it is in part the people, taken on its {{{ 695 }}} two sides, that of the state or the demos proper and that of family singularity; — and in part it is self-conscious pure knowledge, the universal's rational thinking. — This _demos_, the universal mass, knowing itself as lord and ruler and as the understanding and insight that command respect, coerces and befools itself by way of the particularity of its own actuality, and it puts on display the ludicrous contrast that holds between its opinion of itself and the existence it immediately has, between its necessity and its contingency, between its universality and its commonness. When the principle of its singularity, cut off from the universal, comes out in the proper shape of actuality and openly lays claim to the communal being, whose secret damage it is, and arranges that communal being to suit itself, then all the more immediately does the contrast betray itself between the universal taken as a theory and what practice is actually about, namely the complete liberation of immediate singularity's purposes from the universal order and the mockery those purposes heap on that order.

Rational _thinking_ lifts the divine essence clear of its contingent shape; and, set against the conceptless wisdom of the chorus, which trots out assorted moral maxims and lets a whole crowd of laws and of determinate concepts of duty and of right pass as valid, it elevates them into simple ideas: the _beautiful_ and the _good_. — What this {{{ 696 }}} abstraction moves toward is a consciousness of the dialectic that these maxims and laws carry in them, and thereby a consciousness that the absolute validity in which they earlier appeared is vanishing. {{ 399 }} As that contingent determination, and that superficial individuality, which representation had lent to the divine essentialities vanish, nothing is left to them on their _natural_ side but their immediate existence in its nakedness; they are clouds, a vapor in the act of vanishing, just as those representations are. Having become, in respect of their _thought_ essentiality, _simple_ thoughts, the _beautiful_ and the _good_, they put up with being filled by any content one pleases. The force of dialectical knowledge abandons determinate laws and maxims of acting to the pleasure and the levity of the youth thus — by this very means — seduced, and it hands weapons for deception to the anxiety and care of an old age confined to the singularity of life. So the pure thoughts of what is beautiful and what is good put on the comic spectacle of turning empty by way of their liberation from opinion (which contains both their determinateness as content and their absolute determinateness, the holding fast of consciousness), and, by that very liberation, of turning into the plaything of the opinion and the caprice of contingent individuality.

Here, then, that formerly unconscious fate, which consists in empty repose and in oblivion {{{ 697 }}} and which stands severed from self-consciousness, is united with the latter. The _singular self_ is that negative force in and through which the gods vanish, and their moments with them, existent nature together with the thoughts of their determinations; and yet it is not the emptiness of this vanishing, but maintains itself within that very nullity, is at home with itself, and is the one actuality there is. In this self the religion of art has reached its completion and has gone back into itself entirely. Because what exhibits itself as this absolute power is the singular consciousness in the certainty it has of itself, that power has lost the form of something _represented_, something _severed_ from _consciousness_ at large and alien to it, the form that the statue had, and the living beautiful corporeality, and the epic's content, and tragedy's powers and persons; — nor is the unity here the _unconscious_ one belonging to cult and mysteries; — rather the actor's proper self coincides with his person, as does the spectator, who is wholly at home in what is represented to him and watches himself at play. What this self-consciousness intuits is this: that whatever takes on the form of essentiality against it dissolves within it instead, dissolves in its thinking, in its existence, and in its doing, and is given up; it is everything universal going back into the certainty it has of itself, a certainty that accordingly is that perfect fearlessness and {{{ 698 }}} essencelessness of everything alien, a well-being and a letting-oneself-be-well on the part of consciousness such as is found nowhere else outside this comedy.
