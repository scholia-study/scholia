## {{{ 434 }}} I. The World of Self-Estranged Spirit

This spirit's world falls apart into a twofold world; the first is the world of actuality, or of spirit's own estrangement; the second is the one it erects for itself, rising above the first, within pure consciousness's ether. Being _opposed_ to that estrangement, this second world is for precisely that reason not free of it, but is rather just estrangement's other form, the form that consists precisely in having one's consciousness in two kinds of world, and that spans them both. So what is under examination here is not the self-consciousness of absolute essence as it is _in_ and _for itself_, nor religion, but rather _faith_, to the extent that faith amounts to _flight_ from the actual world and hence is not _in_ and _for itself_. {{ 267 }} Such flight away from the realm of what is present is thus immediately, in its own self, twofold. The element into which spirit lifts itself is pure consciousness; but that element belongs not to _faith_ alone, it belongs equally to the _concept_; the two accordingly make their entrance together, and faith comes under consideration only in its opposition to the concept.
