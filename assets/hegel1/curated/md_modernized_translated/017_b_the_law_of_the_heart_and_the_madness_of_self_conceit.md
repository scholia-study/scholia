## {{{ 305 }}} b. The Law of the Heart, and the Madness of Self-Conceit

{{ 202 }} Necessity, as it is in truth in self-consciousness, is precisely what it is for the latter's new shape, wherein self-consciousness stands to itself as the necessary; _immediately_ it takes itself to have the _universal_, or the _law_, within it, and this law, given the determination that it lies _immediately_ within the being-for-itself of consciousness, goes by the name of the _law_ of the _heart_. This shape is, _for itself_, essence as _singularity_, just as the previous shape was, yet it is richer by one determination, namely that this _being-for-itself_ is valid for it as something necessary or universal.

The law, then, which is immediately self-consciousness's own, or a heart which nonetheless has a law within it, is the _purpose_ that self-consciousness sets out to actualize. It remains to be seen whether the actualization will correspond to this concept, and whether in it self-consciousness will experience this law of its own as the essence.

This heart has an actuality standing over against it; for within the heart the law is as yet only _for itself_, not actualized, and therefore at the same time an _other_ than what the concept is. By that very fact this other is determined as an actuality standing opposed to whatever is to be actualized, and so as the _contradiction_ between the _law_ and _singu_ {{{ 306 }}} _larity_. So it is, for one thing, a law that weighs down upon singular individuality, an ordering of the world grounded in violence, standing in contradiction with the law of the heart itself; — and, for another, a humanity suffering beneath such an ordering, a humanity subject to an alien necessity rather than following the law of the heart. — That actuality, showing itself _over against_ the present shape of consciousness, is plainly nothing else than the relationship already gone through, the relationship split in two between individuality and its truth, a relationship of merciless necessity under which individuality is crushed. _For us_ the preceding movement comes to stand over against the new shape, since that shape has in itself issued from the movement, so that the moment from which it derives is necessary to it; to the shape, however, the movement appears as something _already found on hand_, for it possesses no consciousness of its own _origin_, and its essence lies rather in being _for itself_, that is, in being the negative over against this positive in-itself.

This individuality, then, is bent upon sublating the necessity that contradicts the law of the heart, together with the suffering that necessity brings with it. {{ 203 }} It is therefore no longer the frivolity of the earlier shape, which wanted nothing but singular pleasure; it is instead the seriousness of a lofty purpose, seeking its pleasure in the display of its own _excellent_ essence and in producing the _welfare of humanity_. That {{{ 307 }}} which it brings into actuality is the law itself, so that its pleasure is at once the pleasure universal to all hearts. The two are for it _undivided_: pleasure on its part is what conforms to law, while actualizing the law of humanity at large prepares its own singular pleasure. For within this individuality itself, the necessary and individuality are _immediately_ one and the same; the law of the heart is precisely this law. Nothing has as yet displaced individuality from its position, and the unity of the two has come about neither through any mediating movement between them nor through discipline. The actualization of the immediate, _undisciplined_ essence passes for the display of an excellence and for the producing of the welfare of humanity.

The law which stands over against the law of the heart, by contrast, is severed from the heart and free for itself. Humanity as it belongs to that law has no life in the happy unity of law with heart; its life is one either of cruel separation and suffering, or at the very least of a privation, the privation of enjoying _itself_ while _complying_ with the law, and the lack of any consciousness of its own excellence while _transgressing_ it. Since that divine and human ordering, which has power on its side, stands severed from the heart, the heart takes it for a mere _semblance_, one destined to lose what still attends it, namely its power and its actuality. In {{{ 308 }}} its _content_ this ordering may of course chance to fall in with the law of the heart; where it does, the heart can put up with it; yet what counts as essence for the heart is not lawfulness purely as such, but rather that within the ordering it possesses consciousness _of itself_, that _it_ has been satisfied there. But wherever universal necessity has a content out of accord with the heart, that necessity, in respect of its content as well, is nothing in itself and has to yield before the law of the heart.

So the individual _carries out_ the law of its heart; that law becomes a _universal ordering_, and pleasure becomes an actuality lawful in and for itself. But the law, in becoming actual, has in fact escaped the individual; what it immediately becomes is nothing but the very relationship that was to have been sublated. The law of the heart ceases, precisely by being actualized, to be the law of the _heart_. For in the process it acquires the form of _being_ and has become a _universal power_ to which _this_ particular heart is indifferent, with the result that the individual, in the very act of _setting up_ what is _its own_ ordering, no longer finds that ordering to be its own. By actualizing its law, therefore, it does not bring forth _its_ law; since the actual ordering is _in itself_ its own but for it something alien, all it brings forth is its own entanglement in that actual ordering, and indeed in an ordering that confronts it as a superior power, not merely alien but hostile. {{ 204 }} — Through its deed the individual posits itself _in_, or rather {{{ 309 }}} _as_, the universal element of the actuality that has being, and by its own reckoning its deed is to carry the worth of a universal ordering. But with that it has let the deed go _free_ of itself; the deed grows on as universality for itself and purges itself of singularity; the individual, which wants to cognize universality only in the form of its immediate being-for-itself, thus does not cognize itself in this free universality, even though it belongs to that universality all the same, for the universality is its own doing. This doing therefore takes on the inverted meaning of _contradicting_ the universal ordering, since the individual's deed is meant to be the deed of _its_ singular heart and not a free universal actuality; and yet at the same time the individual has in fact _recognized_ that ordering, for its doing amounts to positing its essence as _free actuality_, that is, to recognizing actuality as its essence.

By the concept of what it does, the individual has itself settled the closer manner in which actual universality, the universality to which it has made itself belong, turns against it. Its deed, as _actuality_, belongs to the universal; the content of the deed, however, is its own individuality, which wills to maintain itself as this _singular_ individuality opposed to the universal. No determinate law is at issue whose setting up would be under discussion; what is at issue is that universality and the singular heart stand in immediate unity, a thought elevated into law and claiming to be valid, the thought that in whatever counts as law {{{ 310 }}} _each heart_ must cognize itself. Yet it is this individual's heart alone that has posited its actuality in its own deed, a deed expressing for it _its being-for-itself_, or _its pleasure_. This deed is to be valid immediately as something universal; that is, in truth it is particular and wears universality only as a form, its _particular_ content being supposed to count _as such_ for universal. In this content, accordingly, the others find carried out not the law of their own heart but instead that _of someone else_, and by the very universal law which says that each is to find its heart in whatever is law, they turn against the actuality which _this individual_ set up, exactly as it had turned against theirs. Where at first it found only the rigid law, the individual now finds the very hearts of human beings ranged against its excellent intentions, and finds them detestable.

Since this consciousness knows universality as yet only in its _immediate_ form, and knows necessity only as a necessity of the _heart_, it has no acquaintance with what actualization and efficacy are by nature, namely that, as what _has being_, they are in their truth the _universal in itself_, wherein the singularity of consciousness, which entrusts itself to them so as to _be_ as _this_ immediate _singularity_, instead goes under; and so, in place of _its own being_, what it gains within being is estrangement _from itself_. {{ 205 }} What it fails to cognize itself in, however, is {{{ 311 }}} not dead necessity any more, but necessity enlivened by universal individuality. It had taken this divine and human ordering, which it found already in force, for a dead actuality in which those who belong to it would have no consciousness of themselves, any more than the individual has, fixing its heart as it does as this heart that has being for itself and stands opposed to the universal; what it finds instead is that the ordering is enlivened by the consciousness of all, and that it constitutes the law of all hearts. It undergoes the experience that actuality is an enlivened ordering, and it does so, indeed, by the very act of actualizing the law of its heart; for this means nothing other than that individuality makes itself into an object as something universal, an object in which, however, it does not cognize itself.

What emerges as the true for this shape of self-consciousness out of its experience _contradicts_ what the shape is _for itself_. Yet what the shape is for itself has, in its own eyes, the form of absolute universality, namely the law of the heart, immediately at one with _self_-consciousness. The subsisting, living ordering is at the same time no less its _own essence_ and its own work; it produces nothing else than this ordering; and it stands united with self-consciousness with equal immediacy. Self-consciousness, then, belonging in this manner to a doubled and mutually opposed essentiality, is in contradiction with its own self and shattered at its very core. The {{{ 312 }}} law of _this_ heart is the sole thing in which self-consciousness cognizes itself; but through the actualizing of that law the ordering valid for all has equally become for it its very own _essence_ and its very own _actuality_; what contradicts itself within its consciousness therefore confronts it in both cases under the form of essence and of an actuality belonging to it.

As it gives voice to this moment of a downfall of which it is conscious, and thereby to what its experience has resulted in, it exhibits itself as just this inward inverting of itself, as the derangement of a consciousness whose essence is immediately un-essence and whose actuality is immediately unactuality. — Derangement must not be taken to lie in this, that something essenceless gets held for essential and something unactual for actual at all, such that whatever is essential or actual for one person would fail to be so for someone else, with the consciousness of actuality and of unactuality, or of essentiality and unessentiality, drifting apart from one another. — Suppose something really does count as actual and essential for consciousness at large, yet not for me: then, together with my consciousness of its nullity, I also have, being myself consciousness at large, a consciousness of its actuality, — and when both are held fast the result is a unity, and that unity is what madness amounts to in general. In madness, though, what is deranged is merely {{{ 313 }}} an _object_ for consciousness, and not consciousness itself in and for itself. {{ 206 }} In the result of the experience that has come out here, by contrast, consciousness within its law is conscious _of itself_ as this actual consciousness; and since at the same time that very essentiality, that very actuality, is _estranged_ from it, then, being self-consciousness, being absolute actuality, it is conscious of its own unactuality; or rather, the two sides, in their contradiction, hold immediately for it as _its essence_, which is therefore deranged in its inmost depths.

The heart's throbbing on behalf of the welfare of humanity therefore passes over into the rampaging of deranged self-conceit, into the fury of a consciousness bent on preserving itself against its own destruction, and doing so by hurling out of itself the invertedness which it is, and straining to view and to pronounce that invertedness as another. So what it pronounces the universal ordering to be is something devised by fanatical priests, by despots wallowing in luxury and by servants of theirs who make up for their own debasement by debasing and crushing those beneath them, and then wielded to the nameless misery of a deceived humanity: an inversion, that is, of the law of the heart together with the heart's happiness. — In this derangement of its own, consciousness pronounces _individuality_ to be what does the deranging and the inverting, though _an alien_ and _contingent_ individuality. But the heart, or _the immediate, wanting-to-be-universal {{{ 314 }}} singularity of consciousness_, is itself this deranging and inverted element, and its doing merely brings it about that this contradiction becomes a contradiction for _its own_ consciousness. For the truth, in its eyes, is the law of the heart — a thing merely _meant_, which, unlike the subsisting ordering, has not withstood the daylight, but rather goes to ruin the moment it shows itself to that daylight. This law of its own was supposed to have _actuality_; in that respect the law as _actuality_, as a _valid ordering_, is for it purpose and essence, yet just as immediately _actuality_, precisely the law as a _valid ordering_, is for it rather what is null. — In the same way its _own_ actuality, _it itself_ as singularity of consciousness, is essence to itself; but its purpose is to posit that actuality as _having being_, so that immediately it is rather its self as non-singular that is essence for it, or the purpose as law, and therein as a universality which it is to be for its own consciousness. — This concept of its own becomes its object through its doing; hence it experiences its self rather as the unactual, and unactuality as its actuality. It is therefore no contingent, alien individuality that is at work here, but precisely this heart, which on every side within itself is what is inverted and what inverts.

But since immediately universal individuality is what is inverted and what inverts, this universal ordering, itself the law of all _hearts_, that is, of what is inverted, {{{ 315 }}} is no less in itself inverted, exactly as raving derangement declared; in the first place, the ordering proves to be the _law_ of all hearts through the resistance that one heart's law meets in the other singular individuals. {{ 207 }} When the subsisting laws are upheld against one individual's law, this is because they are no dead, empty, unconscious necessity, but rather spiritual universality and substance, a substance in which those who give it its actuality live as individuals and possess consciousness of themselves; hence, however much such people may complain of this ordering as though it ran counter to the inner law, and may set the heart's opinions against it, they cling to it in their hearts as to their own essence, and were the ordering taken from them, or were they to put themselves outside of it, all would be lost to them. Because just here lie the actuality and the power of the public ordering, this ordering appears as the self-equal essence enlivened throughout, with individuality as the form it takes. — But this ordering is just as much what is inverted.

For since this ordering is the law of all hearts, since every individual is immediately this universal, it constitutes an actuality that is nothing but the actuality of individuality _that has being for itself_, or of the heart. A consciousness that sets up the law of its own heart accordingly runs into resistance from the others, since it contradicts the _no less singular_ {{{ 316 }}} laws belonging to their own hearts, and in resisting these others do nothing but set up their own law and press its validity. The _universal_ that is on hand comes therefore to no more than a universal resisting and a mutual combat, everyone against everyone, in which each presses the validity of its own singularity and yet never arrives at it, since that singularity runs into the same resistance and is reciprocally dissolved by the others. So what passes for a public _ordering_ is this universal state of feud, in which everyone snatches for himself what he can, exercises justice upon the singularity of the others and secures his own, which just as surely vanishes at the hands of others. This is _the way of the world_, a semblance of some abiding course, which is really only a _merely meant universality_, while its content is in truth the essenceless play in which singularities are fixed and dissolved again.

If we set the two sides of the universal ordering against each other, the content of this last universality turns out to be restless individuality, individuality for which opinion, or singularity, counts as law, for which the actual is unactual and the unactual actual. And yet this same individuality constitutes the ordering's _side of actuality_, since to it belongs the _being-for-itself_ of individuality. — On the other side stands the _universal_ as _quiescent_ essence, but for just that reason only as something _inner_, which is not nothing at all and yet is no actuality either, and which {{{ 317 }}} can attain actuality itself only when the individuality that has arrogated actuality to itself is sublated. This shape of consciousness, becoming, within the law, within what is true and good _in itself_, not singularity but only _essence_, while knowing individuality as what is inverted and what inverts, and therefore obliged to sacrifice the singularity of consciousness, is _virtue_.
