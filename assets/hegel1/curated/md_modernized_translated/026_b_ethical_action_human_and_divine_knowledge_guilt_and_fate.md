## b. Ethical Action, Human and Divine Knowledge, Guilt and Fate

Given how the opposition is constituted within this realm, however, self-consciousness has not yet stepped forward in its right as _singular individuality_; inside that realm it counts, from the one side, merely as _universal will_, and from the other as the family's _blood_; _this singular one_ counts merely as the _unactual shade_. — _As yet no deed_ has been performed; the deed, however, is the _actual self_. — It disturbs the ethical world's calm organization and its movement. Within that world, order and the accord of its two essences, each corroborating and completing the other, is converted by the deed into a transition of terms that are _opposed_, in which each demonstrates its own nullity {{{ 404 }}} and the nullity of the other rather than any corroboration; — it turns into the negative movement, that is, into the eternal necessity of terrible _fate_, a fate that swallows into the abyss of its _simplicity_ the divine law along with the human, and likewise the two self-consciousnesses in which those powers possess their existence, — and which, for us, goes over into the _absolute being-for-itself_ of purely singular self-consciousness.

The _ground_ that this movement starts out from — and upon which it runs its course, is the realm of ethical life; the _activity_ of the movement, however, is self-consciousness. Being _ethical_ consciousness, it is the _simple, pure directing_ of itself upon ethical essentiality, or duty. No arbitrariness resides in it, and no struggle either, no indecision, since the giving of laws and the testing of laws have both been surrendered; for it, ethical essentiality is instead what is immediate, unshakable, and free of all contradiction. {{ 252 }} It therefore offers neither the sorry spectacle of finding itself caught in a collision of passion with duty, nor the comic one of a collision of duty with duty, — a collision whose content is the very same as the one obtaining between duty and passion; for passion admits of being represented as duty quite as readily, since duty, once consciousness has withdrawn out of duty's immediate substantial essentiality back into itself, turns into something formally universal into which any content whatever fits equally well, as came out above. What makes the collision {{{ 405 }}} of duties comic is that it gives voice to the contradiction, namely of an _opposed absolute_, and hence to something absolute and, immediately, to the nullity of that supposed absolute, or duty. — The ethical consciousness, though, knows what its task is; and it stands decided, be that for belonging to the divine law or to the human one. The immediacy of this decidedness amounts to a being-_in-itself_, and so carries with it the sense of a natural being as well, as we saw earlier; what allots the one sex to the one law and the other sex to the other is nature, not the contingency of circumstance or of choice, — or, put the other way around, it is the two ethical powers that give themselves their individual existence and realization in the two sexes.

Two things now follow: for one thing ethical life has its essential subsistence in this immediate _decidedness_, so that for consciousness the one law alone is the essence; for another, the ethical powers possess actuality in the _self_ of consciousness; and by this they take on the meaning of _shutting each other out_ and of standing _opposed_; — within self-consciousness they are _for themselves_, whereas within the ***realm*** of ethical life they are only _in themselves_. Since ethical consciousness stands _decided_ for one of the two, it is essentially _character;_ the equal _essentiality_ of the two is not present for it; hence the opposition shows up as an _unhappy_ collision between duty and a merely rightless _actuality_. Ethical consciousness, {{{ 406 }}} being self-consciousness, stands inside this opposition, and as such it also sets out to bring that opposed actuality by violence under the law it belongs to, or else to deceive it. Because each sees the right lying on its own side alone and the wrong on the opposite side, the one of the two that belongs to the divine law catches sight, over on that other side, of a human and contingent _violent behavior_, while the one assigned to the human law catches sight there of the stubbornness and _disobedience_ of an inward being-for-itself; for the government's commands are the universal, publicly manifest sense that lies in the daylight, whereas what the other law wills is the subterranean sense, shut up in inwardness, a sense that in its existence makes its appearance as the will of singularity and that, contradicting the first, is sacrilege.

In consciousness there thereby arises the opposition of the _known_ to the _not-known_, and, in the substance, of the _conscious_ to what is _unconscious_; and the absolute _right_ belonging to ethical _self-consciousness_ enters into strife with the divine _right_ of the _essence_. {{ 253 }} To self-consciousness qua consciousness, objective actuality as such has essence; taken according to its substance, however, self-consciousness is the unity of itself with what stands opposed to it; and it is the substance of which ethical self-consciousness is the consciousness; the object, standing opposed to self-consciousness, has for that reason forfeited altogether the meaning {{{ 407 }}} of having essence for itself. The spheres in which the object counts merely as a _thing_ disappeared long ago, and so likewise have those spheres in which consciousness fastens something down out of itself and elevates a singular moment into the essence. A force of its own belongs to actuality in the face of such one-sidedness; actuality is leagued with truth against consciousness, and only then does it set before consciousness what the truth is. Ethical consciousness, however, has drained from the bowl of absolute substance a forgetting of every one-sidedness of being-for-itself, of its purposes and of the concepts peculiar to it, and in that Stygian water it has simultaneously drowned every essentiality of its own and all self-subsistent significance belonging to objective actuality. Its absolute right, accordingly, is this: that in acting according to the ethical law it should come upon nothing else in that realization than the fulfilling of that law itself, and the deed exhibit nothing else than what ethical doing is. — Since the ethical is at one and the same time the absolute _essence_ and the absolute _power_, it can suffer no inversion of its content. If it were the absolute _essence_ alone, without the power, individuality could indeed work an inversion upon it; but individuality, as ethical consciousness, gave up inverting when it gave up one-sided being-for-itself; and in the reverse case bare power would be the thing inverted, inverted by the essence, supposing it were still a being-for-itself of that kind. For the sake of this unity {{{ 408 }}} individuality amounts to the substance's pure form, the substance being the content, and doing amounts to the passage out of thought into actuality merely as the movement of an opposition without essence, an opposition whose moments possess no particular content or essentiality differing one from the other. So the absolute right of ethical consciousness lies in this: that the _deed_, which is the _shape_ its _actuality_ takes, should be nothing other than what it _knows_.

The ethical essence, however, has cleft itself into two laws, and consciousness, whose relating to the law is undivided, is allotted to one of them only. This _simple_ consciousness stands upon the absolute right that to it, as ethical, the essence should have _appeared_ just as the essence is _in itself_; and in the same way that essence stands upon the right of its _reality_, that is, upon being something doubled. Yet this right of the essence does not confront self-consciousness as something located elsewhere; it is rather self-consciousness's own essence; there alone does it possess its existence and its power, and what opposes it is the _deed_ of the _latter_. {{ 254 }} For self-consciousness, precisely by being itself as self and stepping forward to the deed, lifts itself clear of _simple immediacy_ and posits the _splitting-in-two_ on its own account. By the deed it surrenders that determinateness of ethical life which consists in being the simple certainty of an immediate truth, and it posits a separation within itself: into itself as the active one and into the actuality that stands over against it and is negative {{{ 409 }}} for it. Through the deed, then, it turns into _guilt_. For that deed constitutes its own _doing_, and doing constitutes its ownmost essence; and _guilt_ takes on the meaning of _crime_ besides: being simple ethical consciousness, it has turned toward the one law while refusing the other, and it violates the other by what it does. — _Guilt_ is not some indifferent, two-sided essence, as though the deed, in the shape it _actually_ presents in the daylight, might be the _doing_ of one's own self or might just as well not be, as though something external and contingent could get attached to the doing without being part of it, so that on that side the doing would be innocent. On the contrary, doing is itself just this splitting-in-two, namely the positing of itself for itself and, over against that, of an alien external actuality: that such an actuality is, belongs to doing itself and exists through it. So the only innocent thing is not-doing, the sort of being a stone has, and not even that of a child. — In respect of content, however, the ethical _action_ carries the moment of crime within it, since it leaves unsublated the _natural_ apportioning of the two laws between the two sexes and instead, being an _undivided_ directing toward the law, stays inside _natural immediacy_; and as doing it makes this one-sidedness into guilt, the guilt of laying hold of only the one side of the essence and relating negatively toward the other, that is, of violating it. Just where, within universal ethical life, {{{ 410 }}} guilt and crime, doing and action, are to be placed will be stated more determinately later on; this much is immediately plain, that the one who acts and is guilty is not _this singular one_; for as _this_ self he is nothing but the unactual shade, or he is only as universal self, individuality being merely the _formal_ moment of _doing_ in general, while the content is the laws and the customs, and, for the singular one, the laws and customs of his estate; he is substance as genus, and although through its determinateness the genus does become a species, the species none the less goes on being the universal of the genus. Inside a people, self-consciousness comes down from the universal as far as particularity only, not down to singular individuality, which in its doing would posit an exclusive self, an actuality negative toward itself; its action rests instead on a settled trust in the whole, into which nothing alien mixes itself, no fear and no enmity.

{{ 255 }} Ethical self-consciousness now comes to experience, in its own deed, the developed nature that _actual_ acting has, and it does so whether it surrendered itself to the divine law or to the human one. In the essence, the law manifest to it is tied together with the law opposed to that one; the essence constitutes the unity of the two; but what the deed has carried out is the one only, and against the other. Since in the essence the one is tied to the other, however, fulfilling the one summons the other forth, and summons it forth in the character the deed conferred on it: as an essence violated, {{{ 411 }}} now hostile and demanding revenge. For acting, only one side of the resolve lies in the daylight at all; but the resolve is _in itself_ the negative, which sets over against itself another, something alien to itself as the knowing. Actuality keeps that other side, the side alien to knowledge, hidden within itself, and it does not reveal itself to consciousness in its being in and for itself, — it does not show the son his father in the man who insults him and whom he strikes down; — nor the mother in the queen he takes as his wife. A light-shy power thus stalks ethical self-consciousness, a power that breaks out only once the deed has taken place and catches consciousness in it; for with the deed accomplished, the opposition of the knowing self to the actuality facing it has been sublated. What acts cannot disown the crime and its guilt; — the deed amounts to just this, setting the unmoved into motion and bringing out what was so far locked up in mere possibility, and so in tying the unconscious to the conscious, non-being to being. In this truth, then, the deed steps out into the sunlight; — steps out as that in which something conscious is joined to something unconscious, one's own to what is alien, as the essence split in two, whose other side consciousness comes to experience, and experiences as belonging to itself as well, yet as a power that it has violated and stirred to enmity.

{{{ 412 }}} It may happen that the right which lay in ambush is present for the acting _consciousness_ not in the shape peculiar to it, but only _in itself_, within the inward guilt that belongs to the resolve and to the acting. Yet ethical consciousness is the more complete, and its guilt the purer, where it _is acquainted in advance_ with the law and the power it confronts, takes them for violence and wrong, for an ethical contingency, and commits the crime knowingly, as Antigone does. The accomplished deed inverts its view; the _accomplishing_ pronounces of itself that what is _ethical_ must also be _actual_; for the purpose of acting is the _actuality_ of the purpose. What acting pronounces is precisely the _unity_ of _actuality_ and _substance_: it pronounces that actuality is nothing contingent for the essence, but that, leagued with the essence, it is granted to nothing which is not true right. {{ 256 }} For the sake of this actuality, and for the sake of its own doing, ethical consciousness must take what is opposed to it as its own; it must acknowledge that it is guilty; _because we suffer, we acknowledge that we have erred_ —

This acknowledgment gives expression to the sublated discord between the ethical _purpose_ and _actuality_; it gives expression to a return to that ethical _disposition_ which knows that only what is right is valid. With this, though, the agent surrenders its _character_ and the _actuality_ of its self, and has gone to ruin. {{{ 413 }}} Its _being_ amounts to belonging to its ethical law as to its substance; but in acknowledging what is opposed to it, that law has stopped being substance for it; and what it has arrived at, in place of its actuality, is unactuality, mere disposition. — Substance does indeed make its appearance _on_ individuality, as the _pathos_ of the latter, with individuality appearing as what animates substance and therefore stands above it; yet this pathos is at once the individuality's own character; ethical individuality is immediate and is in itself one with this its universal, it has its existence in that universal alone, and it cannot outlive the ruin which the opposed power inflicts on this ethical power.

It possesses, along with this, the certainty that whatever individuality has that opposed power for its pathos — _undergoes no more ill than it has itself dealt out_. That the two sides undergo one and the same ruin is the only way in which the movement of the ethical powers against one another, and of the individualities that put them into life and action, has reached its _true end_. For not one of the powers holds anything over the other by which it would be the _more essential_ moment of the substance. Their equal essentiality and their mutually indifferent subsistence alongside each other is their selfless being; in the _deed_ they are as self-essences, but as differing ones, and this runs counter to the unity of the self and makes up their rightlessness {{{ 414 }}} and their necessary ruin. _Character_ likewise belongs, so far as its pathos or substance goes, to one power alone, while on the side of knowledge the one character no less than the other is split in two into a conscious and an unconscious element; and since each character calls this opposition forth on its own, and since through the deed the not-knowing is its work too, each puts itself into the guilt that consumes it. Victory for the one power and its character, and the succumbing of the other side, would thus be no more than a part, an unfinished work, one that presses on without check toward an equilibrium between the two. Absolute right is accomplished only where both sides are subjugated alike, and only then has the ethical substance come forward as the negative power that swallows both sides, that is, as almighty and just _fate_.

{{ 257 }} If both powers are taken in their determinate content and in the individualization of that content, the picture of their shaped conflict presents itself, on the formal side, as a conflict of ethical life and self-consciousness with unconscious nature and with a contingency that is there by way of nature, — nature has a right against ethical life, since ethical life is only the _true_ spirit, only in _immediate_ unity with its substance; — and, on the side of content, it presents itself as the discord of the divine and the human law. — The youth departs from the unconscious essence, out of the family's spirit, and becomes the individuality of the communal being; that {{{ 415 }}} he nonetheless still belongs to the nature he tore himself from is shown by his emerging in the contingency of two brothers, who with equal right take possession of that communal being; the inequality of earlier and later birth carries no significance _for them_, entering as they do into the ethical essence, as a difference of nature. The government, however, as the simple soul or the self of the spirit of a people, will not put up with a duality of individuality; and against the ethical necessity of this unity nature steps forward as the contingency of plurality. The two therefore fall into discord, and since their right to the power of the state is equal, that equal right smashes them both, both being equally in the wrong. Regarded in a human way, the crime was committed by the one who, not being _in possession_, attacks the communal being with the other at its head; the right lies on the side of the one who was able to take hold of the other merely as a _singular one_, cut off from the communal being, and drove him out in that powerlessness; he laid hands only on the individual as such, and not on the communal being, not on the essence of human right. The communal being, attacked and defended by empty singularity alike, maintains itself, and the two brothers each meet their reciprocal ruin at each other's hands; for the individuality that fastens the danger of the whole to _its being-for-itself_ has thrown itself out of the communal being and dissolves within itself. The {{{ 416 }}} one who took its side it will honor; the other, by contrast, who from the walls already proclaimed its devastation, will be punished by the government, the restored simplicity of the self of the communal being, with the loss of the last honor; whoever came to lay violent hands on the highest spirit of consciousness, the community, must forfeit the honor belonging to his whole completed essence, the honor of the departed spirit.

But if in this fashion the universal knocks the pure apex off its pyramid without effort and does carry off the _victory_ over the family, that principle of singularity which rebels against it, it has by that very act let itself in for nothing more than a _struggle_ with the divine law, self-conscious spirit for a struggle with the unconscious; for the unconscious is the other essential power, undestroyed by the first and merely offended. Against the law that holds violence in its hands and lies in the daylight, however, the divine law has for its help toward _actual_ execution nothing but the bloodless shade. Being a law of weakness and darkness, it therefore succumbs at first to the law of the day and of force, since the violence of the former is valid down below and not upon the earth. {{ 258 }} But the actual, having taken honor and power away from what is inward, has thereby eaten up its own essence. Manifest spirit has the root of its force in the underworld; a people's _certainty_, sure of itself and assuring itself, holds the _truth_ of the oath that binds all into one only in the unconscious {{{ 417 }}} and mute substance of them all, in the waters of forgetting. In this way the accomplishment of manifest spirit changes round into its opposite, and that spirit comes to experience that its highest right is the highest wrong, and its victory rather its own ruin. The dead man whose right has been injured therefore knows how to find, for his revenge, instruments equal in actuality and violence to the power that violated him. Such powers are other communal beings, whose altars dogs or birds befouled with the corpse, a corpse that was not raised into unconscious universality through the restitution owed to it to the elemental individual, but stayed above the earth in the realm of actuality, and that now, as the force of the divine law, receives a self-conscious actual universality. They set out in hostility and lay waste the communal being that has dishonored and broken its own force, namely the piety of the family.

Within this representation the movement of the human and the divine law expresses its necessity in individuals, individuals on whom the universal shows up as a _pathos_ while the movement's activity shows up as _individual_ doing, which lends that necessity the semblance of contingency. Individuality and doing, however, make up the principle of singularity as such, the principle that in its pure universality went by the name of the inner divine {{{ 418 }}} law. Taken as a moment of the manifest communal being, it has not merely that subterranean efficacy, or — an efficacy external in its existence, but an existence and a movement equally manifest and actual in the actual people. Once it is taken in this form, what had been represented as a simple movement of individualized pathos acquires another aspect, and the crime, along with the destruction of the communal being founded on it, acquires the form proper to its existence. — The human law, then, in its universal existence the communal being, in its activation generally manhood, in its actual activation the government, _is, moves_ and _preserves_ itself through consuming into itself the separating-off of the Penates, that is, the self-subsistent isolation into families over which womankind presides, and through holding them dissolved in the continuity of its own fluidity. At the same time, though, the family is quite generally its element, and singular consciousness the ground that activates universally. {{ 259 }} Because the communal being gives itself its subsistence only by disturbing family happiness and by dissolving self-consciousness into the universal, it breeds its own inner enemy out of what it represses and what is at once essential to it, out of womankind in general. Womankind — the everlasting irony of the communal being — uses intrigue to turn the government's universal purpose {{{ 419 }}} into a private one, changes its universal activity into the work of this determinate individual, and perverts what is the state's universal property into a possession and finery for the family. By this she turns the earnest wisdom of mature age, which, having died to singularity — to pleasure and to enjoyment, and to actual activity as well — thinks about and attends to the universal alone, into a thing of mockery for the high spirits of unripe youth and of contempt for youth's enthusiasm; and she raises the force of youth generally into what is valid, — the force of the son in whom the mother has given birth to her master, of the brother in whom the sister has the man as her equal, of the youth through whom the daughter, drawn out of her non-self-subsistence, attains the enjoyment and the dignity of womanhood. — The communal being, however, is able to preserve itself only through repressing this spirit of singularity, and since it is an essential moment, the communal being produces it just as much, and produces it as a hostile principle precisely through its repressive stance toward it. Since in separating from the universal purpose it is nothing but evil and null within itself, this principle would be capable of nothing were the communal being not itself to recognize the force of youth, manhood that is not yet ripe and still stands within singularity, as the _force_ belonging to the whole. For the communal being is a people, is itself individuality and is essentially for _itself_ only in this way, that _other individualit_ {{{ 420 }}} _ies are for it_, that it _excludes_ them from itself and knows itself independent of them. The communal being's negative side, which _inwardly_ represses the isolation of individuals but is _self-active_ toward the _outside_, finds its weapons in individuality. War is that spirit and that form in which there is present, in its actuality and corroboration, that essential moment of ethical substance which is the ethical _self-essence_ and its absolute _freedom_ from all existence. In one respect war lets the singular _systems_ of property and of personal self-subsistence, and singular _personality_ itself as well, feel the force of the negative; in another respect this very negative essence raises itself up within war as what preserves the whole; the valiant youth in whom womankind takes its delight, the repressed principle of corruption, comes out into the daylight and is what is valid. What now decides about the existence of the ethical essence and about spiritual necessity is natural force together with what looks like the accident of good fortune; and since the existence of the ethical essence rests upon strength and good fortune, it is _already decided_ that this essence has gone to ruin. {{ 260 }} — Just as before it was only Penates that went to ruin within the spirit of a people, so now the _living_ spirits of peoples go to ruin through their own individuality within a _universal_ communal being, one whose _simple universality_ is spiritless and dead and whose vitality is the _singular_ individual, singular as such. The ethical shape that spirit had {{{ 421 }}} has disappeared, and another steps into its place.

What determines this ruin of the ethical substance, along with its transition into a different shape, is thus that ethical consciousness is essentially directed upon the law in an _immediate_ way; and it lies in this determination of immediacy that nature as such gets into the action of ethical life. Its actuality brings to light nothing but the contradiction and the seed of corruption which the fine unanimity and the calm equilibrium of ethical spirit carry in that very calm and beauty; for immediacy carries the contradictory sense of being at once the unconscious calm of nature and the self-conscious, restless calm of spirit. — Because of this naturalness, this ethical people is throughout an individuality that nature has determined and that is therefore limited, and so it meets its sublation in another such people. But once this determinateness, which as posited in existence is limitation, yet equally the negative as such and the self of individuality, — disappears, the life of spirit is lost, and with it this substance that in all of them is self-conscious of itself. It comes out upon them as a _formal universality_, no longer dwelling within them as living spirit; instead the simple solidity of their individuality has shattered into a multitude of points.
