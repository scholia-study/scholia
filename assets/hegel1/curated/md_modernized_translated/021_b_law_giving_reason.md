## b. Law-Giving Reason

Considered in its simple being, the spiritual essence is _pure consciousness_, and it is _this self_-consciousness. The individual's originally-_determinate nature_ no longer carries its positive significance, namely that _in itself_ it is the element and the purpose of that individual's activity; it is nothing more than a sublated moment, and the individual is a _self;_ as universal self. Conversely, the _formal matter at hand_ finds its fulfillment in an individuality that acts and differentiates itself within itself; for it is the differences belonging to that individuality which make up the _content_ of the universal in question. The category is _in itself_ in that it is the universal of _pure consciousness;_ and it is no less _for itself_, for the _self_ of consciousness counts equally as a moment of the category. The category is absolute _being_, for the universality in question is simple _self-equality of being_.

For consciousness, then, the object has this significance: to be the _true_; _it is_ and _is valid_ {{{ 359 }}} in the sense that, _in_ and _for its own self_, it _is_ and _is valid;_ it is the _absolute matter_, one that is no longer afflicted by the opposition between certainty and its truth, between the universal and the singular, between the purpose and the reality that belongs to it, but whose existence is self-consciousness in its _actuality_ and its _doing;_ this matter is accordingly the _ethical substance;_ the consciousness of it is _ethical_ consciousness. {{ 229 }} For that consciousness its object is valid equally as the _true_, for it brings self-consciousness and being together into one unity; it is valid as the _absolute_, since self-consciousness neither can nor wants to move out beyond this object any longer, being at home with itself in it; it _cannot_, for the object is all being and power; — it does not _want_ to, for the object is the _self_, or the will belonging to this self. Regarded in its own self as object, it is the _real_ object, since it has the difference of consciousness upon it; it partitions itself into masses, and these masses are the absolute essence's _determinate laws_. Yet these masses do not muddy the concept, since within the concept those moments, being and pure consciousness and the self, remain enclosed, — a unity which constitutes the essence of these masses and which, within this difference, no longer permits these moments to step apart from one another.

Laws or masses of this kind, belonging to the ethical substance, are acknowledged immediately; no question may be raised about their origin and their warrant, and no other ground may be sought, since anything other than {{{ 360 }}} the essence that is _in_ and _for itself_ could only be self-consciousness taken by itself; yet self-consciousness is nothing but this essence, since it is itself this essence's being-for-itself, and the essence counts as the truth for precisely this reason, that it is just as much the _self_ of consciousness as it is its own _in-itself_, or pure consciousness.

In knowing itself as a moment of this substance's _being-for-itself_, self-consciousness gives expression to the law's existence within it in this way: what is _right_ and _good_ is immediately known to _sound reason_. As _immediately_ as reason _knows_ it, just so immediately is it also _valid_ for reason, and reason says immediately: this _is_ right and good. And precisely _this;_ what is meant are _determinate_ laws, a matter at hand that is fulfilled and rich in content.

What offers itself so immediately must be taken up and examined with equal immediacy; just as with what sense-certainty immediately pronounces to be, so also with the being that this immediate ethical certainty addresses, or with the immediately existing masses of the ethical essence, one has to see how they are constituted. Examples of a few such laws will show this, and if we take them as pronouncements made by _knowing_ sound reason, we have no need to bring in beforehand the moment that, in their case, would have to be brought to bear once they are regarded as _immediate_ sensuous laws.

"_Everyone ought to speak the truth_." {{ 230 }} — With a duty pronounced in this unconditional way, a condition will immediately {{{ 361 }}} be conceded; _if_ he knows the truth. The commandment will accordingly now run as follows; _everyone ought to tell the truth, each time in accordance with his acquaintance and conviction_ regarding it. Sound reason, precisely this ethical consciousness to which what is right and good is immediately known, will also declare that the condition was all along bound up with its universal pronouncement in such a way that this is how it had _meant_ the commandment. By that, however, it in fact concedes that it had rather already violated the commandment in the very act of pronouncing it; what it _spoke_ was this; everyone ought to speak the truth; yet it _meant instead_ that he should speak it in keeping with his acquaintance and conviction regarding it; which means that it _spoke in one way and meant another;_ and to speak in one way while meaning another is not to speak the truth. The untruth, or clumsiness, once corrected now expresses itself thus: _everyone is to speak the truth in keeping with whatever acquaintance and conviction regarding it he has at the time._ — But with that, what was to be _universally necessary and in itself_ valid, which the proposition set out to pronounce, has instead been inverted into complete _contingency_. For whether the truth gets spoken is left to the accident of my being acquainted with it and able to become convinced of it; and nothing further has been said than that what is true and what is false are to be spoken indiscriminately, just as it happens that someone is acquainted with, means, and comprehends them. This _contingency of the content_ possesses _universality_ merely by virtue of the {{{ 362 }}} _propositional form_ in which it finds expression; yet a proposition that is ethical holds out the prospect of a universal and necessary _content_, so that through the contingency of that content it contradicts itself. — Suppose, finally, that the proposition is corrected in this way: that the contingency of acquaintance and conviction concerning the truth should drop away and that the truth _ought_ likewise to be _known;_ such a commandment would then stand in direct contradiction to what was taken as the starting point. Sound reason was supposed at the outset to have the capacity to pronounce the truth _immediately_; but now the claim is that it _ought to know_ the truth, which is to say that it does not know how to pronounce it _immediately_. — Regarded from the side of the _content_, the content has fallen away in the demand that one ought to _know_ the truth; for that demand has to do with _knowledge as such_: one ought to know; what gets demanded is thus rather what is free of all determinate content. But what was under discussion here was a _determinate_ content, _a difference_ within the ethical substance. Only, that substance's _immediate_ determination is a content which turned out instead to be a complete contingency, and which, once raised into universality and necessity, so that _knowledge_ is what gets pronounced as the law, rather vanishes.

Another famous commandment runs: _Love your neighbor as yourself_. It is addressed to the singular in its relationship to singulars, and _asserts it ***as*** a relationship of the singular_ to the _singular_, {{{ 363 }}} or as a relationship of feeling. {{ 231 }} Love that is active, — since love that is inactive has no being at all, and is therefore surely not what is intended, — sets out to separate what is evil from a human being and to bestow good upon him. To that end one must distinguish what in him is the evil, what counts against this evil as the good that serves the purpose, and what his welfare consists in generally; which is to say, I must love him with _understanding_; love without understanding will do him harm, perhaps more harm than hatred. Beneficence that is understanding and essential, however, has its richest and most important shape in the state's understanding universal doing, — a doing beside which the doing of a singular, as a singular, becomes something so trifling in general that speaking of it is scarcely worth the effort. That doing, moreover, has such great power behind it that should the singular deed set itself in opposition to it and try either to be outright crime on its own account or, out of love for someone else, to cheat the universal out of the right and the share it has in him, the deed would be useless altogether and would be destroyed beyond resistance. What is left to beneficence, in so far as it is feeling, is only the significance of an altogether singular deed, of a help in emergency that is as contingent as it is momentary. Contingency determines not merely the occasion for it, but also this: whether it amounts to a _work_ at all, and whether it is not immediately dissolved again and, indeed, inverted into evil {{{ 364 }}} instead. This acting for the welfare of others, then, which gets pronounced to be _necessary_, is so constituted that it may perhaps be able to exist and perhaps not; that, should the occasion happen to present itself by chance, it is perhaps a work, perhaps good, perhaps not. This law accordingly has no more of a universal content than did the first one considered, and it fails to express, as an absolute ethical law ought to, anything that is _in and for itself_. Or: laws of this sort get no further than the _ought_ and possess no _actuality;_ what they are is not _laws_ but only _commandments_.

In fact, however, it emerges from the very nature of the matter itself that a universal absolute _content_ has to be renounced; for whatever _determinateness_ is posited in the simple substance, whose essence lies precisely in being this simple, is _inadequate_ to that substance. The commandment, in its simple absoluteness, itself pronounces _immediate ethical being_; and the difference that makes its appearance in it is a determinateness, hence a content standing _beneath_ the absolute universality that this simple being has. Since an absolute content must therefore be renounced, all that can belong to the commandment is _formal universality_, that is, the requirement that it not contradict itself; for universality without content just is formal universality, and absolute content itself says no more than a difference that is none, or contentlessness.

{{{ 365 }}} {{ 232 }} What law-giving is left with, then, is _universality_ in its _pure form_, or really the _tautology_ of consciousness, which steps over against the content and is a _knowledge_ whose object is not the _existent_ or proper _content_, but rather the _essence_ of that content, its self-equality.

The ethical essence is thus not immediately a content in its own right, but only a criterion of whether some content is fit to be law or not, in that it does not contradict itself. Law-giving reason has been demoted to a reason that is merely _testing_.
