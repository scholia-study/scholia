## {{{ 101 }}} IV. The Truth of Self-Certainty

{{ 103 }} In the modes of certainty treated up to this point, the true stands over against consciousness as something other than consciousness itself. Yet the concept of this true vanishes in the experience we have of it; just as the object immediately was _in itself_, namely the being of sense-certainty, the concrete thing of perception, the force of the understanding, so it turns out rather not to be that in truth, for this _in-itself_ shows up instead as a way in which the object merely is for another; the concept of it is sublated on the actual object, or rather the first immediate representation is sublated within the experience, and certainty was lost in truth. Now, however, something has arisen that never came off in those earlier relationships, namely a certainty equal to its own truth, since here certainty has itself as its object, and what is true for consciousness is consciousness itself. Admittedly there is otherness in this too; consciousness does draw a distinction, but it distinguishes something that at the same time is, for it, {{{ 102 }}} not distinguished. If we call the movement of knowledge the _concept_, and call knowledge as motionless unity, or as I, the _object_, then what we see is that the object answers to the concept, and not merely for us but for knowledge itself. — Or, to put it the other way round: if we call _concept_ what the object is _in itself_, and call object what it is as _object_, that is, _for an_ other, then it becomes plain that being-in-itself is one and the same as being for another; for the _in-itself_ just is consciousness; yet it is no less that _for which_ there is another (namely the _in-itself_); and it holds for consciousness that the object's in-itself and its being for another are the same; I is at once the content of this relation and the relating itself; over against another it is itself, and simultaneously it overreaches this other, which for it is likewise nothing but itself.

With self-consciousness we have thus now entered the realm where truth is at home. We have still to see in what way the shape of self-consciousness first steps forward. Should we set this new shape of knowledge, knowledge of oneself, alongside its predecessor, the knowledge of another, then that predecessor has indeed disappeared; but its moments have equally been kept safe at the same time; and what has been lost lies in this: that here those moments are on hand just as they are in themselves. {{ 104 }} Meaning's _being_, the _singularity_ and the opposed {{{ 103 }}} _universality_ of perception, together with the understanding's _empty inner_, count no longer as essences but as moments of self-consciousness, that is, as abstractions or distinctions which, _for_ consciousness itself, are at the same time null, or are no distinctions at all and essences that purely vanish. So it looks as though what has gone missing is only the chief moment itself, namely the _simple self-subsistent subsistence_ for consciousness. In truth, though, self-consciousness is the reflection that comes out of the sensuous and perceived world's being, and essentially it is the return out of _otherness_. Movement is what it is as self-consciousness; yet in distinguishing from itself _only itself as_ itself, the distinction is for it _immediately_ _sublated_ as an otherness; the distinction _is_ not, and _it_ is merely the tautology, devoid of movement: I am I; insofar as the distinction does not also take on for it the shape of _being_, it is not self-consciousness. Otherness is thereby there for it _as a being_, that is, as a _distinguished moment_; but equally there is for it the unity of itself with this distinction, as a _second distinguished_ moment. Through that first moment self-consciousness is preserved as _consciousness_, and the sensuous world in its entire extension is retained for it; yet only in so far as it is related to the second moment, namely the unity that self-consciousness has with itself; and that world is accordingly for it a subsistence which is nevertheless mere _appearance_, or {{{ 104 }}} a distinction that has no being _in itself_. But the essence of this opposition of its appearance to its truth is nothing other than the truth itself, that is, the unity self-consciousness has with itself; this unity must become essential to it; in other words, it is _desire_ in general. As self-consciousness, consciousness now has a doubled object: one of them immediate, the object of sense-certainty and of perceiving, which _for it_ bears, however, the _character of the negative_; and the second of them, namely _itself_, which constitutes the true _essence_ and which at first is present only over against the first. In this, self-consciousness presents itself as that movement wherein the opposition just named is sublated and its equality with its own self comes to be for it.

The object that counts as the negative for self-consciousness has, for its part, _for us_ or _in itself_, likewise withdrawn into itself, just as consciousness has on its side. Through this reflection into itself it has become _life_. Whatever self-consciousness sets apart from itself _as a being_ carries about it, insofar as it is posited as a being, not merely the mode belonging to sense-certainty and to perception; it is rather a being reflected into itself, and what immediate desire has for its object is something _living_. {{ 105 }} For the _in-itself_, the _universal_ result, that is, of how the understanding stands to the {{{ 105 }}} inner of things, consists in distinguishing what admits of no distinction, or in the unity of what has been distinguished. This unity, however, is, as we have seen, just as much its own repelling of itself, and this concept _splits in two_ into the opposition between self-consciousness and life, the former of the two being the unity _for which_ there is the infinite unity of the distinctions; the latter, though, merely _is_ this unity itself, so that it is not simultaneously _for its own self_. Consciousness, then, is self-subsistent, and its object is _in itself_ self-subsistent to just the same degree. Self-consciousness, which is _for itself_ pure and simple and which stamps its object right away with the character of what is negative, or which to begin with is _desire_, will therefore undergo rather the experience of that object's self-subsistence.

The determination of life, as it issues from the concept, or from the universal result with which we enter this sphere, suffices to designate life without our having to develop its nature any further from that result; its circle closes itself within the moments that follow. The _essence_ is infinity, taken as the _being-sublated_ of all distinctions, the pure movement rotating on its axis, the repose of itself as an infinity that is absolutely restless; _self-subsistence_ itself, in which the distinctions of the movement are dissolved; time's simple essence, which in this self-equality possesses the solid shape of space. The {{{ 106 }}} _distinctions_ are nonetheless present in this _simple universal_ medium just as much _as distinctions_; for the negative nature of this universal fluidity belongs to it only insofar as it is a _sublating of those distinctions_; yet it cannot sublate the distinct ones if they have no subsistence. This very fluidity, as self-equal self-subsistence, is itself their _subsistence_, or their _substance_, in which they accordingly are as distinct members and as parts that are _for themselves_. _Being_ no longer carries the meaning of the _abstraction of being_, nor does their pure essentiality carry that of the _abstraction_ of _universality_; instead, their being consists in just that simple fluid substance which is pure movement within its own self. But the _distinction_ of these members _from one another_, _as_ distinction, rests on no _determinateness_ whatever apart from that of the moments of infinity, in other words of pure movement itself.

The self-subsistent members are _for themselves_; this _being-for-itself_, however, is quite as _immediately_ their reflection into unity as this unity is the splitting-in-two into self-subsistent shapes. The unity is split in two for the reason that it is unity that is absolutely negative, or infinite; and since _it_ is the _subsistence_, the distinction too has self-subsistence only _in it_. The shape's self-subsistence shows itself as a _determinate something, for an other_, since the shape is something split in two; and to that extent the _sublating_ of the splitting-in-two happens through another. {{{ 107 }}} {{ 106 }} Yet it lies just as much in the shape itself; for that very fluidity is the substance of the self-subsistent shapes; this substance, however, is infinite; and so the shape, in its very subsistence, is itself the splitting-in-two, that is, the sublating of its own being-for-itself.

Distinguishing more closely the moments contained in this, we find that what we have for the _first_ moment is the _subsistence of the self-subsistent_ shapes, or the suppression of that which distinguishing is in itself, namely of not being in itself and of having no subsistence. The _second_ moment, by contrast, _subjects_ that subsistence to the infinity of the distinction. In the first moment there is the subsisting shape; as _being-for-itself_, or as substance that is infinite in its determinateness, it steps forth against the _universal_ substance, denies this fluidity and its continuity with it, and asserts itself as not dissolved in this universal, but rather as maintaining itself through separation from this inorganic nature of its own and through consuming that nature. Life in the universal fluid medium, a _tranquil_ laying-out of the shapes side by side, becomes by this very fact the movement of those shapes, or life as _process_. The _in-itself_ is the simple universal fluidity, while the distinction of the shapes is the _other_. But through this distinction the fluidity itself becomes _the other_; for it now is _for the distinction_, which is in and for itself and therefore is {{{ 108 }}} the infinite movement by which that tranquil medium is consumed, life as something _living_. — This _inversion_, however, is for that very reason once more the _invertedness in its own self_; the essence is what gets consumed; the individuality that keeps itself in being at the universal's cost, and that confers on itself the feeling of being one with itself, sublates precisely thereby _its opposition to the other, by means of which it is for itself_; and the _unity_ with itself which it confers on itself is precisely the _fluidity_ of the distinctions, or the _universal dissolution_. But conversely, the sublating of individual subsistence is equally the producing of it. For since the individual shape's _essence_, universal life and likewise what is for itself, is in itself simple substance, then in positing the _other_ within itself it sublates this its own _simplicity_, or its essence; that is, it splits that simplicity in two, and this splitting-in-two of the undifferentiated fluidity is precisely the way individuality is posited. So the simple substance of life is its own splitting-in-two into shapes, and simultaneously the dissolving of these subsisting distinctions; and the dissolution of the splitting-in-two is just as much a splitting-in-two, or an articulating into members. Both sides of the whole movement that were distinguished, namely the forming of shapes laid out tranquilly in the universal medium of self-subsistence, and the process of life, thereby fall into each other; the latter is no less a forming of shapes than it is the sublating of shape {{{ 109 }}}; and the first, the forming of shapes, is no less a sublating than it is the articulating into members. {{ 107 }} This fluid element is nothing but the _abstraction_ of the essence, or else it is _actual_ solely as shape; and its articulating itself into members is once again a splitting-in-two of what has been articulated, or a dissolving of it. Life is constituted by this entire cycle: not by what is stated first, the immediate continuity and solidity of its essence, nor by the subsisting shape and the discrete that is for itself, nor by their pure process, nor even by the simple gathering-together of these moments, but by the whole that develops itself, dissolves its own development, and in this movement maintains itself simply.

Since the starting point is the first immediate unity, and since the path runs through the moments of shape-formation and process to the unity of both these moments, thereby returning again to the simple substance we began with, this _reflected unity_ differs from the first one. As against that _immediate_ unity, the one stated as a _being_, this second unity is the _universal_ one, which has all these moments within it as sublated. It is the _simple genus_, one that within the movement of life itself has no _existence for itself ***as***_ that _simple thing_; rather, in this _result_ life refers beyond itself to something other than it is, namely to consciousness, for which life is there as this unity, that is, as genus.

{{{ 110 }}} That other life, though, namely self-consciousness, for which the _genus_ as such has being and which for its own self is genus, is at first only this simple essence to itself and has _pure I_ for its object; in its experience, which is now to be considered, this abstract object will grow richer for it and will take on the unfolding we have witnessed in life.

This genus, or the simple universal, one for which there are no distinctions, is what the simple I amounts to only in being the _negative essence_ of those moments that are shaped and self-subsistent; and self-consciousness is accordingly certain of itself only by sublating this other, which presents itself to it as self-subsistent life; it is _desire_. Being certain that this other is null, it posits that nullity _for itself_ as its truth, annihilates the self-subsistent object, and in that way gives itself certainty of itself as a certainty that is _true_, as one that has come to be for it in an _objective manner_.

In this satisfaction, however, it comes to experience the self-subsistence of its object. Desire, together with the self-certainty reached in desire's satisfaction, is conditioned by the object, for it comes about by sublating this other; and if such sublating is to be, this other must be. Self-consciousness is thus unable, by its negative relation, to sublate the object; it therefore produces the object again instead, and desire along with it. {{ 108 }} In {{{ 111 }}} fact the essence of desire is something other than self-consciousness; and by way of this experience that very truth has come to be for self-consciousness itself. Yet at the same time it is quite as absolutely for itself, which it is only by sublating the object, and its satisfaction must come to be for it, since it is the truth. Because the object is self-subsistent, then, satisfaction can be reached only if the object itself carries out the negation upon itself; and the object must carry out this negation of itself on itself, for _in itself_ it is what is negative, and it must be for the other that which it is. In being the negation upon its own self, and in being therein at the same time self-subsistent, the object is consciousness. In the case of life, which desire has for its object, the _negation_ falls either _upon an other_, namely upon desire, or it is there as _determinateness_ over against some other indifferent shape, or as life's _inorganic universal nature_. But this universal self-subsistent nature, on which the negation obtains absolutely, is the genus as such, that is, as _self-consciousness. Only in another self-consciousness does self-consciousness attain its satisfaction_.

Only in these three moments is the concept of self-consciousness brought to completion; a) pure undifferentiated I is the first immediate object it has. b) This immediacy, however, is absolute mediation in its own right; it consists only in the sublating of the self-subsistent object, in other words it is desire. The satisfaction {{{ 112 }}} that desire gets is indeed self-consciousness reflected into its own self, or certainty that has turned into truth. c) The truth of that certainty, however, is rather the doubled reflection, the redoubling of self-consciousness. It is an object for consciousness which in its own self posits its otherness, or the distinction, as something null, and which is therein self-subsistent. The distinct shape that is merely _living_ does indeed sublate its self-subsistence as well within the process of life itself, but along with its distinction it ceases to be what it is; whereas the object of self-consciousness is just as self-subsistent in this negativity of its own; and thereby it is genus for its own self, universal fluidity within the ownness of its separating off; it is living self-consciousness.

What is present here is a _self-consciousness for a self-consciousness_. Only through this is it in fact what it is; for only herein does its unity with itself in its otherness come to be for it; the _I_ that is the object of its own concept is in fact no _object_; the object of desire, by contrast, is merely _self-subsistent_, for it is the universal, indestructible substance, the fluid self-equal essence. Since a self-consciousness is the object, that object is I just as much as it is object. — With this the concept _of spirit_ is already present for us. What comes to be for consciousness from here on is its experience of what {{{ 113 }}} spirit is, namely this absolute substance which, in the complete freedom and self-subsistence of its opposite, that is, of distinct self-consciousnesses that are for themselves, constitutes their unity; an _I_ that is _We_, and a _We_ that is _I_ as well. {{ 109 }} It is only in self-consciousness, taken as the concept of spirit, that consciousness has its turning point, the point at which it steps out of the colorful semblance of the sensuous here below, and out of the empty night of the supersensible beyond, into the spiritual day of the present.
