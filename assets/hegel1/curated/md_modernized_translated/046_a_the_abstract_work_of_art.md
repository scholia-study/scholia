## a. The Abstract Work of Art

The first work of art, taken as the immediate one, is the abstract and singular work. On its side it has to move out of the immediate, objective manner and toward self-consciousness, while for its part self-consciousness, in the cult, is bent on sublating the distinction {{{ 656 }}} it initially sets up between itself and its own spirit, and on thereby bringing forth the work of art that is alive within itself.

Of the ways in which artistic spirit sets its shape at the furthest remove from its own active consciousness, the first is the immediate one: that shape simply _is there_ as a _thing_. — Within it the shape splits into a difference: on one side singularity, which bears the shape of the self upon it, — on the other universality, which sets forth the inorganic essence in relation to the shape, as its surroundings and its dwelling. This universality acquires its pure form, the form that belongs to spirit, when the whole is raised into the pure concept. It is not the understanding's crystal, which gives housing to what is dead or takes its light from a soul lying outside it, — nor is it that mixture of nature's forms with those of thought which first comes forth out of the plant, thought's activity here being still an _imitating_. Rather, the concept sloughs off whatever still clings to these forms from root, bough and leafage, and refines them into figures whose crystalline straightness and flatness have been lifted into incommensurable proportions, with the result that the abstract form of the understanding absorbs the ensoulment of the organic while its essence, incommensurability for the understanding, is preserved along with it.

But the god that dwells within is a black stone drawn out of the animal's housing, a stone shot through with the {{{ 657 }}} light of consciousness. {{ 379 }} Human shape sheds the animal element it had been mixed with; the animal now serves the god as nothing more than a contingent costume; it takes its place beside his true shape, is no longer valid on its own account, and has sunk to signifying something else, to being a mere sign. In the very same move the god's shape sheds from itself the neediness that the natural conditions of animal existence impose, and it merely alludes to the inner arrangements of organic life as melted into its surface and belonging to that surface alone. — The god's _essence_, however, unites nature's universal existence with self-conscious spirit, which in its own actuality makes its appearance as standing opposite that existence. Being at first also a _singular_ shape, the god has for his existence one among nature's elements, while his self-conscious actuality is one singular spirit of a people. But within this unity nature counts as the element that has been reflected into spirit: nature transfigured by thought, made one with self-conscious life. That is why the divine shape carries its natural element within it only as sublated, as a dim recollection. The waste being and the muddled struggle belonging to the free existence of the elements, that unethical realm of Titans, has been defeated and banished to the hem of an actuality now clear to itself, to the dim frontiers of a world that has found itself in spirit and settled. Those ancient {{{ 658 }}} gods into which the light-essence, begetting with darkness, first particularizes itself, namely the sky and the earth, the ocean and the sun, the blind Typhonic fire within the earth, and so forth, have given way to shapes that retain of them no more than a darkly recollecting echo of those Titans, shapes that are natural essences no longer but rather the lucid ethical spirits of self-conscious peoples.

So this simple shape has in itself wiped out the restlessness of infinite singularization — the restlessness it has as the natural element, which is necessary only as universal essence but relates contingently in its existence and its movement, — as well as the restlessness it has as the people, which, scattered among particular masses of doing and among the individual points of self-consciousness, possesses an existence of manifold sense and doing — and has gathered that restlessness into a tranquil individuality. Therefore over against it, over against the _essence_, stands the moment of restlessness — _self-consciousness_, which, as the birthplace of that shape, retained for itself nothing beyond being _pure activity_. Everything that belongs to substance the artist handed over wholly to his work, while granting himself, as a determinate individuality, no actuality in the work; he could bring the work to completion only by externalizing his own particularity, disembodying himself and heightening himself into the abstraction of pure doing. — In this first, immediate act of generation the split between the work and its {{{ 659 }}} self-conscious activity has not yet been closed again; the work, accordingly, is not on its own account what is genuinely ensouled, but constitutes a _whole_ only in company with its own _coming-to-be_. {{ 380 }} The ordinary feature of the work of art, that it was generated in consciousness and fashioned by human hands, is the moment in which the concept exists as concept, the concept that steps up against the work. And if that concept, whether in the artist or in the beholder, should be disinterested enough to pronounce the work of art absolutely ensouled in itself and to forget itself as the one who does or the one who looks, then over against this we must hold fast to the concept of spirit, a concept that cannot do without the moment in which spirit is conscious of its own self. This moment, though, stands opposed to the work, since spirit, in this its first splitting-in-two, assigns to each of the two sides, against the other, its abstract determination, that of _doing_ and that of being-a-_thing_, and since the two have not yet managed to return into the unity out of which they set out.

So in his work the artist comes to experience that what he produced was _no essence like himself_. From it a consciousness does indeed come back to him, in that an admiring multitude reveres the work as the spirit that constitutes their own essence. But this ensoulment, in giving him back his self-consciousness only as admiration, amounts rather to a confession made by the ensoulment to the artist that it is not his equal. Since what returns to him is joyfulness and nothing more, {{{ 660 }}} he fails to find in it the pain of his forming and begetting, or the strain of his labor. They may go on passing judgment on the work, or bringing offerings to it, or investing their consciousness in it however they will, — should they place themselves, with their acquaintance, above the work, he knows by how much his _deed_ exceeds their talk and their understanding; — should they place themselves _beneath_ it and cognize in it the _essence_ that rules over them, he knows himself to be that essence's master.

The work of art therefore calls for a different element of its existence, and the god for a different way of coming forth than this one, in which he sinks from the depth of his creative night down into what is opposite to him: into externality, into the determination proper to a _thing_ that lacks self-consciousness. _Language_ is this higher element, — an existence that is immediately self-conscious existence. _Singular_ self-consciousness, in being there within language, is there with the very same immediacy as a _universal_ contagion; the thorough particularizing of being-for-itself is at once the fluid state and the universally imparted unity of the many selves; language is soul that exists as soul. The god, then, whose shape has language for its element, is that work of art which is ensouled within itself, since it has immediately within its existence the pure activity that formerly stood over against the god so long as he existed as a thing. Or: while its essence is becoming objective, self-consciousness remains immediately with itself. Because it is thus {{{ 661 }}} at home in its own essence, it amounts to _pure thinking_, or devotion, and the _inwardness_ of that devotion also possesses _existence_ in the hymn. {{ 381 }} The hymn retains within itself the singularity of self-consciousness, and when heard this singularity is at the same time present as universal; devotion, once kindled in everyone, is the spiritual stream that within the manifoldness of self-consciousness is conscious of itself both as one identical _doing_ of all and as _simple being_; and as this universal self-consciousness belonging to all, spirit holds within a single unity its pure inwardness and equally the being-for-itself of the singulars and their being for others.

This language differs from a further language of the god, one that is not the language of universal self-consciousness. In the religion of art as in the religions that came before it, the god's _oracle_ is his necessary first language, for his _concept_ implies just as much that he is the essence of nature as that he is the essence of spirit, and that he therefore has an existence that is spiritual and not merely natural. So long as this moment lies merely in his _concept_ and has yet to be realized within the religion, religious self-consciousness finds that language to be the language of an _alien_ self-consciousness. A self-consciousness still alien to its own community _is_ not yet _there in such a way_ as its concept requires. The self is that being-for-itself which is simple and thereby wholly _universal_; but the self severed from the community's self-consciousness is as yet merely a _singular_ one. — What this own and {{{ 662 }}} singular language contains follows from the universal determinateness under which absolute spirit is posited in its religion at all. — The universal spirit of the rising, which has as yet given its existence no particularization, accordingly utters propositions about the essence that are equally simple and universal, and the substantial content of these propositions is exalted in its simple truth. Yet precisely because of that universality, this content strikes a self-consciousness that goes on educating itself further as trivial at the same time.

The self that has been cultivated further and lifts itself to _being-for-itself_ has become master of substance's pure pathos and of the objectivity belonging to the rising light-essence, and that simplicity of truth it knows as what _is in itself_, as something whose form is not that of a contingent existence conveyed by an alien language, but rather _as the sure and unwritten laws of the gods, which live forever, and of which no one knows whence they appeared_. — The universal truth that the light-essence revealed has here drawn back into what is inward or nether and has thereby been raised out of the form of contingent appearance; in the religion of art, by contrast, since the god's shape has assumed consciousness and with it singularity as such, the god's own language, the god being the spirit of the ethical people, is the oracle, which knows that people's particular affairs and announces what is useful with regard to them. The universal truths, though, are claimed by _knowing thought_ as belonging to itself, precisely because they are known as what _is in itself_, and their language is {{{ 663 }}} for that thought no longer alien but its own. {{ 382 }} Just as that ancient sage looked for what is good and beautiful in his own thinking, while handing over to his daemon the poor and contingent content of knowledge, whether he would do well to associate with this person or that, or whether an acquaintance would do well to make a certain journey, and other such meaningless matters, so too does universal consciousness gather what it knows of the contingent from birds, or from trees, or from the fermenting earth whose vapor takes from self-consciousness its level-headedness; for the contingent is what is not level-headed and is alien, and ethical consciousness therefore also allows itself to be determined about it in a manner that is not level-headed and is alien, as though by a throw of dice. When the singular individual determines himself through his understanding and deliberately picks out what would be useful to him, the ground of this self-determination is the determinateness of his particular character; that determinateness is itself something contingent; and so the understanding's knowledge of what is useful to the singular individual turns out to be a knowledge of just the same kind as the knowledge of oracles or of the lot; the one difference being that whoever puts a question to the oracle or the lot thereby gives voice to the ethical disposition of indifference toward the contingent, whereas the other person handles what is contingent in itself as though it were the essential interest of his thought and his knowledge. Higher than either of these, however, is to make deliberation into the oracle of contingent doing while all the same knowing this deliberated action itself, in respect of its relation to the particular {{{ 664 }}} and of its usefulness, as something contingent.

The true self-conscious existence which spirit obtains in a language that is not the language of an alien and therefore contingent, non-universal self-consciousness, that existence is the work of art we saw a moment ago. It stands opposed to the thing-like existence of the statue. The statue is the existence that rests, whereas the work of art in language is the existence that disappears; and where in the statue objectivity, set free, goes without any immediate self of its own, in the latter it stays by contrast too closely locked inside the self, arrives too little at any shaping, and, like time, has immediately ceased to be there in the very act of being there.

What constitutes the _cult_ is the movement of both sides, a movement in which the divine shape, _set moving_ within self-consciousness's pure element of feeling and _at rest_ within the element of thinghood, reciprocally surrenders its differing determination, and in which there comes into existence the unity that is the concept of their essence. Within the cult the self procures for itself the consciousness that the divine essence descends out of its beyondness to the self, and that essence, which previously is the unactual and merely objective one, thereby obtains the actuality proper to self-consciousness.

In itself this concept of the cult is already contained and at hand within the stream of hymnic song. {{ 383 }} Such devotion is the self's immediate, pure satisfaction by way of itself and in itself. It {{{ 665 }}} is the soul made pure, a soul that in this purity is immediately essence and nothing else, one with the essence. Because of its abstraction the soul is not a consciousness that would distinguish its object from itself, and so it amounts only to the night of that object's existence and the _place made ready_ for its shape. The _abstract cult_, then, raises the self up so that it is this pure _divine element_. Consciously the soul carries out this purification; yet it is not yet the self that has climbed down into its own depths and knows itself as the evil one, but is instead something _that is_, a soul that washes its externality clean, dresses it in white garments, and conducts its inwardness along the represented path of labors, roads and rewards, the path of a culture that externalizes particularity in general, a path by which the soul reaches the dwellings and the fellowship of blessedness.

As yet this cult is only _something secret_, that is, an accomplishing that is merely represented and unactual; it has to be an _actual_ action, for an unactual action stands in contradiction with itself. By this means _consciousness proper_ lifts itself up into its _pure_ self-consciousness. Within consciousness the essence carries the significance of a free object; the actual cult brings that object back into the self — and insofar as, within pure consciousness, it signifies the pure essence that dwells beyond actuality, this essence climbs down by way of this mediation from its universality {{{ 666 }}} to singularity and so closes together with actuality.

The way in which the two sides enter into the action settles itself thus: for the self-conscious side, insofar as it is _actual_ consciousness, the essence puts itself forward as _actual nature_; for one thing, nature belongs to that side as possession and property and is valid as the existence that is not _in itself_; — for another, nature is that side's _own_ immediate actuality and singularity, which it likewise regards as non-essence and sublates. For its _pure_ consciousness, however, this same external nature carries the _opposite_ significance at the same time, namely that of being the essence _which is in itself_, against which the self sacrifices its own unessentiality, just as, in the reverse direction, it sacrifices to itself the unessential side of nature. The action is on this account a movement of spirit, since it is this two-sided affair: to sublate the abstraction of the _essence_, as devotion determines the object, and to make the essence into something actual, and to sublate the _actual_, as the agent determines both the object and himself, and to raise it into universality.

The cult's own action therefore begins with the pure _giving over_ of a possession, which its owner, to all appearance quite uselessly so far as he is concerned, pours out or sends up in smoke. {{ 384 }} In doing this he gives up, in the presence of the essence of his pure consciousness, possession together with the right of property and of enjoying it, gives up personality and the return of his doing {{{ 667 }}} into the self, and he reflects the action into the universal or the essence instead of reflecting it into himself. — Conversely, though, the _essence that is_ founders in this act just as much. The animal offered up in sacrifice is a god's _sign_; the fruits that get eaten are _living_ Ceres and Bacchus _themselves_; — within the animal the powers of the upper right die, the right that has blood and actual life; within the fruits, however, the powers of the nether right die, the bloodless right that possesses the secret and cunning power. — Insofar as it is a _doing_, the sacrificing of the divine substance belongs to the self-conscious side; and for such an actual doing to be possible at all, the essence must itself have already made a sacrifice of itself _in itself_. It has done this in giving itself _existence_ and making itself into the _singular animal_ and into the _fruit_. This renunciation, which the essence has thus already carried out _in itself_, is displayed by the acting self within existence and for its own consciousness, and it puts in place of that _immediate_ actuality of the essence a higher one, namely _the actuality of the self itself_. For the unity that has emerged, being the result of the sublated singularity and separation of both sides, is not fate in its merely negative form but carries a positive significance. Only to the abstract subterranean essence is what has been sacrificed to it surrendered whole, and thereby the reflection of possession and of being-for-itself into the universal is marked off as something distinguished from the self as such. Yet this is only {{{ 668 }}} a slight _portion_, while the other sacrificing merely destroys what is unusable and is rather the dressing of the sacrificial offering for a meal, whose feasting defrauds the action of its negative significance. Of that first offering the sacrificer holds back the greater part, and of the second what is usable, reserving it for _his own enjoyment_. This enjoyment is the negative power sublating both the _essence_ and _singularity_, and it is simultaneously the positive actuality within which the essence's _objective_ existence is transformed into a _self-conscious_ one, and the self becomes conscious of its being one with the essence.

This cult, to be sure, is an actual action, and yet its significance lies more nearly in devotion alone; what belongs to devotion has not been produced objectively, just as the result, in _enjoyment_, robs itself of its own existence. So the cult moves on and makes up for this lack, in the first place, by conferring an _objective subsistence_ on its devotion, in that it is the labor, common or singular, that lies within everyone's power and that brings forth the god's dwelling and finery in his honor. {{ 385 }} — In part this sublates the objectivity of the statue, for by consecrating his gifts and his labors in this way the worker inclines the god toward himself and intuits his own self as something that belongs to the god; and in part this doing is not the singular laboring of the artist, but this particularity has been dissolved in universality. What comes about, however, is not merely the {{{ 669 }}} god's honor, nor does the blessing of his inclination flow to the worker in _representation_ alone; the labor also bears the significance that is the reverse of the first, the reverse of externalization and of honor rendered elsewhere. The god's dwellings and halls serve the use of human beings, and the treasures kept in them belong, in an emergency, to the human being; the honor the god enjoys in his ornament is the honor of the people that is rich in art and magnanimous. At the festival that same people adorns its own dwellings and clothing no less, and its business too, with elegant utensils. In this way it receives for its gifts the grateful god's return and the proofs of that inclination in which it joined itself to him through labor, not in hope and in some later actuality; rather, in the very act of paying honor and presenting the gifts it immediately enjoys its own riches and finery.
