## b. Faith and Pure Insight

The spirit of estrangement from its own self has its existence within the world of culture; but since this whole has undergone estrangement from itself, what stands beyond that world is a world without actuality, the world _of pure consciousness_, or the world of _thinking_. Whatever is purely thought constitutes its content; thinking is its absolute element. Yet since thinking is at the outset the _element_ of this world, consciousness merely _has_ these thoughts; it does not yet {{{ 475 }}} _think_ them, nor does it know them to be thoughts; for it they exist instead in the form of _representation_. {{ 287 }} For consciousness does step out of actuality into pure consciousness, and yet it remains, taken as a whole, within actuality's own sphere and determinateness. Consciousness in its torn-apart state is as yet the _self-equality_ of pure consciousness only _in itself_, for us, not for its own self. It is therefore only the _immediate_ elevation, one not yet brought to completion within itself, and it still carries within itself its own opposed principle, that which conditions it, without having gained mastery over it through the mediated movement. Hence what is valid for it as the _essence_ of its own thought does not take the form of the abstract in-itself alone; it takes the form of something _commonly actual_, an actuality raised merely into another element and yet not stripped there of the determinateness of an actuality that is not thought. — This must be essentially differentiated from the _in-itself_ that forms the essence of the _stoic_ consciousness; for that consciousness only the _form of thought_ as such had validity, a form which along with it has some content or other, foreign to it, taken over from actuality, whereas for the consciousness now in question the _form of thought_ is not what has validity; — and equally it must be differentiated from the _in-itself_ belonging to the virtuous consciousness, a consciousness for which the essence does indeed stand in relation to actuality, for which the essence is actuality's own essence, — though as yet only an unactual essence; — whereas for the consciousness now in question the essence counts as an actual essence, even though it lies beyond {{{ 476 }}} actuality. In the same way, neither what is right and good in itself for law-giving reason nor the universal of the law-testing consciousness carries the determination of actuality. — So although within the world of culture pure thinking itself fell on one side of the estrangement, namely as the measure of the abstractly good and bad in judging, it has now passed through the movement of the whole and been enriched by the moment of actuality and thereby by that of content. This actuality of the essence, though, is likewise nothing more than an actuality of _pure_ consciousness and not of _actual_ consciousness; lifted into the element of thinking it may be, yet for that actual consciousness it does not yet count as a thought, and lies instead beyond its own actuality, since the former is the flight away from the latter.

_Religion_ — for it is evident that religion is what we are speaking of — makes its appearance here as the faith of the world of culture, and in appearing thus it does not yet appear in the way it is _in and for itself_. — Under other determinatenesses it has already made its appearance for us, namely as _unhappy consciousness_, as a shape taken by the substanceless movement of consciousness itself. — In the ethical substance too religion appeared, as faith in the underworld; but the consciousness of the departed spirit is not properly _faith_, is not the essence as posited in pure consciousness's element on the far side of what is actual, for that spirit has an immediate {{{ 477 }}} presence of its own; its element is the family. — Here, however, religion has in part come forth out of the _substance_, of which it is the pure consciousness; and in part this pure consciousness has become estranged from the actual consciousness that belongs to it, the _essence_ estranged from its _existence_. {{ 288 }} Religion is thus no longer the substanceless movement of consciousness, and yet it retains the determinateness of an opposition to actuality taken as _this_ actuality in general, and to that of self-consciousness in particular, so that in essence it is nothing more than a _faith_.

This _pure consciousness_ of the absolute essence is an _estranged_ consciousness. How that of which it is the other determines itself is what must now be examined more closely, and pure consciousness can be considered only in conjunction with that other. At first glance this pure consciousness seems to have nothing but the _world_ of actuality confronting it; but since it is the flight out of that world, and thereby the _determinateness_ of _opposition_, it carries this opposition on itself; pure consciousness is thus essentially estranged from itself within its own self, and faith constitutes only one side of it. Meanwhile the other side has already arisen for us. Pure consciousness, that is to say, is a reflection away from culture's world, and is so in this manner: this world's substance, along with the masses into which it divides itself, showed themselves to be what they are in themselves, namely _spiritual_ essentialities, absolutely restless movements or determinations that sublate themselves immediately in their own opposite. Their essence, the simple {{{ 478 }}} consciousness, is accordingly the simplicity of the _absolute difference_, a difference that immediately is no difference at all. It is therewith pure _being-for-itself_, not as the being-for-itself of _this singular_, but as the self that within itself is _universal_, a restless movement that assails and penetrates the _quiescent essence_ of the _matter_. Within it, accordingly, there is present the certainty which immediately knows its own self as truth, present as pure thinking, as the _absolute concept_ wielding the power of its _negativity_, a negativity that annihilates every objective essence supposed to stand over against consciousness and turns such essence into a being belonging to consciousness. — At the same time this pure consciousness is equally _simple_, for the very reason that its difference is not a difference at all. But as this form of simple reflection into itself, pure consciousness constitutes the element of faith, the element within which spirit holds, over against that being-for-itself of self-consciousness, the determinateness _of positive universality_, the determinateness of _being-in-itself_. — Driven back into itself from the essenceless world that does nothing but dissolve itself, spirit is, in accordance with truth, in undivided unity both the _absolute movement_ and _negativity_ belonging to its appearing and the essence of that appearing as _satisfied_ within itself, its positive _quiescence_. Lying, however, quite generally under the determinateness of _estrangement_, these two moments come apart as a doubled consciousness. The former is _pure insight_, the spiritual _process_ that draws itself together in _self_-consciousness, a process confronted by the consciousness of the positive, by objectivity's form {{{ 479 }}} or that of representing, and directed against it; its own object, however, is nothing other than the _I in its purity_. — By contrast, the simple consciousness of what is positive, of quiescent self-equality, has the inner _essence_ as essence for its object. Pure insight therefore has at the start no content of its own, since it is the negative being-for-itself; to faith, on the contrary, the content belongs, but without insight. {{ 289 }} While the former never steps out of self-consciousness, faith likewise holds its content in pure self-consciousness's element, though in _thinking_ and not in _concepts, in pure consciousness and not in pure self-consciousness_. So faith is indeed pure consciousness of the _essence_, that is, of the _simple inner_, and therefore _is_ thinking; — this is the chief moment in the nature of faith, and it is the one commonly overlooked. The _immediacy_ with which the essence is present in faith rests on the fact that faith's object is _essence_, that is, _pure thought_. This _immediacy_, however, once _thinking_ steps into _consciousness_, or pure consciousness into self-consciousness, takes on the significance of an objective _being_ lying on the far side of the self's consciousness. It is owing to that significance, one that _pure thinking_'s immediacy and simplicity acquire within _consciousness_, that the _essence_ of faith drops down out of thinking into _representation_ and becomes a supersensible world, essentially an _other_ of self-consciousness {{{ 480 }}}. — In pure insight, by contrast, the transition of pure thinking into consciousness carries the opposite determination; here objectivity signifies a content that is merely negative, a content sublating itself and going back into the self; which is to say that, properly, the self alone is its own object, or the object has truth only in so far as it bears the self's form.

Faith and pure insight share the element of pure consciousness, and just as much they share the character of being a return out of the actual world of culture. They accordingly offer themselves for consideration from three sides. In the first case each stands _in_ and _for itself_, outside every relationship; in the second, each takes up a relation to the _actual_ world, which stands opposed to pure consciousness; and in the third, each takes up a relation, within pure consciousness, to the other.

Within the _believing_ consciousness, the side of _being in_ and _for itself_ is that consciousness's absolute object, and the content and determination of this object have already emerged. For according to the concept of faith it is nothing other than the real world as lifted into pure consciousness's universality. The way the real world is articulated therefore also makes up the organization of that object, save that within the latter the parts, in their inspiriting, do not become estranged from themselves but are essences that are in and for themselves, spirits that have returned into themselves and remain by themselves. — The movement {{{ 481 }}} of their transition is accordingly an estrangement of the determinateness in which they stand within their difference, and it is so for us alone; for us alone, too, it is a _necessary_ series; faith, by contrast, takes their difference to be a quiescent diversity and their movement to be a _happening_.

{{ 290 }} To name them briefly according to how their form is externally determined: within the world of culture it was state power, or the good, that came first, and here likewise the first is _the absolute essence_, spirit that is in and for itself, in so far as spirit is the eternal _substance_ in its simplicity. But in realizing its concept, which is to be spirit, the substance makes the transition into _being for another_; what was its self-equality becomes the _actual_ absolute essence that _sacrifices_ itself, and it becomes a _self_, but a perishable one. The third is accordingly the return of this estranged self and of the abased substance into its first simplicity, and only in this way is the substance represented as spirit. —

These distinct essences, drawn back into themselves by thinking, away from the changeableness of the actual world, are unchanging eternal spirits whose being consists in thinking the unity that they constitute. Removed as they are from self-consciousness, these essences nevertheless reach into it; if the essence stayed unmoved within the form belonging to the first simple substance, it would stay foreign to self-consciousness. The externalization of this substance, however, and then its spirit, does carry the moment of actuality upon itself, and thereby takes a share in the believing {{{ 482 }}} self-consciousness; or, put differently, the believing consciousness belongs to the real world.

Taken in this second relationship, the believing consciousness in part possesses an actuality of its own within culture's real world, and makes up that world's spirit and existence, which we have already examined; in part, however, it sets itself over against this actuality of its own as against something vain, and is the movement of sublating it. This movement does not consist in its having a witty consciousness of that world's inversion; for it is the simple consciousness, which counts wit among the things that are vain, on the ground that wit still takes the real world as its purpose. Rather, actuality stands over against the quiescent realm of its thinking as a spiritless existence, one that has therefore to be overcome externally. Such obedience of service and of praise, by sublating sensuous knowledge and sensuous doing, brings forth the consciousness of unity with the essence that is in and for itself, though not as a unity actually intuited; this service is instead only a perpetual bringing-forth that never fully reaches its goal in the present. The community does reach that goal, for the community is universal self-consciousness; but the realm of pure thinking must necessarily stay, for the singular self-consciousness, a beyond of its actuality, or rather, once that realm has entered actuality through the externalization of the eternal essence, this actuality is a sensuous actuality that has not been comprehended {{{ 483 }}}; and one sensuous actuality remains indifferent toward another, so that the beyond has gained nothing further than the determination of distance in space and in time. — The concept, however, the actuality of spirit present to its own self, remains in the believing consciousness the _inner_ that is everything and effects everything but never itself comes forward.

{{ 291 }} In _pure insight_, on the other hand, the concept is the sole actuality; and this third side of faith, that of being an object of pure insight, is the proper relationship in which faith makes its appearance here. — Pure insight itself has likewise to be considered in part in and for itself, in part in its relationship to the actual world in so far as that world is still on hand positively, namely as vain consciousness, and in part, finally, in the relationship to faith just named.

We have already seen pure insight in and for itself; faith is spirit's quiescent pure _consciousness_, spirit taken as _essence_, whereas pure insight is spirit's _self_-consciousness; and so for it the essence is known not as _essence_ at all but as absolute _self_. Its aim, then, is to sublate every self-subsistence that is _other_ to self-consciousness, whether the self-subsistence of what is actual or of what has being-_in-itself_, and to make that self-subsistence into the _concept_. It is not merely reason's self-conscious certainty of being all truth; it also _knows_ that this is what it is.

{{{ 484 }}} In the manner, however, in which the concept of pure insight makes its entrance, it is not yet _realized_. Its consciousness accordingly still appears as something _contingent and singular_, and what counts for it as the essence appears as a _purpose_ it has to bring about. As yet it has only the _intention_ of making _pure insight universal_, that is, of turning everything that is actual into the concept, and into one and the same concept in every self-consciousness. This intention is _pure_, since pure insight forms its content; and this insight is equally _pure_, since its content is nothing but the absolute concept, a concept that meets with no opposite in any object and is not restricted within its own self; in the unrestricted concept the two sides lie immediately together: that everything objective bears only the significance of _being-for-itself_, of self-consciousness, and that this being-for-itself bears the significance of something _universal_, so that pure insight comes to be owned by every self-consciousness. This second side of the intention results from culture in so far as within culture the differences of objective spirit, the parts and judgment-determinations of its world, have foundered, and with them the differences that appear as originally determinate natures. Genius, talent, particular capacities in general, belong to actuality's world in so far as that world still has upon it the side of being a spiritual animal kingdom, one that in mutual violence and confusion fights and cheats over the essences of the real world. — Within that world the differences have {{{ 485 }}} no place, to be sure, as genuine coin; individuality neither rests content with the unactual _matter at hand_, nor does it possess a _particular_ content and purposes belonging to itself. Rather, it counts only as something of universal validity, namely as something cultured; and the difference reduces itself to a lesser or greater energy; — a difference of _magnitude_, that is, an inessential one. This last diversity, though, has foundered in this, that within the complete being torn apart of consciousness the difference flipped over into an absolutely qualitative one. What there is other to the I is nothing but the I itself. {{ 292 }} This infinite judgment wipes out all one-sidedness and ownness of the original being-for-itself; the self knows itself, as pure self, to be its own object; and the absolute equality of the two sides is what constitutes pure insight's element. — Pure insight is accordingly the simple _essence_, undifferentiated within itself, and just as much the universal _work_ and a universal possession. Self-consciousness, within this _simple_ spiritual substance, both grants and secures to itself, in every object alike, the consciousness of _this_ its _singularity_, or of its _doing_, just as, conversely, its individuality is therein _equal to itself_ and universal. — This pure insight, then, is the spirit that cries out to _all_ consciousness: _be for your own selves_ what all of you already are _in your own selves_ — _rational_.
