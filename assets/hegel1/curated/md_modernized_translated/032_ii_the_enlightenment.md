## {{{ 486 }}} II. The Enlightenment

Faith is the distinctive object against which pure insight turns the force of the concept, faith being the form of pure consciousness that confronts it within one and the same element. But it stands, too, in a relation to the actual world, since, like faith, it is a return out of that world into pure consciousness. What has to be seen first is how its activity is constituted when it turns against that world's impure intentions and inverted insights.

Mention was already made above of the quiescent consciousness that stands over against this whirl, a whirl which dissolves itself within itself and generates itself once more; it is that consciousness which makes up the side belonging to pure insight and intention. Into this quiescent consciousness, however, as we saw, there falls no _particular insight_ concerning the world of culture; that world rather has the most painful feeling about itself and the truest insight into itself — the feeling of being the dissolution of everything that steadies itself, of having been racked upon the wheel through all the moments of its existence and smashed in every bone; — and it is likewise the language of this feeling and the judging {{{ 487 }}} witty discourse upon every side of its own condition. Pure insight can therefore have here no activity and no content of its own, and can accordingly relate only as the formal, faithful _apprehending_ of this witty insight that the world has of itself, and of the world's language. Since this language is scattered, since the judging is a raving of the moment that forgets itself again at once, and since it amounts to a whole only for a third consciousness, that third consciousness can mark itself off as _pure_ insight solely by gathering those self-dispersing traits into a universal picture and then making them into an insight belonging to everyone.

{{ 293 }} By this simple means it will bring the confusion of this world to dissolution. For it has emerged that the essence of this actuality lies not in the masses, nor in the determinate concepts and individualities, but that this actuality has its substance and its hold solely in the spirit that exists as judging and as discussing, and that the interest in having some content for this ratiocinating and chattering is alone what sustains the whole together with the masses of its articulation. Within this language of insight, self-consciousness is still for itself _something that is for itself, this singular_; yet the content's vanity is at once the vanity of that very self which knows the content to be vain. Now once the quietly apprehending consciousness gathers into a collection the aptest formulations, those cutting clean through the matter, out of all this witty gossip of vanity, then, alongside the {{{ 488 }}} remaining vanity of existence, the very soul that still sustains the whole, namely the vanity of witty judging, founders as well. To most people the collection displays a wit better than their own, or at any rate displays to everyone a more many-sided one, and it displays knowing-better and judging in general as something universal and by now universally familiar; with that, the sole interest still remaining is wiped out, and singular insight dissolves into universal insight.

Above this vain knowledge, however, the knowledge of essence still stands firm, and pure insight first appears in its proper activity only insofar as it steps forward against faith.
