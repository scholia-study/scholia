## {{{ 234 }}} b. The Observation of Self-Consciousness in its Purity and its Relation to External Actuality; Logical and Psychological Laws

{{ 167 }} In inorganic nature the observation of nature comes upon the concept as realized — laws whose moments are things which at the same time relate as abstractions; this concept, however, is not a simplicity reflected within itself. Organic nature's life, by contrast, is nothing but this simplicity reflected within itself; its opposition to itself, as the universal and as the singular, does not break apart within the essence of this life itself; and the essence is not a genus that would divide and move itself within its own element free of difference and that, in its own opposition, would at the same time remain undifferentiated for its own self. Observation finds this free concept — whose universality holds developed singularity just as absolutely within itself — only in the concept that itself exists as concept, that is, in self-consciousness.

{{{ 235 }}} Now, in turning back into itself and directing itself upon the concept that is actual as free concept, observation first finds the _laws of thought_. This singularity, which thinking is in its very self, is the movement of the negative in its abstraction, taken wholly back into simplicity, and the laws lie outside reality. — That they have no _reality_ says nothing else whatever than that they are without truth. They are supposed, admittedly, to be not the _whole_ truth, but still a _formal_ truth. Yet the purely formal without reality is a thought-thing, or the empty abstraction lacking the splitting-in-two within it, which would be nothing other than the content. — On the other side, however, since they are laws of pure thinking, while this thinking is what is universal in itself and hence a knowledge that immediately has being, and therein all reality, within it, these laws are absolute concepts and are, undivided, the essentialities of the form as much as of things. Because the universality that moves within itself is the simple concept _split in two_, it thereby possesses _content_ in itself, a content that is every content, save only a sensuous being. This is a content which stands neither in contradiction to the form nor separated from it in any way, but is rather essentially the form itself, since the form is nothing but the universal as it divides itself into its own pure moments.

But in the way this form or content is _for ***the*** observation_ qua observation, it takes on the determination {{{ 236 }}} of a content that is _found_, given, that is, of one that _merely is_. {{ 168 }} It turns into a _placid being_ of relations, a host of necessities set apart from one another, which as a _fixed_ content in and for itself are supposed to possess truth _in their determinateness_, and which are thereby in fact removed from the form. — Such an absolute truth belonging to fixed determinatenesses, or to a plurality of different laws, contradicts, however, the unity of self-consciousness, that is, of thinking and of form as such. Whatever gets stated as a fixed law that abides in itself can be no more than a moment of the unity reflecting itself within itself, and can come on the scene only as a vanishing magnitude. But when they are torn by consideration out of this context of the movement and set down in isolation, what they lack is not content — for they do have a determinate content — rather, what they are deprived of is the form, which is their essence. In fact these laws fail to be the truth of thinking not on the ground that they are meant to be merely formal and devoid of content, but on the opposite ground: that in their determinateness, or precisely _as a content_ stripped of its form, they are meant to count as something absolute. Taken in their truth, as moments that vanish within the unity of thinking, they would need to be understood as knowledge, or as thinking movement, and not as _laws_ of knowledge. Observing, however, is not knowledge itself and has no acquaintance with it; it rather inverts the nature of knowledge into the {{{ 237 }}} shape of _being_, that is, it apprehends its negativity only as _laws_ of being. — Here it suffices to have shown, out of the general nature of the matter, that the so-called laws of thought have no validity. Their more detailed development belongs to speculative philosophy, where they exhibit themselves as that which in truth they are: singular moments that vanish, whose truth is nothing but the whole of the thinking movement, knowledge itself.

This negative unity of thinking is for its own self, or rather it amounts to _being-for-its-own-self_, that is, to the principle of individuality, and it is _doing consciousness_ in its reality. It is to this consciousness, as the reality of those laws, that observing consciousness is thereby led on by the nature of the matter. Because this connection does not exist for it, it takes thinking, in its laws, to remain standing on the one side for it, while on the other side it obtains a further being in what is now its object, namely the doing consciousness, a consciousness that is for itself in the sense that it sublates otherness, its actuality lying in this intuiting of itself as the negative.

For _observation_, then, a _new field_ opens up in the _acting actuality of consciousness_. {{ 169 }} In psychology there is that mass of laws by which spirit relates in differing ways to the differing modes of its own actuality, taken as an _otherness found {{{ 238 }}} already present_; in part by receiving these modes into itself and _becoming conformable_ to the habits, the customs, and the way of thinking it has found already there, wherein it is object to itself as actuality — in part by knowing itself as self-active over against them, by seizing out of them, with inclination and passion, only what is particular for itself, and by _making what is objective conformable to itself_; in the one case relating negatively toward itself as singularity, in the other toward itself as universal being. — Taken on the first side, self-subsistence lends to what it has found already there nothing beyond the _form_ of conscious individuality in general, and as regards content it halts within the universal actuality it has come upon; taken on the other side, however, it lends that actuality at least a modification peculiar to itself, one that does not run counter to the actuality's essential content, or else a modification through which the individual, as a particular actuality and as a content peculiar to itself, sets itself in opposition to that actuality — and becomes a crime, in that it sublates that actuality in a merely singular manner, or, doing so in a universal manner and thereby for all, puts another world, another right, other law and customs in the place of those now present.

Observational psychology, whose first move is to state its perceptions of the _universal modes_ that turn up for it in active consciousness, comes upon a diversity of faculties, inclinations, and passions, and since the recollection {{{ 239 }}} of the unity of self-consciousness will not let itself be suppressed while this collection is being reeled off, such psychology has to proceed at least as far as astonishment that so many things, so heterogeneous and so contingent to one another, can be together in spirit as though in a sack, all the more so as they display themselves not as dead things at rest but as restless movements.

While reciting these various faculties, observation stands on the universal side; the side opposed to this universality, the _actual_ individuality, is the unity of these multiple capacities. — Yet apprehending the distinct actual individualities in turn and recounting them so as to say that this person has more inclination toward one thing, that person more toward another, that one has more understanding than the next, holds something far duller in it than even a listing of the kinds of insects, mosses, and the like; for the latter give observation the right to take them in so singular and conceptless a fashion, since they belong essentially to the element of contingent isolation. To take conscious individuality, by contrast, spiritlessly as a _singular_ appearance that merely is, has this contradictory feature, that its essence is the universal of spirit. But because this apprehending at the same time lets individuality step into the form of universality, it discovers _its law_, and now seems to be pursuing a rational purpose and carrying on a necessary business.

{{{ 240 }}} What makes up the content of the law are, as moments, individuality itself on the one hand and, on the other, that individuality's universal inorganic nature, namely, the circumstances found already present, the situation, habits, customs, religion, and so on; out of these the determinate individuality is to be comprehended. {{ 170 }} They contain what is determinate no less than what is universal, and they are at once _something present_, which offers itself to observation and which, on the other side, gives expression to itself in the form of individuality.

Now the law of this relationship between the two sides would have to state which effect and which influence these determinate circumstances exercise upon individuality. But this individuality is precisely this: _just as much_ to be the _universal_, and therefore to flow together in a placid, immediate way with the _present_ universal, with customs, habits, and so on, and to become conformable to them, _as_ to relate to them in opposition and rather to invert them — and likewise to relate to them in its singularity with complete indifference, neither letting them work upon it nor being active against them. _What_ is to have an influence upon individuality and _which_ influence it is to have — which actually amounts to the same thing — therefore depends solely upon individuality itself; to say that _through this_ the individuality _has become this determinate one_ means nothing else than that _it has already been this_. Circumstances, situation, customs, and so on, which on the one side {{{ 241 }}} are exhibited as _present_ and on the other _within this determinate individuality_, express only the indeterminate essence of that individuality, which is not what is at issue. If these circumstances, this way of thinking, these customs, this condition of the world had not existed at all, then the individual would indeed not have become what it is; for all who find themselves in this condition of the world are this universal substance. — But in the way this condition has particularized itself in _this_ individual — and such an individual is what is to be comprehended — it would have to have particularized itself in and for its own self and, in this determinateness it had given itself, to have worked upon an individual; only so would it have made that individual into the determinate one it is. If the external had constituted itself in and for itself just as it appears in the individuality, then the individuality would be comprehended out of the external. We would then have a twofold gallery of images, the first of them a reflected shine of the second: the first being the gallery of the complete determinateness and delimitation of external circumstances, the second that same gallery rendered into the mode in which those circumstances are within the conscious essence; the former the spherical surface, the latter the center point that represents that surface within itself.

But the surface of the sphere, the world of the individual, immediately carries the ambiguous sense of being _a world and situation existing in and for itself_, and of being _the world of the individual_, either insofar as the individual had merely flowed together with it, had let it pass into itself just as it is {{{ 242 }}} and had related to it only as formal consciousness; — _or_ else of being the world of the individual in the way that what is present has been _inverted_ by the individual. {{ 171 }} — Because actuality, for the sake of this freedom, admits of this doubled sense, only out of the individual itself can the individual's world be comprehended, and the _influence_ upon the individual exercised by an actuality that is represented as _being_ in and for itself takes on, through the individual, the absolutely opposite sense, that the individual either lets the stream of inflowing actuality _run its course_ within itself, or breaks it off and inverts it. By this, however, _psychological necessity_ becomes so empty a word that, with regard to whatever is supposed to have had this influence, there is the absolute possibility that it might just as well not have had it.

With this there drops away the _being_ that would be _in and for itself_, the being that was to constitute the one and indeed the universal side of a law. Individuality is whatever _its_ world amounts to as _its own_; individuality is itself the circle of its doing, wherein it has exhibited itself as actuality, and it is nothing whatever but the unity of the being that is _present_ and the being that is _made;_ a unity in which the sides do not come apart, as they do in the representation of the psychological law, as a world present _in itself_ and as an individuality that is _for itself_; or, if each side is taken by itself in that way, then there is no necessity present, and no law of how the two relate to each other.
