## {{{ 376 }}} VI. Spirit

{{ 238 }} Reason is spirit inasmuch as the certainty that it is all reality has been raised to truth, and inasmuch as reason is conscious of itself as its own world and of the world as itself. — How spirit comes to be was exhibited by the movement immediately preceding, in which consciousness's object, the pure category, raised itself to the concept of reason. Within reason that _observes_, this pure unity of _I_ and _being_, of the _for-itself_ and of being-_in-itself_, carries the determination of the _in-itself_, or of _being_, and reason's consciousness _finds_ itself. The truth of observing, however, is rather the sublation of this immediate instinct of finding, this unconscious existence that reason has. The category as _intuited_, the _found thing_, steps into consciousness as that _being-for-itself_ which belongs to the I, and now, in the objective essence, the I knows itself as the _self_. But when the category is determined in this way, as being-for-itself set over against being-in-itself, that determination is just as one-sided, a moment that sublates itself. For consciousness, then, the category comes to be determined the way it stands in its universal truth, {{{ 377 }}} namely as an essence _in_ and _for itself_. What makes up the _matter at hand_, this still _abstract_ determination, is at first only the _spiritual essence_, and consciousness of it only a formal knowledge that drifts about among its many sorts of content; in fact such consciousness is still marked off from the substance as something singular, and it either lays down arbitrary laws, or supposes that in its knowledge as such it possesses the laws as these are in and for themselves; and it takes itself to be the power that sits in judgment over them. — Or, looked at from the side of the substance, the substance is that spiritual essence which has being _in_ and _for itself_ and is still not _consciousness_ of itself. — But the essence having being _in_ and _for itself_, which is at the same time actual to itself as consciousness and represents itself to itself, is _spirit_.

The spiritual _essence_ of spirit has already been given the name _ethical substance_; spirit itself, though, is _ethical actuality_. Spirit is the _self_ of actual consciousness, and over against that consciousness spirit, or rather actual consciousness itself, stands as an objective and actual _world_; yet for the self this world has forfeited every significance of something alien, and the self likewise has forfeited every significance of a being-for-itself severed from the world, whether dependent or independent. {{ 239 }} The _substance_ and the universal, self-equal, abiding essence — spirit is the unshifted and undissolved _ground_ and _starting point_ of the doing of all, — and their _purpose_ and _goal_, as the thought _in-itself_ of all self-consciousness. — This substance is equally the universal _work_ that {{{ 378 }}} produces itself through the _doing_ of all and of each as their unity and equality, for it is _being-for-itself_, the self, the doing. As the _substance_, spirit is the unwavering, just _self-equality;_ but as _being-for-itself_ the substance is the dissolved, self-sacrificing, benevolent essence in which each accomplishes his own work, tears universal being apart, and takes his portion of it for himself. Precisely this dissolving and singularizing of the essence is the _moment_ belonging to the doing and the self of all; this is what moves the substance and is its soul, and it is the universal essence that has been brought about. It is just because the substance is being that has been dissolved in the self that it is no dead essence, but rather _actual_ and _living_.

Spirit is thus the absolute real essence that carries itself. All the shapes of consciousness so far are abstractions of spirit; they amount to this, that spirit analyzes itself, distinguishes its moments, and lingers with singular ones. This isolating of such moments has spirit itself for its _presupposition_ and its _subsistence_, or it exists only within spirit, which is existence. So isolated, they carry the semblance that as such they _have being;_ but their onward rolling and their return into their ground and essence showed how they are only moments or vanishing magnitudes; and this essence is precisely that moving and dissolving of these moments. At this point, where what has been posited is spirit, or the reflection of those moments into itself, our own reflection can briefly call them to mind under this aspect; {{{ 379 }}} these were consciousness, self-consciousness, and reason. Thus spirit is _consciousness_ as such, comprehending within itself sense-certainty, perceiving, and the understanding, insofar as, in analyzing itself, it holds on to the moment that it is to itself an _objective, existing_ actuality, and abstracts from the circumstance that its own being-for-itself is what this actuality is. Should it instead hold on to the other moment in the analysis, namely that its _being-for-itself_ is its object, then spirit is self-consciousness. Yet as the immediate consciousness of what is _in and for itself_, as consciousness and self-consciousness unified, spirit is the consciousness that _has reason_, the one which, as the word _having_ indicates, has its object determined as rational _in itself_, or as bearing the value of the category, though in such a way that this object does not yet bear the value of the category for the consciousness of it. It is the consciousness from whose consideration we have just now come. Once this reason which spirit _has_ is finally intuited by spirit as a reason that _is_, or as the reason which is _actual_ within spirit and is spirit's world, then spirit stands in its truth; spirit _is_ spirit, it is that _actual ethical_ essence.

{{ 240 }} Spirit is the _ethical life_ of a _people_, insofar as spirit _is the immediate truth;_ the individual which is a world. Spirit has to advance to consciousness of what it immediately is, to sublate that beautiful ethical life, and {{{ 380 }}} to reach, by way of a series of shapes, the knowledge of itself. What sets these apart from the preceding ones is that they are the real spirits, actualities in the proper sense, and, in place of shapes merely of consciousness, shapes of a world.

It is as the _living ethical_ world that spirit exists in its _truth;_ as spirit first arrives at the abstract _knowledge_ of its essence, ethical life goes under in the formal universality of right. Spirit, now split in two within itself, sketches out in its objective element, as in a hard actuality, one among its worlds, the _realm of culture_, and, over against it, in the element that is thought, the _world of faith_, which is the _realm of essence_. Both worlds, however, once grasped by the spirit that withdraws into itself out of this loss of itself, grasped by the _concept_, are thrown into confusion and revolutionized by _insight_ and its diffusion, the _Enlightenment_; and the realm that had been divided and spread out into the _here below_ and the _beyond_ goes back into self-consciousness, which in _morality_ now grasps itself as the essentiality and grasps essence as actual self, no longer places its _world_ and that world's _ground_ outside itself, but lets everything smolder away within itself, and, as _conscience_, is the spirit _certain of itself_.

{{{ 381 }}} The ethical world, then, the world torn apart into the here below and the beyond, and the moral worldview are the spirits whose movement and return into the simple self of spirit that is for itself will unfold, and as their goal and result the actual self-consciousness belonging to absolute spirit will step forth.
