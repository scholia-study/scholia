## B. The Actualization of Rational Self-Consciousness through Itself

{{ 193 }} Self-consciousness came upon the thing as being itself, and upon its own self as being a thing; that is, _it is for it_ that _in itself_ it is objective actuality. It has ceased to be that _immediate_ certainty which consists in being all reality; it is instead a certainty for which everything immediate has taken on the form of the sublated, so that its _objectivity_ now ranks only as a surface, the inner core and essence of which is _itself_. — The object to which it relates itself positively is therefore a self-consciousness; that object stands in the form of thinghood, that is, it is _self-subsistent_; yet self-consciousness carries the certainty that this self-subsistent object is nothing alien to it; it thereby knows that _in itself_ it is recognized by that object; it is _spirit_, which has the certainty of possessing its unity with itself within the doubling of its self-consciousness and within the self-subsistence of the two. This certainty must now raise itself for spirit to the level of truth; what has validity for it, namely what it is _in itself_ and in the _inner_ {{{ 288 }}} certainty it holds, is to step into its consciousness and become _for it_.

Which general stations this actualization will pass through is already indicated in a broad way by comparison with the path traveled so far. For just as observing reason, taking the category as its element, went back over the movement made by _consciousness_, namely sense-certainty, perceiving, and the understanding, so too will this reason travel a second time through the doubled movement of _self-consciousness_, passing over out of self-subsistence into its freedom. At the outset this active reason is aware of itself as nothing more than an individual, and as such it must demand its actuality in the other and bring it forth there — but then, as its consciousness raises itself to universality, it turns into _universal_ reason and knows itself as reason, as that which in and for itself has already been recognized and which gathers all self-consciousness into its pure consciousness; it is the spiritual essence in its simplicity, which, in that it likewise arrives at consciousness, is the _real substance_, the ground into which the earlier forms go back, so that measured against this ground they are only single moments of its becoming, moments that do indeed break loose and show themselves as shapes in their own right, though in fact they are borne by it alone and have _existence_ and _actuality_, while they have their _truth_ only to the extent that they are and remain within it.

{{{ 289 }}} {{ 194 }} Suppose we take up in its reality this goal, the _concept_ that has arisen for _us_ already, namely the self-consciousness that is recognized, which in the other free self-consciousness has the certainty of its own self and precisely therein has its truth — or suppose we lift out this still inward spirit as the substance that has by now matured into its existence: then what unfolds within this concept is _the realm of ethical life_. For ethical life is nothing other than the absolute spiritual _unity_ that the essence of individuals has within their self-subsistent _actuality_; it is a self-consciousness universal in itself, one that is actual to itself within a second consciousness in such fashion that this second consciousness possesses complete self-subsistence, or is a thing for it, and that just therein it knows its _unity_ with that other, and only in this unity with the objective essence is it self-consciousness at all. This ethical _substance_, when it is held in the _abstraction_ of _universality_, amounts to no more than the law as _thought_; but it is quite as much _self-consciousness_ actual in an immediate way, or it is _custom_. Conversely, the _singular_ consciousness is this existent One only insofar as it is conscious, in its singularity, of the universal consciousness as its own being, and insofar as its doing and its existence are the universal custom.

Within a people's life the concept of self-conscious reason's actualization, the concept of intuiting one's complete _unity_ with the _other_ in the very self-subsistence of that other, or of having as one's object this {{{ 290 }}} free _thinghood_ of another, which I find before me and which is the negative of my own self, as being _mine_ for _me_ — that concept has in fact its consummated reality. Reason is on hand as the universal _substance_ in its fluidity, as the simple unalterable _thinghood_, which shatters into many wholly self-subsistent beings much as light shatters into the stars, into countless points shining for themselves, beings that in their absolute being-for-itself are dissolved in the simple self-subsistent substance not merely _in themselves_ but _for their own selves_; they know themselves to be these singular self-subsistent beings by the very fact that they sacrifice their singularity and that this universal substance is their soul and their essence; and this universal is conversely the _doing_ of them as singular beings, or the work that they have brought forth.

The _purely singular_ doings and dealings of the individual bear upon the needs it has as a natural being, that is, as _existent singularity_. The fact that even these, its most ordinary functions, are not brought to nothing but instead possess actuality, is due to the universal medium that preserves them, to the _power_ of the people taken as a whole. — Yet it is not only this _form_ of the _subsistence_ of its doing in general that the individual has in the universal substance, but just as much _its content_; what it does _is_ the universal skill and custom of all. This content, insofar as it individuates itself completely, {{{ 291 }}} is in its actuality interlaced with the doing of everyone. The individual's _labor_ for its own needs satisfies the needs of others no less than its own, and it attains satisfaction of its own only by way of the labor of others. {{ 195 }} — Just as the single individual, in its _singular_ labor, already carries out a _universal_ labor _unconsciously_, so it carries out the universal labor once more as an object of which it is _conscious_; the whole, precisely _as a whole_, turns into its work, and for that work it sacrifices itself, receiving itself back from it by that very act. — Nothing is present here that would not be mutual, nothing in which the self-subsistence of the individual would fail to confer on itself its _positive_ significance of being for itself, and to confer it precisely where its being-for-itself dissolves, in the _negation_ of that self-subsistence. It is within the customs and the laws of its people that this universal substance, this unity of being-for-another, or of making oneself into a thing, with being-for-itself, utters its _universal language_; but this existent unalterable essence is nothing other than the expression of the very singular individuality that appears to stand opposed to it; the laws declare what each single individual _is_ and _does_; the individual cognizes them not only as its _universal_ objective thinghood but equally cognizes itself within them, or cognizes them as _individuated_ in its own individuality and in every one of its fellow citizens. Each one, then, possesses within the universal spirit nothing but the {{{ 292 }}} certainty of his own self, the certainty of finding in existent actuality nothing other than himself; of the others he is as certain as he is of himself. — In all of them I intuit that for their own selves they are just these self-subsistent beings, as I too am; and the free unity with the others I intuit in them in such a way that it exists through the Others themselves just as it exists through Me. They as Me, Me as Them.

In a free people, therefore, reason is actualized in truth; it is living spirit at hand in the present, and within it the individual does not merely find its _determination_, that is, its universal and singular essence, pronounced and available as thinghood, but is itself this essence and has attained its determination as well. This is why the wisest men of antiquity issued the dictum _that wisdom and virtue lie in living in keeping with the customs of one's people_.

But self-consciousness, which to begin with is spirit only in an _immediate_ way and _according to the concept_, has stepped out of this happiness of having attained its determination and of living within it, or else, — it has not yet reached that happiness; for either statement holds equally well.

{{ 196 }} Reason _must step out of this happiness_; for a free people's life is _real ethical life_ only _in itself_ or _immediately_, or rather it is an ethical life that merely _is_, and with that this universal spirit is itself a singular one as well, the totality of {{{ 293 }}} customs and laws, a _determinate_ ethical substance which strips off its restriction only at the higher moment, namely in the _consciousness about its own essence_, and whose absolute truth lies in that cognition alone and not immediately in its _being_; within that being it is in part something restricted, and in part the absolute restriction consists in just this, that spirit stands in the form of _being_.

Further, then, the _singular_ consciousness, taken as it exists immediately within real ethical life, that is, within the people, is a solid trust, one for which spirit has not resolved itself into its _abstract_ moments, and which therefore does not know itself as pure _singularity that is for itself_. But once it has come to this thought, as come it must, what is lost is its trust, that is, its _immediate_ unity with spirit and its _being_ therein; _isolated_ for itself, it is now the essence to itself, and the universal spirit is that essence no longer. _This moment of singularity belonging to self-consciousness_ does indeed lie within the universal spirit itself, yet only as a vanishing quantity which, in stepping forth for itself, dissolves within that spirit just as immediately and reaches consciousness only in the form of trust. In fixing itself in this manner — and each moment, since it is a moment of the essence, must itself come to exhibit itself as essence — the individual has taken up a position over against the laws and customs; these count for no more than a thought lacking absolute essentiality, an abstract {{{ 294 }}} theory lacking actuality, while the individual, as this I, is the living truth to itself.

Or else self-consciousness _has not yet attained this happiness_, namely that of being the spirit of a people, of being ethical substance. For spirit, on its return from observation, is at the start not yet actualized as such by its own doing; it is posited only as _inner_ essence, or as the abstraction. — Or spirit as yet only _is_, and _immediately_ so; but existing immediately, it is _singular_; it is that practical consciousness which steps into the world it finds before it with the purpose of doubling itself in this determinateness of a single individual, of producing itself as This One, its own existent counterpart, and of arriving at consciousness of the unity between its actuality and the objective essence. This unity it possesses as a _certainty_; what counts for it is that the unity holds _in itself_, that this agreement between itself and thinghood is already on hand and has merely still to come about _for it_ through its own doing, or that its making is equally the _finding_ of that agreement. Since this unity goes by the name of _happiness_, the individual is hereby sent out by its spirit into the world _to seek its happiness_.

{{ 197 }} Since for us, then, the ethical substance is what this rational self-consciousness truly is, what begins here for self-consciousness itself is its ethical experience of the world. Insofar as it has not yet become that substance, this movement presses toward it, and what gets sublated in the movement are the singular {{{ 295 }}} moments that hold for self-consciousness in isolation. These moments take the shape of an immediate willing, or of a _natural drive_, which reaches its satisfaction, and that satisfaction is in turn the content of a further drive. — But insofar as self-consciousness has forfeited the happiness of being within the substance, these natural drives are joined with a consciousness of their purpose as the true determination and essentiality; the ethical substance has sunk into a selfless predicate whose living subjects are the individuals, who must fill out their universality by their own means and must see to their determination out of themselves. — Taken in that first significance, then, those shapes constitute the becoming of the ethical substance and run ahead of it; taken in the second, they come after it and unravel for self-consciousness what its determination is; on the first side, within the movement in which their truth is experienced, the immediacy or rawness of the drives is what falls away, and the content of the drives crosses over into a higher content; on the second side, what falls away is the false representation of a consciousness that places its determination in those drives. On the first side the _goal_ they reach is the immediate ethical substance; on the second, however, it is the consciousness of that substance, a consciousness that knows the substance as the essence belonging to itself; and to this extent the movement would be the becoming of morality, a shape standing higher than the first. These shapes, however, make up at the same time only One side of that becoming, {{{ 296 }}} namely the side falling within _being-for-itself_, the side in which consciousness sublates the purposes that are _its own_; not the side along which morality proceeds out of the substance itself. These moments are not yet able to carry the significance of being turned into purposes in opposition to an ethical life now lost, and so they do indeed hold good here according to their unprejudiced content, and the goal toward which they press is the ethical substance. But since the form these moments take once consciousness has forfeited its ethical life and, in searching for it, repeats those very forms, lies nearer to our own age, they may better be presented in the idiom of this latter manner.

Self-consciousness, which as yet is only the concept of spirit, sets out upon this path with the determinateness of being the essence to itself as a singular spirit, and its purpose is accordingly to give itself actualization as something singular and, as this singular, to enjoy itself within that actualization.

In the determination of being the essence to itself as something _being for itself_, it is the _negativity_ of the other; within its consciousness it therefore steps forth as the positive over against something that indeed _is_, but that for it has the significance of not being in itself; consciousness appears split in two, into this actuality it finds before it and into the _purpose_ that it carries out by sublating that actuality and that it makes actual in place of it. {{ 198 }} Its {{{ 297 }}} first purpose, however, is the abstract _being-for-itself_ that belongs to it _immediately_, that is, to intuit itself as _this singular individual_ within another, or to intuit another self-consciousness as being itself. What it experiences as the truth of this purpose sets self-consciousness higher, and it is now a purpose to itself insofar as it is _universal_ as well and carries the _law immediately_ upon it. It learns, however, in fulfilling this _law_ of its _heart_, that the _singular_ essence cannot maintain itself in the process, and that the good can be carried out only by sacrificing that essence, and so it becomes _virtue_. The experience that virtue undergoes can be none other than this: that its purpose is in itself already executed, that happiness is found immediately within the doing itself, and that the doing itself is the good. Within the movement of this whole sphere its concept, namely that thinghood is spirit's own _being-for-itself_, comes to be for self-consciousness. Having found this concept, self-consciousness is thus reality to itself as an individuality that expresses itself immediately, one that no longer meets any resistance in an opposed actuality, and for which this expressing alone is object and purpose.
