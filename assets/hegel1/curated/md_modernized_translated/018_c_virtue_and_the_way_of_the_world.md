## c. Virtue and the Way of the World

{{ 208 }} Within the first shape that active reason took, self-consciousness was pure individuality to itself, and standing over against it was empty universality. In the second, each of the two parts of the opposition had _both_ moments in it, law and individuality; but the one part, the heart, constituted their immediate unity, while the other constituted their being set in opposition. In the present relationship, that of virtue to the way of the world, each of the two members is at once a unity and an opposition of these moments, or a movement of law and of individuality against one another, though one that runs oppositely. To the consciousness of virtue, _law_ counts as what is _essential_ and individuality as what must be sublated, and that holds {{{ 318 }}} within its own consciousness no less than in the way of the world. Within the former, one's own individuality has to be taken under discipline beneath the universal, beneath what is true and good in itself; yet even there a personal consciousness still remains; the only true discipline is the sacrifice of the entire personality, serving as the corroboration that consciousness has in fact not gone on clinging to singularities. In this singular sacrifice individuality is at the same time extinguished in _the way of the world_, since it too is a simple moment that the two have in common. — In the latter, individuality relates itself in the manner inverse to the way it is posited in the virtuous consciousness, namely by making itself into the essence and, in turn, subordinating to itself what is good and true _in itself_. — Moreover, what the way of the world amounts to for virtue is not merely this universal _inverted by individuality_; absolute _order_ is just as much a moment the two have in common, one that is on hand within the way of the world not as an _actuality that has being_ for consciousness, but as its _inner essence_. Order, therefore, is not something virtue would first have to produce in any proper sense, for producing is, as _doing_, the consciousness of individuality, and individuality is precisely what is to be sublated; by this sublating, though, all that happens is that space is cleared for what the way of the world is _in itself_, so that it may enter existence in and for itself.

What the actual way of the world holds as its universal _content_ has already come out; examined more closely, it {{{ 319 }}} is once more nothing but the two movements of self-consciousness that went before. Out of them the shape of virtue has arisen; being its origin, they lie before it; what virtue is bent on, however, is sublating its origin and realizing itself, that is, becoming _for itself_. So the way of the world is, on the one hand, the singular individuality that goes after its pleasure and enjoyment, that admittedly meets its ruin in doing so, and that thereby gives the universal satisfaction. {{ 209 }} But this satisfaction itself, and likewise the remaining moments of this relationship, is an inverted movement and shape of the universal. Here actuality is nothing but the singularity of pleasure and of enjoyment, with the universal set over against it; a necessity that amounts to no more than the empty shape of that universal, a reaction that is merely negative and a doing void of content. — As its other moment the way of the world has individuality, which wills to be law in and for itself and which, in this delusion, disrupts the order that subsists; against such self-conceit the universal law does indeed hold itself, and it now comes forward no longer as something empty and set over against consciousness, no longer as a dead necessity, but as _necessity within consciousness itself_. But taken as it exists in the shape of the _conscious_ relation of an actuality that contradicts itself absolutely, it is derangement; taken as it is in the shape of _objective_ actuality, it is invertedness in general. So in both sides the universal does {{{ 320 }}} present itself as the power behind their movement, but the _existence_ of that power is nothing but inversion become universal.

Now it is from virtue that the universal is to receive its genuine actuality, by way of the sublating of individuality, the principle of inversion; virtue's purpose is, in this way, to invert the inverted way of the world back again and to bring its true essence forth. As it stands in the way of the world, this true essence is as yet only its _in-itself_, not yet anything actual; virtue accordingly does no more than _believe_ in it. What virtue sets out to do is to raise this faith into beholding, though without enjoying the fruits of its labor and its sacrifice. For insofar as virtue is _individuality_, it is the _doing_ of the struggle that it takes up against the way of the world; its purpose and its true essence, however, lie in defeating what is actual in the way of the world; and so the existence of the good that this brings about is the cessation of virtue's _doing_, that is, of the _conscious_ness of individuality. — In what way this struggle is actually withstood, what virtue comes to experience in it, whether the sacrifice it takes upon itself makes the way of the world succumb and virtue prevail — that has to be settled by the nature of the living _weapons_ that the two fighters wield. For those weapons are nothing else than the fighters' own _essence_, which comes forth only for each of them in relation to the other. Their weapons have thereby already resulted from what is on hand in itself within this struggle.

{{{ 321 }}} To the virtuous consciousness the _universal_ is what is true in _faith_, or _in itself_; it is a universality that is not yet actual but still _abstract_; within this consciousness itself the universal stands _as purpose_, within the way of the world as what is _inner_. It is in precisely this determination that the universal exhibits itself, in virtue, for the way of the world as well; for virtue as yet only _wills_ to carry out the good and does not itself yet give it out as actuality. {{ 210 }} This determinateness admits of being viewed in another way too: the good, by entering the struggle against the way of the world, thereby exhibits itself as having being _for another;_ as a something which is not _in and for itself_, since otherwise it would not be looking to acquire its truth only through the subjugation of its opposite. To say that it is as yet only _for an other_ amounts to the same thing that emerged about it earlier under the opposite consideration, namely that it is as yet no more than an _abstraction_, one whose reality lies solely in the relationship and not in and for itself.

The good, or the universal, in the form in which it here comes on the scene, is what one calls _gifts, capacities, forces_. It is one way for the spiritual to be, a way in which the spiritual is represented as a universal requiring the principle of individuality for its animation and its movement, and having its _actuality_ in that principle. Where that principle lies in the consciousness of virtue, this universal is _put to good use_ by it; where the principle lies in the way of the world, the same universal is _misused;_ — a passive tool {{{ 322 }}} which, ruled by the hand of free individuality and indifferent toward whatever use that individuality makes of it, can also be misused to bring forth an actuality that spells its destruction; a lifeless matter, devoid of any self-subsistence of its own, which can be shaped one way or another, and even shaped to its own ruin.

Since this universal is equally at the command of the consciousness of virtue and of the way of the world, one cannot foresee whether virtue, thus equipped, will get the better of vice. The weapons on either side are identical; they are these capacities and forces. Virtue, to be sure, has placed in ambush its faith that its own purpose and the essence belonging to the way of the world were originally one, a faith that is to strike the enemy from behind during the struggle and to accomplish that purpose _in itself_; so that in truth the knight of virtue finds his own _doing_ and fighting to be nothing but shadow-boxing, which he _cannot_ take seriously, since he stakes his genuine strength on the good's being _in and for itself_, that is, on its accomplishing itself — shadow-boxing which he moreover _may not_ let become serious. For whatever he directs at the enemy, and finds directed back at himself, and whatever wearing-down and damage he risks in himself no less than in his enemy, is not meant to be the good as such; for it is for the good's preservation and execution that he fights; rather, what {{{ 323 }}} gets risked in this are merely the indifferent gifts and capacities. These, however, are in fact nothing but that same universal without individuality which the struggle is supposed to preserve and actualize. — Yet through the concept of the struggle itself it is at the same time immediately _already actualized;_ it is the _in-itself_, that is, the _universal;_ and its actualization signifies nothing more than that it should be _at the same time for an other_. {{ 211 }} The two sides specified above, on each of which it became an abstraction, _are no longer separated_; rather, by means of the struggle and within it the good is posited in both manners at once. — The virtuous consciousness, though, enters the struggle against the way of the world as against something set over against the good; and what the way of the world holds out to it in this is the universal, and not the abstract universal alone but one quickened by individuality and having being for another, that is, the _actual good_. So wherever virtue takes hold of the way of the world, it always hits upon places that are themselves the existence of the good, a good inseparably interwoven with every appearance of the way of the world, as its _in-itself_, and having its existence within that way's actuality as well; the way of the world is therefore invulnerable to virtue. Existences of the good of just this kind, and hence inviolable relationships, are all the moments that virtue was to stake and sacrifice in its own case. The fighting can accordingly amount to no more than a {{{ 324 }}} vacillation between preserving and sacrificing; or rather, neither a sacrifice of what is one's own nor an injury to what is another's can occur at all. Virtue is like that fighter whose one concern in combat is to keep his sword unstained; but more than that, the very reason it took up the fight was to keep the weapons intact; and it is not merely unable to employ its own weapons, it must also keep the enemy's from harm and shield them against itself, since all of them are noble portions of the good on whose behalf it went into combat.

To this enemy, by contrast, the essence is not the _in-itself_ but _individuality_; its force is therefore the negative principle, for which nothing is subsistent and _absolutely_ sacred, a principle able to stake and to bear the loss of anything and everything. On this basis victory is as certain for it in its own right as it is by virtue of the contradiction into which its adversary entangles itself. Whatever counts for virtue as being _in itself_ counts for the way of the world merely as something for _it;_ the way of the world is free of every moment that virtue holds fixed and is bound to. It holds such a moment in its power for the very reason that the moment is valid for it only as something it can just as readily sublate as leave subsisting; and with the moment it holds the virtuous knight who is fastened to it. The knight cannot extricate himself from it the way one slips out of a cloak flung over one from outside, becoming {{{ 325 }}} free by abandoning it; for to him this moment is the essence that cannot be surrendered.

As for the ambush, finally, from which the _good in-itself_ is supposed to fall cunningly upon the way of the world from behind, that hope is null in itself. The way of the world amounts to the alert consciousness that is certain of itself, one that does not let itself be approached from the rear but presents its brow on all sides; for what it consists in is just this, that everything is _for it_, that everything stands _before it_. {{ 212 }} As for the good _in-itself_: if it is _for_ its enemy, then it stands within the struggle we have watched; but insofar as it is not _for it_ and is instead _in itself_, it is that passive tool made of gifts and capacities, the matter lacking actuality; represented as existence, it would be a consciousness asleep and lingering somewhere at the rear, one knows not where.

Virtue is therefore defeated by the way of the world, since what it actually has for its purpose is the abstract, non-actual _essence_, and since, where actuality is concerned, its doing rests on _distinctions_ that reside in _words_ alone. Virtue was to have consisted in this, that through the _sacrifice of individuality_ the good should be brought to _actuality_; yet the side of _actuality_ is itself nothing other than that very side of _individuality_. The good was meant to be what is _in itself_ and stands opposed to what _is_, but the _in-itself_, when one takes it in its reality and its truth, is instead _being itself_. In the first instance the _in-itself_ is {{{ 326 }}} the _abstraction of essence_ as against actuality; but abstraction is just that which is not something true and is only _for consciousness_; and this means that abstraction is itself the very thing one calls _actual_; for what is actual is that which essentially has being _for an other_, or it simply is _being_. The consciousness of virtue, however, is founded on just this difference of the _in-itself_ from _being_, a difference that possesses no truth whatever. — The way of the world, having _individuality_ for its principle, was meant to count as the inversion of the good; but individuality is the very principle of _actuality;_ for it is precisely the consciousness by which _what has being in itself_ is just as much _for an other_; and the way of the world does indeed invert the unalterable, but the inversion it actually accomplishes leads away from the _nothingness of abstraction and over into the being of reality_.

The way of the world thus wins out over whatever constitutes virtue in its opposition to it; it wins out over a virtue for which the essenceless abstraction counts as the essence. What it wins out over, though, is nothing real; it is the fabrication of differences that are none, that grandiloquent talk about the good of humanity and about humanity's oppression, about sacrifice for the good and the misuse of gifts; — ideal essences and purposes of this kind sink together into empty words that lift up the heart while leaving reason empty; that edify without building anything up; declamations whose one definite content is that the individual {{{ 327 }}} who pretends to act for such noble purposes, and who deals in such superb phrases, takes himself to count as a superb being; — a swelling-up that enlarges its own head and other people's, but enlarges them with a hollow bloatedness. — The virtue of antiquity had a determinate, secure significance, for its _ground filled with content_ lay in the _substance_ of a people, and its purpose was a good that was _actual and already in existence_; and so it was not directed against actuality as a _universal invertedness_ or against a _way of the world_. {{ 213 }} The virtue under consideration here, by contrast, has stepped outside the substance; it is an essenceless virtue, a virtue belonging merely to representation and to words, both of which go without that content. — The emptiness of this oratory that battles the way of the world would uncover itself immediately if one were required to state what its phrases mean; — hence they are _presupposed as familiar_. The demand to state what is thus familiar would be met either by a fresh flood of phrases, or else countered by an appeal to the heart, which is said to say _inwardly_ what they mean, that is, an inability to state it _in fact_ would be conceded. — The nullity of that oratory seems to have won a kind of unconscious certainty for the culture of our own age as well, inasmuch as all interest has drained away from the entire mass of those phrases and from the manner of puffing oneself up with them; a loss that {{{ 328 }}} finds its expression in their producing nothing but boredom.

The result issuing from this opposition, then, is that consciousness drops, as an empty cloak, the representation of something good _in itself_ that would as yet have no actuality. In its struggle it has undergone the experience: the way of the world is nowhere near as bad as it seemed; for the actuality it has is the universal's own actuality. Along with this experience there falls away the means of bringing the good forth through the _sacrifice_ of individuality; for individuality is precisely the _actualization_ of what has being in itself; and inversion ceases to be looked upon as an inversion of the good, since it is rather nothing else than the inverting of the good, taken as a mere purpose, into actuality; individuality in its movement is the very reality of the universal.

In fact, however, what confronted the consciousness of what has being in itself as the _way of the world_ has thereby been defeated and has disappeared as well. In it the _being-for-itself_ of individuality stood opposed to the essence, or to the universal, and made its appearance as an actuality cut off from _being-in-itself_. But since it has now shown itself that actuality and the universal stand in unseparated unity, the _being-for-itself_ belonging to the way of the world proves to be gone as well, exactly as the _in-itself_ of virtue proves to be nothing more than a {{{ 329 }}} _view_. Individuality within the way of the world might well suppose that its acting is merely _for itself_ or _self-interested;_ it is better than it supposes, for its doing is at once a doing that has being _in itself_, a _universal_ doing. When it acts out of self-interest, it merely fails to know what it is doing, and when it assures us that all human beings act out of self-interest, it is only claiming that no human being has any consciousness of what doing is. — When it acts _for itself_, this is precisely the bringing forth into actuality of what so far only has being _in itself_; the purpose of _being-for-itself_, then, which supposes itself opposed to the in-itself, — its hollow craftiness, as well as its subtle explanations that manage to point out self-interest everywhere, have vanished quite as much as has the purpose of the _in-itself_ together with its oratory.

{{ 214 }} And so _the doing and driving of individuality is purpose in itself; the use of forces, the play of their expressions, is what_ gives life to them, which apart from this would be nothing but the dead in-itself, and the in-itself is not an uncompleted, existenceless, abstract universal, but is itself immediately this presence and actuality of the process of individuality.
