## {{{ 174 }}} A. Observing Reason

This consciousness, for which _being_ carries the significance of what is _its own_, we now see entering once more into meaning and perceiving, yet not as entering the certainty of something merely _other_, but with the certainty of itself being this other. Formerly it had merely _befallen_ this consciousness to perceive and to _experience_ this or that about the thing; here it sets up the observations and the experience on its own. Meaning and perceiving, which earlier sublated themselves for us, consciousness now sublates for itself; reason's aim is to _know_ the truth, to locate as concept whatever counts as a thing for meaning and perceiving, which amounts to having within thinghood nothing but the consciousness of itself. Reason's _interest_ in the world is therefore now universal, for reason is the certainty that it has presence there, or that presence is rational. It searches for its other while knowing that in it there is nothing to possess but itself; what it searches for is only its own infinity.

{{{ 175 }}} At first merely sensing itself in actuality, or knowing actuality only as _its own_ in a general way, reason advances in this sense to a universal taking-possession of the property guaranteed to it, and upon every height and in every depth it plants the emblem of its sovereignty. {{ 138 }} But this superficial "mine" is not reason's ultimate interest; the joy of this universal taking-possession still finds within its property another that stays alien to it, another abstract reason does not have in its own self. Reason senses itself to be an essence deeper than the pure I _is_, and it has to demand that difference, _manifold being_, become for it its own, that it intuit itself as _actuality_, and that it find itself present as shape and thing. But though reason burrow through all the innards of things and lay open all their veins so that it might leap forth to meet itself out of them, it will not arrive at this good fortune; rather, it must beforehand have brought itself to completion in itself, so as then to be capable of experiencing its completion.

Consciousness _observes_; that is, reason wants to discover itself and to have itself as an object that has being, as an _actual, sensuously present_ mode. This observing consciousness does, to be sure, mean and say that it wants to experience _not itself_ but, on the contrary, _the essence that things have as things_. This _consciousness_ means and says so because it _is_ reason while reason {{{ 176 }}} is not yet an object for it as such. If it knew _reason_ to be the same essence in things as in itself, and knew that only within consciousness can reason be present in the shape proper to it, then it would descend into its own depth and look for reason there rather than in things. Had it found reason in that depth, reason would be sent back out from there to actuality, so as to intuit within actuality its sensuous expression, yet would at once take that expression essentially as _concept_. Reason, in the way it comes forward _immediately_ as consciousness's certainty of being all reality, understands its reality to mean the _immediacy of being_, and likewise understands the I's unity with this objective essence to mean an _immediate unity_, a unity in which it has not yet held the moments of being and I apart and then joined them again, or which it has not yet cognized. As observing consciousness it therefore goes at things under the opinion that it is taking them in truth as sensuous things set over against the I; yet its actual doing runs counter to that opinion, for it _cognizes_ things, it transforms their sensuousness _into concepts_, that is, precisely into a being that is simultaneously I, and thereby transforms thinking into a thinking that has being, or being into a being that is thought, and in fact it asserts that only as concepts do things have truth. What emerges in this for this observing consciousness is only what _the things_ {{{ 177 }}} are, while for us it is what _it itself_ is; the result of its movement, though, will be that it comes to be for itself what it is in itself.

_The doing_ of observing reason has to be examined through the moments belonging to its movement, in which it takes up nature, spirit, and finally the relation between the two as sensuous being, and searches for itself as an actuality that has being.
