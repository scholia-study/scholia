## {{{ 435 }}} a. Culture and its Realm of Actuality

The spirit of this world is a spiritual _essence_ that a _self-consciousness_ has pervaded, a self-consciousness knowing itself immediately present as _this being that is for itself_, and knowing the _essence_ as an actuality set over against it. Yet the existence of this world, and equally the actuality of self-consciousness, has its basis in a movement: that self-consciousness externalizes its personality, brings forth its world thereby, and relates to that world as to something alien, so that it now has to take possession of it. But its renouncing of being-for-itself is itself the generating of actuality, and through that renouncing it therefore takes immediate possession of actuality. — Or: self-consciousness is only _something_, has _reality_ only, insofar as it estranges itself from itself; in doing so it posits itself as universal, and it is this universality of its own that constitutes its validity and its actuality. Such _equality_ with all others is therefore not the equality of right, not that immediate being-recognized and validity which self-consciousness has simply because it _is;_ its being valid comes about instead through the estranging mediation of having brought itself into conformity with the universal. Right's spiritless universality takes up into itself every natural mode of character and of existence {{{ 436 }}} alike, and entitles it. The universality that is valid here, however, is the universality that _has come to be_, and this is why it is _actual_.

What gives the individual validity and actuality here is therefore _culture_. Its true _original nature_ and substance is the spirit of the _estrangement_ of _natural_ being. This externalization is therefore that spirit's _purpose_ no less than its _existence;_ it serves at once as the _means_, or the _transition_, by which _substance as thought_ passes over into _actuality_ and, conversely, _determinate individuality_ into _essentiality_. This individuality _cultivates_ itself up to what it _in itself_ is, and only in that way _is_ it _in itself_, possessing actual existence; how far its culture reaches is the measure of its actuality and its power. Although the self knows itself here as _this_ self to be actual, its actuality all the same consists solely in the sublating of the natural self; the originally _determinate_ nature is therefore reduced to the _unessential_ difference of magnitude, to more or less energy of will. {{ 268 }} The will's purpose and content, however, are the property of the universal substance alone and cannot be anything but universal; where a nature's particularity turns into purpose and content, it is something _impotent_ and _unactual;_ it is a _kind_ that labors, in vain and ludicrously, to get itself put into effect; it is the contradiction of conferring actuality on the particular, when actuality is immediately the universal. If, then, individuality is falsely placed in the _particularity_ {{{ 437 }}} of nature and of character, then no individualities and characters are to be found in the real world; rather, individuals have an existence that is the same for one another; that supposed individuality is precisely only the _meant_ existence, which has no permanence in this world, where only what externalizes itself, and hence only the universal, attains actuality. — What is _meant_ therefore counts as what it is, as a kind. Kind does not amount to quite the same thing as _espèce_, "the most frightful of all nicknames, since it marks out mediocrity and voices contempt in its highest degree." _Kind_, though, and being _good of its kind_, is a German turn of phrase that lends this meaning an honest look, as though nothing so bad were intended by it, or that in fact still does not contain within itself the consciousness of what kind is, and of what culture and actuality are.

What shows up, in relation to the single _individual_, as its culture is precisely the essential moment of _substance_ itself: substance's thought universality passing over immediately into actuality, or substance's simple soul, whereby the _in-itself is something recognized_ and _existence_. The movement of the individuality that cultivates itself is therefore immediately substance's becoming as the universal objective essence, that is, the becoming of the world of actuality. That world, though individuality is what brought it about, is for self-consciousness something {{{ 438 }}} immediately estranged, and it bears for self-consciousness the form of an actuality that cannot be budged. Being all the while certain, however, that this world is substance of its own, self-consciousness sets about taking possession of it; the power it gains over the world comes through culture, which viewed from this side looks like a bringing of itself into conformity with actuality, carried as far as the energy of its original character and talent will let it go. What looks here like the individual's violence, to which substance is supposed to succumb and by which it is thereby sublated, is one and the same with substance's own actualization. For wherein the individual's power lies is just this, that it makes itself conform to substance, that is, that it externalizes its own self and so posits itself as the objective substance that has being. Its culture, and its own actuality along with it, thus amount to the actualizing of substance itself.

The self is actual to itself only as _sublated_. For itself, accordingly, it does not make up any unity of the _consciousness_ of itself with the object; the object is instead, for the self, its own negative. {{ 269 }} — By way of the self as soul, then, substance is worked out in its moments so that each opposite inspirits the other, each through its estrangement giving the other its subsistence and receiving its own from it in equal measure. Each moment, meanwhile, holds its determinateness as a validity not to be overcome, and as a fixed actuality over against the other. Thinking pins this difference down in the most universal {{{ 439 }}} manner by setting _good_ and _bad_ in absolute opposition, two terms that flee each other and can by no means become the same. Yet what this fixed being has for its soul is the immediate transition into what is opposed to it; existence is rather the inversion of every determinateness into the determinateness opposed to it, and this estrangement alone is the essence and the preservation of the whole. This actualizing movement and inspiriting of the moments is what we must now consider; estrangement will estrange itself, and by means of it the whole will retrieve itself into its concept.

First the simple substance itself is to be considered in the immediate organization of its existing, still uninspirited moments. — Just as nature unfolds itself into the universal elements, among which _air_ is the _enduring_, purely universal, transparent essence, — water, however, the essence that is always _sacrificed_, — _fire_ their _ensouling_ unity, which just as much always dissolves their opposition as it divides their simplicity into that opposition, — the _earth_, finally, the _firm knot_ of this articulation and the _subject_ of these essences as well as of their process, of their going forth and their return, — so the inner _essence_, or simple spirit, belonging to self-conscious actuality lays itself out as a world into masses of just this sort, universal but spiritual, — into the _first_ mass, the _in itself universal_, _self-equal_ spiritual essence; — into the second, the _being-for-itself_ {{{ 440 }}} essence that has become _unequal_ within itself, the essence that _sacrifices_ and _gives itself away;_ and into the _third_, which is subject in being self-consciousness and holds the force of fire immediately in it; — it is in the first essence that it has awareness of itself as _being-in-itself;_ in the second, though, it undergoes the becoming of _being-for-itself_ by way of the universal's sacrifice. Spirit itself, however, is what the whole is _in_ and _for itself_, that which _splits_ itself in two, into substance as enduring and into substance as self-sacrificing, and which likewise _takes back_ that substance into its unity, being at once the flame that breaks out and devours it and the enduring shape of it. — We see that these essences answer to the communal being and the family of the ethical world, without possessing, though, the native spirit that those two have; conversely, whereas fate is alien to that spirit, self-consciousness here is the actual power of these essences and knows itself as such.

These members must be considered under two aspects: as they are first represented inside pure consciousness, in the guise of _thoughts_, that is, of what has being-_in-itself_, and as they are represented within actual consciousness in the guise of _objective_ essences. — Under that form of simplicity the first is the _good_, as the _self-equal_, immediate and unchangeable _essence_ of all consciousness, — the independent spiritual power belonging to the _in-itself_, beside which the movement of consciousness that is for itself merely plays along. {{ 270 }} The other, by contrast, {{{ 441 }}} is spiritual essence in its _passive_ form, or the universal so far as it surrenders itself and lets individuals take from it the consciousness of their singularity; it is the essence that is null, the _bad_. — This absolute being-dissolved of essence is itself something that endures; just as the first essence is the ground, the starting point, and the result of individuals, who are purely universal within it, so the second is, on the one hand, the self-sacrificing _being for another_ and, on the other hand and for just that reason, their constant return into themselves as _the singular_ and their enduring _becoming-for-itself_.

These simple _thoughts_ of good and bad, however, are estranged from themselves with the same immediacy; they are _actual_, and within actual consciousness they stand as _objective_ moments. The first essence, accordingly, is _state power_, the other _wealth_. — State power is, as the simple _substance_, likewise the universal _work;_ — the absolute _matter at hand_, in which the individuals' _essence_ is expressed to them and their singularity is simply nothing but the consciousness of their _universality;_ — it is likewise the work and simple _result_ from which the fact that it comes out of their _doing_ disappears; it remains the absolute ground and subsistence of all their doing. — This ethereal substance of their life, _simple_ as it is, becomes _being_ through this determination of its unchangeable self-equality, and with that only _being for another_. It is therefore in itself immediately the opposite {{{ 442 }}} of itself, _wealth_. Although wealth is what is passive or null, it is likewise universal spiritual essence, just as much the constantly _becoming result_ of the _labor_ and the _doing of all_ as it dissolves again into the _enjoyment_ of all. In enjoyment individuality does become _for itself_, or become _singular_, yet enjoyment of this kind issues from the universal doing, just as it in turn produces universal labor and the enjoyment of all. What is _actual_ carries, quite simply, the spiritual significance of being immediately universal. Every singular one, to be sure, means at this moment to be acting _self-interestedly;_ for this is the moment where the consciousness of being for himself is what he gives himself, and so he takes it for nothing spiritual; yet even on a merely external view it turns out that in his enjoyment each one gives all of them something to enjoy, that in his labor he works for all as much as for himself, and that all work for him. Thus his being-_for-itself_ is in itself _universal_, while self-interest is something merely meant, unable ever to make actual what it means, namely to do something that would not redound to the good of all.

Self-consciousness thus cognizes, in these two spiritual powers, its substance, its content, and its purpose; it intuits its double essence there, in the one its _being-in-itself_, in the other its _being-for-itself_. {{ 271 }} — As spirit, though, it is at once the negative _unity_ both of their subsistence and of the separation between individuality and the universal, or between {{{ 443 }}} actuality and the self. For the individual, therefore, mastery and wealth are on hand as objects, that is, as things from which it knows itself to be _free_ and of which it means that it can choose between them or take neither. As this free and _pure_ consciousness it steps over against the essence as one that is only _for it_. It then has the essence as _essence_ within itself. — Within this pure consciousness the substance's moments are for it not state power and wealth but rather the thoughts of _good_ and _bad_. — Self-consciousness, further, is the relation in which its pure consciousness stands to its actual one, in which what is thought stands to the objective essence; it is essentially _judgment_. — It has indeed already emerged, for the two sides of the actual essence and through their immediate determinations, which is the good and which the bad: the former is state power, the latter wealth. Only this first judgment is not to be viewed as a spiritual judgment; for in it the one side has been determined merely as what is being-_in-itself_, or positive, the other merely as what is being-_for-itself_, and negative. But as spiritual essences each of them interpenetrates both moments and is therefore not exhausted in those determinations; and the self-consciousness that relates itself to them is _in_ and _for itself;_ so it has to relate to each of them in the doubled manner, whereby their nature as self-estranged determinations will be turned outward.

{{{ 444 }}} For self-consciousness, then, an object counts as _good_ and _in itself_ when self-consciousness finds itself in it, and as bad when what it finds there is its own opposite; the _good_ consists in objective reality's _equality_ with it, the _bad_ in that reality's _inequality_. At the same time, whatever is good and bad _for it_ is good and bad _in itself_, since self-consciousness is just that in which these two moments, being _in itself_ and being _for it_, coincide; it is the actual spirit of those objective essences, and judgment is the proof of its power over them, a power that _makes_ them into what they are _in themselves_. What furnishes their criterion and their truth is not how they immediately are in themselves the _equal_ or the _unequal_, that is, the abstract in-itself or being-for-itself, but what they are in spirit's relation to them, their equality or inequality with spirit. Spirit's _relation_ to them, posited at first as _objects_ and becoming, _through spirit_, the _in-itself_, turns at the same time into their _reflection into themselves_, by which they take on actual spiritual being and by which what _their spirit_ is emerges. But just as their first _immediate determination_ differs from spirit's _relation_ to them, so too the third, their own spirit, will differ from the second. {{ 272 }} — To begin with, their _second in-itself_, which comes forth through spirit's relation to them, must already turn out otherwise than the _immediate_ one; for this _mediation_ by spirit rather sets the _immediate_ determinateness in motion and makes it into something other.

{{{ 445 }}} Accordingly, consciousness in its being _in_ and being-_for-itself_ does find within _state power_ its _simple essence_ and _subsistence_ at large, only not its _individuality_ as such, finds its _in-itself_ there but not its being-_for-itself;_ what it finds there instead is doing disowned as singular doing and yoked into obedience. The individual therefore reflects itself into itself in the face of this power; that power is for it the oppressive essence and the _bad;_ for instead of being what is equal, it is what is simply unequal to individuality. — _Wealth_, by contrast, is the _good;_ it aims at universal enjoyment, gives itself up, and procures for all the consciousness of their self. _In itself_ it is universal beneficence; should it withhold some benefit and fail to accommodate every need, that is a contingency that detracts nothing from its universal and necessary essence, which is to impart itself to all singular individuals and to be a giver with a thousand hands.

These two judgments furnish the thoughts of good and bad with a content that is the reverse of what they had for us. — So far, however, self-consciousness has related to its objects only incompletely, namely only by the standard of _being-for-itself_. Yet consciousness is equally an essence having being-_in-itself_, and it must make this side too into a standard, which is what first completes the spiritual judgment. On this side it is _state power_ {{{ 446 }}} that pronounces its _essence_ to it; that power is in part law at rest, in part government and command, which arranges the universal doing's singular movements; the former is simple substance itself, the latter substance's own doing, which animates and sustains both itself and everyone. The individual thus finds its ground and essence expressed, organized, and put into activity there. — Through the enjoyment of _wealth_, by contrast, it does not experience its universal essence but obtains only the _transient_ consciousness and enjoyment of itself as a _singularity_ that is for itself, and of its _inequality_ with its essence. — The concepts of good and bad thus receive here the content opposed to the previous one.

Both of these ways of judging come upon an _equality_ as well as an _inequality;_ for the first judging consciousness, state power is _unequal_ to it and the enjoyment of wealth _equal_ to it, whereas for the second the former is equal and the latter _unequal_ to it. What is on hand is a doubled _finding of equality_ and a doubled _finding of inequality_, a relation to the two real essentialities that runs in opposite directions. — This differing judging we have ourselves to judge, applying to it the standard already set up. {{ 273 }} Accordingly, the relation of consciousness that _finds equality_ is the _good_, the one that finds inequality the _bad;_ and these two ways of relating are now themselves to be held fast as _different shapes of consciousness_. {{{ 447 }}} Because it relates in differing ways, consciousness falls itself under the determination of the difference of being good or bad, and not because its principle would be either _being-for-itself_ or else pure _being-in-itself_, since both are equally essential moments; the doubled judging just considered set the principles out separately and therefore holds only _abstract_ ways of _judging_. Consciousness in its actuality carries both principles within it, and the difference lies solely in its _essence_, that is, in the _relation_ it bears to the real.

The mode of this relation is the opposed one: the one is a relating to state power and wealth as to something _equal_, the other as to something _unequal_. — The consciousness of the relation that finds equality is the _noble-minded_ one. In the public power it regards what is equal to itself, in that it has in that power its _simple essence_ and the activation of that essence and stands in the service of actual obedience as well as of inner respect toward it, and likewise in wealth, in that wealth procures for it the consciousness of its other essential side, of _being-for-itself;_ hence it regards wealth too as an _essence_ in relation to itself, recognizes the one from whom it enjoys as a benefactor, and holds itself obliged to gratitude.

The consciousness of the other relation, by contrast, is the _base_ one, which holds fast to its _inequality_ with {{{ 448 }}} the two essentialities; which sees in the ruler's power, then, a fetter and a suppression of _being-for-itself_, so that it hates the ruler, renders obedience only out of spite, and stands forever poised for revolt, — and which in wealth, through which it attains the enjoyment of its being-for-itself, likewise regards only the inequality, namely with the enduring _essence;_ since through wealth it comes only to the consciousness of singularity and of transient enjoyment, it both loves and scorns wealth, and once enjoyment has vanished, enjoyment being of itself something that vanishes, it takes its relationship to the rich man to have vanished as well.

As yet these relations express only the _judgment_, the determination of what the two essences are for consciousness as _objects_, and not yet their being _in_ and _for themselves_. The reflection that is represented in the judgment is, in part, so far only _for us_ a positing of the one determination as well as of the other, and hence an equal sublating of both, not yet the reflection of those determinations for consciousness itself. In part they as yet merely _are_ immediately _essences_, having neither _become_ such nor having any _self_-consciousness in them; whatever it is for which they are does not yet amount to their animation; they are predicates that are not yet subject themselves. {{ 274 }} On account of this separation the whole of spiritual judging still comes apart into two consciousnesses, each lying under a one-sided determination. — Now, just as at the start the _indifference_ {{{ 449 }}} between the two sides of estrangement — on the one hand the _in-itself_ of pure consciousness, that is, the determinate _thoughts_ of good and bad — on the other side their _existence_ as state power and wealth, lifted itself to a relation of the two, to _judgment;_ so this external relation has to lift itself to inner unity, or into thinking's relation to actuality, and the spirit of the two shapes of judgment has to step forth. This happens in that _judgment_ becomes _syllogism_, the mediating movement in which the necessity and the middle of the two sides of the judgment come forth.

In the judgment, then, the noble-minded consciousness finds itself over against state power in such a way that this power is indeed not yet a self but so far only the universal substance, of which, however, it is conscious as of its _essence_, as of its purpose and absolute content. Relating to that power so positively, it relates negatively to purposes of its own, to its particular content and existence, letting these disappear. This is the heroism of _service_, — the _virtue_ that offers up singular being to the universal and so brings the universal into existence, — the _person_ who renounces possession and enjoyment unbidden, acting for the power on hand and being actual for it.

Through this movement the universal is joined together with existence in general, just as the existing consciousness through this externalization cultivates itself into essentiality. What this consciousness estranges from itself in {{{ 450 }}} service is its consciousness sunk into existence; but the _being_ that has been estranged is the _in-itself;_ through this culture it therefore obtains respect for itself and respect from others. — But state power, which so far was only the _thought_ universal, the _in-itself_, becomes through this very movement the universal that _has being_, an actual power. It is this actual power only in the actual obedience it attains through the _judgment_ of self-consciousness that it is the _essence_, and through the free self-sacrifice of that consciousness. This doing, which locks essence together with the self, brings forth a _doubled_ actuality: itself, as that which possesses _true_ actuality, and state power in turn as the _true_, which _is valid_.

Through this estrangement, though, state power has still not become a self-consciousness knowing itself to be state power; what is valid is only its _law_, or its _in-itself;_ no _particular will_ belongs to it yet; for the serving self-consciousness has not yet externalized its pure self and inspirited state power with it, but only its being; it has sacrificed to that power only its _existence_, not its being-_in-itself_. {{ 275 }} — Such a self-consciousness is valid as one conforming to the _essence;_ its _being-in-itself_ is what wins it recognition. The others find their _essence_ put into activity in it, though not their being-for-itself, — their thinking or pure consciousness fulfilled, though not their individuality. It is therefore valid in their _thoughts_ and {{{ 451 }}} enjoys _honor_. It is the _proud_ vassal who is active for state power insofar as that power is not a will of its own but an _essential_ will, and who is valid to himself only in this _honor_, only in the _essential_ representation of general opinion, not in the _grateful_ representation of individuality, for he has not helped individuality to its _being-for-itself_. His _language_, were he to relate himself to a will of state power's own, a will that has not yet come to be, would be the _counsel_ that he gives for the universal best.

State power is therefore still without will in the face of counsel, and does not decide among the different opinions about the universal best. It is not yet _government_, and hence not yet in truth actual state power. — _Being-for-itself_, the _will_ that as will remains unsacrificed, is the inner, sequestered spirit of the estates, a spirit that, over against its own speech about the _universal_ best, keeps back its _particular_ best, and that tends to turn this prattle about the universal best into a surrogate for action. The sacrifice of existence carried out in service is indeed complete once carried through to death; but the very danger of death withstood and outlived leaves a determinate existence behind, and with it a _particular for-itself_, which renders counsel for the universal best ambiguous and suspect, and which in fact keeps back its own opinion and its particular will against the {{{ 452 }}} power of the state. It therefore still relates unequally to that power and falls under the determination of the base consciousness, that of standing forever poised for rebellion.

This contradiction, which consciousness has to sublate, carries within the present form, that of standing in _being-for-itself_'s inequality over against state power's universality, the further form that the externalization of existence just spoken of, in reaching completion, namely in death, is itself something that has being and not something returning into consciousness, — that consciousness does not outlive it and be _in_ and _for itself_, but merely passes over into the unreconciled opposite. Hence the only true sacrifice of _being-for-itself_ is the one in which it surrenders itself as completely as it does in death and yet preserves itself just as much in this externalization; what it is in itself is thereby made actual, namely the self-identical unity of itself with itself as the opposed. {{ 276 }} In that the sequestered inner spirit, the self as such, steps forth and estranges itself, state power is at that same stroke raised into a self of its own; whereas without this estrangement the deeds of honor and of the noble consciousness, and the counsels issuing from its insight, would stay the ambiguous thing that still harbored that sequestered ambush of particular intention and self-will.

But this estrangement occurs solely in _language_, which here steps forth in its own peculiar significance {{{ 453 }}}. — In the world of ethical life, as _law_ and _command_, — in the world of actuality, at first as _counsel_, language takes the _essence_ as its content and is the form of that essence; here, though, the form which language itself is becomes its own content, and it is valid as _language;_ it is the force of speaking, as such, that carries out what is to be carried out. For language is the pure self's _existence_, as self; in language the _singularity of self-consciousness that is for itself_ enters into existence as such, in such a way that it is _for others_. _I_ as this _pure_ I is nowhere else _there;_ in any other expression it lies sunk in an actuality and in a shape out of which it can pull back; out of its action, as out of its physiognomic expression, it is reflected into itself, and it leaves such incomplete existence, where there is always as much too much as too little, lying about without soul. Language, however, holds it in its purity; language alone gives utterance to _I_, to the I itself. Being _existence_, this _existence_ of the I is an objectivity that has the I's true nature in it. _I_ is _this_ I — but just as much a _universal_ one; its appearing is just as immediately the externalization and the disappearance of _this_ I, and thereby its remaining in place within its universality. The _I_ giving utterance to itself is _heard;_ it is a contagion, in which it has immediately passed over into unity with all those for whom it is there and is universal self-consciousness. — In its being _heard_, its {{{ 454 }}} _existence_ has of itself immediately _died away;_ this otherness of its own is drawn back into itself; and just this is its existence, as a self-conscious _Now_: to be, as it stands there, not there, and by this disappearing to be there. So this disappearing is itself immediately its remaining; it is its own knowledge of itself, and its knowledge of itself as something that has gone over into another self, that has been heard, and that is universal.

Spirit comes by this actuality here for the reason that the extremes whose _unity_ it is have, with equal immediacy, the determination of being actualities of their own for themselves. Their unity has been decomposed into brittle sides, each of them being for the other an actual object shut out from it. Unity therefore steps forth as a _middle_ that is shut out from, and distinguished from, the sequestered actuality of the sides; it thus has an actual objectivity of its own, distinct from its sides, and is _for them_, that is, it is something existing. {{ 277 }} The _spiritual substance_ enters into existence as such only once it has won for its sides such self-consciousnesses as know this pure self to be an _immediately valid_ actuality and in this just as immediately know that they are this only through the estranging _mediation_. Through the former the moments are purified into the self-knowing category and thereby purified to the point of being moments of spirit; through the latter spirit steps as spirituality into {{{ 455 }}} existence. — Spirit is thus the middle that presupposes those extremes and is generated through their existence, — but just as much the spiritual whole that breaks out between them, that divides itself into them, and that only through this contact generates each of them into a whole in its own principle. — That the two extremes are already _in themselves_ sublated and decomposed brings forth their unity, and this unity is the movement that joins the two together, exchanges their determinations, and joins them together, and indeed does so _in each extreme_. This mediation thereby places each of the two extremes' _concept_ into that extreme's actuality; or, what each of them is _in itself_ it turns into that one's _spirit_.

The two extremes, state power and the noble-minded consciousness, are decomposed by this, the former into the abstract universal that is obeyed and into the will that is for itself, which, however, does not yet belong to it; — the latter into the obedience of a sublated existence, that is, into _being-in-itself_ as self-respect and honor, — and into that pure being-for-itself not yet sublated, the will still lying in ambush. Both sides are purified into two moments, moments of language accordingly, and these are the _abstract universal_, which goes by the name of the universal best, and the _pure self_ that renounced, in service, its consciousness sunk into a manifold existence. Both are in their concept the same, for the pure {{{ 456 }}} self is precisely the abstractly universal, and hence their unity is posited as their middle. But the _self_ is so far actual only at the extreme of consciousness, — the _in-itself_ only at the extreme of state power; what consciousness lacks is that state power should have passed over to it not merely as _honor_ but actually, — what state power lacks is that obedience be rendered to it not merely as to the so-called _universal best_ but as to a will, or that it be the deciding self. That unity of the concept, where state power yet stands and to which consciousness has purified itself, becomes actual through this _mediating movement_, whose simple existence, serving as the _middle_, is language. — For its sides, however, it does not yet have two selves on hand as _self;_ for state power is only being inspirited into a self; hence this language is not yet spirit as spirit fully knows and utters itself.

Being the extreme of the self, the noble-minded consciousness shows up as the one from which the _language_ issues by which the sides of the relationship shape themselves into ensouled wholes. {{ 278 }} — The heroism of mute service becomes the _heroism_ of _flattery_. This speaking reflection of service constitutes the spiritual middle that decomposes itself, and it reflects not only its own extreme into itself but also the extreme of universal power back into that extreme itself, and makes {{{ 457 }}} that power, which so far is only _in itself_, into _being-for-itself_ and into the singularity of self-consciousness. It thereby becomes the spirit of this power, — to be an _unlimited monarch;_ — _unlimited_, for the language of flattery raises the power into its purified _universality;_ — this moment, as the product of language, of existence purified into spirit, is a cleansed self-equality; — _monarch_, for that language likewise raises _singularity_ to its peak; what the noble-minded consciousness externalizes on this side of simple spiritual unity is the _in-itself of its thinking_ in its purity, the I that is its own. More determinately, language raises singularity, otherwise only something _meant_, into its existing purity by conferring on the monarch his own _name;_ for the name is the one place where the _difference_ of the singular one from all the rest is not _meant_ but rather made actual by everyone; in the name, the singular one as purely singular _is valid_ no longer within his own consciousness merely, but within the consciousness of all. Through the name, then, the monarch is simply set apart from all, singled out and alone; in it he is the atom that can impart nothing of its essence and has no equal. — This name is thereby the reflection into itself, or the _actuality_, that universal power has _in it itself;_ through the name that power is the _monarch_. He, _this singular one_, conversely knows _himself, this singular one_, {{{ 458 }}} to be the universal power in that the nobles place themselves around the throne not merely as ready for the service of state power but as _ornaments_, and in that they are always _telling_ the one who sits on it what he _is_.

The language of its praise is in this way the spirit that closes the two extremes together within _state power itself_; abstract power is by this language reflected back into itself and given the moment belonging to the other extreme, the _being-for-itself_ that wills and decides, and with it a self-conscious existence; or by this route this _singular actual_ self-consciousness arrives at _knowing itself with certainty_ to be the power. It is that point of the self in which, through the externalization of _inner certainty_, the many points have converged. — But since this spirit peculiar to state power has its actuality and its sustenance in the sacrifice of the doing and the thinking of the noble-minded consciousness, state power is _self-estranged self-subsistence;_ the noble-minded consciousness, the extreme of _being-for-itself_, gets back the extreme of _actual universality_ in exchange for that universality of thinking into which it had externalized itself; the state's power has _passed over_ into it. {{ 279 }} Only in the noble-minded consciousness does state authority first become truly active; within its _being-for-itself_ that authority stops being the _inert essence_ which it seemed to be while it appeared as the extreme constituted by abstract being-in-itself. — Considered _in itself_, what is meant by _state power reflected into itself_, or by its having become spirit, is nothing beyond this: {{{ 459 }}} that it has turned into a _moment_ of _self-consciousness_, that is, that it is only as something _sublated_. Therewith it is now the essence as one whose spirit consists in being sacrificed and given away, or it exists as _wealth_. — It does indeed go on subsisting as an actuality alongside the wealth into which, by its concept, it is forever becoming; but as an actuality whose concept is precisely this movement: to pass over, by way of the service and the veneration through which it comes to be, into its opposite, into the externalization of power. For itself, then, the peculiar _self_ that is its will turns, through the noble-minded consciousness's throwing of itself away, into a universality that externalizes itself, into a complete singularity and contingency lying at the mercy of any stronger will; of _universally_ acknowledged and incommunicable self-subsistence there remains to it only the empty name.

If, then, the noble-minded consciousness defined itself as that which would relate to the universal power in an _equal_ manner, the truth about it is rather that, while serving, it keeps back for itself its own being-for-itself, whereas in the genuine renunciation of its personality it is the actual sublating and tearing apart of the universal substance. Its spirit is the relationship of utter inequality: on the one hand, keeping its will in its honor; on the other hand, in surrendering that will, partly estranging itself from its own inwardness {{{ 460 }}} and becoming the highest inequality with itself, partly subjugating the universal substance to itself in that very surrender and making that substance completely unequal to itself. — Plainly, then, the determinateness it possessed in the _judgment_ over against what was called the base consciousness has thereby vanished, and with it that consciousness as well. The base consciousness has reached its purpose, which was to bring the universal power under being-for-itself.

Self-consciousness, thus made rich by the universal power, exists as _universal beneficence_, or that beneficence is _wealth_, which is in turn itself an object for consciousness. For wealth is for consciousness the universal in subjugated form, a universal that this first sublating has not yet brought back absolutely into the self. — What the _self_ has as its object is not yet _itself as self_, but the _sublated universal essence_. Because this object has come into being only now, what is posited is the _immediate_ relation of consciousness to it, a consciousness which accordingly has not yet displayed its inequality with the object; it is the noble-minded consciousness, which obtains its being-for-itself from the universal that has become unessential, and which therefore acknowledges that universal and is grateful toward its benefactor.

{{ 280 }} In wealth itself the moment of being-for-itself is already present. It is not the selfless universal that state power is, nor the ingenuous inorganic nature of spirit; it is that nature {{{ 461 }}} as it clings to itself by will against whoever would seize hold of it for enjoyment. But since wealth has only the form of essence, its being-for-itself is one-sided, not _in itself_ but rather the in-itself in sublated form, and so it is the individual's return into itself within enjoyment, a return devoid of essence. Wealth accordingly stands in need of animation itself; and its reflective movement amounts to this, that what only is for itself should become _in_ and _for itself_, that what is the sublated essence should become essence; in this way it comes to have its own spirit in it itself. — The form of this movement having been laid out already, it suffices here to determine its content.

Here, then, the noble-minded consciousness does not relate itself to the object as to essence in general; rather, what is alien to it is _being-for-itself_ itself; it _finds_ its self as such estranged and _already there_ for it, as a fixed objective actuality which another fixed being-for-itself must hand over to it. Being-for-itself is its object, hence what is _its own;_ but in being an object it is at once and immediately an alien actuality possessing a being-for-itself and a will of its own, which means that consciousness sees its self placed in an alien will's power, dependent on whether that will chooses to release the self to it.

{{{ 462 }}} From any singular side self-consciousness is able to abstract, and for that reason, in an obligation touching such a side, it keeps its being-recognized and its _in-itself validity_ as an essence that is for itself. Here, though, on the side of its pure and most proper _actuality_, or of its I, it sees itself outside itself and in the possession of another; it sees that its _personality_ as such hangs upon another's contingent personality, upon the chance of a moment, upon an arbitrary whim or upon whatever circumstance is most indifferent. — In the condition of right, whatever stands under the objective essence's power appears as a _contingent content_ that can be abstracted from, and that power does not touch the _self_ as _such;_ on the contrary, the self is there recognized. Here alone it sees that the certainty of itself, taken as such, is the most essenceless of all, that pure personality is absolute impersonality. Its gratitude therefore has for its spirit a feeling of this deepest abjection and equally of the deepest rebellion. Since the pure I intuits itself outside itself and torn apart, in this being torn apart whatever possesses continuity and universality, whatever goes by the name of law, of good, of right, has at the same time fallen apart and come to ruin; all that is alike is dissolved, for the _purest inequality_ is on hand, the absolute unessentiality of what is absolutely essential, being-for-itself's own being-outside-itself; the pure I is absolutely broken up.

{{{ 463 }}} {{ 281 }} If, then, this consciousness does get back from wealth the objectivity of being-for-itself and sublates it, this is not merely, in terms of its concept, as incomplete as the preceding reflection was; it is also unsatisfying for consciousness itself; since here the self takes itself in as something objective, the reflection sets up the immediate contradiction within the pure I itself. Yet as self it stands at the same time immediately over this contradiction: it is that absolute elasticity which sublates once again this having-been-sublated of the self, which casts off the abjection of having its being-for-itself come to it as something alien, and which, in revolt against this receiving of itself, is _for itself_ in the very act of _receiving_.

Since the relationship of this consciousness is thus tied up with this absolute being torn apart, the difference within its spirit, its being determined as noble-minded over against the _base_, falls away, and the two are one and the same. — The spirit of wealth as benefactor may further be distinguished from the spirit of the consciousness that receives the benefaction, and calls for separate consideration. — It was the essenceless being-for-itself, the essence given away. By imparting itself, however, it becomes the _in-itself_; in fulfilling its determination of sacrificing itself, it sublates the singularity of enjoying only for itself, and, as singularity sublated, it amounts to _universality_ or _essence_. — That which it imparts, that which it hands over to others, is _being-for-itself_. It {{{ 464 }}} does not, however, surrender itself in the manner of a selfless nature, as the condition of life that ingenuously gives itself away, but as an essence that is self-conscious and holds on to itself for itself: the element's inorganic power, which the receiving consciousness knows to be perishable in itself, is not what wealth is; wealth is rather the power over the self, a power aware of itself as _independent_ and _arbitrary_, and aware at the same time that what it doles out is another's self. — Wealth thus shares abjection with its client, but where the client has rebellion, wealth has arrogance. For on the one side wealth, like the client, knows _being-for-itself_ as a contingent _thing;_ but this contingency, in whose power personality stands, is what wealth itself is. In this arrogance, which fancies that by a meal it has obtained an alien I-self and has thereby purchased the subjugation of that self's innermost essence, wealth overlooks the other's inner rebellion; it overlooks the complete throwing-off of every fetter, this pure being torn apart, for which, since the _self-equality_ of being-for-itself has become utterly unequal to it, everything alike, all subsistence, has been torn apart, and which therefore tears apart the benefactor's opinion and view most of all. Wealth stands immediately before this innermost abyss, before this bottomless depth where every hold and all substance has disappeared; and in that depth what it makes out is merely a vulgar thing, a toy for its {{{ 465 }}} mood, an accident of its caprice; its spirit is the wholly essenceless opinion that it is the spirit-forsaken surface.

{{ 282 }} As self-consciousness had a language of its own over against state power, or as spirit came forth between those extremes as an actual middle, so too does it have a language over against wealth, and its rebellion has a language of its own still more. The first of these languages, which supplies wealth with the consciousness of its essentiality and thereby gets hold of wealth, is a language of flattery too, though an ignoble one; — for the essence it proclaims is one it knows to be given away, an essence that is not _in itself_. Flattery's language, however, as was noted earlier, is spirit that is still one-sided. Its moments are, to be sure, the _self_ that the culture of service has purified into pure existence, and power's _being-in-itself_. But that pure concept where the simple _self_ is the same as the _in-itself_, that pure I the same as this pure essence or thinking, — this unity of the two sides between which reciprocal action occurs, is absent from the consciousness of this language; for that consciousness the object still counts as the _in-itself_ set over against the self; or the _object_ is not at once its own _self_ as such. — The language of being torn apart, though, is the perfect language, and it is the true existing spirit of this entire world of culture. This self-consciousness, to which {{{ 466 }}} there belongs the rebellion that casts off its own abjection, is directly the absolute self-equality within absolute being torn apart, pure self-consciousness mediating itself purely with itself. It is the equality belonging to the identical judgment, where subject and predicate are one and the same personality. Yet this same identical judgment is simultaneously the infinite judgment; for the personality in question is absolutely split in two, so that subject and predicate are simply _indifferent beings_, each of no concern to the other, lacking necessary unity, indeed such that each of them is the power of a personality of its own. For its object _being-for-itself_ has _its own being-for-itself_, as something simply _other_ and at the same time just as immediately as _itself_, — as itself in the shape of another, not because that other would carry a different content, but rather the content is one and the same self, and the form is that of absolute opposition and of an entirely separate, indifferent existence. — What is present here, then, is the spirit belonging to this real world of culture, a spirit _conscious of its concept_ and of itself in its truth.

Spirit here is that absolute and universal inversion, the estrangement of actuality and of thought alike; it is _pure culture_. The experience made in this world is that truth belongs neither to the _actual essences_ of power and of wealth, — nor to their determinate _concepts_, good and bad, nor to consciousness of the good and the bad, the noble-minded and the base; rather, all these {{{ 467 }}} moments invert themselves one within the other, and each is its own opposite. {{ 283 }} — Universal power, being the _substance_, in arriving at a spirituality of its own by way of the principle of individuality, takes the self that is its own only as a name attached to it, and, being _actual_ power, is rather the powerless essence that sacrifices itself. — But this selfless essence given away, this self turned into a thing, is on the contrary essence returning into itself; it amounts to _being-for-itself that is for itself_, the existence of spirit. — The _thoughts_ of these essences, of the _good_ and of the _bad_, undergo the same inversion in this movement; whatever is determined to be good is bad; whatever is bad is good. The consciousness of each of these moments, judged as the noble and as the base consciousness, is in truth just as much the inversion of what such determinations ought to be: the noble-minded is quite as base and abject as abjection turns round into the nobility of self-consciousness in its most cultivated freedom. — Everything likewise, formally regarded, is _outwardly_ the inverse of what it is for _itself;_ what it is for itself, in its turn, it is not in truth, but is something other than it wants to be, being-for-itself is rather the loss of itself, and estrangement from itself is rather self-preservation. — What is on hand, then, is this: that all the moments exercise a universal justice upon {{{ 468 }}} one another, each estranging itself from its own self to just the degree that it fancies itself into its opposite and thereby inverts that opposite. — True spirit, however, is just this unity of what is absolutely separated, and indeed it comes into existence as their middle precisely by means of the _free actuality_ these _selfless_ extremes themselves possess. Its existence is universal _speaking_ and the _judging_ that tears apart, before which every one of those moments that are meant to count as essences and as actual members of the whole dissolves, and which is likewise this self-dissolving play with itself. Such judging and speaking is therefore the true and the invincible, since it overpowers everything; it is that with which, in this real world, one has _truly and solely_ to do. Every part of this world thereby reaches the point where its spirit is pronounced, or where one speaks of it with spirit and says of it what it is. — The honest consciousness takes each moment for an abiding essentiality, and it is the uncultured thoughtlessness of not knowing that it is doing the inverted thing just as much. The consciousness torn apart, by contrast, is the consciousness of inversion, and indeed of absolute inversion; what rules within it is the concept, which draws together thoughts that lie far apart for honesty, and whose language is on that account witty.

What spirit's discourse from and about itself contains is therefore the inversion of all concepts and {{{ 469 }}} all realities, the universal deceit practiced on itself and on others, and the shamelessness of uttering that deceit is for precisely that reason the greatest truth. {{ 284 }} This discourse is the derangement of the musician "who piled up and blended thirty arias, Italian and French, tragic and comic, of every sort of character; at one moment, with a deep bass, he descended as far as hell, then he tightened his throat and with a falsetto tore apart the heights of the air, by turns frenzied, mollified, imperious, and derisive." — To the calm consciousness, which honestly puts the melody of what is good and true into a sameness of tones, that is, into a single note, this discourse looks like "a rambling of wisdom and madness, a mixture of adroitness and baseness in equal measure, of ideas as correct as they are false, of a perversity of feeling as total, a shamefulness as complete, as its candor and truth are entire. It will be unable to refuse to enter into all these tones and to run up and down the entire scale of feelings, from deepest contempt and rejection up to highest admiration and emotion; fused into these there will be a ridiculous trait that robs them of their nature;" those tones will possess, in their very candor, a reconciling trait, and in their shattering depth that all-powerful trait which gives spirit to itself.

{{{ 470 }}} Set the discourse of this confusion that is clear to itself alongside the discourse of that _simple consciousness_ of what is true and good, and the latter can only be monosyllabic against the open eloquence, conscious of itself, that belongs to the spirit of culture; for there is nothing it can tell that spirit which the spirit does not already know and say of its own accord. Should it go beyond its monosyllables, it says just what spirit pronounces, and in doing so commits the additional folly of imagining that what it says is something new and different. Its very syllables, _shameful, base_, are already this folly, since spirit says them of itself. When this spirit in its discourse inverts everything monotonous, on the ground that what is thus self-same is a mere abstraction while in its actuality it is inversion in it itself, and when the straightforward consciousness, on the contrary, defends the good and the noble, that is, what keeps itself the same in its expression, in the one way here available to it, — namely by pleading that the good does not forfeit its worth merely for being _tied_ to the bad or _mixed_ with it, since this is its _condition_ and _necessity_, since herein consists the _wisdom_ of nature; — then this consciousness, believing itself to be contradicting, has only gathered the content of spirit's discourse into a trivial formula, one which thoughtlessly makes the very _opposite_ of what is noble and good into the _condition_ for it and the _necessity_ of it, and supposes itself to be saying something other than just this, that what goes under the name of noble and {{{ 471 }}} good has for its essence the inversion of itself, just as the bad, conversely, is what is excellent.

{{ 285 }} Suppose the simple consciousness puts in place of this spiritless _thought_ the _actuality_ of what is excellent, adducing that excellence in the _example_ of an invented case or else of a genuine anecdote, and so showing that it is not an empty name but _is present_: then over against it stands the _universal_ actuality of inverted doing, the whole real world, within which such an example makes up only something quite isolated, an _espèce;_ and to exhibit the existence of what is good and noble as a single anecdote, invented or genuine, is the harshest thing that could be said of it. — If the simple consciousness demands, in the end, that this whole world of inversion be dissolved, it cannot require of the _individual_ a withdrawal from that world, for it is this very world that conditions Diogenes in his tub, and the demand addressed to the singular one is exactly what counts as the bad, namely to look after oneself _for oneself_ as a _singular one_. Directed at universal _individuality_, however, the demand for such a withdrawal cannot mean that reason is to surrender once more the spiritual, cultivated consciousness it has reached, is to sink the spread-out wealth of its moments back into the simple natural heart, and is to relapse into the wildness and the nearness of animal consciousness, which goes by the name of {{{ 472 }}} nature and also of innocence; the demand for dissolution can be raised only against the _spirit_ of culture itself, namely that out of its confusion it should return to itself as _spirit_ and gain a still higher consciousness.

In fact, though, spirit has already carried this out in itself. Consciousness's being torn apart, self-conscious of itself and pronouncing itself, is the scornful laughter directed at existence as well as at the confusion of the whole and at itself; and it is likewise the dying-away of this whole confusion, a dying-away that still hears itself. — This vanity of all actuality, as of every determinate concept, a vanity that hears itself, is the way the real world is doubly reflected into itself; once in _this self_ of consciousness, taken as _this_ one, and a second time in the pure _universality_ of that self, or in thinking. On the first side, spirit that has come to itself keeps its gaze turned into the world of actuality, which is still its purpose and its immediate content; on the other side, its gaze is turned partly inward alone and negatively against that world, partly away from it toward heaven, so that the beyond of that world is its object.

On that side where the return into the self takes place, the _vanity_ of all _things_ counts as consciousness's _own vanity_, or consciousness itself _is_ vain. It is the self that is for itself, a self that knows how not merely to pass judgment on everything and chatter about it, but wittily to state the fixed essences of actuality {{{ 473 }}} together with the fixed determinations that judgment sets up, and to state them in their _contradiction_, and this contradiction is their truth. — Looked at from the side of form, it knows all things as estranged from themselves: _being-for-itself_ cut off from _being-in-itself;_ likewise what is meant, and the purpose, cut off from truth; and cut off from both of these again, _being for another_, the pretense cut off from the real opinion and from the true matter and intention. {{ 286 }} — It thus knows how to pronounce each moment correctly over against the other, and in general the inversion of them all; it knows what each is better than each is, no matter how each may be determined. Since its acquaintance with the substantial reaches only to the side of _disunity_ and _conflict_ that the substantial unites within itself, and not to the side of that uniting, it understands very well how to _judge_ the substantial, but has lost the capacity to _grasp_ it. — This vanity, meanwhile, needs the vanity of all things, so as to draw from them the consciousness of the self; it therefore produces that vanity itself and is the soul that sustains it. Power and wealth are the supreme purposes of its exertion; it is aware that renunciation and sacrifice are what cultivate it into the universal, bring it into possession of the universal, and give it, in that possession, universal validity; they are the actual recognized powers. But this validity of its own is itself vain, and in the very act of mastering power and wealth it knows them not to be self-essences; it knows itself, rather, as their power and knows them as vain. That it thus stands outside power and wealth even while {{{ 474 }}} possessing them, it exhibits in witty language, which is for that reason what interests it most and is the truth of the whole; in such language _this_ self, as the pure self belonging neither to actual determinations nor to determinations of thought, becomes for itself something spiritual, something of truly universal validity. It _is_ the nature of all relationships that tears itself apart, and it is the conscious tearing-apart of them; yet only as rebellious self-consciousness does it know its own being torn apart, and in knowing it, it has immediately raised itself above it. Within that vanity all content turns into something negative, which can no longer be grasped positively; nothing remains as positive object except the _pure I itself_, while the consciousness torn apart, _in itself_, is this pure self-equality belonging to self-consciousness that has returned into itself.
