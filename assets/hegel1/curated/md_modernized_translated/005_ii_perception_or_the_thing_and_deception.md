## {{{ 38 }}} II. Perception; or the Thing, and Deception

{{ 71 }} Immediate certainty does not take what is true for itself, since the universal is its truth, whereas what it wants to take is the _This_. Perception, on the other hand, takes what has being for it as something universal. Universality being its principle throughout, the moments that immediately set themselves apart within perception are likewise universal: the I is universal, the object universal. That principle has _come about_ for us, and therefore our taking-in of perception is not, like that of sense-certainty, one that merely appears, but one that is necessary. With this coming-about of the principle the two moments have likewise arisen, the moments that in their appearing merely _drop out_ of it: the one is the movement of pointing-out, the other that same movement but taken as something simple; the first is _perceiving_, the second the _object_. The object is, in essence, the same as what the movement is: the movement is the unfolding and differentiating of the moments, the object is their being gathered together. The universal as principle is, for us or {{{ 39 }}} in itself, the _essence_ of perception, while over against this abstraction the two distinguished terms, the perceiving and the perceived, are the _unessential_. In fact, though, both of them are essential, since each is itself the universal, that is, the essence; but because their relation to one another is that of opposites, within that relation only one can hold the rank of the essential, and so the difference between essential and unessential must get distributed across them. The one that is determined as the simple, namely the object, is the essence, indifferent to whether it is perceived or not; perceiving, however, as the movement, is what is unstable, what may be or may not be, and is the unessential.

This object must now be determined more closely, and this determination developed briefly out of the result that has come about; the fuller development does not belong here. Since its principle, the universal, is in its simplicity something _mediated_, the object must express this at itself as its nature; it thereby shows itself to be _the thing that has many properties_. It is to perception, and not to immediate certainty, that the wealth of sensuous knowledge belongs, since in immediate certainty that wealth was merely what played alongside; for perception alone has _negation_, difference, or manifoldness in its essence.

{{ 72 }} The This is accordingly posited as being _not this_, or as _sublated;_ and with that it is not nothing but {{{ 40 }}} a determinate nothing, that is, _a nothing possessed of a content_, namely _the This_. Through this the sensuous is itself still there, yet not as immediate certainty required it to be, namely as the singular that is meant, but as something universal, or as what is going to determine itself as _property_. _Sublating_ displays here its true doubled meaning, the one we already noticed in the negative: it is at one and the same time a _negating_ and a _keeping_; the nothing, being the _nothing of the This_, holds onto immediacy and is sensuous itself, though an immediacy that is universal. — Being, though, is universal because it carries mediation, the negative, at itself; and where it _expresses_ this at its own immediacy, it amounts to a _differentiated, determinate_ property. With that, _many_ such properties are posited at once, each the negative of the others. Since they find expression in the universal's _simplicity_, these _determinatenesses_, which properly become properties only once a further determination is added, relate _to themselves_, stand _indifferent_ toward one another, each by itself, free of the rest. The simple self-equal universality, however, is once more distinguished from these determinatenesses of its own, and free of them; it is the pure self-relating, the _medium_ in which all of these determinatenesses have their place and thus _interpenetrate_ within it as within a _simple_ unity, yet without _touching one another;_ for it is precisely {{{ 41 }}} their share in this universality that leaves them indifferent on their own account. — This abstract universal medium, which one may call _thinghood_ at large or the _pure essence_, is nothing but the _Here_ and the _Now_ as they have turned out to be, namely a _simple together_ of many; yet the many, _in their determinateness_, are _simply universal_ themselves. This salt is a simple Here, and manifold at once; it is white, _also_ pungent, _also_ cubical in shape, _also_ of a determinate heaviness, and so on. All of these many properties sit in one and the same simple _Here_, and there they interpenetrate; no one of them occupies a Here of its own apart from the rest, each is instead everywhere present, at the very spot where the others are; and although no distinct Heres divide them, they still leave one another unaffected in this interpenetration; the white neither affects nor alters the cubical, and neither of the two affects the pungent, and so on; rather, each being simply a _relating of itself to itself_, it lets the others be and reaches them only by way of the indifferent _Also_. This _Also_, then, is the pure universal in its own right, the medium, the _thinghood_ that gathers them together in just this way.

{{ 73 }} In the relationship that has resulted, what has so far been observed and worked out is only the character of positive universality; a further side, though, offers itself, and it too has to be taken in. For if the many determinate properties were flatly indifferent {{{ 42 }}} and related to nothing but themselves, they would be nothing _determinate;_ they are determinate only to the extent that they _differentiate_ themselves and _relate_ to _others_ as opposites. Given this opposition, however, the simple unity of the medium they share, a unity no less essential to them than negation is, cannot hold them together; their differentiation, insofar as it is not indifferent but exclusive, negating what is other, therefore falls outside this simple medium; and the medium is on that account not an _Also_, an indifferent unity, alone, but equally a _One, an exclusive unity_. — The One is that _moment of negation_ which relates itself to itself in a simple manner and shuts out what is other, and through which _thinghood_ gets determined as _thing_. At the property, negation stands as _determinateness_ immediately at one with the immediacy of being, and that immediacy, through being united with negation, is universality; as _One_, on the other hand, negation stands as it is once released from this unity with its opposite, in and for itself.

Taken together in these moments, the thing is completed as what is true in perception, to the extent that developing it here is needed. It is _α_) indifferent passive universality, the _Also_ belonging to the many properties, or rather to the many _matters, β_) negation, likewise as something simple, or the _One_, which shuts out properties that stand opposed, and _γ_) the many _properties_ in their own right, the relation {{{ 43 }}} between the first two moments: negation as it bears upon the indifferent element and spreads itself out there into a multitude of differences; the point of singularity radiating into plurality within the medium of subsistence. Regarded from the side on which these differences fall under the indifferent medium, they are universal themselves, refer to nothing but themselves, and leave one another untouched; regarded from the side on which they fall under the negative unity, they are simultaneously exclusive, and they necessarily hold this opposed relation toward properties kept out of _***their*** Also_. Sensuous universality, that is, the _immediate_ unity that being forms with the negative, becomes _property_ only on these terms, insofar as the One and pure universality have been developed out of it and marked off from each other and it locks the two of them together; only this relation of sensuous universality to the pure essential moments brings the _thing_ to completion.

Such, then, is how the thing of perception is constituted; and consciousness is determined as something perceiving so long as this thing counts as its object; consciousness has _merely to take_ the object and to relate as pure apprehending; whatever results for it in that way is what is true. Were consciousness itself to do something in this taking, it would alter the truth by any such adding or omitting. {{ 74 }} Because the object is what is true and universal, self-equal, while consciousness is to itself the changeable {{{ 44 }}} and unessential one, consciousness may well come to apprehend the object wrongly and so deceive itself. What perceives has the consciousness of the possibility of deception; for within the universality that is the principle, _otherness_ itself is immediately there for it, though as what is _null_, as something sublated. Its criterion of truth is therefore _self-equality_, and its relating consists in apprehending what it takes as self-equal. Since what is diverse is there for it at the same time, its relating amounts to bringing the diverse moments of its apprehending into relation with one another; should an inequality show itself in that comparison, however, what it registers is no untruth on the part of the object, which is self-equal, but an untruth on the part of perceiving.

We must now see what experience consciousness undergoes in the course of its actual perceiving. _For us_ that experience already lies within the development just laid out of the object and of the relating of consciousness toward it, and it will amount to nothing more than an unfolding of the contradictions lodged there. — Whatever object I take in offers itself as _purely one;_ and I likewise become aware at it of the property, which is _universal_ and on that score reaches past singularity. So the initial being of the objective essence, its being as a One, was not the being it truly has; and because _the object_ is what is true, it is into me that the untruth falls, and my apprehending was faulty. What the _universality_ {{{ 45 }}} of the property requires is that I take the objective essence rather as a _community_ at large. Going on, I now perceive the property as something _determinate_, as _opposed_ to what is other and as shutting it out. In fact, then, my apprehending of the objective essence went wrong when I determined it to be a _community_ shared with others, or continuity; what the _determinateness_ of the property requires instead is that I break the continuity apart and posit the essence as an exclusive One. At the One thus broken off I come upon many such properties, which leave one another unaffected and stand indifferent toward each other; so my perceiving of the object went wrong when I apprehended it as something _exclusive;_ the object is rather, having been mere continuity at large before, a universal _shared medium_ now, one in which many properties each exist on their own as sensuous _universalities_ and, being _determinate_, shut the others out. But with this, what is simple and true in what I perceive turns out not to be a universal medium either, but the _singular property_ standing on its own, which, so taken, is no property at all and no determinate being either, since it now stands neither at a One nor in relation to others. Yet it is a property only at the One, and determinate only in relation to others. {{ 75 }} Being this pure self-relation and nothing else, it stays merely _sensuous being_ at large, for the character of negativity no longer attaches to it; and consciousness, {{{ 46 }}} which now has a sensuous being before it, is nothing but a _meaning_, that is to say, it has gone right out of perceiving and back into itself. Sensuous being and meaning, however, themselves pass over into perceiving; I am hurled back to where I started and dragged once more into the very same cycle, which sublates itself at every moment and as a whole.

Consciousness therefore has to traverse the cycle once more, yet not in the same manner as on the first pass. For it has had the experience about perceiving that the result of perceiving, and what is true in it, is its own dissolution, or the reflection into itself out of what is true. With this there has become determinate, for consciousness, the essential constitution of its perceiving: not a simple pure apprehending, but rather being, _in its apprehending_, at the same time _reflected out of the true and into itself_. This going-back of consciousness into itself, which _mixes into_ pure apprehending immediately — for it has shown itself to be essential to perceiving — alters what is true. Consciousness at the same time recognizes this side as its own and takes it upon itself, whereby it will keep the true object pure. — So now, as happened with sense-certainty, there is present in perceiving the side according to which consciousness is pushed back into itself, but at first not in {{{ 47 }}} the sense in which this was the case there, as though the _truth_ of perceiving fell within consciousness; rather, consciousness recognizes that the _untruth_ occurring in perceiving is what falls within it. Through this cognition, however, it is at the same time capable of sublating that untruth; it distinguishes its apprehending of what is true from the untruth of its perceiving, corrects the latter, and insofar as it undertakes this correction itself, truth does indeed fall, as the truth of _perceiving, within perceiving_. The relating of consciousness that now falls to be considered is accordingly constituted in such a way that consciousness does not perceive alone, but is aware of its own reflection into itself as well, and it detaches that reflection from simple apprehension proper.

So what I first grow aware of is the thing as a _One_, and this true determination is what I have to hold onto; should anything contradicting it turn up while perceiving runs its course, that is to be recognized as my own reflection. Now diverse properties turn up in perception too, properties that look as though they were the thing's; the thing, however, is One, and about this diversity, which would put an end to its being One, we are conscious that it falls within us. This thing is thus in fact only white when brought before _our_ eye, _also_ pungent for _our_ tongue, _also_ cubical for _our_ touch, and so on. We take the complete diversity of these sides not from {{{ 48 }}} the thing but from ourselves; they fall apart for us in this way at our eye, which is entirely distinct from our tongue, and so on. {{ 76 }} We ourselves, then, are the _universal medium_ in which moments of this kind get set apart and are for themselves. Since it is as our own reflection that we regard the determinateness of being a universal medium, we keep intact the thing's self-equality and truth, its being One.

These _diverse sides_, however, which consciousness takes upon itself, are each _determinate_ when considered on its own as situated within the universal medium; white exists only in opposition to black, and so forth, while the thing is One precisely by setting itself in opposition to others. It does not, though, shut others out from itself by virtue of being One; for being One is the universal self-relating, and in being One it is on the contrary like all the rest; but rather by virtue of its _determinateness_. Things themselves, then, are _in and for themselves determinate;_ what they possess are properties that mark them off from others. Because the _property_ is the thing's _own_ property, a determinateness at the thing itself, the thing has _several_ properties. For in the first place the thing is what is true, it is _at itself;_ and whatever is at it is at it as the thing's own essence, not on account of anything else; hence, in the second place, the determinate properties exist not merely for the sake of other {{{ 49 }}} things and on their behalf, but at the thing itself; they are determinate properties _at it_, however, only in being several that mark themselves off from one another; and in the third place, standing in thinghood in this manner, they are in and for themselves and indifferent toward each other. In truth, then, what is white and _also_ cubical, _also_ pungent, and so forth is the thing itself; or the thing is the _Also_, the _universal medium_ within which the many properties subsist apart from one another without touching or sublating each other; and taken so, it is taken as what is true.

In the course of this perceiving, consciousness is aware simultaneously that it _also_ performs a reflection into itself, and that within perceiving there occurs the moment standing opposed to the _Also_. This moment, though, is the thing's _unity_ with itself, which shuts difference out of itself. It is this unity, accordingly, that consciousness has to shoulder, since the thing itself amounts to the _subsistence belonging to the many diverse_ and _independent properties_. So it is said of the thing that _it is_ white, _also_ cubical, and also pungent, and so on. But it is not cubical _insofar_ as it is white, and, _insofar_ as it is at once cubical and white, it is not pungent, and so on. The _positing into one_ of these properties falls to consciousness alone, which for that reason must not let them collapse into a One at the thing. To that end it brings in the _Insofar_, by which it keeps them apart {{{ 50 }}} and preserves the thing as the Also. Strictly speaking, _being one_ is first taken upon itself by consciousness only in that what was called property is represented as _free matter_. In this way the thing is raised to the genuine _Also_, in that it turns into a collection of matters and, in place of being One, into a merely enclosing surface.

{{ 77 }} If we look back on what consciousness took upon itself earlier and takes upon itself now, on what it earlier ascribed to the thing and now ascribes to it, then it turns out that, by turns, it makes both itself and the thing into both, into the pure _One_ without multiplicity as well as into an _Also_ dissolved into self-subsistent matters. Through this comparison, then, consciousness finds that not only does _its_ taking of what is true have at itself the _diversity of apprehending_ and _of going back into itself_, but rather that what is true itself, the thing, shows itself in this twofold way. So the experience is now on hand that the thing _presents_ itself in a determinate way _to the_ apprehending _consciousness_, but _at the same time_ stands _reflected out_ of the manner in which it proffers itself _and back into itself_, or has at its own self a truth that runs counter to that.

{{ 78 }} Consciousness has thus stepped out of this second way of relating in perceiving as well, namely of taking the thing as what is truly self-equal {{{ 51 }}} while taking itself as what is unequal, as what goes back out of equality into itself, and the object is for it now this entire movement, which was formerly parcelled out between object and consciousness. _One_ is what the thing is, reflected into itself; it has being _for itself;_ yet it has being _for an other_ as well; and indeed what it is for itself is _other_ _than what_ it is for another. On this showing the thing has being for itself and _also_ being for another, a _doubled_ diverse being; but it is _also One;_ and being one contradicts this diversity of its own; consciousness would on this showing have to shoulder the positing-into-one once again and hold it off from the thing. So it would have to declare that the thing, _insofar_ as it has being for itself, does not have being for another. Yet being one belongs to the thing itself too, as consciousness has learned by experience; the thing is reflected into itself essentially. The _Also_, or indifferent difference, thus falls in the thing just as much as _being one_ does; but since the two are diverse, they fall not in the same thing but in _diverse_ things; the contradiction that attaches to the objective essence in general distributes itself between two objects. The thing, then, is indeed self-equal in and for itself; but other things throw this unity of its with itself into disorder; and so the thing's unity is retained, while otherness at the same time lies outside it, as it lies outside consciousness.

{{{ 52 }}} Even though the contradiction belonging to the objective essence gets parcelled out in this way among diverse things, difference will come to the isolated singular thing itself all the same. The _diverse things_ are thus posited _for themselves;_ and the conflict falls into them reciprocally, so that each is diverse not from its own self but only from the other. {{ 79 }} With this, however, each is determined _itself as something differentiated_ and carries the essential difference from the others _at it;_ but not, at the same time, so as to make this an opposition at its own self; on its own account it is rather _simple determinateness_, which makes up the _essential_ character distinguishing it from others. In fact, indeed, since diversity is at the thing, that diversity is necessarily at it as an _actual_ difference of manifold constitution. But since it is determinateness that makes up the thing's _essence_, whereby the thing marks itself off from others and has being for itself, this further manifold constitution is the _unessential_. So the thing does carry the _doubled Insofar_ at it within its unity, though with _unequal worth;_ which is why this being-opposed never becomes the thing's own actual opposition; rather, so far as the thing does enter into opposition by way of its _***absolute*** difference_, the opposition it has is against another thing outside it. The remaining manifoldness, though, is also necessarily at the thing, so that it cannot be absent from {{{ 53 }}} it, but it is _unessential_ to it.

The determinateness that makes up the thing's essential character and sets it apart from all others is now determined thus: through it the thing stands in opposition to others, yet is supposed to maintain itself for itself therein. But a thing, or a One having being for itself, is what it is only so long as it does not stand within this relation to others; for within that relation what gets posited is rather connection with another, and connection with another spells the end of being-for-itself. It is precisely by its _absolute character_ and the opposition this brings that it _relates_ to _others_, and essentially it is nothing but this relating; the relationship, though, negates its self-subsistence, and so the thing perishes by way of its essential property.

The necessity of the experience for consciousness that the thing perishes precisely through the determinateness which constitutes its essence and its being-for-itself can be considered briefly, according to the simple concept, as follows. What the thing is posited as is being _for itself_, that is, negation that is absolute over against all otherness, and hence absolute negation relating to nothing but itself; but negation that relates to itself is the sublating _of itself_, or having its essence in another.

In fact the determination of the object as it has come about contains nothing else; it {{{ 54 }}} is supposed to have an essential property that constitutes its simple being-for-itself, yet alongside this simplicity to have diversity at itself as well, a diversity that is supposed to be _necessary_ but not to constitute the _essential_ determinateness. But this is a distinction that lies only in the words; the _unessential_, which is nevertheless supposed to be _necessary_ at the same time, sublates itself, or is what was just now called the negation of itself.

With this the last _Insofar_ falls away, the one that kept being-for-itself apart from being for another; the object is rather, _in one and the same respect, its own opposite: for itself insofar as it is for an other_, and _for another insofar as it is for itself_. The object has being _for itself_, is reflected into itself, is One; but this being _for itself_, reflected into itself, One, stands in a single unity with its opposite, _being for an other_, and is therefore posited only as sublated; or _being-for-itself_ in this sense is every bit as _unessential_ as what was meant to be the unessential all on its own, namely the relationship to another.

With this the object is sublated in its pure determinatenesses, the determinatenesses that were meant to make up its essentiality, just as it became something sublated in its sensuous being. Out of sensuous being the object turns into something universal; but this universal, _stemming as it does from the sensuous_, is essentially _conditioned_ by {{{ 55 }}} the sensuous, and so is not genuinely self-equal universality at all but universality _affected by an opposition_, and it therefore splits apart, into singularity and universality as its extremes, the properties' One and the free matters' Also. What these pure determinatenesses look like they express is _essentiality_ itself, but they amount only to a _being-for-itself_ saddled with _being for an other_; since the two of them are essentially _in one unity_, however, unconditioned absolute universality is now on hand, and it is here that consciousness genuinely steps into the realm of the understanding for the first time.

Sensuous singularity, then, vanishes, to be sure, in immediate certainty's dialectical movement, and turns into universality, but only _sensuous universality_. Meaning has disappeared, and perceiving takes the object the way _it is in itself_, that is, as universal at large; singularity accordingly comes forward at the object as true singularity, namely as the _being-in-itself_ belonging to the _One_, in other words as _being reflected into_ itself. It remains, though, a _conditioned_ being-for-itself, _beside which_ another being-for-itself turns up, the universality opposed to singularity and conditioned by it; but these two extremes, contradicting each other, are not merely _side by side_, but in one unity, or, what comes to the same, what the two have in common, _being-for-itself, is_ saddled with opposition at large, which is to say that it is at once not a _being-for-itself_. These {{{ 56 }}} moments the sophistry of perceiving tries to rescue from their contradiction, holding them fast through a distinction of _respects_, through the _Also_ and the _Insofar_, and finally trying to seize what is true through a distinction between the _unessential_ and an _essence_ set against it. {{ 80 }} But these makeshifts, far from keeping deception out of apprehending, prove null themselves, and what is true, the truth that this logic of perceiving is meant to win, proves in one and the same respect to be its opposite, and thus to have for its essence a universality without distinction and without determination.

These empty abstractions, _of singularity_, of the _universality_ set against it, of the _essence_ that is tied to something unessential, of an _unessential_ that is nevertheless necessary at the same time, are the powers whose play the perceiving understanding, often called sound common sense, amounts to; taking itself for solid, real consciousness, in perceiving it is nothing but the play _of these abstractions;_ generally speaking, it is always at its poorest just where it fancies itself richest. Driven about by these null essences, thrown by the one into the arms of the other, and struggling through its sophistry to hold fast and assert now the one and then its very opposite by turns, thereby setting itself against truth, {{{ 57 }}} it imagines that philosophy has to do only with _things of thought_. Philosophy does have to do with them as well, in fact, and cognizes them as the pure essences, as the absolute elements and powers; yet in cognizing them it cognizes them at the same time _in their determinateness_, and is master over them on that account, whereas that perceiving understanding takes them for what is true and gets sent by them out of one error into another. It never comes to the consciousness that what holds sway within it are simple essentialities of this sort; it takes itself, rather, to be occupied throughout with thoroughly solid stuff and content, much as sense-certainty is unaware that pure being's empty abstraction is its essence; but in fact these essentialities are what it runs along, back and forth, through every bit of stuff and content; they are what holds that stuff together and rules it, and they alone constitute what the sensuous _as essence_ is for consciousness, what determines consciousness's relationships to it, and along which the movement of perceiving and of its truth plays out. This course, a constantly alternating determining of what is true and sublating of that determining, is what actually makes up the daily and constant life and activity of the consciousness that perceives and supposes itself to be moving within truth. In this it presses on unstoppably toward the result, the like sublation of all these essential essentialities or determinations, {{{ 58 }}} yet at each individual moment what it is conscious of is only this _one determinateness_ as what is true, and then again the opposite one. It does catch a scent of their unessentiality; to save them from the threatening danger it resorts to sophistry, now asserting to be true what it had itself just been asserting to be not true. {{ 81 }} This understanding is really being driven by the nature of these untrue essences toward the thoughts of that _universality_ and of _singularity_, of the _Also_ along with the _One_, of that _essentiality_ which is _necessarily bound up with an unessentiality_, and of an _unessential_ that is nevertheless necessary — driven to _bring together_ the _thoughts_ of these unessences and so sublate them; and against just that it braces itself with the props of the _Insofar_ and the various _respects_, or by taking the one thought upon itself in order to keep the other separate and preserve it as the true one. But the nature of these abstractions brings them together in and for itself; sound common sense is their prey, driven about by them in their whirling circle. {{ 82 }} In wanting to give them truth by now taking their untruth upon itself, now calling the deception a semblance belonging to unreliable things, and by separating the essential from something that is necessary to them and yet is supposed to be unessential, holding fast to the former as their truth against the latter, it does not preserve their truth for them but rather gives untruth to itself.
