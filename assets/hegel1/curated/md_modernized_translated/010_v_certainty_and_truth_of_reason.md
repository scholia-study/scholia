## {{{ 162 }}} V. Certainty and Truth of Reason

{{ 132 }} Consciousness goes back into itself within the thought it has laid hold of, the thought that the _singular_ consciousness is _in itself_ absolute essence. _Being-in-itself_, for the unhappy consciousness, amounts to _the beyond_ of itself. Yet its movement has already carried this out on it: singularity in its full development, or singularity as _actual consciousness_, has been posited as the _negative_ of itself, namely as the _objective_ extreme, and its being-for-itself has been wrestled outward from it and turned into being; in that, its _unity_ with this universal has likewise come to be for it, a unity which, for us, no longer falls outside it, since the singular that has been sublated is the universal; and because consciousness preserves itself within this negativity of its own, its essence lies in it as such. Its truth is what shows itself as the middle in that syllogism where the extremes had stepped forth absolutely held apart, the middle which announces to the unchangeable consciousness that the singular {{{ 163 }}} has renounced itself, and announces to the singular that the unchangeable is for it no longer an extreme, but is instead reconciled with the singular itself. This middle is that unity which has immediate knowledge of both and relates them to one another, and it is the consciousness of their unity, which it announces to consciousness and thereby to _its own self_, namely as the certainty that it is all truth.

Since self-consciousness is reason, the relationship it has had to otherness, negative up to this point, swings over into a positive one. What has mattered to it hitherto was solely its self-subsistence and its freedom: to salvage and hold on to itself, for its own sake, at the cost of the _world_ or of the actuality belonging to it, both of which it had seen as the negative of its own essence. But as reason that is assured of its own self, it has received calm in the face of them and is able to bear them; for it has certainty of its own self as the reality, or rather certainty that all actuality is nothing other than it; its thinking is immediately the actuality itself; and so it relates itself to actuality as idealism. In grasping itself in this way, it seems to it as if the world were only now coming to be for it; earlier it does not understand the world; it desires it and works on it; it withdraws out of it into itself, and annihilates it for itself, and annihilates itself as consciousness, as consciousness of the world as the essence, and equally as consciousness of the world's nullity. {{ 133 }} It is at this point and no earlier, once the grave of its truth has been lost, once the annihilating of its actuality has itself been annihilated, {{{ 164 }}} and once the singularity of consciousness is for it in itself absolute essence, that it discovers the world to be _its own_ new actual world, a world that holds interest for it now in its remaining, as earlier only in its vanishing; for the _subsistence_ of that world becomes for it the _truth_ and _presence_ that are its own; and it is certain that what it experiences there is nothing but itself.

Reason is consciousness's certainty of being all reality: in these terms idealism states reason's concept. Just as the consciousness that _steps forth_ as reason possesses that certainty _immediately_ in itself, so too does _idealism_ enunciate it _immediately_: I am I, and the sense of this is that the I which is my object is not, as it is in self-consciousness generally, nor as in free self-consciousness, in the former case merely an _empty_ object in general, in the latter merely an object that pulls back from the others which are still valid _alongside_ it, but is rather an object accompanied by the consciousness of the _non-being_ of any other whatever, the one and only object, all reality and presence. But self-consciousness is all reality not only _for itself_ but also _in itself_, and it is so only by _becoming_ this reality, or rather by _proving_ itself to be such. It proves itself so along _the path_ on which, first, in the dialectical movement of meaning, perceiving, and the understanding, otherness as _in itself_ vanishes, and then, in the movement through the self-subsistence of consciousness in mastery and servitude, through the thought of freedom, the skeptical liberation, {{{ 165 }}} and the struggle for the absolute liberation of the consciousness split in two within itself, otherness, insofar as it is only _for it_, vanishes _for it itself_. Two sides came forward in succession: on the one, the determinateness of _being_ belonged, for consciousness, to the essence or the true; on the other, the essence had merely to be _for it_. Yet the two of them came down to a single truth: that whatever _is_, or the _in-itself_, has being only to the extent that it is _for_ consciousness, while whatever is _for it_ is likewise _in itself_. In stepping forth _immediately_ as reason, the consciousness which is this truth has that path behind its back and has let it fall into forgetting; or, otherwise put, reason in its immediate stepping forth arises solely as the _certainty_ belonging to that truth. So it merely _gives assurance_ of being all reality, without itself comprehending what it says; for the comprehension of this immediately expressed assertion is precisely that forgotten path. And likewise, for anyone who has not traveled it, this assertion — heard in this pure form, since in a concrete shape he no doubt makes it himself — is incomprehensible.

Idealism, which does not exhibit that path but begins with this assertion, is therefore likewise pure _assurance_, one that neither comprehends itself nor can make itself comprehensible to others. {{ 134 }} It pronounces an _immediate certainty_ that faces other immediate certainties standing over against it, certainties which were lost on that path alone. By the same right, then, the _assurances_ belonging to these other certainties set themselves _alongside_ the _assurance_ {{{ 166 }}} given for that certainty. Reason invokes the _self_-consciousness present in each consciousness: _I am I;_ what is object and essence to me is _I_; and none will deny reason this truth. But by founding it upon this appeal, reason gives its sanction to the truth of that other certainty as well, namely to this one: _there is ***something other*** for me;_ what is other than _I_ counts as object and essence for me, or, when _I_ am my own object and essence, I am so only insofar as I draw back from the other in general and take my place as an actuality _alongside_ it. — Only when reason steps forth as _reflection_ out of this opposed certainty does its assertion about itself step forth not merely as certainty and assurance but as _truth_; and not _alongside_ others, but as the _sole_ one. The _immediate stepping forth_ is the abstraction of its _being-at-hand_, whose _essence_ and _being-in-itself_ is the absolute concept, that is, the _movement of its having-become_. — How consciousness determines its relationship to otherness, that is, to its object, will vary with the particular stage of the world-spirit's becoming conscious of itself at which consciousness happens to stand. The manner in which, on each occasion, it _immediately_ comes upon itself and its object and gives them their determination, or the manner in which it is _for itself_, turns on this: what it has by now _become_, or what it _in itself_ already is.

{{{ 167 }}} Reason is the certainty of being all of _reality_. This _in-itself_, however, or this _reality_, remains something wholly universal as yet, reality in its pure _abstraction_. What we have here is the first _positivity_ that self-consciousness has _in its own self, for itself_, so that the I amounts to nothing more than the _pure essentiality_ of what is, in other words the simple _category_. Formerly the _category_ signified the essentiality of what is, _indeterminately_ the essentiality of what is in general or of what is as set over against consciousness; now it is the _essentiality_, or the simple _unity_, that what is has only as thinking actuality; that is to say, it is this: that being and self-consciousness are one and the _same_ essence; and they are the _same_ not through any comparison, but in and for itself. Only the one-sided, bad idealism lets this unity step forth once more as consciousness on the one side, with an _in-itself_ over against it. — This category, though, this _simple_ unity of being and of self-consciousness, has _difference_ in it all the same; for its essence consists in just this, in being immediately equal to itself within _otherness_, within absolute difference. Difference therefore _is_; but it is completely transparent, and is a difference that at the same time is none. It makes its appearance as a _multiplicity_ of categories. {{ 135 }} When idealism pronounces self-consciousness's _simple unity_ to be all reality, and when it makes that unity the essence _immediately_, without having comprehended it as absolutely negative essence — only such an essence has negation, determinateness, or difference in {{{ 168 }}} itself — then this second point, that within the category there are _differences_ or _species_, proves still more incomprehensible than the first. This assurance in general, as well as the assurance of any _determinate number_ of the category's species, is a new assurance, though one that contains in itself the point that it no longer needs to be put up with as an assurance. For since _difference_ begins within the pure I, within the pure understanding itself, it is thereby posited that here _immediacy_, the _assuring_ and the _finding_, are to be given up, and that _comprehending_ is to begin. But to pick up the multiplicity of categories once again in one way or another as something found, from the judgments, for example, and simply to acquiesce in them, is in truth to be counted a disgrace for science; where else could the understanding show a necessity, if it cannot show one in its own case, the case of pure necessity itself.

Since the pure essentiality that things have, along with their difference, now belongs to reason, strictly speaking there could no longer be any talk at all of _things_, that is, of anything that for consciousness would be nothing but the negative of itself. For if the pure category has the many categories as its _species_, this says that _it_ remains their _genus_ or _essence_ and is not set over against them. But they are already the ambiguous, which at the same time has in itself the otherness {{{ 169 }}} _against_ the pure category in its _multiplicity_. By this multiplicity they do in fact contradict it, and the pure unity is bound to sublate them within itself, whereby it constitutes itself as the differences' _negative unity_. In its character as _negative_ unity, however, it shuts out from itself the _differences_ taken as such no less than it shuts out that first pure unity, the _immediate_ one, taken as such; and so it is _singularity_, a new category, one that is excluding consciousness, that is, one that consists in there being _something other_ for it. Singularity is the transition the category makes out of its concept toward an _external_ reality: the pure _schema_ that is consciousness quite as much as, in being singularity and an excluding One, it is the pointing toward something other. But what counts as this _other_ of this category is nothing but the _other first categories_, namely _pure essentiality_ together with _pure difference_; and within this category, that is, precisely within the being-posited of the other, or within that other itself, consciousness is just as much its own self. Every one of these distinct moments points to another; yet within them it never comes to any otherness. {{ 136 }} It is to the _species_ that the pure category points, and they in turn make their transition into the negative category, or singularity; the latter, though, points back to the former; the latter is itself pure consciousness, which in each of them remains for itself this clear unity with itself, though a unity that is likewise directed toward something other, toward what has vanished in the very act of being and is generated once more in the very act of having vanished.

{{{ 170 }}} We see pure consciousness posited here in a doubled way: at one time as the restless _going back and forth_ that runs through all its moments, with otherness hovering before it in them, an otherness that sublates itself in the act of grasping; at another time rather as the _calm_ _unity_ which has certainty of its truth. That movement counts as the _other_ where this unity is concerned, while this calm unity counts as the other where the movement is concerned; and consciousness and object take turns in these reciprocal determinations. So at one time consciousness is for itself the searching that goes back and forth, having as its object the _pure in-itself_ and essence; at another time it is for itself the simple category, and the object is then the differences in their movement. But consciousness as essence is this whole course itself: passing over out of itself as simple category into singularity and into the object, intuiting this course in the object, sublating it as something distinct, _appropriating_ it, and giving voice to itself as this certainty: that it is all reality, both it itself and its object.

What it pronounces first is nothing but this abstract, empty word: that everything is _its own_. This is because the certainty that it is all reality is as yet nothing more than the pure category. Empty idealism gives expression to this first reason that cognizes itself in the object, an idealism which apprehends reason only as reason at first is for itself, and which, {{{ 171 }}} by exhibiting within all being this pure _Mine_ belonging to consciousness and by pronouncing things to be sensations or representations, imagines it has shown that Mine to be completed reality. It must therefore be absolute empiricism at the same time, for in order to _fill out_ the empty _Mine_, that is, for difference and for all development and shaping of that Mine, its reason requires an alien impetus, in which alone the _manifoldness_ of sensing or representing is supposed to reside. This idealism therefore becomes the same sort of self-contradicting ambiguity as skepticism, except that skepticism expresses itself negatively and idealism positively, though it just as little brings together its contradictory thoughts, pure consciousness as all reality, and equally the alien impetus, or sensuous sensing and representing, as an equal reality, but instead flings itself back and forth from the one to the other and has lapsed into the bad, namely the sensuous, infinity. Inasmuch as all reality, for reason, has the significance of the abstract _Mine_, while the _other_ is to it an _indifferent alien_, what gets posited in this is precisely the knowledge reason has of another, the very knowledge that showed up earlier as _meaning, perceiving_, and as the _understanding_ that apprehends what is meant and what is perceived. At the same time it is the concept {{{ 172 }}} of this idealism itself that asserts such knowledge not to be true knowledge, since the truth of knowledge lies in the unity of apperception and nowhere else. So the pure reason of this idealism, in order to arrive at this _other_ which is _essential_ to it, that is to say, which is the _in-itself_, and which it nevertheless does not possess within its own self, is sent back by its own doing to a knowledge that is no knowledge of the true; knowingly and willingly it thus sentences itself to an untrue knowledge, and it proves unable to give up meaning and perceiving, though these hold no truth for it. {{ 137 }} It stands in an immediate contradiction, asserting as the essence a doubled and flatly opposed pair, the _unity of apperception_ and equally the _thing_, which, whether one calls it an _alien impetus_, or an _empirical_ essence, or _sensuousness_, or _the thing in itself_, stays in its concept just what it was, something alien to that unity.

What holds this idealism inside the contradiction is its asserting reason's _abstract concept_ to be the true; hence reality arises for it immediately, and arises just as much as a reality that is precisely not reason's reality, whereas reason was meant at the same time to be all reality; and so it stays a restless searching, one that in the very searching declares the satisfaction of finding to be flatly impossible. — Actual reason, however, is not so inconsistent {{{ 173 }}} as that; rather, being as yet no more than the _certainty_ that it is all reality, it is aware within this _concept_ that as _certainty_, as _I_, it is not yet the reality in truth, and it is driven on to lift its certainty into truth and to fill out the _empty_ Mine.
