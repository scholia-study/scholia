## {{{ 637 }}} A. Natural Religion

{{ 369 }} Spirit that has knowledge of spirit is conscious of its own self, and it is there to itself in the form of the objective, it _is_; and at the same time it is _being-for-itself. It exists for itself_, it is the side of _self_-consciousness, and this as against the side of its consciousness, that is, of relating itself to itself as _object_. The opposition lies within its consciousness, and with it the _determinateness_ of the shape in which spirit knows itself and appears to itself. This determinateness is our sole concern in the present consideration of religion, since spirit's shapeless essence, its pure concept, has already come out. Yet the difference between consciousness and self-consciousness falls, at one and the same time, inside self-consciousness itself; what the shape of religion contains is not the existence of spirit as nature freed from thought, nor as thought freed from existence; it is rather existence preserved within thinking, and equally something thought which is there to itself. — One religion is marked off {{{ 638 }}} from another by the _determinateness_ of the shape within which spirit has knowledge of itself; but it must be noted at the same time that presenting this self-knowledge of spirit in terms of this _single determinateness_ by no means exhausts an actual religion as a whole. The series of the various religions that will emerge exhibits, once more and just as much, only the various sides of a single religion, and indeed of _each single one_, and those representations which appear to set one actual religion apart from another are found in every one of them. At the same time, though, the diversity must also be considered as a diversity of religion. For since spirit is situated within the difference between its consciousness and its self-consciousness, what the movement aims at is the sublating of this principal difference and the conferring of the form of self-consciousness upon the shape which is the object of consciousness. But this difference is not already sublated merely because the shapes that consciousness contains carry the moment of the self about them too, and because God is _represented as self-consciousness_. A self that is _represented_ is not the _actual_ self; if it is to belong in truth to the shape, as every other closer determination of the shape must, it has in part to be posited in the shape by what self-consciousness does, while in part the lower determination has to show itself as sublated and comprehended by the higher one. For what is represented ceases to be represented, and ceases to be alien to spirit's knowledge, only through this, that the self has produced it and hence intuits the object's determination {{{ 639 }}} as _its own_, and so intuits itself in the object. {{ 370 }} — By this activity the lower determination is at once made to disappear; for doing is the negative, which carries itself through at the expense of something else; to the extent that the lower determination still turns up, it has withdrawn into inessentiality; conversely, in cases where the lower is still what dominates while the higher turns up as well, the one stands selflessly beside the other. If, then, the various representations within one single religion do exhibit the whole movement of its forms, what fixes the character of each religion is nonetheless the particular unity in which consciousness and self-consciousness stand, that is, the fact that self-consciousness has taken the determination of the first one's object into itself, has fully appropriated it through its own doing, and knows it as the essential determination over against the others. — That faith in some determination of the religious spirit is true shows itself in the fact that _actual_ spirit is constituted in the same way as the shape in which it intuits itself in religion — as, for example, God's becoming man, which occurs in Eastern religion, holds no truth, since that religion's actual spirit lacks this reconciliation. — This is not the place to go back from the totality of the determinations to the single determination and to show in what shape, inside it and inside its particular religion, the completeness of the remaining ones is contained. A higher form, when placed back under a lower one, is stripped {{{ 640 }}} of its significance for self-conscious spirit and belongs to that spirit only superficially, belonging merely to its representation. It is to be considered in its own peculiar significance, and considered there where it functions as this particular religion's principle and stands corroborated by that religion's actual spirit.
