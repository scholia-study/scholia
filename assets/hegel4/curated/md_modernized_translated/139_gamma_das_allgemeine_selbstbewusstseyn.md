## γ) Universal Self-Consciousness

## {{ 436 }} § 436.

_Universal_ _self-consciousness_ is the affirmative knowing of itself in the other self, each of which, as free singularity, has _absolute_ _self-subsistence_, but, by virtue of the negation of its immediacy or desire, does not distinguish itself from the other, is universal and objective, and has real universality as reciprocity in that it knows itself recognized in the free other, and knows this insofar as it recognizes the other and knows it free.

::: remark
{{{ 20.433 }}} {{ 436 A }} In this universal reflected shining of self-consciousness the concept knows itself, in its objectivity, as a subjectivity that is identical with itself and therefore universal; this is the form in which there is consciousness of the _substance_ of all essential spirituality, of the family, the fatherland and the state, and likewise of all the virtues, love, friendship, bravery, honor, fame. But this _appearing_ of the substantial can also be separated from the substantial and held fast for itself in contentless honor, vain fame and so forth.
:::

## {{ 437 }} § 437.

This unity of consciousness and self-consciousness contains at first the singulars as shining in one another. But their difference is in this identity the wholly indeterminate diversity, or rather a difference that is none. Their truth is therefore the universality and objectivity of self-consciousness that is in and for itself, – _reason_.

::: remark
{{ 437 A }} Reason as the _idea_ (§ 213) appears here in this determination: the opposition between the concept and reality generally, of which it is the unity, has taken on here the closer form of consciousness, the concept existing for itself, and of the object present externally over against it.
:::
