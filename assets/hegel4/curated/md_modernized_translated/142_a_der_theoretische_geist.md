## a. Theoretical Spirit

## {{ 445 }} § 445.

Intelligence _finds_ itself _determined_; that is its shine, and in its immediacy it sets out from it, yet as _knowing_ its task is to posit what it finds as its own. What its activity deals with is the empty form of _finding_ reason, while its purpose is that its concept exist _for_ _it_, which means being reason _for_ _itself_, and with this the _content_ becomes rational for it in the same stroke. This activity is _cognition_. Since reason is concrete, the formal knowing of certainty raises itself to determinate knowing in accord with the concept. The course of this raising is itself rational: it is a transition of necessity, fixed by the concept, from one determination of intelligent {{{ 20.440 }}} activity (a so-called _faculty_ of spirit) [into] another. The refutation of the shine of finding the rational, which cognition is, proceeds [from] certainty, that is, from the faith of intelligence in its capacity to know rationally, in the possibility of being able to appropriate to itself the reason that it and the content are in themselves.

::: remark
{{ 445 A }} The distinction of _intelligence_ from _the_ _will_ often carries the incorrect sense that both are taken as a fixed concrete existence separated from one another, so that willing could be without intelligence, or the activity of intelligence without will. The possibility that, as it is called, the _understanding_ can be cultivated without the _heart_ and the _heart_ without the _understanding_, that there are also, one-sidedly, hearts without understanding and understandings without heart, indicates in any case only this, that bad concrete existences, untrue in themselves, take place, but it is not philosophy that is to take such untruths of existence and of representation for the truth, the bad for the nature of the matter. – A multitude of other forms that are used for intelligence, that it _receives_ _impressions_ from outside, _takes_ them _up_, that representations arise through the _effects_ of external things as causes, and so forth, belong to a standpoint of categories that is not the standpoint of spirit and of philosophical consideration.

A favorite form of reflection is that of the _forces_ and _faculties_ of the _soul_, of intelligence or of spirit. – The _faculty_, like the _force_, is the _fixed_ _determinateness_ of a _content_, represented as reflection-into-itself. Force (§ 136) does have the _infinity_ of form, the infinity of inner and outer, yet its essential _finitude_ includes the _indifference_ of the _content_ toward the form (ibid., Remark). Therein lies the lack of reason that is brought into spirit, as into nature, by this form of reflection and by the consideration of spirit as a multitude of _forces_. What can be _distinguished_ {{{ 20.441 }}} in its activity is held fast as a _self-subsistent_ _determinateness_, and spirit is in this way made into an ossified, mechanical _collection_. It makes no difference at all whether the expression _activities_ is used instead of faculties and forces. The _isolating_ of the activities likewise makes spirit only an aggregate being, and regards their relation as an external, contingent connection.

The doing of intelligence as theoretical spirit has been called _cognition_, not in the sense that it cognizes _among_ _other_ _things_ and in addition also intuits, represents, recollects, imagines and so forth; such a position is connected in the first place with the isolating of the activities of spirit just censured, but further with it is connected too the great question of recent times, whether true cognition, that is, the cognition of truth, is possible, so that if we see that it is not possible, we have to give up this endeavor. The multitude of aspects, grounds and categories by which an external reflection inflates the compass of this question will be settled in their own place; the more externally the understanding behaves in this, the more diffuse a simple object becomes for it. Here is the place for the simple concept of cognition, which confronts the quite general point of view of that question, namely the one that puts in question the _possibility_ of true cognition generally, and passes it off as a possibility and an arbitrary choice either to pursue cognition or else to leave it. The concept of cognition has proved to be intelligence itself, the certainty of reason, so that cognition itself now _is_ the actuality of intelligence. Hence it is nonsense to talk of intelligence while speaking in the same breath of cognition as a mere possibility or a matter of arbitrary choice. But cognition is true just insofar as intelligence actualizes it, that is, posits its concept _for_ _itself_. This formal determination takes its concrete sense from the very thing from which cognition takes its own. The moments of its realizing activity are intuiting, representing, recollecting and so forth; the activities have no other immanent sense; their purpose alone is the concept of cognition (see Remark to § 442). Only when they are isolated is it represented, in part, {{{ 20.442 }}} that they are useful for something other than cognition, and in part that they grant satisfaction for their own sake, and the enjoyment-rich character of intuiting, of recollection, of fantasizing and so forth is praised. Isolated, that is, spiritless, intuiting, fantasizing and so forth can admittedly also grant satisfaction; what in physical nature is the basic determinateness, being-outside-itself, the presenting of the moments of immanent reason outside one another, this is within intelligence in part something that arbitrary will is capable of, and in part it happens to intelligence insofar as it is itself only natural, uncultivated. But the _true_ _satisfaction_, it is conceded, is granted only by an intuiting pervaded by understanding and spirit, a rational representing, productions of fantasy that are pervaded by reason and present ideas, and so forth, that is, _cognizing_ intuiting, representing and so forth. The _true_ that is ascribed to such satisfaction lies in this, that intuiting, representing and so forth are present not isolated, but only as a moment of the totality, of cognition itself.
:::
