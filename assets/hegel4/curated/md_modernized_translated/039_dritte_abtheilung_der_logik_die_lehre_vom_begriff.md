## {{{ 20.177 }}} Third Division of the Logic. The Doctrine of the Concept

## {{ 160 }} § 160.

The concept is the _free_; it is the _substantial_ _power_ that _is_ _for_ _itself_, and it is _totality_, in that _each_ of its moments _is_ _the_ _whole_ which _it_ is, and is posited as an undivided unity with the whole; in this self-identity it is what is _determined_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_.

## {{ 161 }} § 161.

The concept's advance is neither a passing over any longer nor a shining in an other but _development_: what is distinguished is posited immediately and simultaneously as identical, with each other and with the whole, and determinateness as a free being of the whole concept.

## {{ 162 }} § 162.

The doctrine of the concept falls into the doctrine 1) of the subjective or _formal_ concept, 2) of the concept as determined to immediacy, or of _objectivity_, 3) of the _idea_, the subject-object, the unity of concept and objectivity, absolute truth.

::: remark
{{ 162 A }} _Ordinary_ _logic_ encompasses solely those materials which turn up here as a _portion_ of the whole's _third_ part; beyond them it has the so-called laws of thought met with above and, in applied logic, a little about cognition, with which psychological, {{{ 20.178 }}} metaphysical and other empirical material gets combined, since those forms of thought in the end no longer sufficed on their own; in this way, though, the science has lost its firm direction. – Those forms, which at least belong to the proper domain of logic, are moreover grasped only as determinations of conscious thinking, and of it only as the understanding's, not reason's.

Those logical determinations that precede, the ones of being and of essence, are certainly not bare determinations of thought either; when they pass over (the dialectical moment), and when they return into themselves and form a totality, they show themselves to be _concepts_. Yet they are only _determinate_ concepts (compare §§ 84 and 112), concepts in themselves, or equivalently _for_ _us_; for each determination _passes_ _over_ into an _other_, or _shines_ in it and so is relative; but that other is not determined as the _particular_, nor is the third determined as the _singular_ or _subject_, and the determination's identity within its opposite, its freedom, is not _posited_, since it is not _universality_. – What people ordinarily understand by _concepts_ are _determinations_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_, indeed mere universal _representations_, and hence _finite_ determinations throughout; compare § 62.

The logic of the concept is customarily taken for a merely _formal_ science: its concern, it is said, is the _form_ as such of concept, judgment and syllogism, and by no means whether anything is _true_, which depends wholly and solely on the _content_. Were the logical forms of the concept really dead, inert and indifferent containers of representations or thoughts, acquaintance with them would be a most superfluous and dispensable _history_ so far as truth goes. In fact the opposite holds: as forms of the concept they are _the_ _living_ _spirit_ _of_ _the_ _actual_, and what in the actual is true is only what owes its truth to _the_ _power_ _of_ _these_ _forms_, and is _true_ _by_ _them_ and _within_ _them_. The truth of these forms for themselves, however, has never been considered and examined until now, any more than their necessary coherence.
:::
