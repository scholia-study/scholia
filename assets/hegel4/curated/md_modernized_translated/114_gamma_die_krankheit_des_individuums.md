## γ) The Disease of the Individual

## {{ 371 }} § 371.

In the two relations considered, the process of the genus's self-mediation with itself goes through its diremption into individuals and the sublating of their difference. But in that it further (§ 357) assumes the shape of an external {{{ 20.371 }}} universality, of inorganic nature against the individual, it brings itself to concrete existence in the individual in an abstract negative manner. The single organism can, in that relation of the externality of its existence, just as well fail to correspond to its genus as, in it, maintain itself returning into itself (§ 366). – It is in the state of _disease_ insofar as one of its systems or organs, _excited_ in conflict with the inorganic power, fixes itself for itself and persists in its particular activity against the activity of the whole, whose fluidity and process passing through all the moments is thereby inhibited.

## {{ 372 }} § 372.

The peculiar appearance of disease is therefore that the identity of the whole organic process presents itself as a _successive_ course of the movement of life through its distinct moments, sensibility, irritability and reproduction, that is, as _fever_, which, however, as the course of the _totality_ against the _singularized_ activity, is just as much the attempt and beginning of _cure_.

## {{ 373 }} § 373.

The remedy excites the organism to sublate the _particular_ excitation in which the formal activity of the _whole_ is fixed, and to restore the fluidity of the particular organ or system into the whole. The means brings this about by being a stimulus, but one hard to assimilate and to overcome, and by thereby presenting to the organism something external against which it is compelled to summon its strength. Directing itself against something external, it steps out of the limitedness, become identical with it, in which it was entangled and against which it cannot react insofar as it is not an object for it.

::: remark
{{{ 20.372 }}} {{ 373 A }} The chief viewpoint under which medicines must be considered is that they are something _indigestible_. But the determination of indigestibility is relative, though not in the indeterminate sense that only what weaker constitutions can tolerate is called easily digestible; such things are rather indigestible for the stronger individuality. The immanent _relativity_ of the _concept_, which has its actuality in life, is of a qualitative nature, and consists, expressed in quantitative respect, insofar as that holds here, in a _homogeneity_ the higher, the more _self-subsistent_ in themselves the opposed are. For the lower animal formations, which have not come to any _difference_ _within_ _themselves_, only the _neutral_, lacking individuality, as water for the plant, is the digestible; for children the digestible is partly the wholly _homogeneous_ animal lymph, the mother's milk, something already digested or rather merely transformed immediately and in general into animality and not further differentiated within it; – partly, of different substances, those that have matured least toward individuality. Substances of this kind are, on the other hand, indigestible for strengthened natures. For these, by contrast, animal substances, as the individualized, or the vegetable juices ripened by light into a more vigorous self and therefore called _spirituous_, are something more digestible than, for instance, the vegetable productions that still stand merely in the neutral color and closer to the peculiar chemism. Through their more intensive selfhood those substances make an all the stronger opposition; but precisely thereby they are more homogeneous stimuli. – Medicines are in this respect _negative_ stimuli, poisons; an exciting and at the same time indigestible thing, presented to the organism estranged in disease as something foreign and _external_ to it, against which it must {{{ 20.373 }}} gather itself together and enter into a process through which it may attain anew to self-feeling and its subjectivity. – However empty a formalism _Brownianism_ was when it was to be the whole system of medicine, and when the determination of diseases was reduced to sthenia and asthenia and perhaps also to direct and indirect asthenia, and the efficacy of means to strengthening and weakening, and when these differences were further reduced to carbon and nitrogen with oxygen and hydrogen, or to the magnetic, electrical and chemical moment, and similar formulas meant to make it philosophy of nature, it has nevertheless contributed to widening the view of the merely particular and specific in diseases as well as in means, and to recognizing in both rather the _universal_ as the essential. Through its opposition to the previous method, on the whole more _asthenizing_, it has also been shown that the organism reacts to the most opposite mode of treatment not in so opposite a manner but often in a manner that is the same, at least in the final results, and therefore _universal_, and that its _simple_ _identity_ with itself proves itself to be the substantial and truly effective activity against a particular entanglement of individual systems of it in specific stimuli. – However universal, and therefore insufficient in comparison with the so manifold appearances of disease, the determinations set out in the paragraph and in the Remark are, it is nonetheless only the firm basis of the concept that is able both to lead through the particular and fully to make intelligible what appears extravagant and bizarre to habit sunk in the externalities of the specific, in appearances of disease as well as in ways of healing.

## {{{ 20.374 }}} {{ 374 }} § 374.

In disease the animal is entangled with an inorganic power and, in one of its particular systems or organs, held fast against the unity of its livingness. Its organism, as existence, is of a quantitative strength, and is capable, namely, of overcoming its disunion but just as well of succumbing to it, and of having therein a mode of its death. In general, the overcoming and passing away of a single inadequacy does not sublate the universal inadequacy that the individual has in this, that its idea is the _immediate_ one, that as animal it stands _within_ _nature_, and that its subjectivity is the concept only _in_ _itself_ but not _for_ _itself_. The inner universality therefore remains, against the natural singularity of the living thing, the _negative_ power, from which it suffers violence and perishes, because its existence as such does not itself have this universality in it and is thus not the reality corresponding to it.
