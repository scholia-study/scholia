## δ) The Judgment of the Concept

## {{ 178 }} § 178.

The _judgment_ of the _concept_ takes the concept, the totality in simple form, the universal with its complete determinateness, as its content. The subject is 1) first a singular, whose predicate is the _reflection_ of {{{ 20.190 }}} particular existence on its universal, – whether the two determinations agree or not: good, true, right and so forth – _assertoric_ judgment.

::: remark
{{ 178 A }} In ordinary life too, only such judging as decides whether an object or deed and the like is good or bad, true, beautiful and the like is called judging; no one will credit anyone with the power of judgment who, for instance, knows how to produce the positive or negative judgments: this rose is red, this painting is red, green, dusty and so forth.

Even in philosophy, assertoric judging, which in society, when it claims on its own to be valid, rather passes for unseemly, has been made, through the principle of immediate knowing and faith, the sole and essential form of doctrine. The so-called philosophical works that uphold that principle offer hundreds upon hundreds of _assurances_ about reason, knowledge, thinking and so forth, which, since external authority counts for little any more, try to gain credence by endlessly repeating one and the same thing.
:::

## {{ 179 }} § 179.

The relation of the particular and the universal, which the predicate expresses, is not contained in the at first immediate subject of the assertoric judgment. This judgment is therefore only a _subjective_ particularity, and the opposite assurance stands against it with equal right, or rather wrong; it is therefore 2) at once only a _problematic_ judgment. But when 3) the objective particularity is _posited_ in the _subject_, its particularity as the constitution of its existence, the subject now states how this stands to its determination, that is, to its genus, and so expresses what constitutes (preceding §) the content of the predicate; (_this_ – immediate singularity, – _house_, – genus, – of _such_ _and_ _such_ _a_ _constitution_ – particularity – is good or bad) – _apodictic_ judgment. – _All_ _things_ are, {{{ 20.191 }}} in a _singular_ actuality of a _particular_ constitution, a _genus_ (their determination and purpose); and their finitude consists in the possibility that the particular in them conform to the universal or not.

## {{ 180 }} § 180.

Thus subject and predicate are each themselves the whole judgment. The subject's immediate constitution first shows itself as the _mediating_ _ground_ between the actual's singularity and its universality, as the ground of the judgment. What has actually been posited is the unity of subject and predicate as the concept itself; this is the fulfilment of the empty _is_ of the copula, and because its moments are distinguished at once as subject and predicate, it stands posited as their unity, as the mediating relation between them, – _the_ _syllogism_.
