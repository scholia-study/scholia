## c. The Syllogism

## {{ 181 }} § 181.

The syllogism unites concept and judgment; – it is the concept as the simple identity to which the formal differences of the judgment have gone back, and it is the judgment in that it is, at the same time, posited in reality, that is, in the difference of its determinations. The syllogism is the _rational_, and _all_ that is rational.

::: remark
{{ 181 A }} The syllogism is, to be sure, usually put forward as the _form_ _of_ _the_ _rational_, yet as a subjective form, with no connection {{{ 20.192 }}} ever shown between it and any rational content otherwise, for instance a rational principle, a rational action, an idea and so forth. Reason is much and often spoken of and appealed to without any account of its _determinateness_, of _what_ it is, and least of all is syllogizing thought of in that connection. In fact, _formal_ _syllogizing_ is the rational in so reasonless a manner that it has nothing to do with rational import. But because such an import can be rational only by the determinateness through which _thinking_ is reason, it can be so only by the form that the syllogism is. – This form, however, is nothing other than the _posited_, (at first formally –) _real_ _concept_, as the paragraph states. For this reason the syllogism is the _essential_ _ground_ _of_ _all_ _that_ _is_ _true_; _and_ _the_ _definition_ _of_ _the_ _absolute_ reads, from here on, that it is the syllogism, or, stating the determination as a proposition: _Everything_ _is_ _a_ _syllogism_. Everything is _concept_, and its existence is the difference among its moments, so that its _universal_ nature, by way of _particularity_, gives itself external reality, and thereby, as negative reflection-into-itself, turns itself into the _singular_. – Or, conversely, what is actual is a _singular_ that, by way of _particularity_, lifts itself into _universality_ and makes itself identical with itself. – What is actual is one, yet equally the falling apart of the concept's moments, and the syllogism is the cycle of mediation among its moments, by which it posits itself as one.
:::

## {{ 182 }} § 182.

In the _immediate_ syllogism the determinations of the concept, being _abstract_, stand to one another only in external _relation_: the two _extremes_ are _singularity_ and _universality_, and the concept, the _middle_ _term_ that joins both together, is likewise merely abstract _particularity_. The extremes are hereby posited as subsisting _indifferently_ _for_ _themselves_, no less against one another than against their middle. Accordingly this syllogism is the {{{ 20.193 }}} rational in conceptless form, – the formal _syllogism_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_. – In it the subject is closed together with an _other_ determinateness; or the universal subsumes, by this mediation, a subject _external_ to it. The rational syllogism, by contrast, consists in the subject's joining _itself_ _with_ _itself_ by way of the mediation. Only so is it subject, or is the subject on its own part the syllogism of reason.

::: remark
{{ 182 A }} The syllogism of the understanding is presented in what follows in its usual, familiar sense, in the subjective manner that belongs to it in so far as _we_ are the ones who draw such syllogisms. In fact it is _only_ a _subjective_ syllogizing; yet this too has the objective meaning that it expresses merely the _finitude_ of things, though in the determinate way the form has reached here. In finite things subjectivity, as thinghood, is separable from their properties and their particularity, and equally separable from their universality, whether this is the mere quality of the thing together with its external connection to other things, or its genus and concept.
:::
