## {{{ 20.401 }}} b. The Feeling Soul

## {{ 403 }} § 403.

The feeling individual is the _simple_ _ideality_, the subjectivity, of sensing. The point is that it _posits_ its substantiality, the fulfilment that is only _in_ _itself_, as subjectivity, takes possession of itself, and becomes for itself as the power of itself. As feeling, the soul is no longer merely natural but inward individuality; this its being-for-itself, at first merely formal in the merely substantial totality, is to be made self-subsistent and freed.

::: remark
{{ 403 A }} With the soul, and still more with spirit, what matters most for understanding is to keep firmly in view the determination of _ideality_: ideality is the _negation_ of the real, a real that is nonetheless at once _preserved_ and retained virtually, though it does not exist. It is the determination that we surely have before us with respect to representations, to memory. Every individual is an infinite wealth of determinations of sensation, representations, items of knowledge, thoughts and so forth; yet _I_ am for all that a wholly _simple_, – a determinationless shaft, in which all this is preserved without existing. Only when _I_ recollect _a_ representation do I bring it out of that inwardness into existence before consciousness. In diseases it happens that representations, items of knowledge, come again to light that are said to have been forgotten for many years, because in so long a time they were not brought into consciousness. We were not in possession of them, and perhaps through such a reproduction occurring in disease do not come into possession of them henceforth either, and yet they were in us and remain henceforth in us. Thus one can never know how much knowledge one in fact _has_ _within_ _oneself_, although one may have forgotten it; – it belongs not to one's actuality, not to one's subjectivity as such, but only to one's being that is in itself. This _simple_ _inwardness_ is {{{ 20.402 }}} and remains individuality in all determinateness and mediation of consciousness that is later posited in it. Here this _simplicity_ of the soul, first as feeling, in which corporeality is contained, is to be held fast against the representation of this corporeality, which is for consciousness and the understanding a materiality existing outside one another and outside it. As little as the _manifoldness_ of the many _representations_ grounds a being-outside-one-another and a real plurality in the _I_, so little has the real being-asunder of corporeality a truth for the feeling soul. In sensing it is _immediately_ determined, thus natural and corporeal, but the being-outside-one-another and the sensuous manifoldness of this corporeal counts for the soul, just as little as for the concept, as something real, and therefore not as a restriction; the soul is the _existing_ concept, the concrete existence of the speculative. It is therefore in the corporeal a simple, _omnipresent_ unity; as for representation the body is _one_ representation, and the infinitely manifold of its material makeup and organization is pervaded to the _simplicity_ of a determinate concept, so in the feeling soul corporeality, and with it all that falls as being-outside-one-another into its sphere, is reduced to _ideality_, the _truth_ of natural manifoldness. In itself the soul is the totality of nature, and as individual soul it is a monad; it is itself the totality of its _particular_ world, posited as such, and this world, enclosed within it, is its fulfilment, in relation to which it relates only to itself.
:::

## {{ 404 }} § 404.

Being _individual_, the soul is _exclusive_ in general and sets the difference _within_ _itself_. What gets distinguished from it is no external object yet, as it is in consciousness; it consists of the determinations of its sensing totality. In this judgment the soul is subject in general, and the {{{ 20.403 }}} object it has is its _substance_, which is at once its predicate. That substance is not the content of its natural life; it figures rather as the content of the individual soul that sensation has filled; since, however, the substance is in this content at the same time _particular_, the content is the soul's particular world, to the extent that this world lies implicitly enclosed within the ideality of the subject.

::: remark
{{ 404 A }} This stage of spirit is for itself the stage of its obscurity, in that its determination does not develop into conscious and understanding content; it is in this respect formal generally. It acquires a peculiar interest insofar as it _is_ _as_ _form_ and thereby appears as _state_ (§ 380), into which the development of the soul, already further determined to consciousness and understanding, can sink down again. The truer form of spirit, existing in a subordinate, more abstract one, contains an inadequacy, which is _disease_. In this sphere the abstract formations of the soul are to be considered, on one occasion for themselves, on another also as the states of disease of spirit, for the reason that these are to be understood entirely and solely from those.
:::
