## b. Contract

## {{ 493 }} § 493.

As something _inner_, the two wills and their accord in the contract differ from the realizing of that accord, the _performance_. The relatively ideal utterance in the _stipulation_ contains the _actual_ relinquishing of a property by the one party, its passing over into the other will and being taken up there. The contract is _valid_ in and for itself; it does not become so only through one party's or the other's {{{ 20.484 }}} performance, which would bring with it an _infinite_ _regress_, an infinite division of the thing, of the labor and of the time. In the stipulation the utterance is complete and exhaustive. In the realm of representing lies the inwardness of the will relinquishing the property and of the will taking it up; in this realm the _word_ is _deed_ and _matter_ (§ 462), and [indeed] the _fully valid_ deed, since the will does not count here as moral will (whether it is _meant_ in earnest or deceitfully) but is merely will over an external thing.

## {{ 494 }} § 494.

The stipulation distinguishes the _substantial_ element of the contract from the performance, the real utterance, which is demoted to a consequence; correspondingly the thing or performance itself is split into its immediate specific constitution and its _substantial_ element, _value_, in which that qualitative side turns into quantitative determinateness; one property thereby becomes comparable with another, and can be equated with what is qualitatively quite heterogeneous. Thus it is posited as an abstract, _universal_ thing in general.

## {{ 495 }} § 495.

The contract arises from arbitrary will and concerns a contingent thing; at the same time it therefore contains the positedness of the accidental will, which may just as well fail to accord with right, and so produces _wrong_; right, which is in and for itself, is not thereby sublated, however, but there arises merely a _relation_ _of_ _right_ _to_ _wrong_.
