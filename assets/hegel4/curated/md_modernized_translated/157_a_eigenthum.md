## a. Property

## {{ 488 }} § 488.

Spirit in the immediacy of its freedom that is for itself is a _singular_, but one that knows its singularity as an absolutely free will; it is _person_, the self-knowing of this freedom, which, being _abstract_ and _empty_ in itself, finds its particularity and fulfilment not yet within itself but in an external _thing_. Over against the subjectivity of intelligence and of arbitrary will, such a thing is a will-less being devoid of right, and that subjectivity turns it into its accident, the external sphere of its freedom – _possession_.

## {{ 489 }} § 489.

The predicate of the _mine_, merely practical in itself, which the thing at first receives through the judgment of possession in external seizure, has here, however, the meaning that I put my personal will into it. By this determination possession becomes _property_: a _means_ so far as it is possession, yet a _purpose_ so far as it is the existence of personality.

## {{ 490 }} § 490.

In property the person is joined together with itself. But the thing is abstractly external, and I am abstractly external in it. The concrete return of myself into myself in externality is that I, the _infinite_ relation of myself to myself, am, as person, the repulsion of myself from {{{ 20.483 }}} myself, and possess the existence of my personality in the _being_ _of_ _other_ _persons_ – in how I relate to them, and in the recognition by them that is thus mutual.

## {{ 491 }} § 491.

The thing is the _middle term_ through which the extremes join together, persons who in knowing their identity are free and yet at the same time self-subsistent toward one another. For them my will has its _determinate_ _cognizable_ _existence_ in the thing, through the immediate bodily seizure of possession, or through the forming of it, or else through the mere designation of it.

## {{ 492 }} § 492.

The contingent side of property is that I put my will into _this_ thing; to this extent my will is _arbitrary_ _will_, so that I can just as well put it into the thing or not, and withdraw it or not. But as long as my will lies in a thing, it is I alone who can withdraw it; the thing passes to another only with my will, and becomes that other's property likewise only with the other's will; – _contract_.
