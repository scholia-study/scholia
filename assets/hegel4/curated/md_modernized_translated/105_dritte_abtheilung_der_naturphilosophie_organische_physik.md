## {{{ 20.344 }}} Third Division of the Philosophy of Nature. Organic Physics

## {{ 337 }} § 337.

The real totality of the body, as the infinite process in which individuality determines itself to particularity or finitude and equally negates it, and returns into itself, restoring itself at the end of the process to its beginning, is thus an elevation into the first ideality of nature, in such a way, however, that this has become a _fulfilled_ ideality and, essentially, a _negative_ unity relating to itself, _self-like_ and _subjective_. The idea has thereby come to concrete existence, at first to the immediate, to _life_. This is
+ A. as _shape_, the universal image of life, the _geological_ organism;
+ B. as particular, formal subjectivity, the _vegetable_ one;
+ C. as singular concrete subjectivity, the _animal_ organism.

Truth and actuality belong to the idea only insofar as it is _subjective_ within itself (§ 215); life as merely _immediate_ idea is thereby outside itself, non-life, only the corpse of the life-process, the organism as _totality_ of the mechanical and physical nature existing as lifeless.

Distinguished from this, subjective livingness begins, the living in the _vegetable_ nature; the individual, but still as being outside itself, falling apart into its members, which are themselves individuals.

Only the _animal_ organism is developed into such differences of formation as exist essentially only as its members, whereby it is {{{ 20.345 }}} _subject_. Livingness as natural does indeed fall apart into the indeterminate plurality of living things, which are, however, subjective organisms in themselves; that they are _one_ life, one organic system of it, holds only in the idea.
