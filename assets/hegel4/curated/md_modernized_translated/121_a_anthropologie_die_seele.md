## A. Anthropology. The Soul

## {{ 388 }} § 388.

Spirit has _become_ as the truth of nature. Besides the fact that in the idea generally this result has the significance of the truth, and indeed of what is first relative to what precedes, becoming or passing over has in the concept {{{ 20.388 }}} the more determinate significance of the _free_ _judgment_. Spirit that has become therefore has the sense that nature sublates itself in itself as the untrue, and that spirit thus presupposes itself as this universality, which is no longer _being-outside-itself_ in corporeal singularity but _simple_ in its concretion and totality, and in which it is _soul_, not yet spirit.

## {{ 389 }} § 389.

Not only is the soul immaterial for itself; it is nature's universal immateriality, nature's simple ideal life. It is the _substance_, and so the absolute foundation of all particularization and individuation of spirit, so that spirit has within it the whole material of its determination, while the soul stays the identical ideality that pervades that determination. But in this still abstract determination it is only the _sleep_ of spirit; – the _passive_ nous of Aristotle, which is all things in respect of _possibility_.

::: remark
{{ 389 A }} The question about the immateriality of the soul can still have an interest only if matter is represented as something _true_ on the one hand, and spirit as a _thing_ on the other. Even for the physicists, however, matter has in recent times grown thinner under their hands; they have arrived at _imponderable_ materials such as heat, light and so forth, among which they could easily count space and time as well. Those imponderables have forfeited gravity, the property peculiar to matter, and in a certain sense also the capacity to resist, yet in other respects they still possess a sensuous existence, a being-outside-itself; but _the_ _matter_ _of_ _life_, which one can find counted among them as well, lacks not only gravity but also every other existence by which it could still be reckoned among what is _material_. In truth, in the idea of life the being-outside-itself of nature is already sublated _in_ _itself_, and the concept, the substance of life, is as subjectivity, though only in such a way that concrete existence or objectivity has at the same time still fallen prey to that being-outside-itself. In spirit, however, the concept has as its concrete existence not immediate {{{ 20.389 }}} singularity but absolute negativity, freedom; its object, or its reality, is thus the concept itself, and the being-outside-itself that forms the basic determination of matter is entirely volatilized into the subjective ideality of the concept, into universality. Spirit is the existing truth of matter, that matter itself has no truth.

A question connected with this is that about the _community_ _of_ _soul_ _and_ _body_. This community was assumed as a _fact_, and the sole concern was how it is to be _comprehended_? As the usual answer one may take the claim that this is an _incomprehensible_ mystery. And indeed, once the two are presupposed as _absolutely_ _self-subsistent_ against each other, they are as impenetrable to one another as any matter is to another, while each is taken to be located only in the mutual non-being of the two, in their pores; just as Epicurus assigned the gods their abode in the pores, but consistently burdened them with no community with the world. – The answer that all philosophers have given since this relation became a question cannot be regarded as equivalent in meaning to this one. _Descartes_, _Malebranche_, _Spinoza_ and _Leibniz_ have all given _God_ as this relation, and indeed in the sense that the finitude of the soul and matter are only ideal determinations over against each other and have no truth, so that God in those philosophers [is] not merely, as is often the case, another word for that incomprehensibility, but is rather grasped as the only genuine _identity_ of the two. This _identity_ is, however, at one time too abstract, like the Spinozist, at another, like the Leibnizian monad of monads, indeed also _creating_ but only as _judging_, so that a difference of the soul and the corporeal, the material, comes about, while identity is only as the _copula_ of the judgment and does not proceed to the development and the system of the absolute syllogism.
:::

## {{{ 20.390 }}} {{ 390 }} § 390.

The soul is first
+ a. in its immediate _natural_ _determinateness_, – the merely _being_, _natural_ soul;
+ b. as _individual_ it enters into the relation to this its immediate being and is, in the determinatenesses of that being, abstractly _for_ _itself_ – _feeling_ soul;
+ c. the same is formed into it as its corporeality, and it is therein _actual_ soul.
