## B. Morality

## {{ 503 }} § 503.

The free individual, a mere _person_ within (immediate) right, is now determined as _subject_, – a will reflected into itself; the determinateness of will as such, as existence, accordingly becomes its _own_, set apart from the existence that freedom has in an external thing. Because the determinateness of will has been posited in this way within the _inner_, the will is at once a _particular_ one, and its further particularizations, with their relations to one another, {{{ 20.489 }}} step in. The determinateness of will appears, in part, as what is _in_ _itself_, – the reason of the will, what is in itself right (and ethical); – in part as the existence that is present in the active expression, which gives itself over and enters into relation with that expression. Only to the extent that these determinations are _posited_ inwardly _as_ _its_ _own_ and willed by it is the subjective will _morally_ free. When it expresses itself actively with this freedom, the result is _action_; of that action's externality the will acknowledges, and lets be imputed to itself, only what it has itself known and willed within.

::: remark
{{ 503 A }} It is chiefly this _subjective_ or _moral_ freedom that in the European sense is called freedom. In virtue of this freedom's right, human beings must possess a knowledge, properly their own, of how good differs from evil in general, and ethical as well as religious determinations should not merely press on them, as external laws and precepts of an authority, the claim to be followed, but find assent, recognition or even grounding in their heart, disposition, conscience, insight and so forth. Within itself the will's subjectivity is self-purpose, a moment that is absolutely essential.

The _moral_ is to be taken in the wider sense in which it signifies not merely the morally _good_. In French, Le Moral is set against Physique and means the spiritual, the intellectual in general. Here, however, the moral has the meaning of a determinateness of will in so far as it lies in the _inner_ of the will generally, and hence comprises resolve and intention as well as the morally evil.
:::
