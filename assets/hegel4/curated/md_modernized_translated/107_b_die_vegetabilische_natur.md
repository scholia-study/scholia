## {{{ 20.348 }}} B. Vegetable Nature

## {{ 343 }} § 343.

The _subjectivity_ according to which the organic is as a _singular_ item develops itself into an _objective_ organism, the _shape_, a body articulating itself into parts _that_ are _distinguished_ _from_ _one_ _another_. In the plant, the _only_ _as_ _yet_ _immediate_ subjective livingness, the objective organism and the subjectivity of it are still immediately identical, whereby the process of articulation and of self-preservation of the vegetable subject is a coming-out-of-itself and falling apart into several individuals, for which the one whole individual is rather only the ground than the subjective unity of members; the part, the bud, branch and so forth is also the whole plant. Further, for this reason the _difference_ _of_ _the_ _organic_ _parts_ is only a superficial _metamorphosis_, and the one can easily pass over into the function of the other.

## {{ 344 }} § 344.

The process of shaping and reproduction of the _single_ individual coincides in this way with the genus-process, and is a perennial producing of new individuals. The self-like universality, the subjective one of individuality, does not separate itself from the real particularization, but is only sunk into it. The plant, as a subjectivity not yet existing for itself as against its organism existing _in_ _itself_ (§ 342), neither determines its place out of itself, has no motion from its spot, nor is it for itself as against the physical particularization and _individualization_ of this, and therefore has no interrupted intussusception but a continuously streaming nourishment, and does not relate to the individualized {{{ 20.349 }}} inorganic, but to the universal elements. Of animal heat and of feeling it is still less capable, since it is not the process of leading its members, which are rather only parts and themselves individuals, back to negative, simple unity.

## {{ 345 }} § 345.

As organic, however, the plant essentially also articulates itself into a distinctness of abstract formations (cells, fibers and the like) and of more concrete ones, which nevertheless remain in their original homogeneity. The _shape_ of the plant, as not liberated from individuality to subjectivity, also remains close to geometrical forms and crystalline regularity, as the products of its process stand still closer to the chemical ones.

::: remark
{{ 345 A }} _Goethe's_ _Metamorphosis_ of Plants made the beginning of a rational thought about the nature of the plant, in that it tore the representation away from the effort over mere singularities to the cognition of the _unity_ of life. The _identity_ of the organs predominates in the category of metamorphosis; the determinate difference and peculiar function of the members, through which the life-process is posited, is however the other, necessary side of that substantial unity. The _physiology_ of the plant necessarily appears darker than that of the animal body, since it is simpler: assimilation passes through few mediations, and the alteration occurs as _immediate_ _infection_. – In every natural and spiritual life-process alike, what matters chiefly in assimilation, as in secretion, is the _substantial_ alteration, that is, the _immediate_ transformation of an external or particular material in general into an other; a point enters where the pursuit of the mediation, whether in chemical or in the manner of mechanical _gradualness_, is broken off and becomes impossible. This point is everywhere and pervasive, and it is the lack of knowledge, or {{{ 20.350 }}} rather the refusal to acknowledge, this simple identifying as well as the simple diremption, that makes a physiology of the living impossible. – Interesting disclosures on the physiology of the plant are provided by the work of my colleague, Prof. C. H. _Schultz_ (The Nature of the Living Plant, or the Plant and the Plant Kingdom, 2 vols.), which I have all the more to cite here because some of the special basic features stated in the following paragraphs on the life-process of the plant are drawn from it.

## {{ 346 }} § 346.

The process that is livingness must, just as much as it is one, divide itself asunder into the triad of processes (§§ 217–220). 1) The _process_ _of_ _shaping_, the _inner_ process of the plant's _relation_ _to_ _itself_, is, according to the simple nature of the vegetative itself, at once relation to the external, and externalization. On the one hand it is _the_ _substantial_ one, the _immediate_ transformation partly of the nutritive influxes into the specific nature of the plant species, partly of the inwardly transformed fluid (the _sap_ _of_ _life_) into formations. On the other hand, as _mediation_ with itself, α) the process begins with the diremption, at the same time directed _outward_, into root and leaf, and the inner, abstract one of the universal cellular tissue into the woody fiber and into the _vessels_ _of_ _life_, of which the former likewise relate outward, the latter contain the _inner_ _circulation_. The preservation that mediates itself with itself herein is β) _growth_ as production of new formations, diremption into the _abstract_ relation to itself, into the _hardening_ of the wood (to petrification in the tabasheer and the like) and of the other parts, and into the bark (the lasting leaf). γ) The gathering of self-preservation into unity does not close the individual together with itself; it produces a new plant-individual, the _bud_.

## {{{ 20.351 }}} {{ 347 }} § 347.

2) The process of shaping is immediately linked with the second, the _process_ _specifying_ _itself_ _outward_. The seed germinates only when stimulated from without, and the diremption of shaping into root and leaf is itself diremption into the direction toward earth and water and that toward light and air, into the absorption of water and into the assimilation of it mediated by leaf and bark as by light and air. The return-into-itself in which assimilation concludes does not have the _self_, in inner subjective universality against externality, a feeling of self, as its result. Rather, the plant is torn out by the light, as its self external to it, and tendrils toward it, branching out into a plurality of individuals. Within _itself_ it takes from it the specific kindling and strengthening, the spiciness, the spirituality of smell, of taste, the lustre and depth of color, the compactness and vigour of shape.

## {{ 348 }} § 348.

3) But the plant also gives birth to its light out of itself as _its_ _own_ self, in the _blossom_, in which the neutral, green color is first determined to a specific one. The _genus-process_, as the relation of the individual self to the self, _checks_, as return into itself, growth as the sprouting-out, unmeasured in itself, from bud to bud. The plant, however, does not get as far as the relation of individuals as such, but only to a difference whose sides are not at the same time in themselves whole individuals, do not determine the whole individuality, and which thus comes to no more than a beginning and intimation of the genus-process. The _germ_ is here to be regarded as one and the same individual, whose livingness runs through this process and by return into itself has just as much preserved itself as it has come to the ripeness of a seed; this course is, however, on the whole a superfluity, since the process of shaping and of assimilation is itself already reproduction, production of new individuals.

## {{{ 20.352 }}} {{ 349 }} § 349.

What has been posited in the concept, however, is that the process presents individuality gone together with itself, and shows the parts, which are at first as individuals, also as moments belonging to the mediation and passing away within it, and thus shows the _immediate_ _singularity_ and the _externality-to-one-another_ of vegetable life as sublated. This moment of the negative determination grounds the transition into the true organism, in which the external shaping accords with the concept, so that the parts are essentially members and subjectivity exists as the pervading one of the whole.
