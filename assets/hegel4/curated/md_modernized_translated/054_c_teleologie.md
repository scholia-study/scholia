## {{{ 20.209 }}} c. Teleology

## {{ 204 }} § 204.

By negating immediate objectivity, the concept steps into free concrete existence and becomes _being-for-itself_: this is purpose. Since the negation is at first merely _abstract_, so that objectivity for the time being just confronts purpose, purpose is determined as _subjective_. Against the concept's totality, though, this determinateness of subjectivity is _one-sided_, and moreover _for_ _the_ _concept_ _itself_, because every determinateness in it has been posited as sublated. For the concept, then, the object it presupposes is likewise only an ideal reality, _null_ _in_ _itself_. The concept's identity with itself contradicts the negation and opposition posited within it, and by virtue of this contradiction the concept is itself the sublating, the _activity_, whose task is to negate the opposition so as to posit it as identical with the concept. That is the _realizing_ _of_ _the_ _purpose_; there the concept makes itself into the other of its own subjectivity and objectifies itself, whereby it has sublated the difference between the two, has joined _itself_ _only_ _with_ _itself_, and has _preserved_ itself.

::: remark
{{ 204 A }} The concept of purpose is, on one side, superfluous, though elsewhere it has justly been called a _concept_ _of_ _reason_ and contrasted with the abstract universal of the understanding, whose relation to the particular, which it lacks in itself, is merely one of _subsuming_. – A further point of the highest importance is how purpose, as _final_ _cause_, differs from the merely _efficient_ _cause_, which is to say, the cause as ordinarily so called. Cause pertains to a necessity not yet disclosed, to blind necessity; it therefore seems to pass over into its other and in that other to forfeit its originality in positedness; only in itself, or for us, does cause become cause in the effect and go back _into_ _itself_. Purpose, by contrast, is posited as containing the determinateness, or what still {{{ 20.210 }}} appears there as otherness, the effect, _within_ _itself_; consequently it does not pass over in its efficacy but _preserves_ itself, that is, it produces nothing but itself, and at the _end_ is what it was at the _beginning_, in originality; only through this self-preservation is there something genuinely original. – A speculative grasp is required for purpose, as the concept that in the proper _unity_ and _ideality_ of its determinations itself contains the _judgment_, or negation, the opposition of subjective and objective, and equally is the sublating of this opposition.

Purpose should not be thought of at once, or only, in the form it has in consciousness, as a determination present in representation. By introducing the concept of _inner_ purposiveness, _Kant_ revived the idea in general and, in particular, the idea of life. _Aristotle's_ determination of life already includes inner purposiveness, and so ranks infinitely above the concept of modern teleology, which had only _finite_, _external_ purposiveness in view.

Need and drive offer the most immediate examples of purpose. In them a contradiction is _felt_, one arising _within_ the living subject itself, and they proceed to the activity of negating the negation that is still mere subjectivity. _Satisfaction_ restores peace between subject and object: the objective, which in the contradiction still present (– the need) stands _over_ _there_, is likewise sublated as to this its one-sidedness, by being united with the subjective. – People who make so much of the firmness and insuperability of the finite, the subjective and the objective alike, find the opposite exemplified in every drive. A drive is, as it were, the _certainty_ that the subjective is one-sided and without truth, as is the objective too. Beyond that, a drive _carries_ _out_ this certainty; it succeeds in sublating the opposition, in sublating the subjective that is claimed to be and to remain only a {{{ 20.211 }}} subjective, and likewise the objective that is claimed to be and to remain only an objective, and so in sublating their finitude.

Regarding the activity of purpose one can also note that the _negation_ of the extremes essentially occurs in the _syllogism_ which this activity is, namely that of joining the purpose with itself by the means of realization; – the negation, just mentioned, both of the immediate subjectivity found in purpose as such and of the _immediate_ objectivity (of the means and the presupposed objects). Exactly this negation is practised, in the spirit's elevation to God, toward the contingent things of the world and toward one's own subjectivity; and, as noted in the _Introduction_ and at § 192, it is the moment that gets overlooked and dropped when this elevation is cast into syllogisms of the understanding, in the so-called proofs of God's existence.
:::

## {{ 205 }} § 205.

Being immediate, the teleological relation is initially _external_ purposiveness, in which the concept faces the object as something _presupposed_. Purpose is thus _finite_, in part regarding its _content_, in part because, for the _material_ of its realization, it has an external condition in an object that has to be found; to that extent its self-determination is merely _formal_. Looked at more closely, immediacy implies that _particularity_ (which, as _determination_ _of_ _form_, is the _subjectivity_ of the purpose) is reflected into itself, and thereby the _content_ appears as _distinct_ from the _totality_ of form, from subjectivity _in_ _itself_, from the concept. This diversity makes up the _finitude_ of purpose _inside_ _itself_. The content is consequently as limited, contingent and given as the object is particular and found.

## {{{ 20.212 }}} {{ 206 }} § 206.

In the teleological relation the subjective purpose is joined, by means of a middle term, with the objectivity external to it, a syllogism whose middle term unites the two as the _purposive_ _activity_ and as the objectivity placed _immediately_ under the purpose, the _means_.

## {{ 207 }} § 207.

1) _The_ _subjective_ purpose is a syllogism wherein the _universal_ concept joins with singularity, through particularity, in such a way that singularity _judges_ as self-determination, which means that it particularizes that still indeterminate universal and turns it into a determinate _content_, and also posits the _opposition_ of subjectivity and objectivity, – and which is at the same time, in itself, the return into itself, because it marks the concept's subjectivity, presupposed against objectivity, as deficient when compared with the totality that has closed together within itself, and so turns _outward_ at once.

## {{ 208 }} § 208.

2) This _activity_ _turned_ _outward_ is the _singularity_ identical with the particularity in the subjective purpose – a particularity enclosing, besides the content, the _external_ _objectivity_ – and, as such, it refers first _immediately_ to the object and gains mastery over it as a _means_. The concept has this immediate _power_ since it is negativity identical with itself, wherein the _being_ of the object is determined only as an _ideal_ one throughout. – The _entire_ _middle_ _term_ is now the concept's inner power as _activity_, to which the _object_ is immediately joined as _means_ and beneath which it stands.

::: remark
{{ 208 A }} With finite purposiveness the middle term is _broken_ into two moments external to each other: the activity, and the object that works as means. The purpose, as _power_, relates to this object and subjects it to itself _immediately_, – this is the _first_ _premise_ of the syllogism, – to the extent that in the concept, as {{{ 20.213 }}} ideality existing for itself, the object is posited as null _in_ _itself_. This relation, the first premise, _is_ _itself_ _what_ _becomes_ _the_ _middle_ _term_, and at the same time it is the syllogism _in_ _itself_, because by this relation, its own activity, in which the purpose stays contained and dominant, the purpose joins with objectivity.
:::

## {{ 209 }} § 209.

3) Since the purpose is also _not_ identical with the object, purposive activity together with its means still points outward; the purpose must therefore first be mediated with the object as well. Within this _second_ _premise_, the means, as object, is _immediately_ related to the _other_ extreme of the syllogism, objectivity as presupposed, the material. The sphere of this relation is that of mechanism and chemism, which now _serve_ the purpose, whose truth and free concept the purpose is. The _cunning_ of reason consists in this, that the subjective purpose, as the power over these processes, in which the _objective_ rubs itself away and sublates itself, keeps itself _outside_ _them_ while being what _preserves_ _itself_ in them.

## {{ 210 }} § 210.

Thus the realized purpose is the _posited_ _unity_ of subjective and objective. This unity is, though, determined essentially so that subjective and objective are neutralized and sublated merely in their _one-sidedness_, while the objective is subordinated to the purpose, as the free concept and thereby the power over it, and is brought into conformity with it. A purpose _preserves_ itself against and within the objective because it is not only the _one-sided_ subjective, the particular, but also the concrete universal, the identity of the two that is in itself. Simple and reflected into itself, this universal is _the_ _content_, which stays _the_ _same_ throughout all three terms of the syllogism and their movement.

## {{ 211 }} § 211.

With finite purposiveness, however, the purpose when executed is as much something broken within itself as were the middle term and the initial purpose. What has come about is {{{ 20.214 }}} therefore merely a form set _externally_ upon the material that was found, and because the purpose's content is limited, this form is a contingent determination as well. What has been attained is thus only an object, which once more serves as means or material for further purposes, and so on _infinitely_.

## {{ 212 }} § 212.

What takes place _in_ _itself_ when the purpose is realized, however, is the sublation of one-sided _subjectivity_ and of the shine of an objective self-subsistence confronting it. By taking hold of the means, _the_ _concept_ posits itself as the object's essence that is _in_ _itself_; the object's self-subsistence has already dissipated _in_ _itself_ in the mechanical and chemical process, and as that process runs its course under the purpose's dominion, the _shine_ of this self-subsistence, the negative _against_ _the_ _concept_, is sublated. Because the executed purpose is determined _merely_ as means and material, however, this object is at once posited as null in itself, as only ideal. The opposition between _content_ and _form_ has disappeared along with this. When the purpose joins with itself by sublating the determinations of form, form is posited as _identical_ with itself, and thereby as content, with the result that _the_ _concept_, as the _activity_ _of_ _form_, has only _itself_ for _content_. By means of this process, then, what is _posited_ is what the _concept_ of purpose was: _the_ _unity_ _of_ _the_ _subjective_ _and_ _objective_ that is _in_ _itself_, now _as_ _being_ _for_ _itself_, – _the_ _idea_.
