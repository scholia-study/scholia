## c. The Good and Evil

## {{ 507 }} § 507.

These particularities, and their formalism's concrete aspect, find their truth in the content of the _universal_ will, the will _that_ _is_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, the law and substance of all determinateness – _the_ _good_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, and hence the world's absolute final purpose; for the subject, which _ought_ to have _insight_ into the _good_, it is also the _duty_ to adopt it as its _intention_ and bring it about by its own activity.

## {{{ 20.492 }}} {{ 508 }} § 508.

The _good_, to be sure, is the will's universal as determined in itself, and so includes particularity; but since this particularity is itself still abstract at first, there is no principle of determination at hand; determining also arises outside that universality, and, in the determining of the will that is free and _for_ _itself_ over against that universality, the deepest contradiction awakens here. α) Because the determining of the good is indeterminate, there is in general a _variety_ of goods and a _multiplicity_ of _duties_, whose diversity stands dialectically against itself and brings them into _collision_. At the same time they _ought_ to agree, since the unity of the good requires it, and at the same time each, though it is a particular one, is absolute as duty and as good. It is the subject that _ought_ to be the dialectic _resolving_ on a combination of them that excludes the others, and thereby sublates this absolute validity.

## {{ 509 }} § 509.

β) For the subject, which in the existence of its freedom is essentially a _particular_, its _interest_ and _welfare_ are meant, on account of this existence of its freedom, to be an essential purpose and hence duty. Yet at the same time, within the purpose of the _good_, which is not the will's particular but only its universal, the particular interest _ought_ not to figure as a moment. On account of this independence of the two determinations, it is equally contingent whether they accord. But they _ought_ to accord, because the subject as single and universal is _in_ _itself_ one identity.

γ) The subject, however, is not merely a particular in general in its existence; it is also a form of its existence to be _abstract_ certainty of itself, abstract reflection of freedom into itself. So it is distinct from the rationality of the will and able to turn the universal itself into a particular, and so into a shine. For the subject the good is thus posited as a contingency, and the subject can accordingly resolve on something opposed to the good, can be _evil_.

## {{{ 20.493 }}} {{ 510 }} § 510.

δ) External objectivity, likewise in accordance with the difference that has arisen in the subjective will (§ 503), constitutes, over against the will's inner determinations, the other self-subsistent extreme, a world peculiar to itself. Whether it accords with the subjective purposes, whether the _good_ realizes itself in it and the _evil_, the purpose null in and for itself, is null in it, is therefore contingent; – likewise whether the subject finds its welfare in it, and more closely whether the _good_ subject becomes _fortunate_ there and the _evil_ one _unfortunate_. At the same time, though, the world _ought_ to allow the essential, the good action, to be carried out within it, to grant the _good_ subject satisfaction of its particular interest and to withhold it from the _evil_ one, and also to reduce evil itself to nothing.

## {{ 511 }} § 511.

The contradiction on all sides, which this manifold _ought_ expresses – absolute being that at the same time is not – contains spirit's most abstract analysis of itself, its deepest turning inward. In abstract certainty of itself alone do the contradictory determinations stand related, and to this _infinity_ of subjectivity the universal will, the good, right and duty are as much as they are not; it is this certainty that knows itself as choosing and deciding. This pure certainty of itself, pushing itself to its peak, appears in two forms that pass directly into one another, _conscience_ and _evil_. The former is the will of the _good_, which in this pure subjectivity, however, is the _non-objective_, the non-universal, the unsayable, and about which the subject knows _itself_ to be deciding in its _singularity_. _Evil_, however, is this very same knowing of its singularity as what decides, to the extent that it does not stay in this abstraction but gives itself, against the good, the content of a subjective interest.

## {{{ 20.494 }}} {{ 512 }} § 512.

This highest peak of the will's _phenomenon_ – a will evaporated into this absolute vanity, a goodness that is not objective but merely certain of itself, a self-certainty found in the universal's nullity – subsides at once into itself. _Evil_, as subjectivity's innermost reflection into itself against the objective and universal, which for it is mere shine, is the same as the _good_ _disposition_ toward the _abstract_ good, which reserves to subjectivity the determination of it; – the wholly abstract _shining_, the immediate perverting and annihilating of itself. The result, this shining's truth, shows on its negative side the absolute nullity of this willing that is _for_ _itself_ against the good as well as of the good meant to be merely abstract; on its affirmative side, in the concept, that shining, collapsing so into itself, is the very simple universality of the will that the good also is. In this its _identity_ with the good, subjectivity is only the infinite form, the good's activation and development; with that, the standpoint of the mere _relation_ of the two to one another and of the _ought_ is left behind, and the passage to _ethical life_ is made.
