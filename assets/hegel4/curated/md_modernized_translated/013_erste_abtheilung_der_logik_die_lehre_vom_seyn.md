## {{{ 20.121 }}} First Division of the Logic. The Doctrine of Being

## {{ 84 }} § 84.

Being is the concept only _in_ _itself_; its determinations are _beings_, each one an _other_ over against the rest in virtue of the difference between them, and their further determining (the form of the dialectical) consists in a _passing_ _over_ _into_ _an_ _other_. In one stroke this progressive determining _sets_ _the_ concept _out_ and so unfolds what is _in_ _itself_ the concept, and also is a _turning-into-itself_ of being, in which being sinks deeper into itself. As the concept is explicated within the sphere of being, being becomes a totality, and by the same token the immediacy of being, the form of being as such, is sublated.

## {{ 85 }} § 85.

One may look upon being itself, and upon the further determinations that follow, those of being but also logical determinations generally, each as a definition of the absolute, a _metaphysical_ _definition_ _of_ _God_; yet, taken more closely, this holds only for the first, simple determination of a sphere and then for the third, since the third is the return out of difference into simple self-relation. To give a metaphysical definition of God is to put his nature into _thoughts_ as such, and logic comprehends every thought still in the form of thought. As for the _second_ determinations, which present a sphere in its _difference_, they define instead the _finite_. Were the form of definition nevertheless to be applied, this would imply that a substrate of representation is hovering in the mind; for _the_ _absolute_ too, which is meant to give God's expression in the sense and form of thought, remains, relative to its predicate, which is the {{{ 20.122 }}} determinate and actual expression in thought, merely a _supposed_ thought, a substrate that on its own is indeterminate. Since the thought, the one matter that counts here, is lodged solely in the predicate, a proposition's form, just like that subject, is wholly redundant (compare § 31 and, further on, the chapter on the judgment).
