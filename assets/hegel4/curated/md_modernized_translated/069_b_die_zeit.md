## b. Time

## {{ 257 }} § 257.

The negativity that relates itself to space as point and develops in it its determinations as line and surface is, however, in the sphere of being-outside-itself just as much _for_ _itself_, while at the same time positing its determinations in that sphere as in the sphere of being-outside-itself, and in doing so appearing indifferent to the tranquil side-by-sideness. Thus posited for itself it is _time_.

## {{ 258 }} § 258.

Time, as the negative unity of being-outside-itself, is likewise something absolutely abstract, ideal. – It is being that _is_ _not_ precisely by _being_, and _is_ precisely by _not_ _being_; the _intuited_ becoming, that is, the differences, which are indeed absolutely _momentary_, that is, immediately sublating themselves, are determined as _external_, yet as external to _themselves_.

::: remark
{{ 258 A }} Both time and space are _pure_ _forms_ of _sensibility_ or of _intuiting_, the non-sensuous sensuous, – yet, as with space, the difference between objectivity and a consciousness subjective as against it is no concern of time either. If these determinations were applied to space and time, the former would be abstract objectivity, the latter abstract subjectivity. Time is the same principle as the I=I of pure self-consciousness; but the same, or the simple concept, still in its total externality and abstraction, – as the intuited mere _becoming_, pure being-within-itself as absolutely a coming-out-of-itself.

{{{ 20.248 }}} Time is just as _continuous_ as space, for it is the negativity that abstractly _relates_ _itself_ _to_ _itself_, and no real difference is yet to be found in this abstraction.

In time, it is said, everything _comes_ _to_ _be_ and _ceases_ _to_ _be_; if abstraction is made from _everything_, namely the filling of time, and likewise from the filling of space, empty time remains, like empty space, – that is, these abstractions of externality are then posited and represented as if they were for themselves. But it is not in time that everything comes to be and ceases to be; time itself is this _becoming_, coming-to-be and ceasing-to-be, the _existent_ _abstracting_, _Chronos_ who engenders everything and destroys his offspring. – The real is indeed different from time but just as essentially identical with it. It is limited, and the other to this negation is _outside_ it; determinateness is thus _external_ to itself in it, and hence the contradiction of its being; the abstraction of this externality of its contradiction and of the unrest of that contradiction is time itself. The finite is therefore transient and _temporal_ because it is not, like the concept, total negativity in itself, but does indeed have this, as its universal essence, within itself, though not in conformity with it, being _one-sided_, and hence relates itself to it as to its _power_. The concept, however, in its identity with itself that exists freely for itself, I=I, is in and for itself absolute negativity and freedom; time is therefore not its power, nor is it in time and something temporal, but it is rather the power of time, which is only this negativity as externality. Only what is natural is therefore subject to time, insofar as it is finite; what is true, on the other hand, the idea, spirit, is _eternal_. – The concept of eternity must not, however, be grasped negatively, as the abstraction from time, as though eternity existed outside time, so to speak; and certainly not in the sense that eternity comes _after_ time; in that way eternity would be turned into the future, a moment of time.
:::

## {{{ 20.249 }}} {{ 259 }} § 259.

The dimensions of time, _present_, _future_ and _past_, are the _becoming_ of externality as such, and its resolution into the differences of being as a passing over into nothing and of nothing as a passing over into being. The immediate vanishing of these differences into _singularity_ is the present as the _now_, which, _excluding_ as singularity and at the same time absolutely _continuous_ into the other moments, is itself nothing but the vanishing in which its being goes over into nothing and nothing into its being.

::: remark
{{ 259 A }} The _finite_ present is the _now_ fixed as _existent_, distinguished from the _negative_, from the abstract moments of past and future, as the concrete unity and hence as the affirmative; but that being is itself only the abstract being, vanishing into nothing. – Moreover, in nature, where time is _now_, the difference from those dimensions does not attain to _subsistence_; they are necessary only in subjective representation, in _recollection_ and in _fear_ or _hope_. The past and future of time as _existing_ in _nature_, however, is space, for space is time negated, while space sublated is at first the point and, once developed for itself, time.

No such _science_ _of_ _time_ stands over against the _science_ _of_ _space_, _geometry_. The differences of time do not have this _indifference_ of being-outside-itself that constitutes the immediate determinateness of space; they are therefore not capable of figurations as space is. The principle of time acquires this capability only by being paralyzed, its negativity being reduced by the understanding to the _one_. – This dead one, the highest externality of thought, is capable of external combination, and these combinations, the figures of _arithmetic_, are in turn capable of the understanding's determination according to equality and inequality, identifying and distinguishing.

{{{ 20.250 }}} One could go further and conceive the thought of a _philosophical_ _mathematics_, which would cognize from concepts what ordinary mathematical science derives from presupposed determinations by the method of the understanding. But since mathematics is, to begin with, the science of finite determinations of magnitude, which are to remain fixed and valid in their finitude and are not to pass over, it belongs essentially to the sciences of the understanding; and since it has the capacity to be this in a perfect manner, its advantage over the other sciences of this kind is rather to be preserved and neither contaminated by an admixture of the concept, which is heterogeneous to it, nor of empirical purposes. It always remains open that the concept should establish a more determinate consciousness about the guiding principles of the understanding, as well as about the order and its necessity, both in the arithmetical operations and in the propositions of geometry.

It would, further, be a superfluous and thankless labor to want to use for the expression of _thoughts_ so recalcitrant and inadequate a medium as spatial figures and numbers are, and to treat these violently for that purpose. The simple first figures and numbers, because of their simplicity, lend themselves without misunderstandings to being applied as _symbols_, which nevertheless are always a heterogeneous and meager expression for thought. The first attempts of pure thinking resorted to this makeshift; the _Pythagorean_ system of numbers is the famous example of it. But with richer concepts these means become wholly insufficient, since their _external_ composition and the contingency of connection in general is unsuited to the nature of the concept, and it makes wholly ambiguous which of the many relations that are possible in more composite numbers and figures are to be held fast. Besides, what is fluid in the concept evaporates in such an external medium, in which every determination falls into indifferent being-outside-one-another. That ambiguity could be removed only by _explanation_. The essential {{{ 20.251 }}} expression of the thought is then this explanation, and that symbolizing an import-less superfluity.

Other mathematical determinations, such as the _infinite_, _relations_ _of_ _it_, the _infinitely_ _small_, _factors_, _powers_ and so forth, have their true concepts in philosophy itself; it is clumsy to want to take and borrow them from mathematics for philosophy, where they are adopted without concept, indeed so often without sense, and where they have rather to await their rectification and meaning from philosophy. It is only indolence that, to spare itself thinking and the determination of the concept, takes refuge in formulas, which are not even an immediate expression of thought, and in their ready-made schemata.

The truly philosophical science of mathematics as the _doctrine_ _of_ _magnitude_ would be the science of _mass_, but this already presupposes the real particularity of things, a particularity that is found only in concrete nature. It would, however, owing to the _external_ nature of magnitude, be the most difficult science of all.
:::
