## {{{ 20.478 }}} Second Division of the Philosophy of Spirit. Objective Spirit

## {{ 483 }} § 483.

Objective spirit is the absolute idea, though as existing only _in_ _itself_; standing on the soil of finitude by that very fact, its actual rationality retains in itself the aspect of external appearing. At the outset the free will has differences within it immediately: freedom is its _inner_ determination and purpose, while it refers to an _external_ objectivity found given, which divides into the anthropological side of particular needs, the external natural things that exist for consciousness, and the relation of single wills to single wills, each of them a self-consciousness of itself as different and particular; this side constitutes the external material for the will's existence.

## {{ 484 }} § 484.

This will's purposive activity consists, however, in realizing its concept, freedom, in the externally objective side, so that freedom stands there as a world determined by that concept, in which the will is at one with itself, closed together with itself, and the concept is thereby completed into the idea. Shaped into the actuality of a world, freedom receives the _form_ _of_ _necessity_; the coherence this necessity has in substance is the system of freedom's determinations, and its coherence in appearance {{{ 20.479 }}} is _power_ and _being-recognized_, that is, the way these hold good in consciousness.

## {{ 485 }} § 485.

Freedom's simple actuality is made up of this unity of the rational will with the single will, the immediate and peculiar element in which the former is activated. Freedom and its content belong to thinking and are in themselves the _universal_; hence the content has its true determinateness solely in the form of universality. Posited in this form for the consciousness of intelligence, and given the determination of a valid power, the content is the _law_; – and where it has been freed from the impurity and contingency it carries in practical feeling and drive, and is formed into the subjective will, no longer in their form but in its own universality, as that will's habit, temper and character, it is _custom_.

## {{ 486 }} § 486.

As the _existence_ of the free will, this reality in general is _right_, a term to be taken as embracing not just limited juridical right but the existence of _all_ determinations of freedom. In relation to _the_ _subjective_ will, in which, as universal determinations, they are to have their existence and alone can have it, they are its _duties_, just as they are _custom_ when they are in that will as habit and temper. That which is a right is, in the same respect, a duty too, and a duty is likewise a right. An existence is a right only on the ground of the free substantial will; {{{ 20.480 }}} it is the very same content that, in relation to the will distinguishing itself as subjective and single, is duty. It is the same content that subjective consciousness acknowledges as duty and brings to existence in them. To this extent the objective will's finitude is the shine of a difference between rights and duties.

::: remark
{{ 486 A }} Within appearance, right and duty first stand as correlates in this way: a right on my side is met by a duty in an _other_. According to the concept, however, my right to a thing is not mere possession; as the possession of a _person_ it is _property_, legal possession, and holding things as _property_ is a _duty_, that is, being a person; and once set in the relation of appearance, the relation to another person, this develops into the _other's_ duty to respect _my_ right. _Moral_ duty as such, in me as a free subject, doubles as a right: the right of my subjective will, of my disposition. But the moral brings a difference with it: on one side a determination of the will that is merely inner (disposition, intention), has its existence only in me and is merely subjective duty, and on the other the actuality set against it, and with that a contingency and imperfection, in which the one-sidedness of the merely moral standpoint consists. In the ethical, both have come to their truth, to their absolute unity, although here too, in the mode of necessity, duty and right return into each other and close together by way of _mediation_. A father's rights over the members of the family are every bit as much duties toward them; and the duty of obedience laid on the children is likewise their right to be brought up as free human beings. The government's penal justice and its rights of administration and so forth are at the same time its duties to punish, to administer and so forth; so too the taxes, military service and so forth that the members of the state render {{{ 20.481 }}} are duties, and just as much their right to be protected in their private property and in the universal substantial life in which they have their root; all purposes of society and the state are the private persons' own; but the mediation through which their duties return to them as exercise and enjoyment of rights makes diversity appear, to which is added the way _value_ takes manifold shapes in exchange, although in itself it is the same. Essentially, though, it is the case that one without rights is without duties, and the other way round.
