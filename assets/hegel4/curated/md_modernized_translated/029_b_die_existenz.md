## b. Concrete Existence

## {{ 123 }} § 123.

Concrete existence is the immediate unity of reflection-into-itself and reflection-into-other. It is therefore the indeterminate multitude of concrete existents as reflected into themselves, which at the same time just as much shine into an other, are _relative_, and form a _world_ of reciprocal dependence and of an infinite interconnection of grounds and grounded. The grounds are themselves concrete existences, and the concrete existents likewise, in many respects, grounds as well as grounded.

## {{ 124 }} § 124.

But the reflection-into-other of the concrete existent is inseparable from reflection-into-itself; the ground is their unity, from which concrete existence has proceeded. The concrete existent therefore contains relativity and its manifold interconnection with other concrete existents within itself, and is _reflected_ into itself as _ground_. Thus the concrete existent is _thing_.

::: remark
{{ 124 A }} The _thing-in-itself_, so renowned from Kantian philosophy, appears here in the course of its genesis: it is the abstract reflection-into-itself, held fast, as the empty _basis_ of the differentiated determinations in general, against reflection-into-other and against those determinations.
:::
