## c. Place and Motion

## {{ 260 }} § 260.

Space is in itself the contradiction of indifferent asunderness and undifferentiated continuity, the pure negativity of itself and the _passing_ _over_ _first_ _of_ _all_ _into_ _time_. Likewise time, since its opposed moments, held together in one, immediately sublate themselves, is the immediate _collapsing_ into indifference, into the undifferentiated being-outside-one-another or _space_. Thus in space the _negative_ determination, the _excluding_ point, is no longer merely in itself according to the concept but _posited_ and _concrete_ in itself through the total negativity that time is; – the point so concrete is _place_ (§§ 255, 256.).

## {{{ 20.252 }}} {{ 261 }} § 261.

Place, as the _posited_ identity of space and time, is at first likewise the posited _contradiction_ that space and time, each in itself, is. Place is spatial and hence indifferent _singularity_, and is this only as _spatial_ _now_, as time, so that place is immediately indifferent to itself as _this_ place, external to itself, the negation of itself and an _other_ _place_. This _ceasing-to-be_ and _self-regeneration_ of space in time and of time in space, that time posits itself spatially as _place_, but this indifferent spatiality just as immediately as _temporal_, is _motion_. – But this becoming is itself just as much the collapsing into itself of its contradiction, the _immediately_ _identical_ _existent_ unity of both, _matter_.

::: remark
{{ 261 A }} The passage from ideality to reality, from abstraction to concrete existence, here from space and time to the reality that appears as _matter_, is incomprehensible to the understanding, and for it therefore always takes shape as something external and given. The common representation is to consider space and time as _empty_, indifferent to their filling, and yet always as full, to let them be _filled_ with matter _from_ _outside_ while empty, and in this way, on one side, to assume material things as indifferent to space and time, and on the other side at the same time as essentially spatial and temporal.

What is said of matter is, α) that it is _composite_; – this refers to its abstract being-outside-one-another, space. – Insofar as abstraction is made in it from time and in general from all form, it has been asserted to be eternal and unalterable. This does in fact follow immediately; but such a matter is also only an untrue abstraction. β) Matter is _impenetrable_ and offers _resistance_, is palpable, visible and so forth. These predicates are nothing other than that matter is partly for determinate perception, in general _for_ _an_ _other_, but equally _for_ _itself_. Both are determinations that it has precisely as the _identity_ of {{{ 20.253 }}} space and time, of immediate _being-outside-one-another_ and of _negativity_, or of singularity as _being_ _for_ _itself_.

The _passage_ _of_ _ideality_ _into_ _reality_ also occurs in an explicit way in the familiar mechanical phenomena, namely that ideality can take the place of reality and vice versa; and only the thoughtlessness of representation and of the understanding is to blame if, for them, their identity does not emerge from this interchangeability of the two. In the case of the _lever_, for instance, _distance_ can be put in the place of _mass_ and vice versa, and a quantum of the ideal moment brings about the same effect as the corresponding real one. – In the _magnitude_ _of_ _motion_ likewise _velocity_, which is only the quantitative ratio of space and time, takes the place of _mass_, and conversely the same real effect comes about when the mass is increased and that is proportionately diminished. A brick by itself does not kill a person, but produces this effect only through the velocity attained, that is, the person is struck dead by _space_ and _time_. – It is the determination of reflection called _force_ that here stands, once fixed for the understanding, as an ultimate, and hinders it from asking further after the relation of its determinations. But this at least hovers before it, that the _effect_ of the _force_ is something real, perceptible to the senses, and that _in_ _the_ _force_ is the same as in its _expression_, and that precisely _this_ _force_, _by_ _its_ _real_ _expression_, is attained through the relation of the ideal moments, space and time.

It belongs, further, to this conceptless reflection to regard the so-called forces as _implanted_ in matter, that is, as originally _external_ to it, so that precisely this identity of time and space, which hovers before it in the determination of reflection called _force_ and which in truth constitutes the _essence_ of matter, is posited as something _alien_ to matter and _contingent_, brought into it from outside.
:::
