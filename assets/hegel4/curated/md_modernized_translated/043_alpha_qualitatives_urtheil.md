## α) Qualitative Judgment

## {{ 172 }} § 172.

The immediate judgment is the _judgment_ _of_ _existence_: the subject is posited in a universality that serves as its predicate and is an immediate (hence sensuous) quality. 1) _Positive_ judgment: the singular is a particular. But the singular is _not_ a particular; or, more closely, such a single quality fails to correspond to the subject's concrete nature; 2) _negative_ judgment.

::: remark
{{ 172 A }} To hold that qualitative judgments like: the rose is red, or is not red, can contain truth is among the most essential logical prejudices. They can be _correct_, that is, within the limited circle of perception, of finite representing and thinking; this turns on {{{ 20.187 }}} the content, which is equally finite and, for itself, untrue. But truth rests only on the form, that is, on the posited concept and the reality corresponding to it; such truth, however, is not present in the qualitative judgment.
:::

## {{ 173 }} § 173.

In this _first_ negation the subject's _relation_ to the predicate still persists, the predicate being thus a relative universal of which only the _determinateness_ has been negated; (the rose is _not_ red implies that it still has color, though – at first – another one, which would, however, again make only a positive judgment). Yet the singular is also _not_ a universal. Thus 3) the judgment falls apart within itself, – 1) into the empty _identical_ relation: the singular is the singular, – _identical_ judgment; and 2) into itself as the present complete incongruity of subject and predicate; the so-called _infinite_ judgment.

::: remark
{{ 173 A }} Examples of the latter: spirit is no elephant, a lion is no table and so forth. – Propositions that are correct yet absurd, just like the identical propositions: a lion is a lion, spirit is spirit. These propositions are indeed the truth of the immediate, so-called qualitative judgment, yet they are not judgments at all, and can occur only in a subjective thinking, which may also hold fast an untrue abstraction. – Viewed objectively, they express the nature of _what_ _is_ or of _sensuous_ things: that these are a falling apart into an _empty_ identity on one side and a _filled_ relation on the other, which, however, is the _qualitative_ _otherness_ _of_ _the_ _related_, their complete incongruity.
:::
