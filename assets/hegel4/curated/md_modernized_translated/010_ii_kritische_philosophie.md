## {{{ 20.78 }}} II. Critical Philosophy

## {{ 40 }} § 40.

What critical philosophy has in common with empiricism is that it takes experience to be the _only_ soil of cognitions, though it grants these validity not as truths but merely as cognitions of appearances.

Its point of departure is the difference between the elements that turn up when experience is analyzed: _sensory_ _material_ on one side, and on the other the _universal_ _relations_ of that material. To this is joined the reflection set out in the preceding §, that perception taken by itself holds only the _singular_ and only what _takes_ _place_; and so one at the same time _stands_ _by_ the _fact_ that _universality_ and _necessity_ are present, as equally essential determinations, in what goes by the name of experience. This element, because it does not originate in the empirical as such, belongs to the spontaneity of _thinking_, that is, is a priori. – The determinations of thought, or _concepts_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_, make up the _objectivity_ of cognitions of experience. As they contain _relations_ throughout, _synthetic_ judgments a priori (that is, original relations of opposites) come to be formed by means of them.

::: remark
{{ 40 A }} Humean skepticism does not deny that universality and necessity are determinations found in cognition; that is a fact it leaves standing. In Kantian philosophy, too, the matter is nothing but a presupposed fact; in the usual language of the sciences, one may say that it has put forward only a different _explanation_ of that fact.
:::

## {{ 41 }} § 41.

The _critical_ philosophy begins by putting to the test the worth of the _concepts_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_ that are employed in metaphysics – and, for that matter, in the other sciences and in ordinary representing. Yet this critique {{{ 20.79 }}} does not go into the _content_ of these determinations of thought or into their determinate relation to one another; it regards them in terms of the opposition between _subjectivity_ and _objectivity_ as such. As taken here, this opposition concerns (see the preceding §) the difference among the elements _inside_ experience. _Objectivity_ is in this context the name for the element of _universality_ and _necessity_, which is to say, for the determinations of thought themselves, – what is called the _a_ _priori_. The critical philosophy, however, widens the opposition so that the _entirety_ of experience, that is, both those elements together, lands on the side of _subjectivity_, with nothing left standing against it except the _thing-in-itself_.

How the _a_ _priori_ is more closely shaped, that is, thinking, and thinking indeed as an activity that for all its objectivity is only subjective, results in the following way, – a systematization, by the way, that rests merely on psychological and historical foundations.

## {{ 42 }} § 42.

a) _The_ _theoretical_ _faculty_, cognition as such.

As the determinate _ground_ of the concepts of the understanding, this philosophy names the _original_ _identity_ of the _I_ in thinking – (transcendental unity of self-consciousness). Representations given by feeling and intuition are a _manifold_ as regards their _content_, and equally so as regards their form, the _outside-one-another_ of sensibility, in its two forms of space and time, which, being forms (the universal) of intuiting, are themselves a priori. When the I refers this manifold of sensing and intuiting to itself, gathering it within itself as one consciousness (pure apperception), the manifold is thereby brought to identity, to an original combination. The determinate manners of this referring are the pure concepts of the understanding, the _categories_.

::: remark
{{ 42 A }} Everyone knows how little trouble Kantian philosophy took over the _discovery_ of the categories. _I_, the unity of self-consciousness, is wholly abstract and altogether undetermined; by what route, then, does one get to the {{{ 20.80 }}} _determinations_ of the I, the categories? Happily, ordinary logic already has the _various_ _kinds_ _of_ _judgment_ listed, on an empirical basis. To judge, however, is to _think_ a determinate object. The several modes of judging, already tallied up complete, therefore deliver the several _determinations_ _of_ _thinking_. – It remains the deep merit of _Fichte's_ philosophy to have reminded us that the _determinations_ _of_ _thought_ must be shown in their _necessity_, and that they must essentially be _deduced_. – On the method of treating logic, this philosophy should at any rate have had the effect that determinations of thought generally, the customary logical stock, the _kinds_ of concepts, judgments and syllogisms, were no longer simply lifted from observation and so seized in merely empirical fashion, but deduced from thinking itself. Should thinking be able to prove anything whatever, should logic have to insist that _proofs_ be furnished, and wish to teach how to prove, then above all it must be able to prove its own most characteristic content, and to see the necessity of it.
:::

## {{ 43 }} § 43.

Through the categories, mere perception is lifted up into objectivity, into _experience_; yet these concepts, being unities of merely subjective consciousness, are also conditioned by the given material, are empty in themselves, and find their application and use only in experience, whose other component, the determinations of feeling and intuition, is equally nothing but something subjective.

## {{ 44 }} § 44.

Thus the categories cannot serve as determinations of the absolute, since the absolute is not given in any perception, and for this reason understanding, that is, cognition by means of the categories, has no power to cognize _things_ _in_ _themselves_.

::: remark
{{{ 20.81 }}} {{ 44 A }} By the _thing_ _in_ _itself_ (– and spirit and God are comprised under _thing_ as well) the object is meant so far as _abstraction_ is made from everything that it is for consciousness, from all determinations of feeling no less than from every determinate thought of it. What remains is easy to see, – the _complete_ _abstraction_, the wholly _empty_, determined solely as a _beyond_; the _negation_ of representation, of feeling, of determinate thinking, and so forth. Just as simple is the reflection that this caput mortuum is itself only _the_ _product_ of thinking, and specifically of thinking that has gone on to pure abstraction, of the empty I, which makes this empty _identity_ with itself into its _object_. The _negative_ determination this abstract identity gets as _object_ is itself among the Kantian categories, and is as familiar as that empty identity. – All one can do, then, is marvel at having found it said so often, and over and over, that what the _thing-in-itself_ is cannot be known; yet nothing is easier than to know it.
:::

## {{ 45 }} § 45.

The one to see through what is conditioned in these cognitions of experience is _reason_, the faculty of the _unconditioned_. What is here termed the object of reason, the _unconditioned_ or _infinite_, is nothing but what is self-same, which is the _original_ _identity_ of the _I_ _in_ _thinking_ spoken of earlier (§ 42). This _abstract_ I, or thinking, for which this pure _identity_ becomes object or purpose, goes by the name of _reason_. Compare the Remark to the preceding paragraph. Cognitions of experience cannot do justice to this utterly _undetermined_ identity, since they are on the whole of _determinate_ content. And as this unconditioned is taken to be what is absolute and true for reason (the _idea_), cognitions of experience are accordingly pronounced untrue, to be _appearances_.

## {{{ 20.82 }}} {{ 46 }} § 46.

A need then makes itself felt to cognize this identity, the empty _thing-in-itself_. _Cognizing_ means, however, nothing other than knowing an object by its _determinate_ content. Yet a determinate content has within it a manifold _connection_, and founds connection with many other objects. To determine that infinite, or _thing-in-itself_, this reason would have only the _categories_ at its disposal; if it sets out to use them for that, it turns _overreaching_ (transcendent).

::: remark
{{ 46 A }} The second side of the _critique_ _of_ _reason_ now enters; and in itself this second is of greater weight than the first. The first is, to be sure, the view that came up above, according to which the _categories_ originate in the unity of self-consciousness, so that cognition by their means in fact has nothing objective in it, and even the objectivity credited to them (§§ 40, 41) is merely something _subjective_. If this is all that is looked at, Kantian critique is no more than a _subjective_ (flat) _idealism_, which does not engage with the _content_, has before it solely the abstract forms of subjectivity and objectivity, and moreover, one-sidedly, comes to rest in the former, subjectivity, as the final, absolutely affirmative determination. When, however, the so-called _application_ that reason is said to make of the categories in cognizing its objects is examined, the content of the categories is brought into discussion, in some of its determinations at least, or at any rate an occasion arises for it to be. Of special interest is _Kant's_ assessment of this _application_ _of_ _the_ _categories_ _to_ _the_ _unconditioned_, that is, of metaphysics; the procedure is to be presented and criticized here in brief.
:::

## {{ 47 }} § 47.

α) The _first_ _unconditioned_ that comes up for consideration is (see above § 34) the _soul_. – I find myself, in my consciousness, always α) as the _determining_ _subject_, β) as a _singular_, or abstractly simple, γ) as that which, through all the manifold of what I am conscious of, is {{{ 20.83 }}} _one_ and _the_ _same_, – as _identical_, δ) as _distinguishing_ _me_, as thinking, from all _things_ _outside_ _me_.

It is correctly reported of the procedure of the earlier metaphysics that it substitutes for these _empirical_ determinations the corresponding _categories_, _determinations_ _of_ _thought_, from which arise these four propositions: α) the _soul_ _is_ _substance_, β) it is _simple_ substance, γ) across the different times of its existence it is _numerically_ _identical_; δ) it stands _in_ _relation_ to the _spatial_.

What the critique brings to light in this passage is the flaw that two kinds of determination are mixed up with each other, (_paralogism_), namely empirical determinations with categories; that it is unwarranted _to_ _infer_ from the former to the latter, and, generally, to put the latter in the place of the former.

One sees that this critique says nothing other than the <i>Hume</i>an observation, cited above in § 39, that determinations of thought generally, – universality and necessity, – are not met with in perception, and that the empirical differs from the determination of thought in content as well as in form.

::: remark
{{ 47 A }} Were the empirical to supply the credentials of thought, thought would indeed have to be capable of being exactly demonstrated in perceptions. – That substantiality, simplicity, identity with itself, and a self-subsistence that maintains itself in community with the material world cannot be asserted of the soul is something the Kantian critique of metaphysical psychology rests solely on the point that the determinations consciousness lets us _experience_ in the soul are not precisely those which _thinking_ produces here. Yet by the presentation above, Kant himself has _cognition_ generally, indeed even _experiencing_, consist in the _perceptions_ being thought, that is, in the determinations belonging at first to perceiving being _transformed_ into determinations of thought. – It is always to be reckoned a good outcome of the Kantian critique that philosophizing about _spirit_ has been relieved of the soul-<i>thing</i>, of the categories, and with that of the questions regarding the soul's _simplicity_ or _compositeness_, _materiality_, and so forth. – The true standpoint on the {{{ 20.84 }}} _inadmissibility_ of such forms will, however, not be, even for ordinary common sense, that they are _thoughts_, but that such thoughts, in and for themselves, do not contain the truth. – Where thought and appearance fail fully to match, one can first of all choose to regard the one or the other as at fault. Kantian idealism, so far as it deals with the rational, shifts the fault onto the thoughts: they are held inadequate because they do not suit what is perceived, nor a consciousness confined to the range of perceiving, in which the thoughts are not found. What the content of thought is in itself does not come up here.
:::

## {{ 48 }} § 48.

β) When reason tries to cognize the unconditioned of the _second_ object (§ 35), _the_ _world_, it lands in _antinomies_, that is, in asserting two _opposed_ propositions about _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_ object, each of which, moreover, has to be asserted with equal necessity. The upshot is that the worldly content, whose determinations fall into this contradiction, can be appearance only, not _in_ _itself_. The _resolution_ lies in the contradiction's not belonging to the object in and for itself, but only to the cognizing reason.

::: remark
{{ 48 A }} What is brought out here is that the content itself, the categories taken for themselves, produces the contradiction. That the contradiction which the determinations of the understanding set up in what is rational is _essential_ and _necessary_ is a thought to be counted among the weightiest and deepest advances of modern philosophy. Profound as this vantage is, the resolution is trivial; it comes down to nothing but a tenderness toward worldly things. The worldly essence is not to be the one bearing the stain of contradiction; the stain is to belong _only_ to thinking reason, to the _essence_ of _spirit_. Probably no one objects to the _appearing_ world's showing contradictions to the observing spirit, – {{{ 20.85 }}} it is an appearing world just as it is for subjective spirit, for _sensibility_ and _understanding_. Yet when the worldly _essence_ is set beside the spiritual _essence_, it is astonishing with what ease the humble claim has been made, and echoed, that the self-contradictory is not the worldly essence but the thinking essence, reason. It is no help to resort to the turn that reason falls into contradiction _only_ in _applying_ _the_ _categories_. For in doing so it is claimed that this applying is _necessary_, and that for cognizing, reason has no determinations other than the categories. Cognizing is in fact thinking that _determines_ and is _determinate_; if reason is merely empty, undetermined thinking, it thinks _nothing_. But if reason is at last reduced to that _empty_ _identity_ (see the following §), then it too is finally rid, happily, of the contradiction, at the cheap price of surrendering all content and import.

A further point: the want of a deeper treatment of the antinomy was initially also the reason why Kant lists only _four_ antinomies. He reached them by assuming the table of categories, as in the so-called paralogisms, and in so doing employed the manner, since grown so popular, of placing an object under an otherwise ready-made _schema_ in place of deriving its determinations from the concept. What more is needed in carrying out the antinomies I have occasionally shown in my _Science_ _of_ _Logic_. – The chief thing to note is that the antinomy is found not only in the four special objects drawn from cosmology, but in _all_ objects of every kind, in _all_ representations, concepts and ideas. Knowing this, and cognizing objects in this property, is part of what is essential to philosophical consideration; the property constitutes what is further determined as the _dialectical_ moment of the logical.
:::

## {{{ 20.86 }}} {{ 49 }} § 49.

γ) The _third_ object of reason is _God_; (§ 36) it is to be cognized, which is to say, _determined_ _in_ _thought_. Now for the understanding, as against simple _identity_, every determination is merely a _restriction_, negation as such; thus all reality must be taken as unrestricted only, that is, as _undetermined_, and God, as epitome of all realities or as the most real essence, turns into a _simple_ _abstraction_, and all that is left for determining is the equally absolutely abstract determinateness, _being_. What reason seeks is the union of the two moments, abstract _identity_, here too called the concept, and _being_; that union is the _ideal_ of reason.

## {{ 50 }} § 50.

This union allows _two_ _paths_ or forms; for the beginning may be made with _being_, passing from there to the _abstraction_ _of_ _thinking_, or, the other way round, the transition may be carried out from the _abstraction_ to _being_.

With the beginning in being, being, as the immediate, presents itself as a being of infinitely many-sided determination, a filled world. Its closer determination is that of an aggregate made up of infinitely many contingencies generally (_cosmological_ proof), or of infinitely many _purposes_ together with _purposive_ relations (_physico-theological_ proof). – To _think_ this filled being is to strip it of the form of singularities and contingencies and to grasp it as a being that is universal, necessary in and for itself, and determining itself and active according to universal purposes, a being different from that first one; – as _God_. – What the critique of this course chiefly means is that it is an inferring, a transition. The _perceptions_, and their aggregate the world, do not as such show in themselves the universality to which thinking purifies that content, and hence, it is said, this universality gets no warrant from the empirical representation of the world. Against the ascent {{{ 20.87 }}} of thought from that empirical representation of the world to God, the _Humean_ standpoint is accordingly set, (as in the paralogisms, see § 47), – the standpoint declaring it inadmissible to _think_ perceptions, that is, to draw out of them what is universal and necessary.

::: remark
{{ 50 A }} Since human beings think, neither healthy common sense nor philosophy will ever let itself be talked out of rising to God from and _on_ the basis of the empirical view of the world. The foundation of this rising is nothing but the _thinking_ consideration of the world, as opposed to a merely sensory, animal one. For thinking, and _only_ for thinking, there exist the _essence_, the _substance_, the _universal_ _power_ and the _purpose-determination_ of the world. The so-called proofs of God's existence are to be seen merely as _descriptions_ and analyses of _the_ _movement_ _of_ _spirit_ within itself, spirit that thinks and thinks the sensory. The _elevation_ of thinking above the sensory, its _passage_ _beyond_ the finite to the infinite, the _leap_ said to be made into the supersensory by breaking off the series of the sensory, all this is thinking itself, and this passing over is _nothing_ _but_ _thinking_. To say that no such passing over is to be made is to say that there is to be no thinking. Animals, in fact, do not make it; _they_ stay with sensory sensation and intuition, and for this reason have no religion. Two things are to be remarked about the critique of this elevation of thinking, both generally and in particular. _First_, once the elevation is cast in the form of _syllogisms_ (so-called _proofs_ of God's existence), the _point_ _of_ _departure_ is indeed the view of the world, determined in some way as an aggregate of contingencies or of purposes and purposive relations. It may look as if this point of departure, so far as thinking draws syllogisms, _stays_ a _firm_ _foundation_, and is _left_ as empirical as this material initially is. The relation of the point of departure to the endpoint it advances toward is thereby represented as merely _affirmative_, as an inference from _one_ thing, which is and _stays_, to an _other_, which also _is_ in the same way. But it is a grave mistake to want to understand the nature of thinking only in this form of the understanding. Thinking the empirical world is rather {{{ 20.88 }}} essentially to remold its empirical form and turn it into something universal; at once thinking exercises a _negative_ activity on that foundation; perceived material, determined through universality, does _not_ _stay_ in its first empirical shape. The inner _import_ of what is perceived is drawn out, the shell being removed and _negated_ (compare §§ 13 and 23). The metaphysical proofs of God's existence are thus deficient expositions and descriptions of spirit's elevation from world to God, because they do not express, or rather do not bring out, the moment _of_ _negation_ contained in this elevation; for the world's being _contingent_ already means that it is only a _falling_ thing, something appearing, in and for itself _null_. The meaning of the elevation of spirit is that being does belong to the world, but a being that is merely shine, not genuine being and not absolute truth; that truth lies, rather, beyond that appearance, solely in God, who alone is genuine being. This elevation, being _transition_ and _mediation_, is just as much a _sublating_ of transition and mediation, for what might make God seem mediated, the world, is declared null; the bond of the elevation is only the _nullity_ of the world's _being_, so that what stands as the mediating vanishes, and mediation is thereby sublated in this very mediation. – It is above all that relation, conceived merely as _affirmative_, as a relation between two beings, which _Jacobi_ has in view in his campaign against the understanding's proving; his just complaint is that _conditions_ (the world) are sought for the _unconditioned_, and that the _infinite_ (God) is in this fashion represented as _grounded_ and _dependent_. Yet that elevation, as it occurs in spirit, itself corrects this shine; its entire import is, rather, the correction of this shine. What Jacobi failed to recognize is this genuine nature of essential thinking, which sublates mediation itself within mediation; and so he wrongly took the right complaint he lodges against the merely {{{ 20.89 }}} reflecting understanding as a complaint hitting thinking generally, rational thinking included.

By way of illustrating how the _negative_ moment gets overlooked, the charge that is brought against _Spinozism_, of being pantheism and atheism, may serve. Spinoza's _absolute_ _substance_ is, admittedly, not yet absolute _spirit_, and the demand that God be determined as absolute spirit is just. But when Spinoza's determination is represented as confusing God with nature, with the finite world, and as making the world God, it is presupposed that the finite world has genuine actuality, _affirmative_ _reality_. Granted this presupposition, a unity of God and world does of course make God utterly finite, lowering God to the mere finite, external manifoldness of concrete existence. Leaving aside that Spinoza defines God not as the unity of God and world but as the unity of _thinking_ and extension (the material world), this unity already implies, even if taken in that first, thoroughly clumsy way, that Spinoza's system rather determines the world as a mere phenomenon with no actual reality, which is why this system should rather be considered _acosmism_. A philosophy that maintains that God, and _only_ God, _is_ should at the least not be passed off as atheism. Peoples who worship apes, cows, stone or bronze statues, and so forth, as God are still granted a religion. But it goes yet more against the grain of representation to abandon its own presupposition that this aggregate of finitude of its, called _world_, has actual reality. That, as representation might put it, _there_ _is_ _no_ _world_, is something one readily deems wholly impossible, or at least far less possible than that the thought could occur to anyone that _there_ _is_ _no_ _God_. A system's denying God is believed much more easily, and not exactly to one's credit, than its denying the world; that God should be denied is found much easier to grasp than that the world should be.

{{{ 20.90 }}} The _second_ remark has to do with the critique of the _import_ which that thinking elevation first yields. Should that import amount only to determinations such as the _substance_ of the world, its _necessary_ _essence_, or a _cause_ that _arranges_ and _directs_ _purposively_, and so forth, it of course does not fit what is, or is to be, understood by God. Setting aside, however, the habit of presupposing some representation of God and judging a result by that presupposition, these determinations already carry great worth and are necessary moments in the idea of God. To bring before thought, along this path, the import in its genuine determination, the genuine idea of God, the point of departure must of course not be taken from lower content. The _merely_ _contingent_ things of the world form a very abstract determination. Organic formations and their purpose-determinations belong in the higher sphere, _of_ _life_. But apart from the fact that considering living nature and the further relation of existing things to _purposes_ can be sullied by paltry purposes, even by childish enumerations of purposes and of their relations, merely living nature is itself in fact not yet what the genuine _determination_ of the idea of God can be seized from; God is more than living, God is spirit. _Spiritual_ nature alone is the worthiest and truest _point_ _of_ _departure_ for thinking the absolute, so far as thinking takes up a point of departure and wants to take up the nearest.
:::

## {{ 51 }} § 51.

The _other_ _path_ of _union_, along which the _ideal_ is to come into being, proceeds from the _abstraction_ _of_ _thinking_ _onward_ _to_ the determination for which only _being_ is left; – the _ontological_ _proof_ of the _existence_ _of_ _God_. The opposition that arises here is that between _thinking_ and _being_, whereas on the first path being is shared by both sides and the opposition touches only the difference between the singularized and the universal. {{{ 20.91 }}} What the understanding objects to this other path is, in itself, the same as was just cited: that the universal is not found within the empirical, and likewise, the other way round, the determinate is not contained within the universal; and here the determinate is being. Or put differently, being cannot be derived from the concept, nor analyzed out of it.

::: remark
{{ 51 A }} There is no doubt that the Kantian critique of the ontological proof owes its thoroughly favorable reception and acceptance also to Kant's having chosen, to make clear the difference between thinking and being, the example of the _hundred_ _thalers_, which by _concept_ amount to a hundred equally whether they are only possible or actual; but, he says, for _my_ financial standing it makes an essential difference. – Nothing could be more evident than that what I think or represent to myself is not thereby _actual_, – the thought that representing, or even the concept, does not reach as far as being. – Apart from the fact that it would not be unjust to call it barbarism to name a thing such as a hundred thalers a concept, those who keep repeating, over and over, against the philosophical idea that _thinking_ _and_ _being_ are _different_ ought first to grant that philosophers are not ignorant of this either; what could in fact be a more trivial piece of knowledge? But next it would have to be considered that, where God is in question, the object is of another kind than a hundred thalers and _any_ particular concept, representation, or whatever one may call it. All that is _finite_ is this and _only_ this, that its _existence_ _differs_ _from_ _its_ _concept_. God, in contrast, is expressly to be that which can only be »_thought_ _as_ _concretely_ _existing_«, being that in which the concept includes being. This unity of concept and being is what makes up the concept of God. – To be sure, this is still a formal determination of God, one therefore containing in fact only the nature of the _concept_ itself. That the concept, however, in even its wholly abstract sense, includes _being_ is readily seen. However the concept may otherwise be determined, it is at least immediate relation to itself, arising through the sublation of mediation, hence immediate itself; and {{{ 20.92 }}} being is nothing other than this. – It would be strange, one may well say, if this innermost of spirit, the concept, or if the I, or above all the concrete totality that is God, were not rich enough even to contain so poor a determination as _being_, which is the poorest of all, the most abstract. In point of import there can be nothing lesser for thought than _being_. Only what one may first imagine in connection with being could be lesser still, that is, an _external_ _sensory_ concrete existence like that of the paper I have in front of me here; but the sensory concrete existence of a restricted, perishable thing is in any case not what one will want to speak of. – Besides, the critique's trivial remark that thought and being are different is, at the most, able to disturb the way a person's spirit travels from the _thought_ of God to the certainty that God _is_, but not to bar that way. This transition, the absolute inseparability of the thought of God from God's being, is also what has been restored to its right in the view _of_ _immediate_ _knowing_ _or_ _faith_, of which more later.
:::

## {{ 52 }} § 52.

Thus _determinateness_ stays something _external_ for _thinking_ even at its highest peak; what is left is merely _abstract_ _thinking_, which here is always named _reason_. The result is that this reason yields nothing beyond _the_ _formal_ _unity_ for simplifying and systematizing experiences, is a _canon_ and not an _organon_ of _truth_, and can furnish no _doctrine_ of the infinite, only a _critique_ of cognition. Analyzed to the end, this critique amounts to the _assurance_ that thinking is within itself merely the _undetermined_ _unity_ and the _activity_ of this _undetermined_ _unity_.

## {{ 53 }} § 53.

b) _Practical_ _reason_ is grasped as the will determining itself, and determining itself in a _universal_ way, that is, as the _thinking_ will. It is to give imperative, objective laws of freedom, laws, that is, which state what {{{ 20.93 }}} _ought_ _to_ _happen_. The right to assume thinking here as an activity that determines _objectively_ (– in fact _one_ _reason_) is based on the claim that practical freedom is _provable_ _through_ _experience_, that is, can be shown in the appearance of self-consciousness. Against this experience in consciousness, determinism draws on all it can likewise adduce from experience, in particular the skeptical (also Humean) induction from the _infinite_ _diversity_ in what people hold to be right and duty, that is, in the laws of freedom meant to be objective.

## {{ 54 }} § 54.

As to what practical thinking makes a law for itself, as to the criterion for its _determining_ in itself, again there is at hand nothing but the same _abstract_ _identity_ of the understanding, the demand that no contradiction occur in determining; – practical reason thereby gets no further than the formalism that is held to be the final word of _theoretical_ reason.

This practical reason, however, posits the universal determination, _the_ _good_, not merely _in_ _itself_; it becomes properly _practical_ only in demanding that the good have worldly existence, external objectivity, that is, that thought be objective generally and not merely _subjective_. Of this postulate of practical reason, more below.

## {{ 55 }} § 55.

c) The principle of an _intuitive_ _understanding_ is attributed to _the_ _reflecting_ _power_ _of_ _judgment_, that is, an understanding in which the _particular_, said to be _contingent_ relative to the universal (abstract identity) and not derivable from it, is determined by this universal itself; – something said to be experienced in the products of _art_ and of _organic_ nature.

::: remark
{{ 55 A }} What distinguishes the _Critique_ _of_ _the_ _Power_ _of_ _Judgment_ is that _the_ _idea_ finds expression in it, as representation and indeed as thought. {{{ 20.94 }}} To represent an _intuitive_ _understanding_ or _inner_ purposiveness and the like is to represent the _universal_ thought as at the same time _concrete_ in itself. Only in these representations does Kantian philosophy accordingly show itself _speculative_. Many, _Schiller_ in particular, found in the idea of _beauty_ _in_ _art_, the _concrete_ unity of thought and sensory representation, the way out of the _abstractions_ of the separating understanding; others found it in the intuition and the consciousness of _vitality_ generally, natural or intellectual. – The art product, like the living individual, is of course restricted in its content; Kant, however, puts forward the idea that is comprehensive in content too in the postulated harmony of nature or necessity with the purpose of freedom, in the world's final purpose thought as realized. But, with this highest idea, the laziness of _thought_, as one may call it, finds in the _ought_ an all too easy escape: it holds, against the actual realization of the final purpose, to the cleavage of concept and reality. The presence, by contrast, of living organizations and of artistic beauty shows, even to _sense_ and _intuition_, the _actuality_ of the _ideal_. Kant's reflections on these objects would therefore be especially suited to lead consciousness into the grasping and thinking of the _concrete_ idea.
:::

## {{ 56 }} § 56.

Here a thought is put forward of a relation between the _universal_ of the understanding and the _particular_ of intuition that differs from the one underlying the doctrine of theoretical and practical reason. Yet no insight accompanies it that the former relation _is_ what is _genuine_, indeed _truth_ itself. This unity is, instead, merely taken up as it comes into concrete existence in finite appearances, and is shown in _experience_. Such experience is offered, first in the _subject_, partly by _genius_, the capacity to produce aesthetic ideas, that is, representations of the free _imagination_ which serve an idea and give _occasion_ _to_ _think_, though that content is not expressed in a _concept_ nor can be expressed in one; {{{ 20.95 }}} partly by the _judgment_ _of_ _taste_, the feeling of the _concord_ of _intuitions_ or representations, in their freedom, with the _understanding_ in its conformity to law.

## {{ 57 }} § 57.

The principle of the reflecting power of judgment, further, as regards _living_ _products_ _of_ _nature_, is fixed as _purpose_, the active _concept_, the universal that is determined and determining in itself. At the same time the representation of an _external_ or _finite_ _purposiveness_ is set aside, where purpose figures merely as a form imposed from outside on the means, and on the material through which it is realized. In the _living_ thing, by contrast, purpose is a determination and activity immanent in matter, and all members are for one another just as much means as purpose.

## {{ 58 }} § 58.

Although the understanding's relation between purpose and means, between subjectivity and objectivity, is sublated in such an idea, purpose is nevertheless declared again, in contradiction to this, to be a cause that has concrete existence and works _only_ _as_ _representation_, that is, as something _subjective_; with this, the determination of purpose is likewise declared a principle of judging belonging to _our_ understanding.

::: remark
{{ 58 A }} Given that it is a result of critical philosophy that reason can cognize _appearances_ only, one would at least have, for living nature, a choice between two _equally_ _subjective_ ways of thinking, and, by Kant's own presentation, an obligation not to cognize natural products merely by the categories of quality, cause and effect, composition, constituents, and so forth. Held to and developed in scientific application, the principle _of_ _inner_ _purposiveness_ would have led to a quite different, higher way of regarding [them].

## {{{ 20.96 }}} {{ 59 }} § 59.

The idea, by this principle and in its whole unrestrictedness, would be the actualization in the world of the universality that reason determines, – the absolute final purpose, _the_ _good_, and by way of a third, the power that itself posits this final purpose and realizes it, – _God_, in whom, absolute truth, those oppositions of universality and singularity, of subjectivity and objectivity, are accordingly resolved and declared to lack self-subsistence and truth.

## {{ 60 }} § 60.

Yet the _good_, – in which the final purpose of the world is placed, is from the start determined only as _our_ good, as the moral law of _our_ practical reason; the unity therefore reaches no further than the accord of the world's condition and happenings with our morality.[^1] Besides, even with this limitation, the _final_ _purpose_, the _good_, is a determinationless abstraction, as is that which is to be _duty_. More precisely, the opposition that is posited as _untrue_ in the content of this harmony is aroused again and maintained against it, so that the harmony is determined as merely _subjective_, – as something that only _ought_ to be, which means at the same time has _no_ _reality_; – as something _believed_, which has only subjective certainty, not truth, that is, _not_ that objectivity which corresponds to the idea. – Should this contradiction appear to be covered by moving the realization of the idea into _time_, into a future in which the idea also _is_, {{{ 20.97 }}} a sensory condition like time is, rather, the very reverse of a resolution of the contradiction, and the understanding's corresponding representation, _the_ _infinite_ _progress_, is directly nothing but the contradiction itself, set as perennial.

::: remark
{{ 60 A }} One more general remark can be made on the result that critical philosophy has produced for the nature of _cognition_, a result that has been raised to one of the prejudices of the age, that is, to one of its general presuppositions.

The basic defect of every dualistic system, and of the Kantian in particular, reveals itself in the inconsistency of _uniting_ what a moment before was declared self-subsistent and, for that reason, _irreconcilable_. Hardly has the united been declared what is genuine when what is genuine is, instead, immediately declared to be that the _two_ _moments_, denied subsisting-for-themselves in the uniting as their truth, have truth and actuality only as separate. Such philosophizing lacks the plain awareness that this shuttling to and fro itself declares each of the single determinations unsatisfactory; the defect consists in the plain inability to bring two thoughts together, – and, in form, there are only _two_. It is therefore the greatest inconsistency to admit, on one side, that the understanding cognizes only appearances, and on the other to assert this cognizing to be _something_ _absolute_, by saying: cognizing _cannot_ go further, here is the _natural_, absolute _restriction_ of human knowing. Natural things are restricted, and are only natural things so far as they _have_ _no_ _knowledge_ of their universal _restriction_, so far as their determinateness is a restriction merely _for_ _us_ and not _for_ _them_. Something is known, or even felt, as a _restriction_, a deficiency, only when one is at once _beyond_ it. Living things have the privilege of pain over lifeless ones; even for them a _single_ determinateness becomes the sensation of a _negative_, for as living they have within them the {{{ 20.98 }}} _universality_ of vitality, which reaches _beyond_ the single, keep themselves intact within the negative of themselves, and feel this _contradiction_ as concretely existing in them. The contradiction is in them only because both are in one subject: the universality of its feeling of life, and the singularity that negates it. Restriction, deficiency of cognition, likewise gets determined as restriction, deficiency only _as_ such, by _comparison_ with the _present_ idea of the universal, of something whole and completed. It is nothing but unconsciousness, therefore, not to see that the very labeling of something as finite or restricted contains the proof that the infinite, the unrestricted, is _actually_ _present_, and that knowing a limit is possible only because the unlimited is _on_ _this_ _side_ in consciousness.

To what has been said of that result concerning cognition, _the_ _further_ _remark_ may be joined that Kantian philosophy could have no effect on how the sciences are handled. It _leaves_ _the_ _categories_ and _the_ _method_ _of_ _ordinary_ _cognition_ _wholly_ _unchallenged_. If scientific writings of the time now and then take a run at their subject with propositions of Kantian philosophy, the course of the treatise itself makes plain that those propositions served as mere superfluous decoration, and that the same empirical content would have appeared had those first few pages been left out.[^2]

As to the closer comparison of Kantian philosophy with the meta<i>physicizing</i> _empiricism_, the _unprejudiced_ empiricism does keep to sensory perception, yet equally {{{ 20.99 }}} admits a spiritual actuality, a supersensory world, whatever its _content_ may be like and whether it comes from thought, from fantasy, and so forth. As to _form_, this content has its credentials, like all other content of empirical knowledge, in the authority of external perception, in spiritual authority. The _reflecting_ _empiricism_, though, which makes _consistency_ its principle, fights this dualism in the last and highest content, and denies the self-subsistence of the thinking principle and of a spiritual world unfolding in it. _Materialism_ and _naturalism_ are the _consistent_ system of empiricism. – Kantian philosophy opposes to this empiricism the principle of thinking and of freedom, absolutely, and joins up with the first empiricism, without in the least stepping out of its universal principle. One side of its dualism remains the world of perception and of the understanding that reflects upon it. This world is indeed passed off as a world of _appearances_. But that is a mere title, a merely formal determination, for source, import and way of regarding remain entirely the same. The other side is, by contrast, the self-subsistence of thinking as it grasps itself, the principle of freedom, which it shares with the earlier, ordinary metaphysics, though emptied of all content and powerless to provide it with any again. This thinking, called _reason_ here, is stripped of every determination and relieved of all _authority_. The main effect of Kantian philosophy has been to awaken the consciousness of this absolute inwardness, which, while by reason of its abstraction it cannot develop anything out of itself or produce any determinations, neither cognitions nor moral laws, yet flatly refuses to admit or let stand in itself anything bearing the character of an _externality_. From now on, the principle of the _independence_ _of_ _reason_, of its absolute self-subsistence in itself, is to be seen as a universal principle of philosophy, and likewise as one of the prejudices of the age.
