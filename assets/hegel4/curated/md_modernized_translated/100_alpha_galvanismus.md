## α) Galvanism

## {{ 330 }} § 330.

The _beginning_ of the process, and with it the _first_ particular process, is made by indifferent corporeality, _immediate_ in form, which holds the distinct properties together, still undeveloped, in the _simple_ determination of specific gravity – _metallicity_. The metals, merely _diverse_ and not inspirited toward one another, are exciters of the process, in that through that solid unity (fluidity existing _in_ _itself_, capacity for conducting heat and electricity) they communicate to one another their immanent determinateness and difference; as at the same time self-subsistent, they thereby enter into tension against one another, which is thus still _electrical_. But in the neutral and thus separable medium of water, in connection with air, the difference can realize itself. Through the neutrality, and thus the opened-up differentiability, of water (pure, or raised by salt and so forth to a more concrete capacity of action), a real (not merely electrical) activity of the metal and of its tense difference toward the water sets in; with this the _electrical_ process passes over into the _chemical_. Its production is _oxidation_ in general and deoxidation or hydrogenation of the metal (if it goes {{{ 20.331 }}} so far), at least development of hydrogen gas, as likewise of oxygen gas, that is, a positing of the differences into which the neutral has been dirempted, also in abstract existence for themselves (§ 328), while at the same time in the _oxide_ (or _hydrate_) their union with the base comes into existence; – the _second_ _kind_ of corporeality.

::: remark
{{ 330 A }} After this exposition of the process insofar as it is present in its _first_ stage, the distinction of electricity from the chemical moment of the process in general, and here of the galvanic in particular, as well as their connection, is a clear affair. But physics obstinately insists on seeing in galvanism, as _process_, only electricity, so that the syllogism's difference between its extremes and its middle term is reduced to a mere difference of dry and moist _conductors_, and both are grouped together in general under the determination of _conductors_. – It is not necessary to take account here of closer modifications: that the extremes can also be different fluids and the middle a metal; that partly the form of electricity (as stated in the §) can be held fast, [partly] made predominant at one time, the chemical efficacy strengthened at another; that, as against the self-subsistence of the metals, which need water and more concrete neutralities or the already finished chemical opposition of acids or caustics for their differentiation, in order to pass over into _calxes_, the _metalloids_ are so lacking in self-subsistence that, in relation to air, they at once leap over to their differentiation and become _earths_, and so forth. These and many other particularities change nothing, but if anything rather disturb the consideration of the original phenomenon of the galvanic process, to which we wish to leave this first, well-deserved name. What killed the distinct and simple consideration of this process at once, with the discovery of its simple chemical shape in the Voltaic pile, is the basic evil of the representation of _moist_ _conductors_. With that, {{{ 20.332 }}} the grasping, the simple empirical intuition, of the _activity_, which is _posited_ in the water as middle member and manifested _on_ and _from_ it, has been set aside and given up. Instead of an active one, it is taken as an inert _conductor_. Connected with this then is that electricity too is regarded as a ready-made thing merely streaming through the water as through the metals, whence also the metals are taken only as _conductors_ and, as against water, as conductors of the first _class_. The relation of _activity_, however, from the simplest, namely the relation of water to one metal, up to the manifold complications that occur through the modifications of the conditions, is found in Mr. _Pohl's_ writing: _The_ _Process_ _of_ _the_ _Galvanic_ _Chain_ _empirically_ demonstrated, accompanied at the same time with the whole energy of intuition and of the concept of the living activity of nature. Perhaps only this higher demand made on the sense of reason, to grasp the course of the galvanic and of the chemical process in general as a totality of natural activity, has contributed to the lesser demand having hitherto been little fulfilled, namely that of taking notice of what has been empirically demonstrated as _factual_. – Among the conspicuous ignoring of experiences in this field is that, for the purpose of the representation of the _consisting_ of water of oxygen and hydrogen, the appearance of the one at the one pole, of the other at the opposite pole of the pile, in whose active circle the water is posited, is so described as a _decomposition_ of it that from the pole where the oxygen develops the hydrogen, as the other part of the water separated from it, and likewise from the pole where the hydrogen develops the oxygen, secretly _betake_ themselves through the middle, still existing as water, and respectively also through one another, to the opposite side. The impropriety of such a representation in itself is not only left unnoticed but ignored: that, with a separation of the material of the two portions of the water, which however is so arranged that a merely conducting connection (through a metal) still remains, the development of oxygen gas at the one pole and of hydrogen gas at the other _occurs_ in the _same_ _way_ under conditions where that groundless-in-itself, secret marching through of the gases or molecules to their like-named side is impossible even in a wholly external manner; as likewise the experience is passed over in silence {{{ 20.333 }}} that, when an acid and an alkali, applied at the opposite corresponding poles, both neutralize each other, in which it is likewise imagined that for the neutralization of the alkali a portion of acid betakes itself from the opposite side to the side of the alkali, and likewise for the neutralization of the acid a portion of alkali from the opposite side to its side, – that when they are connected through a litmus tincture, in this sensitive medium not a trace of an effect, and thus of the presence, of the acid that is supposed to pass through them is perceived.

It may also be adduced here that the consideration of water as a mere _conductor_ of electricity – together with the experience of the weaker effect of the pile with such a middle than with other, more concrete means – has produced the original consequence that (Biot Traité de Phys. Tom. II. p. 506.) l’eau pure, qui transmet une électricité forte, telle que celle que nous excitons par nos machines ordinaires, devient presqu’ isolante pour les faibles forces de l’appareil électromoteur (in this theory the name of the Voltaic pile). – Only the obstinacy of the theory, which lets itself be shaken not even by such a consequence, can lead to the boldness of making water an insulator of electricity.

But at the center of the theory, the _identification_ of electricity and chemism, it happens to the theory that it, so to speak, shrinks back from the so striking difference between the two, yet then reassures itself that this difference is inexplicable; – certainly, if the identification is presupposed, the difference has by that very fact been made inexplicable. Even the equation of the chemical determinateness of bodies toward one another with positive and negative electricity ought to show itself at once, of itself, as superficial and inadequate; compared with the chemical relation, however much it is tied to external conditions, of temperature for instance, and is otherwise relative, the electrical is perfectly fleeting, mobile, capable of reversal by the slightest circumstance. {{{ 20.334 }}} If, further, the bodies of one side, the acids for instance, are exactly distinguished from one another by their quantitative and qualitative relations of saturation to a potash, the merely electrical opposition, even if it were something firmer, offers by contrast nothing at all of this kind of determinability. But even if the whole visible course of the real corporeal alteration in the chemical process is disregarded and one hurries to the product, its difference from the product of the electrical process is too strikingly different for any astonishment about this to be suppressed, after the preceding identification of both forms. I will keep to the expression of this astonishment, as _Berzelius_ naively sets it forth in his Essai sur la théorie des proport. chim. and so forth\. Paris 1819. On p. 73 it says: Il s’élève pourtant ici une question qui ne peut être résolue par aucun phénomène analogue de la décharge électro-chimique (chemical combination is called discharge for the sake of electricity), – ils restent dans cette combinaison avec une force, qui est supérieure à toutes celles qui peuvent produire une séparation mécanique. Les phénomènes électriques ordinaires – ne nous éclairent pas sur la cause de l’union permanente des corps avec une si grande force, après que l’état d’opposition électrique est détruit. The alteration of specific gravity, cohesion, shape, color and so forth occurring in the chemical process, and further of the acid, caustic, alkaline and so forth properties, is put aside, and everything has gone under in the abstraction of electricity. Let philosophy no longer be reproached with its abstracting from the particular and its empty universalities! if all those properties of corporeality may be forgotten over positive and negative electricity. A former manner of the philosophy of nature, which potentiated, or rather volatilized and thinned, animal reproduction as system and process into magnetism, and the vascular system into electricity, did not schematize more superficially than is the character of that reduction of the concrete corporeal opposition; in that case such a procedure, drawing the concrete together into the brief and passing over the peculiar and leaving it out in abstraction, was rightly rejected; why not also in the present case? –

But there remains yet one circumstance of difficulty in the difference of the concrete process from the abstract schema, namely _the_ {{{ 20.335 }}} _strength_ _of_ _the_ _coherence_ of the materials combined by the chemical process into oxides, salts and so forth. This strength contrasts, taken by itself, very much indeed with the result of the merely electrical discharge, after which the bodies excited to positive and negative electricity have remained precisely in the same state and each as uncombined for itself as it was before and during the rubbing, while the spark has vanished. This is the proper result of the electrical process; with it, therefore, the result of the chemical process should be compared, in view of that circumstance which is supposed to make the difficulty for the asserted equality of both processes. Might this difficulty not be removed by assuming that in the discharge spark the union of positive and negative electricity is of the same strength as any coherence whatever of an acid and an alkaline in a salt. But the spark has vanished, so it can no longer be compared; above all, however, it lies too plainly before the eyes that a salt, an oxide, is yet a further thing in the result of the process beyond that electrical spark; for such a spark, moreover, the development of light and heat that appears in the chemical process is likewise improperly explained. _Berzelius_ remarks on the difficulty stated: Est-ce l’effet d’une force particulière inhérente aux atomes, comme la polarization électrique, – that is, whether the chemical is not something still different in the corporeal from electricity? – Certainly and evidently! – ou est-ce une propriété électrique qui n’est pas sensible dans les phénomènes ordinaires? that is, as above in the properly electrical phenomena; this question is to be answered just as simply in the affirmative, namely that in properly electrical phenomena the chemical is not present and for that reason _not_ _perceptible_, that the chemical is perceptible only in the chemical process. Berzelius, however, replies to the first case, the possibility of the _diversity_ of the electrical and chemical determination of the body: la permanence de la combinaison ne devait pas être soumise à l’influence de l’électricité, that is, two properties of a body must, because they are diverse, stand _in_ _no_ _relation_ _at_ _all_ to one another – the specific gravity of the metal not with its oxidation, the metallic lustre, color likewise not with its oxidation, neutralization and so forth; on the contrary, however, it is the most trivial experience that the properties of bodies _are_ _subject_ essentially to the _influence_ of the activity and alteration of other properties; it is the dry abstraction of the understanding to demand, given a _diversity_ of properties that even belong to the same body, _perfect_ {{{ 20.336 }}} _separation_ and _self-subsistence_ of them. – The other case, that electricity after all has the power to dissolve the strong chemical combinations, though it is not perceptible in _ordinary_ electricity, Berzelius answers with this: le rétablissement de la polarité électrique devrait détruire même la plus forte combinaison chimique; and affirms this with the special example that a Voltaic pile (here called an _electrical_ _battery_) made up of no more than 8 or 10 pairs of silver and zinc plates, each the size of a 5-franc piece, is capable of dissolving potash with the help of mercury, that is, of obtaining its radical in an amalgam. The difficulty had been made by _ordinary_ electricity, which does _not_ show that power, in contrast to the action of a galvanic pile. Now the action of such a pile is substituted for ordinary electricity, by the simple turn that it is called a batterie électrique, as before the name of the theory for it, appareil électromoteur, was cited. But that turn is far too transparent and the proof taken too lightly, in that, for the sake of the difficulty that stood in the way of identifying electricity and chemism, it is here again straightforwardly presupposed that the galvanic pile is only an electrical apparatus and its activity only excitation of electricity.
