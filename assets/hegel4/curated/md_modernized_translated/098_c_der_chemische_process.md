## c. The Chemical Process

## {{ 326 }} § 326.

Individuality in its developed totality consists in this, that its moments are so determined as themselves to be individual totalities, whole particular bodies, which at the same time are related to one another, as different, only as moments. This relation, as the identity of non-identical, self-subsistent bodies, is the contradiction – hence essentially _process_, which, in accordance with the concept, has the determination of positing the distinct as identical, of indifferentiating it, and of differentiating the identical, of inspiriting it and [of] separating it.

## {{ 327 }} § 327.

First the _formal_ process is to be set aside, which is a combination merely of _diverse_, not opposed, items. They need no existing third, in which, as their middle term, they would be one _in_ _itself_; the {{{ 20.328 }}} common or their genus already constitutes the determinateness of their existence toward one another; their combination or separation has the manner of immediacy, and properties of their existence are preserved. Such combinations of bodies chemically uninspirited toward one another are amalgamation and other fusing together of metals, mixing of acids with one another and of these, of alcohol and so forth, with water, and the like.

## {{ 328 }} § 328.

The _real_ process, however, relates at the same time to chemical difference (§ 200 ff.), in that at the same time the whole concrete totality of the body enters into it (§ 325). – The bodies that enter into the real process are mediated in a third, distinct from them, which is the _abstract_ unity, existing as yet only _in_ _itself_, of those extremes, and which is posited into existence by the process. This third consists, therefore, only of elements, and indeed elements themselves diverse: on the one part, for uniting, neutrality in general, _water_; on the other, for differentiating and separating, _air_. Since in nature the distinct moments of the concept also display themselves in particular existence, the separating and neutralizing of the process is likewise in each case a twofold, according to the concrete and according to the abstract side. _Separating_ is at one time the decomposing of neutral corporeality into corporeal constituents, at another the differentiating of the abstract physical elements into the four, thereby still more abstract, chemical moments of nitrogen, oxygen, hydrogen and carbon, which together constitute the totality of the concept and are determined according to its moments. Accordingly the chemical elements have: 1) the abstraction of indifference, _nitrogen_, and 2) the two of the opposition, of difference existing for itself, _oxygen_, the burning, and of indifference belonging to the opposition, _hydrogen_, the {{{ 20.329 }}} combustible, 3) the abstraction of their _individual_ element, _carbon_.

Likewise uniting is at one time the neutralizing of concrete corporealities, at another that of those abstract chemical elements. However different, further, the concrete and the abstract determination of the process, they are just as much united, for the physical elements, as the middle term of the extremes, are that out of whose differences the indifferent concrete corporealities are inspirited, that is, attain the existence of their chemical difference, which presses toward neutralization and passes over into it.

## {{ 329 }} § 329.

The process is, to be sure, _abstractly_ this: the identity of judging and of the positing-in-one of the items distinguished by the judgment, and as course it is a totality returning into itself. But its _finitude_ is that corporeal self-subsistence also pertains to its moments; this involves, thereby, that it has _immediate_ corporealities for its _presupposition_, which, however, are just as much only its products. In accordance with this immediacy they appear as subsisting outside the process, and the process as coming upon them. Further, for this reason the _moments_ of the _course_ of the process itself fall apart as immediate and distinct, and the course, as real totality, becomes a circle of _particular_ _processes_, each of which has the other for its presupposition, but for itself takes its beginning from without and expires in its particular product, without continuing out of itself into the process that is the further moment of the totality and passing over immanently into it. The body occurs in one of these processes as condition, in another as product; and in which particular process it has this position constitutes its chemical peculiarity; only on these positions in the particular processes can a division of bodies be founded.

{{{ 20.330 }}} The two sides of the course are: 1) from the indifferent body, through its inspiriting, to neutrality, and 2) from this union back to separation into indifferent bodies.
