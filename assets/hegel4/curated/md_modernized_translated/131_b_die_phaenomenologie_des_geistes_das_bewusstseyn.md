## B. The Phenomenology of Spirit. Consciousness

## {{ 413 }} § 413.

_Consciousness_ makes up the stage of reflection, or of the _relation_ of spirit, of spirit as _appearance_. I is spirit's infinite relation to itself, yet as _subjective_, as _certainty_ _of_ _itself_; the natural soul's immediate identity has been lifted to this pure ideal identity with itself, and for this reflection that is being-for-itself the content of the former is an _object_. Pure abstract freedom, being for itself, lets go from itself its determinateness, the natural life of the soul, equally free, as a _self-subsistent_ _object_; and of this, as something _external_ _to_ _it_, I at first has knowledge, and {{{ 20.422 }}} thus is consciousness. As this absolute negativity, I is in itself identity in otherness; it is itself, and stretches over the object as something _in_ _itself_ sublated, being _one_ side of the relation and the _whole_ relation; – the _light_ that shows itself and yet also an other.

## {{ 414 }} § 414.

When spirit's identity with itself is first posited as I, it is nothing but spirit's abstract, formal ideality. Spirit as _soul_, in the form of _substantial_ universality, is now subjective reflection-into-itself, related to that substantiality as to a negative of itself, a dark beyond. Consciousness, then, like any relation, is the _contradiction_ between the two sides' self-subsistence and their identity, in which they are sublated. Spirit as I is _essence_; but because in the sphere of essence reality is posited both as immediately being and as ideal, as consciousness spirit is merely the _appearing_ of spirit.

## {{ 415 }} § 415.

Since I exists _for_ _itself_ merely as formal identity, the concept's _dialectical_ movement, the progressive determination of consciousness, is not, for I, its activity; it takes place _in_ _itself_, and for I it appears as the object's alteration. As the given object differs, consciousness accordingly appears differently determined, and its development appears as a change in the determinations of its object. The subject of consciousness, I, is thinking; the logical progressive determination of the object is _the_ _identical_ _in_ _subject_ _and_ _object_, their absolute connection, whereby the object is the subject's own.

::: remark
{{ 415 A }} The Kantian philosophy can be seen most precisely as having grasped spirit as consciousness, and as containing throughout only determinations of the phenomenology, not of the philosophy of spirit. It {{{ 20.423 }}} takes I as a relation to something lying beyond, called in its abstract determination the thing-in-itself, and it is only under this finitude that it conceives intelligence and will alike. Where, in the concept of the _reflecting_ power of judgment, it does come to the _idea_ of spirit, to subject-objectivity, an _intuitive_ _understanding_ and so on, as also to the idea of nature, this idea itself is again lowered to an appearance, a mere subjective maxim (see § 58, Introduction). It is therefore to be counted a correct perception of this philosophy that _Reinhold_ took it up as a theory of _consciousness_, under the title _faculty_ _of_ _representation_. The _Fichtean_ philosophy stands at the same standpoint, and the non-I is determined merely as _object_ of the I, merely within _consciousness_; it stays as an infinite check, that is, as _thing-in-itself_. Both philosophies thus show that they have reached neither the _concept_ nor _spirit_ as it _is_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, but only spirit as it stands in relation to an other.

Concerning _Spinozism_, by contrast, it should be noted that in the judgment by which spirit constitutes itself as _I_, as free subjectivity over against determinateness, spirit steps out of substance, and philosophy, since this judgment is for it the absolute determination of spirit, steps out of Spinozism.
:::

## {{ 416 }} § 416.

Spirit as consciousness aims at making its appearance identical with its essence, at _raising_ _the_ _certainty_ _of_ _itself_ _to_ _truth_. The _concrete_ _existence_ that spirit has in consciousness has its finitude in being the formal relation to itself, mere certainty; since the object is determined only abstractly as _its_ _own_, or spirit is reflected in it only into itself as abstract I, this concrete existence still has a content that is not as its own.

## {{{ 20.424 }}} {{ 417 }} § 417.

The stages of this elevation of certainty to truth are as follows; spirit
+ a) is _consciousness_ generally, which has an object as such,
+ b) is _self-consciousness_, which has _I_ for its object,
+ c) is the unity of consciousness and self-consciousness, in that spirit beholds the object's content as itself and beholds itself as determined in and for itself; – _reason_, _the_ _concept_ _of_ _spirit_.
