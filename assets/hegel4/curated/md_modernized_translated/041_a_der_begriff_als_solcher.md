## a. The Concept as Such

## {{ 163 }} § 163.

The _concept_ as such contains the moments of _universality_, free equality with itself in its determinateness, – of _particularity_, the determinateness in which the universal stays undimmed and equal to itself, and of _singularity_, the reflection-into-itself of the determinatenesses of universality and particularity; and this negative unity with itself is what is _determined_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ while also being identical with itself, that is, universal.

::: remark
{{ 163 A }} The singular is the same as the actual, save that it has proceeded from the concept and so stands _posited_ as universal, as negative identity with itself. The _actual_ is as yet merely the _unity_ of essence and concrete existence _in_ _itself_, or _immediately_, and for that reason it _can_ act; the singularity of the concept, by contrast, is simply what _acts_, and no longer, like the _cause_, with the shine of acting on an other, but as what acts _upon_ _itself_. – Singularity is not to be taken, however, only in the sense of _immediate_ singularity, according to which we speak of singular things and human beings; this determination of singularity first arises with the judgment. Every moment of the concept is itself the whole concept (§ 160); singularity, the subject, however, is the concept as _posited_ totality.
:::

## {{{ 20.180 }}} {{ 164 }} § 164.

The concept is what is simply _concrete_: singularity, its negative unity with itself as a being determined in and for itself, itself constitutes its self-relation, universality. To that extent the moments of the concept cannot be detached; the determinations of reflection _ought_ each to be grasped, and to count, apart from its opposite; in the concept, however, their _identity_ is _posited_, and so each of its moments can be grasped directly only out of the others and with them.

::: remark
{{ 164 A }} Taken abstractly, universality, particularity and singularity are what identity, difference and ground are. The universal, however, is what is self-identical _expressly_ _in_ _the_ _sense_ that the particular and the singular lie contained in it as well. The particular, in turn, is what is distinguished, or determinateness, but with the sense that it is universal within itself and exists as a singular. The singular likewise carries the sense of being _subject_, the foundation that holds genus and species within itself and is itself substantial. In this the undividedness of the moments within their difference is _posited_ (§ 160) – the _clarity_ of the concept, where no difference interrupts or clouds anything, each being equally transparent.

Nothing is more commonly heard than that the concept is something _abstract_. That holds partly because thinking as such, not the empirically concrete sensible, is its element, and partly because it is not yet the _idea_. To that extent the subjective concept remains _formal_, though not at all in the sense that it has, or could receive, any content other than itself. As absolute form itself it is all _determinateness_, yet determinateness as it is in its truth. However abstract it is, then, it is also the concrete, indeed what is simply concrete: the subject as such. What is absolutely concrete is spirit (see the Remark to § 159) – the concept in its _existing_ as concept, in that it distinguishes itself from its objectivity, which despite the act of distinguishing stays _its_ _own_. Every other concrete, however rich, is less intimately identical with itself, and so in {{{ 20.181 }}} itself less concrete; least of all is this true of what is commonly meant by the concrete, a manifoldness held together externally. – What also gets called concepts, and determinate ones at that, for instance human being, house, animal and so forth, are mere simple determinations and abstract representations; they are abstractions which draw from the concept the moment of universality alone and leave out particularity and singularity, so that they are undeveloped in themselves and precisely abstract from the concept.
:::

## {{ 165 }} § 165.

Singularity is the moment that first _posits_ the moments of the concept as differences: being the concept's negative reflection-into-itself, it is _initially_ the concept's free distinguishing, as the _first_ _negation_, through which the _determinateness_ of the concept is posited, but as _particularity_, that is, in that, first, the distinguished have merely the determinateness of the moments of the concept relative to one another, and, second, their identity, the one's being the other, is likewise _posited_; and this _posited_ particularity of the concept is the _judgment_.

::: remark
{{ 165 A }} The usual kinds of concept, _clear_, _distinct_ and _adequate_, belong not to the concept but to psychology, since by a clear and a distinct concept _representations_ are meant: by the former an abstract one with simple determination, by the latter one in which a _mark_, some determinateness that is, has in addition been picked out as a sign for _subjective_ cognition. Nothing is so much the very mark of the externality and decline of logic as the favored category of the _mark_. The _adequate_ hints more at the concept, indeed at the idea, yet says no more than that a concept, or even a mere representation, formally accords with its object, – an external thing. – The so-called _subordinate_ and _coordinate_ concepts are grounded in the conceptless difference of universal and particular, and in the relation of these in an external reflection. Moreover, listing kinds of _contrary_ and _contradictory_ and of _affirmative_ and _negative_ concepts and so forth amounts to nothing more than picking up determinatenesses of thought at random, which in themselves belong to the realm of {{{ 20.182 }}} being or essence and were already handled there, and which have no bearing on the determinateness of the concept as such. – Even the true differences of the concept – universal, particular and singular – constitute _kinds_ of it only to the extent that an external reflection keeps them apart. – The immanent distinguishing and determining of the concept is found in the _judgment_, for judging is the concept's determining.
:::
