## c. The Actual Soul

## {{ 411 }} § 411.

The soul, in its thoroughly formed corporeality made its own, is as _singular_ subject for itself, and corporeality is thus the _externality_ as predicate, in which the subject relates only to itself. This externality does not represent itself, but the soul, and is its _sign_. Being this identity of inner and outer, the outer subjected to the inner, the soul is _actual_; in its corporeality it has its free shape, wherein it feels _itself_ and lets _itself_ be felt, and this shape, being the soul's work of art, bears _human_ expression, pathognomic and physiognomic.

::: remark
{{{ 20.420 }}} {{ 411 A }} To human expression belong, for instance, the upright shape generally, the particular formation of the hand, the absolute tool, and of the mouth, laughing, weeping and so on, and the spiritual tone spread over the whole, by which the body at once reveals itself as the externality of a higher nature. This tone is a modification so light, indeterminate and unsayable because the shape, according to its externality, is an immediate and natural one, and therefore can be only an indeterminate and wholly imperfect _sign_ for spirit and cannot represent it as it is for itself as _universal_. For the animal the human shape is the highest, as spirit appears to it. But for spirit it is only its _first_ appearance and _language_ immediately its more perfect expression. The shape is indeed spirit's nearest concrete existence, yet, as determined physiognomically and pathognomically, it is simultaneously something _contingent_ for it; to want to raise physiognomy, and still more cranioscopy, to _sciences_ was one of the emptiest notions, still emptier than a signatura rerum, when from the shape of plants their healing power was to be cognized.
:::

## {{ 412 }} § 412.

_In_ _itself_ matter has no truth in the soul; as being for itself, the soul separates itself from its immediate being and sets this being over against itself as corporeality, which can offer no resistance to its forming into it. Once the soul has set its being over against itself, sublated it and made it {{{ 20.421 }}} its own, it has ceased to signify _soul_, the _immediacy_ of spirit. The actual soul in the _habit_ of sensing and its _concrete_ self-feeling is, in itself, the _ideality_, the being for itself, of its determinatenesses, _recollected_ in its externality within itself and infinite relation to itself. This being-for-itself of free universality is the higher awakening of the soul to the _I_, to the abstract universality _for_ which abstract universality exists; and this universality is thereby _thinking_ and _subject_ for itself, in particular subject of its judgment, in which it fends the natural totality of its determinations off from itself, as an object and a world _external_ _to_ _it_, while it nonetheless refers itself to that world and, within it, is at once reflected back into itself, – _consciousness_.
