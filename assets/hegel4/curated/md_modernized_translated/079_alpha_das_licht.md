## α) Light

## {{ 275 }} § 275.

The first qualified matter is matter as its _pure_ _identity_ with itself, as unity of _reflection-into-itself_, thus the first, itself still abstract, _manifestation_. Existent in nature, it is the relation to itself as _self-subsistent_ against the other determinations of the totality. This existing universal _self_ of matter is light, – as individuality, the _star_, and the same as moment of a totality, _the_ _sun_.

## {{ 276 }} § 276.

As the abstract _self_ of matter, light is the _absolutely_ _light_, and as matter it is _infinite_ being-outside-itself but, as pure manifesting, material ideality, _inseparable_ and _simple_ _being-outside-itself_.

::: remark
{{{ 20.278 }}} {{ 276 A }} In the Oriental intuition, the substantial identity of the spiritual and the natural, the pure selfhood of consciousness, thinking identical with itself, as the abstraction of the _true_ and the _good_, coincides with light. – When the way of representing that has been called _realistic_ denies that ideality is _present_ in nature, it is to be referred, among other things, also to light, to this pure manifesting, which is nothing but _manifesting_.

That this determination of thought, identity with itself or the at first abstract self of centrality, which matter now has in it, – that this simple ideality as existent is _light_, this proof is, as stated in the Introduction, to be conducted empirically. What is immanently philosophical here, as everywhere, is the proper necessity of the _determination_ _by_ _the_ _concept_, which is then to be pointed out as _some_ natural existence. – Here follow only a few remarks concerning how pure manifestation exists empirically, as light. Heavy matter is _separable_ _into_ _masses_, because it is concrete being-for-itself and quantity; but the wholly _abstract_ ideality of light admits of no such difference; a limitation of it in its infinite spreading does not sublate its absolute coherence in itself. The representation of discrete simple _rays_ of light and _particles_ and _bundles_ of them, of which a light limited in its spread is supposed to consist, belongs with the rest of the barbarism of categories that Newton in particular made dominant in physics. It is the most limited experience that light can no more be packed in sacks than isolated into rays and gathered into bundles of rays. The inseparability of light, in its infinite extension, a physical being-outside-one-another that remains identical with itself, can least of all be declared _incomprehensible_ by the understanding, since its own principle is rather this abstract identity. – When astronomers have come to speak of {{{ 20.279 }}} celestial phenomena that, as we perceive them, are supposed to have taken place already 500 years ago and more, one may on the one hand believe that empirical appearances of the _propagation_ _of_ _light_, which hold in one sphere, are here transferred to another where they have no significance, – yet such a determination in the materiality of light is not in contradiction with its simple inseparability, – but on the other hand one may see a past become a present in the ideal manner of recollection. – From the representation, however, that rays are sent out from _every_ _point_ of a visible surface _in_ _all_ _directions_, so that from every point a _material_ _hemisphere_ of infinite dimension would be formed, the immediate consequence would be that all these infinitely many hemispheres _interpenetrate_. Yet instead of a condensed, confused mass thereby arising between the eye and the object, and of the visibility to be explained, by virtue of this explanation, producing rather invisibility, this whole representation itself is thereby reduced to nullity, just as is the representation of a concrete body that is supposed to consist of many matters in such a way that the others are located in the pores of one, in which, conversely, the others in turn are lodged and circulate; which all-sided interpenetration cancels the assumption of the discrete materiality of the materials supposed to be real, and grounds rather a wholly ideal relation of them to one another, and here of the illuminated and the illuminating, of the manifested and the manifesting and that to which it manifests itself; – a relation from which, as from the reflection-into-itself that is without relation in itself, all the further forms of _mediations_ that are usually called explaining and making comprehensible, little balls, waves, vibrations and so forth, as well as rays, that is, fine rods and bundles, are to be removed.
:::

## {{{ 20.280 }}} {{ 277 }} § 277.

Light, as the universal physical identity, conducts itself at first as a _diverse_ (§ 275), hence here something external and other, toward the matter qualified in the other moments of the concept, which is thus determined as the negative of light, as a _dark_ thing. Insofar as this likewise subsists as different from light for itself, light relates itself only to the surface of what is thus at first opaque, which is thereby manifested, but just as inseparably (without further particularization, _smooth_) manifesting itself, that is, becoming a _shining_ _on_ _something_ _other_. Each thus appearing in the _other_, and thereby only the other appearing in it, this manifesting, through its positing itself outside itself, is the abstractly infinite reflection-into-itself, through which nothing yet comes to appearance _in_ _itself_ _for_ _itself_. In order that something may appear finitely, be able to become visible, further particularization (for instance a roughness, a coloredness and so forth) must therefore be present in some physical way.

## {{ 278 }} § 278.

The manifestation of objects to one another, as bounded by their opacity, is a being-outside-itself, _spatial_ relation, which, determined by nothing further, is therefore _direct_ (rectilinear). Since it is surfaces that relate to one another, and these can enter into different positions, it happens that the manifestation of a visible object on another (smooth) one manifests itself rather on a third and so forth (the image of it, {{{ 20.281 }}} whose place is attributed to the mirror, is reflected into another surface, the eye or another mirror and so forth). In these particularized spatial determinations the manifestation can have only _equality_ as its law, – that the angle at which light falls is equal to the angle at which it is reflected, and that both angles lie in one plane, which is their _unity_; there is nothing at all present by which the identity of the relation would be altered in any way.

::: remark
{{ 278 A }} The determinations of this paragraph, which may already seem to belong to the more determinate physics, mark the transition from light's universal limitation by the dark to a more determinate limitation, one effected by the dark's particular-spatial determinations. This determination is usually linked with the representation of light as an ordinary _matter_. But nothing is contained in it except that abstract ideality, this pure manifesting, as inseparable _being-outside-itself_, is capable for itself of _spatial_ and hence externally determined limitations; – this limitability by particularized spatiality is a necessary determination that contains nothing further than this, and excludes all material categories of transferring, physical throwing back of light and the like.

With the determinations of the paragraph are connected the phenomena that have led to the crude representation of the so-called _fixed_ polarization, polarity of light. Just as the so-called angle of incidence and of reflection in simple mirroring is _one_ plane, so, when a _second_ mirror is applied which communicates further the illumination reflected from the first, the position of that first plane in relation to the plane formed by the direction of the first reflection and of the second bears on how the object appears through the second reflection, in its position, its brightness or its darkening. For the natural, unimpaired brightness of the lightness (light) reflected for the second time, it is therefore necessary, as the normal position, that the planes of every incidence angle and reflection angle concerned coincide in _one_ {{{ 20.282 }}} _plane_. Whereas it equally necessarily follows that darkening and disappearance of the lightness reflected for the second time occur when both planes conduct themselves toward one another, as one must call it, _negatively_, that is, when they stand perpendicular to each other; compare Goethe, On Natural Science, vol. 1, no. 1, p. 28, bottom, the following 2 pp., and no. 3, Entoptic Colors, XVIII, XIX, p. 144 and following. That it has now been inferred (by _Malus_) from the modification that is effected by that position in the brightness of the mirroring that the molecules of light possess _in_ _themselves_, namely even on their various sides, different physical activities, whereby it also happens that the so-called _rays_ _of_ _light_ are taken as _four-sided_, on which foundation, together with the entoptic color phenomena further connected with it, an extensive labyrinth of the most intricate theory has then been built, – is one of the most peculiar examples of physics' _inferring_ from experiences. What was to be inferred from that first phenomenon, from which Malus's polarization proceeds, is only that the condition of the brightness through the second reflection is that the angle of reflection thereby further posited lie in _one_ _plane_ with the angles posited by the first reflection.
:::
