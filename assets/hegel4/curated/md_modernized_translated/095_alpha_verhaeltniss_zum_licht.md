## α) Relation to Light

## {{ 317 }} § 317.

What first determines shaped corporeality is its _self-identical_ selfhood, whose abstract manifesting of itself, as indeterminate simple individuality, is _light_. But shape as such is not luminous; this property is (preceding §) a _relation_ to light; 1) as a _pure_ crystal, the body is _transparent_ and a _medium_ for light, its inner individualization existing neutrally and in perfect _homogeneity_.

::: remark
{{ 317 A }} What, in relation to transparency, the inner lack of cohesion of air is, is in the concrete body the _homogeneity_ of the shape that is coherent in itself and crystallized. – The individual body, taken indeterminately, is of course as much transparent as opaque, translucent and so forth. But transparency is the nearest first _determination_ {{{ 20.313 }}} of it as a crystal, whose physical homogeneity is not yet further particularized and deepened in itself.
:::

## {{ 318 }} § 318.

2) The first, simplest determinateness that the physical medium has is its specific gravity, whose peculiarity, taken by itself, comes to manifestation only in comparison, and so too, with respect to transparency, only in the _comparison_ with the different density of an other medium. What is operative, where both are transparent, from the one (– the one more distant from the eye –) in the other medium (to ease the exposition and the imagining, let the former be taken as water, the latter as air), is solely the _density_, as qualitatively determining place: the volume of water with the image contained in it is therefore seen, within the transparent air, as though the air occupying that same volume had the greater specific density of water and were thus contracted into a so much smaller space – so-called _refraction_.

::: remark
{{ 318 A }} The expression _refraction_ of light is at first a sensible expression, and correct insofar as, for instance, a rod held in water is, as is well known, seen as broken; this expression also lends itself naturally to the geometrical delineation of the phenomenon. But the refraction of light, and of so-called light rays, in a _physical_ sense is something quite different – a phenomenon much harder to understand than it seems at first glance. Setting aside the other untenable features of the ordinary representation, the confusion into which it must fall becomes readily apparent in the delineation of the light rays assumed to radiate from a point as a hemisphere. With regard to the theory by which the phenomenon is usually explained, one must recall the essential experience that the _flat_ bottom of a vessel filled with water appears _flat_, hence _wholly_ _and_ _uniformly_ RAISED – a circumstance that wholly contradicts the theory but, as usually happens in such cases, is therefore ignored or {{{ 20.314 }}} passed over in silence in the textbooks. – What matters is that one medium is merely transparent in general, and only the _relation_ of two media of different specific gravity becomes operative for a particularization of visibility – a determination that at the same time is only place-determining, that is, posited by wholly abstract density. A _relation_ of the media as operative, however, takes place not in indifferent juxtaposition but solely insofar as _the_ _one_ is posited _in_ _the_ _other_ – here, namely, only as visible – as _visual_ _space_. This other medium is, so to speak, infected by the _immaterial_ density of what is posited in it, so that it shows in itself the visual space of the image according to the limitation that it (the medium) itself undergoes and thereby limits that visual space. The purely mechanical property of density – not physically real but _ideal_ – of being only space-determining occurs here expressly; it appears thus to act _outside_ the material to which it belongs, because it acts solely on the place of the visible; without that ideality the relation cannot be grasped.
:::

## {{ 319 }} § 319.

This at first _external_ comparison, and the positing-in-one of different densities determining visibility, which exist in _different_ media (air, water, then glass and so forth), is in the nature of _crystals_ an _internal_ comparison. These are, on the _one_ _side_, transparent in general, but on the _other_ _side_ they possess in their _inner_ individualization (nuclear shape) a form _deviating_ from the formal equality[^1] to which that universal transparency belongs. This too is shape, as nuclear shape, but equally an ideal, subjective form, which, like specific gravity, acts so as to determine {{{ 20.315 }}} place, and therefore also determines visibility, as spatial manifesting, in a specific manner differing from the first, abstract transparency – _double_ _refraction_ of rays.

::: remark
{{ 319 A }} The category _force_ could be suitably used here, in that the rhomboidal form (the commonest of those deviating in themselves from that formal equality of shape) individualizes the crystal inwardly _through_ _and_ _through_ but, unless the crystal happens to be splintered into laminae, does not come to _existence_ as shape, and, without in the least interrupting or disturbing its perfect homogeneity and transparency, is operative only as _immaterial_ determinateness.

I can cite nothing more apt, with regard to the transition from a relation at first externally posited to its form as an inwardly operative determinateness or force, than how _Goethe_ expresses the relation of the external arrangement of two mirrors set toward one another to the phenomenon of entoptic colors, which is generated in the _interior_ of the glass cube placed between them. In Zur Naturwissenschaft, first volume, third issue, XXII, p. 148, it says: “of the natural, transparent, crystallized bodies” – “we thus say of them: that nature has built into _the_ _innermost_ of such bodies an _equal_ _mirror-apparatus_, such as we have made with _external_, _physico-mechanical_ means” – compare the preceding page there.[^2] – It is a matter, as said, in this juxtaposition of the outer and the inner, not of refraction, as in the paragraph, but of an _external_ double reflection and the phenomenon corresponding to it in the interior. So a further distinction is to be made when it says there, p. 147: “one could quite clearly observe in rhombic calcspar that the different passage of the _laminae_ and the _reflections_ _therefore_ acting against one another are the nearest cause of the appearance” – that in the paragraph there is talk of the _force_ or {{{ 20.316 }}} _efficacy_, so to speak rhomboidal, not of the effect of existing laminae (compare first volume, first issue, Zur – Naturwissenschaft, p. 25).
:::

## {{ 320 }} § 320.

3) This immaterial _being-for-itself_ (force) of the form, proceeding to _inner_ _existence_, sublates the neutral nature of crystallization, and the determination of immanent punctuality, _brittleness_ (and then cohesion), sets in, with a perfect but _formal_ transparency still present (– brittle glass, for instance). This moment of brittleness is _diversity_ from the _self-identical_ manifesting, from light and illumination; it is thus the inner beginning or _principle_ of _darkening_, not yet existent darkness, but _operative_ as darkening; (brittle glass, although perfectly transparent, is the well-known condition of entoptic colors).

Darkening does not remain merely a principle but, apart from the clouding and lesser transparencies brought about externally and quantitatively, proceeds against the simple, indeterminate neutrality of shape to the _abstract_, one-sided _extreme_ of solidity, of passive cohesion (metallicity). Thus an _existent_ _dark_ that is also for itself and a _bright_ present _for_ _itself_, posited by means of transparency at the same time in concrete, individualized unity, give the appearance of _color_.

::: remark
{{ 320 A }} Light as such is immediately opposed by abstract darkness (§ 277). But the dark first becomes real as physical, individualized corporeality, and the course of darkening that has been shown is this _individualization_ of the bright, that is, here, of the transparent, namely of the passive manifestation within the circle of shape, to the _being-within-itself_ of individual matter; the transparent is the {{{ 20.317 }}} neutral, homogeneous in its existence; the dark is what is individualized in itself to being-for-itself, but does not exist in punctuality, only as _force_ against the bright, and for that reason can equally exist in perfect homogeneity. – _Metallicity_, as is well known, is the material principle underlying all coloring – the universal _coloring_ _material_, if one wishes to put it so. What comes into consideration of metal here is only its high specific gravity, in which predominant particularization the specific matter withdraws itself against the opened-up inner neutrality of the transparent shape and heightens itself to the extreme; in the chemical, metallicity is then likewise a one-sided, indifferent base.

In the course of darkening shown here, what mattered was not only to state the moments abstractly but to name the empirical ways in which they appear. It is evident of itself that both have their difficulties; but what produces still greater difficulties for physics is the mingling of determinations or properties that belong to wholly different spheres. As essential as it is, for the universal phenomena such as heat, color and so forth, to find out the simple specific determinateness under conditions and circumstances however various, so essential is it, on the other side, to hold fast the _differences_ under which such phenomena show themselves. What color, heat and so forth are cannot, in empirical physics, be put on the footing of the concept, but must be put on that of the _modes_ _of_ _origin_. These, however, are extremely various. The mania for finding only universal laws, however, leaves out for this purpose essential differences, and from an _abstract_ standpoint sets the most heterogeneous chaotically in one line (as in chemistry, say, gases, sulfur, metals and so forth). Not to consider the modes of operation in particularized fashion, according to the different media and circles in which they take place, must have been detrimental to the very demand to find universal laws and determinations. The circumstances under which the phenomenon of color emerges are found set side by side in such chaotic fashion, and experiments belonging to the most special circle of circumstances are wont to be set against {{{ 20.318 }}} the simple universal conditions, the original phenomena, in which the nature of color presents itself to the unprejudiced sense. This confusion, which in truth proceeds with crude superficiality under the show of fine and thorough experience, can be met only by attending to the differences in the modes of origin, which for this purpose one must know and hold apart in their determinateness.

First one has to convince oneself, as the basic determination, that the _inhibition_ of illumination is connected with specific gravity and cohesion. These determinations are, against the abstract identity of pure manifestation (light as such), the peculiarities and particularizations of corporeality; from them corporeality goes further back into itself, into the dark; they are the determinations that immediately constitute the progression from conditioned to free individuality (§ 307), and show themselves here as the relation between the former and the latter. The _entoptic_ colors have this interest, that the principle of darkening here is brittleness as immaterial (operative only as force) _punctuality_, which exists in an _external_ way in the pulverization of a transparent crystal and brings about opacity, as, for instance, also in the foaming of a transparent liquid, and so forth. – The _pressure_ of a lens, which generates the _epoptic_ colors, is an externally mechanical alteration of the specific gravity alone, in which division into laminae and similar _existent_ inhibitions are not present. – In the _heating_ of metals (alteration of specific gravity), fleetingly successive colors arise on their surfaces, which can themselves be fixed at will (Goethe, Farbenlehre, I. p. 191). – In the _chemical_ determination, however, an entirely different principle of the illumination {{{ 20.319 }}} of the dark, of the more immanent self-manifestation, of kindling to fire, enters through the acid. From the consideration of colors for themselves, the chemically determined inhibition, darkening and illumination is initially to be excluded; for the chemical body, like the eye (in the subjective, physiological phenomena of color), is a _concrete_ that contains many further determinations in itself, so that those relating to color cannot be determinately singled out and shown separately for themselves; rather, cognition of abstract color is presupposed, in order to find out in the concrete what relates to it.

What has been said refers to the _inner_ darkening insofar as it belongs to the _nature_ of the body; with regard to color it is of interest to demonstrate it insofar as the clouding brought about by it is not posited in an externally self-existent way, and thus cannot be shown in that way. A medium operative as clouding in _external_ existence, however, is a less transparent, merely translucent medium in general; an entirely transparent one (elemental air is without the concrete such as already lies in the neutrality of unindividualized water), like water or pure glass, has a beginning of clouding, which comes into existence through thickening of the medium, especially in the increase of layers (that is, interrupting delimitations). The most famous externally clouding means is the prism, whose clouding efficacy lies in two circumstances: first, in its external delimitation as such, at its edges; second, in its prismatic shape, the inequality of the diameters of its profile from the whole breadth of its side to the opposite edge. Among the incomprehensible things in the theories of color belongs, among others, that in them the property of the _prism_ to act as clouding, and in particular as unequally clouding according to the unequal thickness of the diameters of the different parts through which the light falls, is overlooked.

Darkening in general, however, is only the _one_ circumstance, brightness the other; a closer determination in the relation of the two belongs to color. Light illuminates, the day _drives_ _out_ the darkness; dimming, as the mere mixing of the bright with dark that is present, yields in general a _gray_. But color is such a {{{ 20.320 }}} combination of both determinations that, while they are held apart, they are equally posited into one; they are separated, and equally the one shines in the other; a combination thus to be called individualization; a relation of the kind shown in so-called refraction, that one determination is operative in the other and yet has an existence for itself. It is the manner of the concept in general, which, as concrete, contains the moments at once as distinguished and in their ideality, their unity. This determination is found expressed, in the sensible way proper to it, in _Goethe's_ presentation – that in the prism the bright is _drawn_ _over_ the dark, or conversely, so that the bright, as bright, equally continues to work through independently as it is clouded, so that (in the case of the prism), apart from the common displacement, it equally stays in its place as it is at the same time displaced. Where the bright or the dark, or rather the illuminating and the darkening (both are relative), exists for itself in the _turbid_ _media_, the turbid medium, placed before a dark background and in this way acting as illuminating – and conversely – retains its peculiar appearance, and at the same time the one is negative in the other, both posited as identical. So the difference of color from mere gray (– although, for instance, a merely gray, uncoloured shadow is perhaps found more rarely than one at first supposes) is to be grasped – it is the same as, within the color-square, the difference of green from red, the former the _mixing_ of the opposition, of the blue and the yellow, the latter their individuality.

According to the well-known _Newtonian_ theory, white, that is, colorless, light _consists_ of _five_ or of _seven_ colors; for the theory itself does not know this exactly. – One can _not_ express oneself _strongly_ _enough_ about the _barbarism_, in the first place, of the representation that, even with light, the worst form of reflection, _composition_, has been reached for, and that the _bright_ should here even consist of seven _darknesses_, as one could let clear water consist of seven {{{ 20.321 }}} earths;

As well as about the _clumsiness_ and _incorrectness_ of Newtonian observing and experimenting, no less about its _insipidity_, indeed even, as _Goethe_ has shown, about its dishonesty; – one of the most striking as well as simplest incorrectnesses is the false assurance that a _single-colored_ part of the spectrum produced by a prism, let through a second prism, also appears again only single-colored, – (Newt. Opt. L. I. P. I. prop. V. in fine);

Then about the equally poor character of the _concluding_, _inferring_ and _proving_ from those impure empirical data; Newton not only used the prism, but the circumstance also had not escaped him that a boundary of bright and dark is required for the production of color through it (Opt. Lib. II. P. II. p. 230. ed. lat. Lond. 1719.), and yet he could overlook the dark as operative in clouding. This _condition_ of color is mentioned by him at all only in connection with one wholly special phenomenon (and even there clumsily), in passing and long after the theory was finished. So this mention serves the defenders of the theory only to be able to say that this condition was not unknown to Newton, but not to place it, as _condition_, together with light at the head of all consideration of color. Rather, that circumstance, that in every phenomenon of color something dark is present, is passed over in silence in the textbooks, as is the quite simple experience that when a wholly white (or in general single-colored) wall is looked at through the prism, one sees _no_ color (in the case of single color none other than just the color of the wall), but as soon as a nail is driven into the wall, some inequality made on it, colors come to view at once, and only then, and only at such a place. Among the improprieties of the presentation of the theory is therefore also to be counted this, that so many refuting experiences are passed over in silence;

Thereupon, finally, especially about the thoughtlessness with which a multitude of the immediate consequences of that theory (for instance the {{{ 20.322 }}} impossibility of achromatic telescopes) have been given up and yet the theory itself is maintained; –

But lastly, about the blindness of the _prejudice_ that this theory rests on something _mathematical_, as if the _measurements_, themselves in part false and one-sided, deserved so much as the name of mathematics, and as if the quantitative determinations brought into the inferences furnished any ground for the theory and for the nature of the fact itself.

A principal reason why _Goethe's_ illumination of this darkness in the light, as clear as it is _thorough_ and even _learned_, has not obtained a more effective reception is without doubt this, that the thoughtlessness and simple-mindedness one would have to confess is far too great. – Instead of these absurd representations having diminished, they have in the most recent times been multiplied, upon the _Malusian_ discoveries, by the _polarization_ of light, and even by the _squareness_ of the sun's rays, by a _left_ _rotating_ _motion_ of red and a _right_ _rotating_ one of blue light-globules, and finally by the resumed Newtonian _fits_, the accès de facile transmission and accès de facile reflexion, into further metaphysical galimatias. – Part of such representations also arose here from the application of differential formulas to phenomena of color, in that the good meanings which members of these formulas have in mechanics were transferred, illicitly, to determinations of a wholly different field.
