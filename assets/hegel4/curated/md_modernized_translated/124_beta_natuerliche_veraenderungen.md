## {{{ 20.393 }}} β) Natural Alterations

## {{ 396 }} § 396.

Determined in the soul as _individual_, the differences are _alterations_ in it, the one subject persisting in them, and are <i>moments</i> of its development. Since they are in one both physical and spiritual differences, the cognition of the cultivated spirit would have to be anticipated for their more concrete determination or description.

::: remark
{{ 396 A }} They are 1) the natural _course_ _of_ _the_ _ages_ _of_ _life_, from the _child_, the spirit enveloped in itself, through the developed opposition, the tension of a universality still itself subjective, of ideals, imaginings, oughts, hopes and so forth, against immediate singularity, that is, against the world at hand that is not adequate to them, and the position toward it, in its existence, of the individual, who on the other side is still not self-subsistent and unfinished in itself _(youth)_, to the true relation, the _recognition_ of the _objective_ necessity and rationality of the already present finished world, in whose work, accomplishing itself in and for itself, the individual procures for its activity a confirmation and a share, and thereby _is_ _something_, has actual presence and objective worth _(man)_, – until the completion of the unity with this objectivity, which unity, as real, passes over into the inactivity of a habit that blunts, and as ideal gains freedom from the limited interests and entanglements of external presence, – _(old_ _man.)_
:::

## {{ 397 }} § 397.

2) The moment of the individual's real opposition to itself, in which it looks for _itself_ in an _other_ individual and finds itself there; – the _sex-relation_, a natural difference, on the one hand of the subjectivity that {{{ 20.394 }}} remains at one with itself in the feeling of ethical life, love and so forth, and does not proceed to the extreme of the universal in purposes, state, science, art and so forth, and _on_ _the_ _other_ _hand_ of the activity that tenses itself within itself into the opposition of universal, objective interests against the existence at hand, its own and the externally worldly, and realizes the former in the latter into a unity that is first produced. The sex-relation attains in the _family_ its spiritual and ethical significance and determination.

## {{ 398 }} § 398.

3) The distinguishing of individuality as _being-for-itself_ from itself as only _being_, as immediate _judgment_, is the _awakening_ of the soul, which at first confronts its natural life, locked up in itself, as natural determinateness and _state_, a state, _sleep_. – Awakening is not distinguished from sleep merely _for_ _us_ or externally; it is itself the _judgment_ of the individual soul, whose being-for-itself is for it the relation of this its determination to its being, the distinguishing of itself from its still undistinguished universality. Into waking falls generally all self-conscious and rational _activity_ of spirit's distinguishing that exists for itself. – Sleep strengthens this activity; it is not a merely negative pause from it but a withdrawal out of the world of _determinatenesses_, out of dispersion and fixity in singularities, back into the universal essence of subjectivity, the substance of those determinatenesses and their absolute power.

::: remark
{{ 398 A }} The difference between sleeping and waking is wont to be made one of the _trick_ _questions_, as one might call them, put to philosophy (– _Napoleon_, too, on a visit to the university at Pavia, put this question to the class on ideology). The determinateness given in the section is abstract, insofar as it concerns first of all awakening as natural, in which the spiritual is certainly contained implicitly but is not yet posited as {{{ 20.395 }}} _existence_. If this difference, which remains the same in its basic determination, were to be spoken of more concretely, the being-for-itself of the individual soul would already have to be taken as determined, as the I of consciousness and as understanding spirit. The difficulty that is raised for the distinguishing of those two states arises properly only insofar as one adds dreaming in sleep and then determines the representations of waking, reflective consciousness also as merely _representations_, which the dreams likewise are. In this superficial determination of _representations_ the two states do indeed agree, that is, the difference between them is thereby overlooked; and with every distinction given of waking consciousness one can return to the trivial remark that this, too, contains only representations. – But the _being-for-itself_ of the waking soul, grasped _concretely_, is _consciousness_ and _understanding_, and the world of the understanding consciousness is something wholly other than a picture of mere representations and images. The latter as such cohere chiefly externally, according to the so-called laws of the so-called _association_ _of_ _ideas_, in a manner lacking understanding, in which categories can indeed also intermingle here and there. In waking, however, the human being behaves essentially as concrete I, as understanding; through this, intuition stands before the human being [as] a concrete totality of determinations, in which every member, every point takes its place, determined through and together with all the others. Thus the content has its confirmation not through the mere subjective representing and distinguishing of the content as something external to the person, but through the concrete connection in which every part stands with all parts of this complex. Waking is the concrete consciousness of this reciprocal confirmation of every single moment of its content by all the remaining ones of the picture of intuition. This consciousness does not need to be distinctly developed, but this comprehensive {{{ 20.396 }}} determinateness is contained and present in concrete self-feeling. – To recognize the difference between dreaming and waking, one needs only to have before one's eyes the Kantian distinction of the _objectivity_ of representation (its being determined by categories) from the _subjectivity_ of it in general; at the same time one must know what has just been remarked, that what is actually present in spirit need not for that reason be posited in its consciousness in an explicit manner, any more than the elevation of the perhaps feeling spirit to God needs to stand before consciousness in the form of the proofs of the existence of God, notwithstanding that, as expounded earlier, these proofs entirely express only the import and content of that feeling.
:::
