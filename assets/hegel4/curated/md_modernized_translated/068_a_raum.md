## a. Space

## {{ 254 }} § 254.

The first or immediate determination of nature is the abstract _universality_ _of_ _its_ _being-outside-itself_, – the unmediated indifference of that being, _space_. It is the wholly ideal _side-by-sideness_, because it is being-outside-itself, and is absolutely _continuous_, because this being-outside-one-another is still wholly _abstract_ and has no determinate difference within itself.

::: remark
{{ 254 A }} Much has been put forward about the nature of space from of old. I mention only _Kant's_ determination that, like time, it is a form of _sensuous_ _intuition_. It has also otherwise become customary {{{ 20.244 }}} to take as a basis that space must be considered only as something subjective in representation. If we set aside what in the Kantian concept belongs to subjective idealism and its determinations, the correct determination remains that space is a mere form, that is, an _abstraction_, namely that of immediate _externality_. – To speak of _points of space_, as if they constituted the positive element of space, is inadmissible, since by reason of its lack of difference space is only the possibility, not the _positedness_, of being-outside-one-another and of the negative, and is therefore absolutely continuous; the point, being-for-itself, is accordingly rather the _negation_ of space, one posited in space. – The question about the infinity of space is likewise decided by this (§ 100, Remark). Space is in general pure _quantity_, no longer merely this as a logical determination, but as immediately and externally existent. – Nature begins with the quantitative and not with the qualitative for this reason, that its determination is not, like logical being, the abstractly first and immediate, but is essentially already what is _mediated_ in itself, being-external and being-other.
:::

## {{ 255 }} § 255.

Space has in itself, as concept in general, the concept's _differences_ in it: a) immediately, in its indifference, as the merely _diverse_, wholly indeterminate three _dimensions_.

::: remark
{{ 255 A }} To deduce the necessity that space has precisely three dimensions is not to be demanded of geometry, insofar as it is not a philosophical science and may presuppose its object, space with its universal determinations. But otherwise too there is no thought of {{{ 20.245 }}} demonstrating this necessity. It rests on the nature of the concept, whose determinations in this first form of being-outside-one-another, in _abstract_ quantity, are however wholly superficial and a thoroughly empty difference. One cannot therefore say how _height_, _length_ and _breadth_ differ from one another, because they only are _supposed_ _to_ _be_ different but _are_ not yet differences; it is entirely indeterminate whether one calls a direction height, length or breadth. – _Height_ has its closer determination in the direction toward the center of the earth; but this more concrete determination is no concern of the nature of space in itself. With that presupposed, it is still indifferent whether one calls the same direction height or depth, just as nothing is determined thereby for length and breadth, which are also often called depth.
:::

## {{ 256 }} § 256.

b\. But the difference is essentially determinate, qualitative difference. As such it is α) at first the _negation_ of space itself, since space is the immediate _undifferentiated_ being-outside-itself: the _point_. β) The negation, however, is negation _of_ _space_, that is, it is itself spatial; the point, as essentially this relation, that is, as sublating itself, is the _line_, the first being-other, that is, spatial <i>being</i>, of the point; γ) but the truth of being-other is the negation of the negation. The line therefore passes over into _surface_, which on one side is a determinateness as against line and point, and so surface in general, but on the other side is the sublated negation of space, hence a restoration of spatial totality, which now has the negative moment in it; – an _enclosing_ _boundary-surface_ that cuts off a _single_ whole space.

::: remark
{{ 256 A }} That the line does not consist of points, nor the surface of lines, follows from their concept, since the line is rather the point as being _outside_ _itself_, namely _relating_ itself to space and sublating itself, {{{ 20.246 }}} and the surface is likewise the sublated line that is outside itself. – The point was here represented as the first and positive and was taken as the starting point. But conversely, insofar as space is in fact the positive, the surface is the first negation and the line the second, which as the second, the negation that by its truth relates itself to itself, is the point; the necessity of the transition is the same. No thought is given to the necessity of this transition in the external grasping and defining of point, line and so forth; yet that first kind of passing over is represented, though as something contingent, in the manner of definition according to which, if the point _moves_, the line arises, and so forth. – The further figurations of space that geometry considers are further qualitative delimitations of an abstraction of space, of the surface, or of a limited whole space. Moments of necessity also occur in them, for instance that the triangle is the first rectilinear figure, that all other figures must be traced back to it or to the square if they are to be determined, and the like. – The principle of these drawings is the understanding's identity, which determines the figurations to _regularity_ and thereby grounds the relations that thereby become possible to cognize.

In passing it may be remarked that it was a strange notion of _Kant's_ to maintain that the definition of _the_ _straight_ _line_, that it is the shortest way between two points, is a synthetic proposition; for my _concept_ _of_ _the_ _straight_, he said, contains nothing of magnitude but only a quality. In this sense every definition is a synthetic proposition; the definiendum, _the_ _straight_ _line_, is as yet only the intuition or representation, and the determination that it is the shortest way between two points first makes up the _concept_ (as it appears, namely, in such definitions, see § 229). That the _concept_ is not already present in the _intuition_ is the difference between the two, and it is what brings on the demand for a definition. That this definition is analytic, however, is easily seen, since the straight line reduces to the simplicity of {{{ 20.247 }}} direction; but simplicity, taken in relation to _multitude_, yields the determination of the _least_ multitude, here of the shortest way.
:::
