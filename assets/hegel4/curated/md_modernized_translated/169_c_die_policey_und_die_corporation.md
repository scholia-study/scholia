## {{{ 20.506 }}} c. The Police and the Corporation

## {{ 533 }} § 533.

The administration of justice of itself excludes what belongs only to particularity in actions and interests, and leaves to contingency both the occurrence of crimes and regard for welfare. In civil society the _purpose_ is the satisfaction of need, and indeed at the same time of the human being, in a fixed universal way, that is, the _securing_ of this satisfaction. Within the mechanics of society's necessity, however, this satisfaction is exposed to contingency in the most manifold ways: through the changeability of the needs themselves, in which opinion and subjective liking have a large part, through the localities, the connections of a people with others, through errors and deceptions that can be introduced into individual parts of the whole machinery and are able to throw it into disorder, as also in particular through the conditioned capacity of the single one to acquire for oneself from that universal wealth. The course of that necessity gives up at the same time the particularities through which it is brought about, does not contain in itself the affirmative _purpose_ of securing the satisfaction of the _singulars_, but can be adequate or not in respect of them, and the singulars are here themselves the morally justified purpose.

## {{ 534 }} § 534.

The consciousness of the essential purpose, the acquaintance with the manner of working of the powers and changeable ingredients of which that necessity is composed, and the holding fast of that purpose in it and against it, stands, _on_ _the_ _one_ _hand_, to the concrete of civil society in the relation of an {{{ 20.507 }}} _external_ universality; as an active power this order is the external state, and the external state, to the extent that it has its root in the higher, substantial state, shows itself as state-_police_. _On_ _the_ _other_ _hand_, within this sphere of particularity the purpose of _substantial_ universality, together with its activation, stays confined to the business of particular branches and interests; – the _corporation_, where the particular citizen, as a private person, finds his or her wealth secured, and just as much leaves behind the single private interest and engages in conscious activity for a relatively universal purpose, and has his or her ethical life in the duties of right and of estate.
