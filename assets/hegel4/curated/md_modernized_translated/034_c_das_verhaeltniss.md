## c. The Relation

## {{ 135 }} § 135.

1) The _immediate_ relation is that of _whole_ and _parts_: the content is the whole and _consists_ of the parts (the form), its own opposite. The parts differ from one another, and are what has self-subsistence. They are parts, though, solely through their identical reference to each other, that is, to the extent that their joint taking makes up the whole. _The_ _togetherness_, however, is the opposite and the negation of the part.

## {{{ 20.160 }}} {{ 136 }} § 136.

2) The one and the same of this relation, the self-reference present in it, is accordingly immediately _negative_ self-reference; and it is so as the mediation in which one and the same is _indifferent_ to the difference, while also _being_ the _negative_ reference _to_ _itself_ that, as reflection-into-itself, repels itself into difference and posits itself as reflection-into-other having concrete existence, and in turn takes this reflection-into-other back into self-reference and indifference, – _force_ and its _expression_.

::: remark
{{ 136 A }} The _relation_ _of_ _the_ _whole_ _and_ _the_ _parts_ is the immediate, and so the thoughtless, relation, in which identity-with-itself flips over into diversity. One passes from the whole to the parts, and from the parts back to the whole, and the opposition to the one is forgotten in the other, since each is taken for itself, now the whole, now the parts, as self-subsistent concrete existence. Or, since the parts are meant to subsist _in_ the whole and the whole to consist _of_ the parts, now the first and now the second is the _subsistent_, and in each case the other of it is the _unessential_. The _mechanical_ relation consists, in its superficial form generally, in the parts' standing as self-subsistent against one another and against the whole.

The _progress_ _into_ _infinity_ that concerns the _divisibility_ of _matter_ can likewise draw on this relation, and then amounts to a thoughtless alternating between its two sides. Now a thing is taken as a _whole_, and one moves on to the _part-determination_; this is then forgotten, and what was part is viewed as whole; next the part-determination comes up once more, and so forth into infinity. This infinity, however, grasped as the negative that it is, is the _negative_ reference of the relation to itself, _force_, the whole identical with itself, as being-within-itself, – and {{{ 20.161 }}} as sublating this being-within-itself and expressing itself, and in reverse the expression that vanishes and returns into force.

For all this infinity, force is finite too; its content, the _one_ _and_ _the_ _same_ of force and expression, is this identity only _in_ _itself_ so far, and the two sides of the relation are not yet, each for itself, the concrete identity of the same, not yet the totality. Hence they are diverse relative to each other, and the relation is a _finite_ one. Force accordingly requires solicitation from without, works blindly, and, owing to this deficiency in form, its content too is limited and contingent. Content is not yet truly identical with form, not yet concept and purpose, which is what is determined in and for itself. – This difference is of the highest importance yet hard to grasp; it must first receive its closer determination in the concept of purpose itself. To overlook it is to fall into the confusion of conceiving God as force, from which _Herder's_ God, above all, suffers.

It is usual to say that the _nature_ of _force_ itself is _unknown_, and that only its expression is cognized. For one thing, the entire _content-determination_ of _force_ is just that of the expression, so that explaining an appearance by a force is an empty tautology. What is meant to stay unknown is therefore in fact nothing but the empty form of reflection-into-itself, by which alone force differs from expression, – a form that is itself quite familiar. This form contributes nothing at all to the content and the law, both of which are meant to be cognized solely from the appearance. Besides, it is everywhere assured that nothing is to be asserted about force by it; why, then, the form of force was brought into the sciences cannot be seen. – For another thing, the nature of force is indeed unknown, since what is still missing is the necessity of the connection of its content within itself, and of that content so far as it is limited for itself and so has its determinateness through an other outside it.
:::

## {{{ 20.162 }}} {{ 137 }} § 137.

Force, being the whole that in itself is negative reference to itself, consists in this: repelling itself from itself and _expressing_ itself. Yet because this reflection-into-other, the difference of the parts, is just as much reflection-into-itself, expression is the mediation through which force, which returns into itself, is force at all. Its expression is itself the sublation of the diversity between the two sides present in this relation, and the positing of the identity that makes up the content _in_ _itself_. Its truth is therefore the relation whose two sides differ only as _inner_ and _outer_.

## {{ 138 }} § 138.

3) The _inner_ is the ground in the way it is the bare form of the one _side_ of appearance and of the relation, the empty form of reflection-into-itself; opposite it stands concrete existence, equally the form of the relation's other side, with the empty determination of reflection-into-other, as the _outer_. Their identity is the filled one, the _content_, the _unity_ of reflection-into-itself and reflection-into-other, posited in force's movement; both are the same _one_ totality, and it is this unity that turns them into content.

## {{ 139 }} § 139.

Accordingly the outer is, _first_ _of_ _all_, _the_ _same_ _content_ as the inner. Whatever is inward is present outwardly as well, and the reverse; appearance shows nothing that is not in essence, and in essence there is nothing that has not been manifested.

## {{ 140 }} § 140.

_Secondly_. Inner and outer, however, are also _opposed_ to each other as determinations of form, and indeed absolutely, being the abstractions of identity with itself and of mere manifoldness or reality. Yet as {{{ 20.163 }}} moments of the one form they are essentially identical, so that whatever is posited at first _only_ in the one abstraction is _immediately_ also _only_ in the other. What is merely an _inward_ thing is therefore for that very reason _merely_ an _outward_ thing; and what is _merely_ an outward thing is likewise at first _merely_ an _inward_ one.

::: remark
{{ 140 A }} Reflection's usual error is _to_ take _essence_ as merely the _inner_. Where that is all it is taken for, the consideration itself is a wholly _external_ one, and the essence an empty external abstraction.

Of the _inner_ of nature a poet says:

No created spirit gets inside, and is too fortunate if it knows only the _outer_ shell.[^1]

What should rather have been said is that precisely when the essence of nature is determined for it as inner, it knows the _outer_ shell alone. – Since in _being_ generally, or in merely sensory perceiving too, the _concept_ is as yet only the inner, it stands to that as an outer, – a subjective being devoid of truth, no less than thinking. – In nature, as in spirit, where concept, purpose and law are as yet only _inner_ dispositions, mere possibilities, they are as yet only an external inorganic nature, a science possessed by a third party, an alien force, and so forth. – Human beings are inwardly what they are outwardly, that is, in their deeds (not, to be sure, in their merely bodily externality); and if they are _only_ inward, that is, _only_ in intentions and dispositions, virtuous, moral, and so forth, with their outer not identical to this, then the one is just as hollow and empty as the other.
:::

## {{ 141 }} § 141.

The empty abstractions, by which the one identical content is still meant to stand in the relation, cancel one another in the immediate passing over, each in the other; the content is itself none other than their identity (§ 138), {{{ 20.164 }}} they being the shine of essence posited as shine. Force's expression _posits_ the inner in concrete existence; and this _positing_ is a _mediating_ through empty abstractions; it vanishes within itself into _immediacy_, where the _inner_ and _outer_ are identical _in_ _and_ _for_ _themselves_ and their difference is determined as mere positedness. This identity is _actuality_.

[^1]: Compare _Goethe's_ Indignant Exclamation, in On Natural Science, volume 1, third part: I hear that repeated for sixty years. And I curse it, but covertly, – Nature has neither kernel nor shell, she is everything at one stroke, and so forth.
