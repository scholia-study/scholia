## More Precise Concept and Division of the Logic

## {{ 79 }} § 79.

Three sides belong to the _logical_ as regards its form: α) the side _of_ _abstraction_ _or_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_, β) the _dialectical_ side, or the _negatively-rational_, γ) the _speculative_, or _positively-rational_, side.

::: remark
{{ 79 A }} What these three sides yield is not three _parts_ of logic; each of them is rather a _moment_ in _everything_ _logically-real_, which is to say in every concept and in whatever is true at all. One may put all of them under the first moment, that of the _understanding_, and so hold them apart from one another, yet they are then not looked at as they truly are. – Both the indication of the logical's determinations given here and the division itself are likewise only anticipatory and historical in this place.
:::

## {{ 80 }} § 80.

α) Thinking in the guise of _understanding_ halts at the firm determinateness and at how this differs from other determinateness; a limited abstraction of this sort is, for the understanding, something that subsists for itself and that is.

## {{{ 20.119 }}} {{ 81 }} § 81.

β) What makes the moment _dialectical_ is that such finite determinations sublate themselves of their own accord and pass over into their opposite.

::: remark
{{ 81 A }} 1) Whenever the dialectical is separated off from the understanding and taken by itself, _skepticism_ results, above all where it is displayed in scientific concepts; skepticism retains nothing but negation as what the dialectical comes to. 2) People usually view dialectic as an art applied from outside, which arbitrarily muddles determinate concepts and conjures up in them a mere _shine_ of _contradictions_, with the upshot that what is null would be this shine rather than the determinations, while the understanding's way would, conversely, be the true one. Frequently dialectic is also no more than a subjective see-saw of ratiocination that swings to and fro, in which import is missing and the emptiness is hidden behind the very cleverness that produces such ratiocination. – Taken in its own determinateness, however, dialectic is the proper and true nature of the understanding's determinations, of things, and of the finite as such. Reflection starts out by moving beyond the isolated determinateness and relating it to something, so that it stands in a relation although its isolated validity is kept. Dialectic, on the other hand, is this _immanent_ moving-beyond, in the course of which the one-sidedness and limitedness of the understanding's determinations exhibit what they are, namely their negation. To sublate itself is what everything finite does. Hence the dialectical makes up the moving soul of scientific advance, and it is the one principle by which _immanent_ _coherence_ _and_ _necessity_ come into the content of a science, just as the genuine elevation above the finite, one that is not external, lies in it as well.
:::

## {{{ 20.120 }}} {{ 82 }} § 82.

γ) In the _speculative_, or _positively-rational_, what is taken up is the unity of the determinations within their opposition, that is, the _affirmative_ that lies in their dissolving and passing over.

::: remark
{{ 82 A }} 1) That dialectic yields a _positive_ result is owing to its having a _determinate_ _content_; put differently, what it truly ends in is not the _empty_, _abstract_ _nothing_ but the negation of _certain_ _determinations_, and these remain in the result for the very reason that it is no _immediate_ _nothing_ but a result. 2) Even though it is thought, and abstract as well, this rational element is accordingly also a _concrete_, since it is no _simple_, _formal_ unity but a _unity_ _of_ _differentiated_ _determinations_. For that reason philosophy is not at all concerned with bare abstractions or formal thoughts, only with concrete ones. 3) Speculative logic includes within itself the bare _logic_ _of_ _the_ _understanding_, and one can produce the latter from it directly, merely by dropping out the dialectical and rational; it then turns into what _ordinary_ _logic_ is, a _history_ of sundry determinations of thought lumped together, which, finite as they are, pass for something infinite.
:::

## {{ 83 }} § 83.

Logic falls into three parts:

+ I. Into _the_ _doctrine_ _of_ _being_.
+ II\. _The_ _doctrine_ _of_ _essence_.
+ III\. _The_ _doctrine_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ _and_ _idea_.

That is, into the doctrine of thought as it is:

+ I. In its _immediacy_, – the _concept_ _in_ _itself_.
+ II\. In its _reflection_ and _mediation_, – the _being-for-itself_ and _shine_ of the concept.
+ III\. In its _having-returned_ _into_ _itself_ and in _its_ _unfolded_ _abiding-with-itself_, – the concept _in_ and _for_ _itself_.
