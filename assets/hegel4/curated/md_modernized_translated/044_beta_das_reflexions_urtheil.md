## {{{ 20.188 }}} β) The Judgment of Reflection

## {{ 174 }} § 174.

Posited in the judgment, the singular _as_ singular (reflected into itself) has a predicate toward which the subject, in relating itself to itself, at once remains an _other_. – In _concrete_ _existence_ the subject is no longer immediately qualitative; it stands in _relation_ and _connection_ _with_ _an_ _other_, with an external world. Universality has thereby taken on the meaning of this relativity. (For instance useful, dangerous; gravity, acid, – then drive and so forth.)

## {{ 175 }} § 175.

1) The singular _as_ singular, as subject (in the _singular_ judgment), is a universal. 2) In this relation it is raised above its singularity. This extension is an external one, the subjective reflection, at first indeterminate _particularity_ (in the _particular_ judgment, which is immediately just as much negative as positive; – the singular is divided within itself, in part it relates itself to itself, in part to something else.) 3) Some are the universal: particularity is thereby extended to universality; or this universality, as determined through the subject's singularity, is _allness_ (communality, the common <i>reflective</i> universality).

## {{ 176 }} § 176.

Because the subject too is determined as universal, the identity of subject and predicate is _posited_, and with it the determination of judgment itself as indifferent. This unity of the _content_, being the universality identical with the subject's negative reflection-into-itself, makes the relation of judgment a _necessary_ one.
