## b. Self-Consciousness

## {{ 424 }} § 424.

_Self-consciousness_ is the truth of consciousness, and underlies it, so that in concrete existence every consciousness of another object is self-consciousness; I know the object as mine (it is my representation), and so know myself in it. – Self-consciousness finds its expression in I=I; – _abstract_ _freedom_, pure {{{ 20.428 }}} ideality. – In this form it lacks reality, since it, being its own _object_, is not truly one, there being no difference between it and itself.

## {{ 425 }} § 425.

As the _first_ negation of consciousness, abstract self-consciousness is also weighed down by an external object, formally by the negation of itself; it is thus the earlier stage, consciousness, at the same time, and so the contradiction of itself as self-consciousness with itself as consciousness. Because the latter, and negation generally, is already in itself sublated in I=I, as this certainty of itself over against the object it is the _drive_ to posit what it is in itself, – that is, to give content and objectivity to the abstract knowing of itself, and, conversely, to liberate itself from its sensibility by sublating the given objectivity and positing it as identical with itself; the two are one and the same; – it identifies its consciousness with its self-consciousness.
