## γ) Habit

## {{ 409 }} § 409.

Self-feeling, sunk into the particularity of feelings (of simple sensations, such as desires, drives, passions and their satisfactions), is undistinguished from them. The self, however, is in itself the simple relating of ideality to itself, a formal universality, and this universality is the truth of the particular; the self is to be posited in this life of feeling as that universality, and so it is a _universality_ {{{ 20.415 }}} _existing_ _for_ _itself_, which sets itself apart from particularity. This is not the substantive truth of the determinate sensations, desires and so forth, for the content of these does not yet come into consideration here. Particularity, so determined, is equally formal, and is merely the soul's _particular_ _being_ or immediacy as against its own formal, abstract being-for-itself. The moment of its _corporeality_ is this particular being of the soul; the soul here breaks with it, setting itself off from it as its _simple_ being, and is the ideal, subjective substantiality of that corporeality, whereas in its concept that is in itself (§ 389) it was merely the substance of it as such.

::: remark
{{ 409 A }} This abstract being-for-itself of the soul in its corporeality is not yet I, not the concrete existence of the universal that is for the universal. It is corporeality set back to its pure _ideality_, which thus pertains to the soul as such; that is to say, space and time, being the abstract being-outside-one-another, are as empty space and empty time merely subjective forms, pure intuiting, and in the same way that pure _being_ is the wholly pure, unconscious intuiting, being-for-itself because the particularity of corporeality, immediate corporeality as such, is sublated in it, yet at the same time the ground of consciousness; into consciousness it goes within itself once it has sublated within itself corporeality, whose subjective substance it is and which still confronts it, as restriction; and so it is posited for itself as subject.
:::

## {{ 410 }} § 410.

_Habit_ is the soul's making itself abstract universal being, and thereby reducing the particular of the feelings (of consciousness too) to a determination in it that merely _is_. In this way the content is in the soul's _possession_, and the soul holds it within itself so that in these {{{ 20.416 }}} determinations it is not sensing, neither relates to them by distinguishing itself from them nor is immersed in them, but holds them in itself devoid of sensation and consciousness and moves within them. To the extent that it takes no interest in them and is not occupied with them, it is _free_ of them; existing in these forms as its possession, – it is at once open for further activity and occupation, – that of sensation as well as that of the consciousness of spirit generally.

When what is particular or corporeal in the determinations of feeling is formed into the soul's _being_, this shows itself as their _repetition_, and the generation of habit shows itself as _practice_. For this being, as abstract universality in relation to the natural-particular that is posited into this form, is the universality of reflection (§ 175), – one and the same as the externally many of sensing reduced to its unity, this abstract unity as _posited_.

::: remark
{{ 410 A }} Like memory, habit is a difficult point in the organization of spirit; the mechanism of intelligence is memory, and that of self-feeling is habit. Age, sleeping and waking, with their _natural_ qualities and alterations, are natural immediately; habit, by contrast, is a determinateness of feeling (also of intelligence, will and so forth, so far as these belong to self-feeling) that has been turned into something naturally existent and mechanical. Calling it a second nature is justified: it is _nature_ since it is an immediate being of the soul, and it is a _second_ nature since the soul itself _posits_ this immediacy, an informing and permeating of corporeality, belonging to the determinations of feeling as such and, as embodied (§ 401), to the determinatenesses of representing and willing.

The human being is in habit in the mode of natural concrete existence, and therefore unfree in it, but free insofar as the natural determinateness of sensation is reduced through habit to _one's_ mere being, and one is no longer in difference and thereby no longer in interest, occupation and dependence with regard to it. The unfreedom in habit is in part only _formal_, as belonging only to the being of the soul; {{{ 20.417 }}} in part only _relative_, insofar as it properly takes place only with _bad_ habits, or insofar as another purpose is opposed to a habit generally; the habit of the right generally, of the ethical, has the content of freedom. – The essential determination is the _liberation_ that the human being gains through habit from the sensations by which one is affected. The different forms of it can be determined thus: α) The _immediate_ sensation as negated, posited as indifferent. To be _hardened_ against external sensations (frost, heat, tired limbs and so forth, pleasant taste and so forth), or the heart against misfortune, is a strength: though the human being does feel the frost and so forth, the misfortune, such affection is reduced merely to an externality and immediacy; the _universal_ being of the soul maintains itself as _abstract_ for itself therein, and self-feeling as such, consciousness, reflection, other purpose and activity, is no longer entangled with it. β) Indifference toward _satisfaction_; desires and drives are dulled by the _habit_ of satisfying them, and this is the rational way of being freed from them; monastic renunciation and violence does not liberate from them, nor is it rational in content; – it goes without saying that the drives, held according to their nature as finite determinatenesses, and they as well as their satisfaction, are subordinated as moments in the rationality of the will. – γ) In habit as _skill_, not only is the abstract being of the soul for itself to be held fast, but, as a subjective purpose, it is to be made valid in corporeality, which is to become subjected to it and wholly pervious. Against such inward determination of the subjective soul corporeality is determined as _immediate_ _external_ being and _restriction_; – the soul, as simple being-for-itself, breaks more determinately within itself against its first naturalness and immediacy; the soul is {{{ 20.418 }}} thereby no longer in first immediate identity, but must first, as external, be reduced to it. The embodiment of the determinate sensations is, further, itself a determinate one (§ 401), and immediate corporeality a _particular_ _possibility_ for a determinate purpose (– a particular side of its distinctness in it, one organ of its organic system among others). The forming of such a purpose into it is this, that the _in_ _itself_ being ideality of the material generally and of determinate corporeality has been _posited_ as ideality, so that the soul, according to the determinateness of its representing and willing, may _exist_ in it as substance. In this way, then, in skill corporeality is made thoroughly pervious and an instrument, such that, as soon as the representation (for instance a series of notes) is in me, the body has also, without resistance and fluidly, expressed it rightly.

The form of habit embraces all kinds and stages of the activity of spirit; the most external, the spatial determination of the individual, that it stands _upright_, has been made habit by its will, an _immediate_, _unconscious_ posture, which always remains the affair of its continuing will; one stands only because and insofar as one wills, and only as long as one wills it unconsciously. Likewise _seeing_ and so on is the concrete habit that unites _immediately_ the many determinations of sensation, of consciousness, of intuition, of the understanding and so forth in one simple act. The wholly free _thinking_, active in the pure element of itself, likewise needs habit and fluency, this form of _immediacy_, through which it is unhindered, pervaded property of my _singular_ _self_. Only through this habit do I _exist_ as thinking for myself. Even this immediacy of thinking being-with-itself contains corporeality (unaccustomedness and long continuation of thinking causes headache), habit lessens this sensation, in that it turns the natural determination into an immediacy of the soul. – Habit as developed and active in the spiritual as such, however, is _recollection_ and _memory_, to be treated further below.

{{{ 20.419 }}} Habit is wont to be spoken of disparagingly and taken as something lifeless, accidental and particular. Wholly accidental content is indeed capable of the form of habit, like any other, and it is the habit of life that brings on death, or, when wholly abstract, is death itself. At the same time, however, habit is what matters most for the _concrete_ _existence_ of any spirituality in the individual subject, so that the subject may be as _concrete_ immediacy, as _psychic_ ideality, so that the content, religious, moral and so forth, may _belong_ to it as to _this_ _self_, to it as to _this_ soul, and be neither in it merely _in_ _itself_ (as disposition), nor as a transient sensation or representation, nor as an abstract inwardness cut off from doing and actuality, but in its being. – In scientific considerations of the soul and of spirit, habit is wont either to be passed over as something contemptible, or, rather, also because it is among the hardest determinations.
:::
