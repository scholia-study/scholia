## AA. The Family

## {{ 518 }} § 518.

Taken in its _immediacy_, ethical spirit holds a _natural_ moment: the individual's substantial existence lies in the individual's natural universality, the _genus_, – the sexual relation, yet lifted {{{ 20.497 }}} into spiritual determination; – the oneness of love and the disposition of confidence; – spirit as family is _sentient_ spirit.

## {{ 519 }} § 519.

1) The natural sexes differ, and this difference shows itself as well as one of intellectual and ethical determination. In their exclusive singularity these personalities join to form _one_ _person_; once subjective inwardness has been determined into substantial unity, this joining becomes an _ethical_ relationship, – _marriage_. Substantial inwardness makes marriage into an undivided bond of the persons, – into _monogamous_ marriage; the bodily union is a consequence of the ethically tied bond. What follows further is the sharing of personal and particular interests.

## {{ 520 }} § 520.

2) The _property_ of the family as one person receives an _ethical_ interest through the community in which the various individuals who make up the family likewise stand in relation to it, as in acquisition, labor and provision.

## {{ 521 }} § 521.

The ethical life connected with the natural generation of the children, at first posited as original (§ 519) in the contracting of marriage, realizes itself in the second birth of the children, the spiritual one, – their education to self-subsistent persons.

## {{ 522 }} § 522.

3) By this self-subsistence the children leave the concrete liveliness of the family they originally belong to; having become _for_ _themselves_ {{{ 20.498 }}}, however, they are destined to found a new actual family of the same kind. Marriage moves toward dissolution essentially through the _natural_ moment contained in it, the death of the spouses; but the inwardness too, as merely sentient substantiality, is in itself subject to chance and transience. Following such contingency, the members of the family enter into the relation of persons against one another, and only with this do _legal_ determinations, which are in themselves foreign to this bond, enter into it.
