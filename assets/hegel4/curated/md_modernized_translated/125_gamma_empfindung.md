## γ) Sensation

## {{ 399 }} § 399.

Sleeping and waking are at first, to be sure, not mere alterations but _alternating_ states, (progress into infinity). In this their formal negative relation the _affirmative_ is just as much present. In the being-for-itself of the waking soul being is contained as an ideal moment; thus it _finds_, _within_ _itself_ and indeed for itself, the determinatenesses of content of its sleeping nature, which are _in_ _itself_ in it, as being in its substance. As determinateness this particular is distinguished from the identity of being-for-itself with itself and at the same time contained simply in its simplicity, – _sensation_.

## {{ 400 }} § 400.

Sensation is the form of the dull weaving of spirit in its individuality devoid of consciousness and understanding, in which _all_ determinateness is still _immediate_, posited as undeveloped, in respect of its content as well as of the opposition of an objective to the subject, as belonging to its most _particular_, natural {{{ 20.397 }}} _peculiarity_. The content of sensing is for that very reason _limited_ and transient, because it belongs to natural, immediate being, the qualitative and thus finite.

::: remark
{{ 400 A }} _Everything_ _is_ _in_ _sensation_, and, if one will, everything that emerges in spiritual consciousness and in reason has its _source_ and _origin_ in it; for source and origin mean nothing other than the first, most immediate way in which something appears. It is said to be not enough that principles, religion and so forth be only in the head; they must be in the heart, in _sensation_. In truth, what one has thus in the head is in consciousness generally, and the content is so _object-like_ to it that, just as much as it is posited in Me, the abstract I, it can also be held away from Me according to my concrete subjectivity; in sensation, by contrast, such content is a determinateness of my whole being-for-itself, albeit a dull one in such a form; it is thus posited as what is _most_ _my_ _own_. One's own is what has not been split off from the actual, concrete I; and the immediate unity of the soul with its substance and with that substance's determinate content is precisely this non-separation, to the extent that it has not been determined as the I of consciousness, still less as the freedom of rational spirituality. That will, conscience and character, moreover, possess a wholly other intensity and firmness of _being-my-own_ than sensation generally and the complex of sensations, the _heart_, lies also in ordinary representations. – It is right enough, to be sure, that the _heart_ above all must be _good_. But that sensation and the heart are not the form by which something is _justified_ as religious, ethical, true, just and so forth, and that the appeal to heart and sensation either says nothing or rather says something bad, should not need to be recalled in its own right. There can be no more trivial experience than that there are at least equally evil, bad, godless, base and so forth sensations and hearts; indeed, that only such content {{{ 20.398 }}} comes from hearts is pronounced in the words: From the _heart_ come forth evil thoughts, murder, adultery, fornication, blasphemy and so forth. In times when heart and sensation are made the criterion of the good, the ethical and the religious by scientific theology and philosophy, – it becomes necessary to recall that trivial experience, just as it is necessary nowadays, too, to remind generally that _thinking_ is the _most_ _distinctive_ trait whereby human beings distinguish themselves from cattle, and that they have sensing in common with them.
:::

## {{ 401 }} § 401.

What the sensing soul finds in itself is, on the one hand, the natural immediate, as made ideal in it and its own. On the other hand, conversely, what originally belongs to being-for-itself, that is, as it, sunk further into itself, is the I of consciousness and free spirit, is determined as natural _corporeality_ and thus sensed. According to this a sphere of sensing is distinguished that is at first a determination of corporeality (of the eye and so forth, generally of every bodily part), which becomes sensation by being made _inward_, _recollected_, in the being-for-itself of the soul, – and another sphere of determinatenesses that have arisen in spirit and belong to it, which, in order to be found, to be sensed, are _embodied_. Thus the determinateness is posited in the subject as in the soul. As the further {{{ 20.399 }}} specification of that sensing lies before us in the system of the senses, so too the determinatenesses of sensing that come from within systematize themselves necessarily, and their embodiment, as posited in living, concretely developed naturalness, is carried out according to the _particular_ content of the spiritual determination in a _particular_ system or organ of the body.

::: remark
{{ 401 A }} Sensing generally is the healthy living along of individual spirit in its corporeality. The senses are the simple system of specified corporeality; a) the physical _ideality_ falls into two, because in it, as immediate, not yet subjective ideality, the difference appears as _diversity_, the senses of determinate _light_ (compare §§ 317 ff.) and of _sound_ (§ 300). b) The different reality is at once doubled for itself, – the senses of smell and taste (§§ 321, 322); c) finally the sense of what is solidly real, of heavy matter, of heat (§ 303) and of shape (§ 310). These specifications group themselves around the center of the sensing individual in a simpler fashion than they do in the development of natural corporeality.

The _system_ of inner sensing, in its embodying _particularization_, would be worthy of being carried out and treated in a peculiar science, – _a_ _psychic_ _physiology_. Something of a relation of this kind is already contained in the feeling of the adequacy or inadequacy of an immediate sensation to the sensuous _inner_ that is determinate for itself, – the _agreeable_ or _disagreeable_; as also in the _determinate_ comparison in the _symbolizing_ of sensations, for instance of colors, tones, smells and so forth. But it would be the most interesting side of a psychic physiology to consider, not {{{ 20.400 }}} mere sympathy but, more determinately, the _embodiment_ that spiritual determinations give themselves, in particular as _affects_. The connection would have to be comprehended through which anger and courage are felt in the chest, in the blood, in the system of irritability, as reflective thinking and spiritual occupation are felt in the head, the center of the system of sensibility. A more thorough understanding than hitherto would have to be gained of the best-known connections through which tears, voice in general, more closely speech, laughter, sighing, and then many other particularizations are formed from the soul outward, which lie toward the pathognomic and physiognomic. The viscera and organs are considered in physiology only as moments of the animal organism, but at the same time they form a system of the embodiment of the spiritual, and thereby acquire a wholly different significance.
:::

## {{ 402 }} § 402.

On account of their immediacy and of their being found, sensations are _singular_ and _transient_ determinations, alterations in the substantiality of the soul, posited in its being-for-itself, which is identical with it. But this being-for-itself is not merely a formal moment of sensing; the soul is the totality of it, reflected in itself – a sensing, within itself, of the total substantiality that it is _in_ _itself_, – _feeling_ soul.

::: remark
{{ 402 A }} For sensation and feeling, linguistic usage does not precisely supply a thoroughgoing difference; yet one does not readily say sensation of right, self-sensation and the like, but feeling of right, self-feeling; with sensation is connected sensitivity; one may therefore hold that sensation emphasizes more the side of passivity, of _finding_, that is, of the immediacy of determinateness in feeling, while feeling at the same time goes more to the self-likeness that is in it.
:::
