## a. Pure Quantity

## {{ 99 }} § 99.

_Quantity_ is pure being, in which determinateness has ceased to be posited as one with being itself and stands instead as _sublated_, or _indifferent_.

::: remark
{{ 99 A }} 1) For quantity the term _magnitude_ does not fit well, since it mainly names _determinate_ _quantity_. 2) Mathematics tends to define magnitude as whatever admits of _increase_ or _decrease_; this definition is faulty in that the very thing to be defined recurs inside it, yet it does express that the determination of magnitude is posited as _changeable_ and _indifferent_: the thing at issue, a house or red for example, remains, whatever alteration its extension or intension undergoes, {{{ 20.136 }}} a house or red. 3) To say that the absolute is pure quantity is a standpoint that largely meets the one giving the absolute the determination of _matter_, a matter in which form is present yet only as an indifferent determination. Quantity likewise forms the basic determination of the absolute where this is conceived so that all difference within the absolutely indifferent is merely quantitative. – Pure space, time and so forth may otherwise serve as examples of quantity, where the real is to be taken as an _indifferent_ filling of space or time.
:::

## {{ 100 }} § 100.

Quantity, taken first in its immediate self-relation, or in the determination of self-equality that attraction posits, is _continuous_ magnitude, – whereas in the other determination it contains, that of the _one_, it is _discrete_ magnitude. But the former is just as much discrete, since it is the continuity only _of_ _the_ _many_; and the latter just as much continuous, its continuity being the one as the _same_ in the many ones, _the_ _unity_.

::: remark
{{ 100 A }} 1) Continuous and discrete magnitude are therefore not to be taken as _species_, as if one lacked a determination the other has; they differ merely in that _the_ _same_ _whole_ is posited now under the one of its determinations, now under the other. 2) The antinomy of space, time or matter, concerning whether they are divisible into infinity or consist of indivisibles, amounts to nothing but asserting quantity once as continuous, once as discrete. Posit space, time and so forth solely with the determination of continuous quantity, and they are _divisible_ into _infinity_; posit them with that of discrete magnitude, and they are in themselves _divided_, consisting of indivisible ones; each view is as one-sided as the other.
:::
