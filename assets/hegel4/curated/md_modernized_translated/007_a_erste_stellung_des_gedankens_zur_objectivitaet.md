## A. First Position of Thought with Respect to Objectivity

## {{ 26 }} § 26.

The first position is the _naive_ procedure, which, still without consciousness of the opposition of thinking in and against itself, contains the _faith_ that the _truth_ is _cognized_ through _reflective_ _thinking_ and what the objects truly are is brought before consciousness. In this faith thinking goes straight to the objects, reproduces out of itself the content of sensations and intuitions into a content of thought {{{ 20.70 }}} and is satisfied in this as in the truth. All initial philosophy, all the sciences, even the daily doing and driving of consciousness, live in this faith.

## {{ 27 }} § 27.

Because of its unconsciousness about its opposition, this thinking _can_ be just as much genuine _speculative_ philosophizing as to its import as it can dwell in _finite_ determinations of thought, that is, in the _still_ _unresolved_ opposition. Here in the introduction the interest can only be to consider this position of thinking according to its limit, and therefore to take up first the latter kind of _philosophizing_. – This, in its most determinate formation and the one nearest to us, was the _former_ _metaphysics_, as it stood among us before the Kantian philosophy. This metaphysics, however, is something _former_ only in relation to the history of philosophy; in itself it is always present, the _mere_ _view_ _of_ _understanding_ of the objects of reason. The closer consideration of its manner and its main content therefore has at the same time this closer present interest.

## {{ 28 }} § 28.

This science considered the determinations of thought as the _basic_ _determinations_ _of_ _things_; by the presupposition that what _is_ is cognized _in_ _itself_ by being _thought_, it stood higher than the later critical philosophizing. But 1) those determinations were taken in their abstraction as valid on their own and as fit to serve as _predicates_ _of_ _the_ _true_. That metaphysics assumed generally that the absolute could be cognized in such a way that _predicates_ _are_ _ascribed_ _to_ _it_, and it examined neither the determinations of the understanding with regard to their own {{{ 20.71 }}} content and worth, nor this very form of determining the absolute by ascribing predicates.

::: remark
{{ 28 A }} Such predicates are, for instance, _existence_, as in the proposition: _God_ _has_ _existence_; _finitude_ or _infinity_, in the question whether the world is finite or infinite; _simple_, _composite_, in the proposition: the soul is _simple_; – further, the thing is _one_, a _whole_ and so forth. – It was not investigated whether such predicates are anything true in and for themselves, nor whether the form of judgment could be a form of truth.
:::

## {{ 29 }} § 29.

Such predicates are in themselves a _limited_ content, and already show themselves as not adequate to the _fullness_ of _representation_ (of God, nature, spirit and so forth) and as by no means exhausting it. Further, by being predicates of one subject they are connected with one another, but different by their content, so that they are taken up _against_ _one_ _another_ from _outside_.

::: remark
{{ 29 A }} The Orientals sought to remedy the first defect, for instance in determining God, through the many _names_ they attributed to him; but at the same time the names were to be _infinitely_ many.
:::

## {{ 30 }} § 30.

2) Their _objects_ were indeed totalities, which in and for themselves fall to _reason_, that is, to the thought of the universal that is _concrete_ in itself; – _soul_, _world_, _God_, – but metaphysics took them up from _representation_, laid them down as _ready-made_ _given_ _subjects_ at the basis of the application of the determinations of the understanding to them, and had in that representation alone the _standard_ of whether the predicates were fitting and sufficient or not.

## {{ 31 }} § 31.

The representations of soul, world, God seem at first to afford thinking a _firm_ _hold_. But besides the fact that the character {{{ 20.72 }}} of particular subjectivity is mixed into them, and that accordingly they can have very different meanings, they rather first need to receive their firm determination through thinking. Every proposition expresses this, in that it is by the _predicate_ (that is, in philosophy, by the determination of thought) that _what_ the subject, that is, the initial representation, is, is first to be stated.

::: remark
{{ 31 A }} In the proposition: God _is_ eternal and so forth, one starts out from the representation: God; but what he _is_ is still not _known_; the predicate alone says what he _is_. In logic, therefore, where the content is determined solely in the form of thought, it would be not only superfluous to make these determinations predicates of propositions whose _subject_ would be God or the vaguer absolute, but it would also have the disadvantage of calling to mind a standard other than the very nature of thought. – The form of the proposition, or more exactly of the judgment, is in any case ill suited to expressing the concrete – and what is true is concrete – and the speculative; by virtue of its form the judgment is partial and so far false.
:::

## {{ 32 }} § 32.

3) This metaphysics became _dogmatism_, because, finite determinations being what they are, it had to take it that of _two_ _opposed_ _assertions_, of the kind those propositions were, one must be _true_ and the other _false_.

## {{ 33 }} § 33.

The _first_ _part_ of this metaphysics in its ordered shape was constituted by _ontology_ – the doctrine of the _abstract_ _determinations_ _of_ _essence_. For these, in their manifoldness and finite validity, a principle is lacking; they must therefore be enumerated _empirically_ and _contingently_, and their closer _content_ can be founded only on _representation_, on the _assurance_ that one thinks precisely this by a word, perhaps also on etymology. What is at stake can only be the _correctness_ of the analysis, in agreement with linguistic usage, and empirical {{{ 20.73 }}} _completeness_, not the _truth_ and _necessity_ of such determinations in and for themselves.

::: remark
{{ 33 A }} The question whether being, existence, or finitude, simplicity, composition and so forth are _true_ _concepts_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _themselves_ must seem striking if one supposes that only the truth of _a_ _proposition_ can be at issue and that the only question can be whether a _concept_ can be truly _attributed_ _to_ _a_ _subject_ (as it was called) or not; untruth would depend on the contradiction found between the subject of representation and the concept that is to be asserted of it. Yet the concept, being concrete, and indeed every determinateness whatever, is essentially in itself a unity of distinct determinations. If truth were therefore nothing more than the absence of contradiction, one would have to consider first, in the case of every concept, whether it does not contain such an inner contradiction in itself.
:::

## {{ 34 }} § 34.

The _second_ _part_ was _rational_ _psychology_ or _pneumatology_, which concerns the metaphysical nature of the _soul_, namely of spirit, as a _thing_.

::: remark
{{ 34 A }} Immortality was sought in the sphere where _composition_, _time_, _qualitative_ _alteration_, _quantitative_ _increase_ or _decrease_ have their place.
:::

## {{ 35 }} § 35.

The _third_ _part_, _cosmology_, dealt with the _world_, its contingency, necessity, eternity, its being bounded in space and time; the formal laws in its alterations, further the freedom of the human being, and the origin of evil.

::: remark
{{ 35 A }} Held here as absolute oppositions are chiefly: contingency and necessity; external and internal necessity; efficient and final causes, or causality in general and purpose; essence or substance and appearance; form and matter; freedom and necessity; happiness and pain; good and evil.
:::

## {{{ 20.74 }}} {{ 36 }} § 36.

The _fourth_ _part_, _natural_ or _rational_ _theology_, examined the concept of God, or its possibility, the proofs for his existence, and his properties.

::: remark
{{ 36 A }} a) In this consideration of God by the understanding what matters chiefly is which predicates fit or do not fit what _we_ _represent_ _to_ _ourselves_ under God. The opposition of reality and negation occurs here as absolute; hence for the _concept_, as the understanding takes it, there remains in the end only the empty abstraction of the indeterminate _essence_, of sheer reality or positivity, the lifeless product of the modern Enlightenment. b) The _proving_ of finite cognition shows in general the perverted position that an objective ground of God's being is to be given, which thus presents itself as _something_ _mediated_ through another. This proving, which has the identity of the understanding as its rule, is entangled in the difficulty of crossing over from the _finite_ to the _infinite_. Thus it could either not free God from the existent world's finitude, which remained positive, so that he had to be determined as its immediate substance (pantheism); – or he remained as an object over against the subject, and so in this way a _finite_ (dualism). c) The _properties_, since they are supposed to be determinate and different, have really sunk away in the abstract concept of pure reality, of the indeterminate essence. But insofar as the finite world still remains in representation as a _true_ being, and God over against it, there also arises the representation of various relations of him to it, which, determined as properties, must on the one hand be relations to finite states, themselves of a finite kind (for instance just, kind, powerful, wise and so forth), but on the other hand are supposed at the same time to be infinite. This contradiction admits on this standpoint only the nebulous resolution, by quantitative heightening, of driving them into the indeterminate, into the sensum eminentiorem. Through this, however, the property is in fact annihilated and only a name is left to it.
:::
