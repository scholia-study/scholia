## {{{ 20.154 }}} c. The Thing

## {{ 125 }} § 125.

The _thing_ is totality: it is the development of the determinations of ground and of concrete existence, posited in one. By one of its moments, _reflection-into-other_, it carries the differences within it, and so it is a _determinate_, concrete thing. α) These determinations are _diverse_ _from_ _one_ _another_; their reflection-into-itself belongs to the thing, not to them themselves. They count as _properties_ of the thing, their relating to it being _having_.

::: remark
{{ 125 A }} _Having_ steps in as relation in place of _being_. Something, it is true, also has _qualities_ on it; yet to carry having over to what is, is imprecise, since determinateness as quality is immediately one with the something, so that the something _ceases_ _to_ _be_ once its quality is lost. Not so the _thing_: it is reflection-into-itself, an identity which is distinguished as well from difference, that is, from its own determinations. – Many languages draw on _having_ to mark the _past_, – and rightly, for the past is _sublated_ _being_, and the reflection-into-itself of this being is spirit, in which alone it still persists, while spirit at the same time sets this being, sublated within it, apart from itself.
:::

## {{ 126 }} § 126.

β) Yet reflection-into-other is, in the _ground_, directly on its own account reflection-into-itself as well; so the properties are equally identical with themselves, _self-subsistent_, released from their being bound to the thing. Since, however, they are the determinatenesses of the thing that are _distinguished_ _from_ _one_ _another_ and reflected into themselves, they are not things (these being concrete) but concrete existences reflected into themselves as abstract determinatenesses: _matters_.

::: remark
{{{ 20.155 }}} {{ 126 A }} Matters, magnetic and electric ones for instance, are likewise not called _things_. – What they are is the genuine qualities, at one with their being, determinateness that has reached immediacy, though the being in question is a reflected one, concrete existence.
:::

## {{ 127 }} § 127.

_Matter_ is thus the _abstract_ or indeterminate reflection-into-_other_, or reflection-into-itself at once as _determinate_; it is therefore the _existent_ _thinghood_, the subsistence of the thing. In this way the thing has its reflection-into-itself in the matters (the opposite of § 125), consists not in itself but _of_ _the_ _matters_, and is merely their superficial connection, an external linking of them.

## {{ 128 }} § 128.

γ) Matter, being the _immediate_ _unity_ of concrete existence with itself, is also indifferent to determinateness; hence the many diverse matters coalesce into the _one_ _matter_, which is concrete existence in the determination of reflection of _identity_; over against it stand these distinct determinatenesses together with their external _relation_ to one another in the thing: they are the _form_, – the determination of reflection of _difference_, but as existing and as totality.

::: remark
{{ 128 A }} This one matter without determination is the same as the thing-in-itself too; the difference is that the latter is wholly abstract in itself, whereas the former is in itself for-other as well, being in the first place for the form.
:::

## {{ 129 }} § 129.

The thing thus splits into _matter_ _and_ _form_; each of the two is the _totality_ of thinghood and is self-subsistent for itself. _Matter_, however, is meant to be the positive, {{{ 20.156 }}} indeterminate concrete existence, yet being concrete existence it holds reflection-into-other just as much as being-within-itself, and in uniting these determinations it is itself the totality of form. Form, for its part, as the totality of determinations already holds reflection-into-itself; _as_ _form_ _that_ _relates_ _itself_ _to_ _itself_ it has just what was to constitute matter's determination. _In-itself_ the two are the same. That their unity is _posited_ amounts, generally, to the _relation_ of matter and form, which are likewise distinct.

## {{ 130 }} § 130.

As this totality the thing is a contradiction: according to its negative unity it is the _form_ in which matter is determined and reduced to _properties_ (§ 125), and yet at the same time it _consists_ _of_ _matters_ which, within the thing's reflection-into-itself, are as self-subsistent as they are negated. Being thus essential concrete existence that sublates itself within itself, the thing is _appearance_.

::: remark
{{ 130 A }} The _negation_ equally _posited_ in the thing, as the self-subsistence of the matters, occurs in physics as _porosity_. Every one of the many matters (coloring material, odorous material, other materials, and by some accounts sound-material too, then of course heat-material, electric matter and the rest) is _also_ _negated_; in this negation of it, its pores, the many other self-subsistent matters have their place, themselves porous, and so in their turn allowing the others to exist reciprocally within them. The pores are nothing _empirical_ but inventions of the understanding, which thus represents the moment of negation in the self-subsistent matters, and screens the further unfolding of the contradictions behind that nebulous confusion in which all are _self-subsistent_ and all equally _negated_ in one another. – Where faculties or activities are hypostasized in the same way in spirit, their living unity turns equally into a confusion of one acting upon another.

{{{ 20.157 }}} Just as the pores (it is not the pores of organic things, of wood or skin, that are meant, but [those] of the so-called matters, such as coloring material, heat-material and the rest, or of metals, crystals and suchlike) find no confirmation in observation, so matter itself, further a form separated from it, and in the first instance the thing and its consisting of matters, or its subsisting itself while merely having properties, are the product of the reflecting understanding; in observing, and professing to state what it observes, it produces rather a metaphysics that is contradiction on every side, though one that stays hidden from it.
:::
