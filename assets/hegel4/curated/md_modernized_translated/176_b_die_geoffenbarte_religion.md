## B. Revealed Religion

## {{ 564 }} § 564.

That it be _revealed_, and revealed _by_ _God_ at that, belongs essentially to the concept of true religion, that is, of the religion whose content is absolute spirit. For knowing – the principle by which substance is spirit – is, as the infinite form existing for itself, _self-determining_, and so is utterly _manifesting_; spirit is spirit only in so far as it is _for_ spirit, and in the absolute religion it is absolute spirit that manifests, no longer abstract moments of itself, but itself.

::: remark
{{ 564 A }} The ancient representation of Nemesis grasped the divine and its efficacy in the world, with the still abstract understanding, solely as a _levelling_ power that smashes whatever is high and great; against it _Plato_ and _Aristotle_ maintained that God is not _envious_. One may likewise set this against the recent assurances that the human being is unable to cognize God; – these {{{ 20.550 }}} assurances (for assertions is all they are) are the more inconsistent when made within a religion that expressly calls itself the _revealed_ one: by those assurances it would be, instead, the religion in which _nothing_ of God was _revealed_ and in which he had _not_ revealed himself, and its adherents would be “the heathen,” “who know nothing of God.” If the word God is meant in earnest in religion at all, then the determination may and must start from him too, as religion's content and principle, and were self-revelation denied him, nothing would remain to be said of any content of his except to attribute _envy_ to him. And if the word _spirit_ is to have a sense at all, then revealing itself is contained in it.

Bear in mind how hard it is to cognize God as spirit – cognition that [can] no longer rest content with the plain representations of faith but moves on to thinking, first to the reflecting understanding, though it should move on to comprehending thinking – and it is hardly any wonder that so many, theologians especially, who are more directly called on to deal with these ideas, have hit upon an easier way of getting clear of them, and have eagerly taken up what was offered them to that end; easiest of all is the result stated: that the human being knows nothing of God. Thorough speculation is needed to grasp rightly and determinately in thought what God is as spirit. What it first contains are the propositions: only in so far as God knows himself is he God; his self-knowing is, moreover, his self-consciousness in the human being, and the human being's knowing _of_ God, which proceeds to the human being's self-knowing in God. – The thorough explanation of these propositions is to be found in the work they are taken from: _Aphorisms_ _on_ _Knowing_ _and_ _Not-knowing_ _and_ _so_ _forth_ _by_ _C._ _F._ _G....l\._ _Berlin._ _1829._
:::

## {{{ 20.551 }}} {{ 565 }} § 565.

In the sublated immediacy and sensuousness of shape and knowing, absolute spirit is, in content, the spirit of nature and of spirit that is in and for itself; in form it is, to begin with, for subjective knowing, that of _representation_. On one side, representation lends the moments of its content self-subsistence and turns them into presuppositions for one another, into _successive_ appearances, and into a connection of _happening_ according to _finite_ _determinations_ _of_ _reflection_; on the other side, such a form of the finite manner of representing is itself sublated too, by faith in the one spirit and by the devotion of the cult.

## {{ 566 }} § 566.

In this separating, _form_ and _content_ part company, and within the form the distinct moments of the concept divide into _particular_ _spheres_, or elements, in each of which the absolute content presents itself: α) as eternal content that stays with itself in its manifestation; β) as the eternal essence's differentiation from its manifestation, which by this difference becomes the world of appearance that the content enters; γ) as infinite return, the reconciliation of the externalized world with the eternal essence, which goes back out of appearance into the unity of its fullness.

## {{ 567 }} § 567.

α) In the moment of _universality_, which is pure _thought_'s sphere, the abstract element of _essence_, absolute spirit is accordingly, first, the _presupposed_ – not one that stays shut up, however, but, as _substantial_ _power_ in the reflection-determination of causality, _creator_ of heaven and earth – yet in this eternal sphere it rather {{{ 20.552 }}} begets only _itself_ as its _son_; it remains, just as much, in original identity with this differentiated one, for this determination, that of being differentiated from the universal essence, sublates itself eternally, and through this mediation of the self-sublating mediation the first substance is, essentially, _concrete_ _singularity_ and subjectivity, – _spirit_.

## {{ 568 }} § 568.

β) In the moment of _particularity_, however, which is that of judgment, it is this concrete eternal essence that stands _presupposed_, and the movement it makes [is] _appearance_'s creation: the eternal moment of mediation, the one son, falls apart into the self-subsistent opposition between heaven and earth, elemental and concrete nature, on one side, and spirit, standing in _relation_ to nature, on the other, hence _finite_ spirit; this spirit, the extreme of negativity that exists in itself, makes itself self-subsistent into evil; it is such an extreme by its relation to a nature standing over against it and by its own naturalness, posited along with that relation, and within this naturalness, as thinking, it is at once directed toward the eternal, yet stands thereby in external relation.

## {{ 569 }} § 569.

γ) In the moment of _singularity_ as such, namely of subjectivity and of the concept itself, in which universality and particularity, as opposed, have returned into their _identical_ _ground_, there presents itself 1) as _presupposition_, the _universal_ substance, actualized out of its abstraction into _single_ self-consciousness, which, being _immediately_ _identical_ with the essence, places that _son_ of the eternal sphere into temporality and exhibits in him evil as sublated _in_ _itself_; but further, this immediate and so sensuous concrete existence of the absolutely concrete puts itself into the judgment and dies away into the pain of _negativity_; in that negativity, as infinite subjectivity identical with itself, it has become _for_ _itself_ as _absolute_ _return_ and universal unity of the universal and the single essentiality, – the idea of spirit as eternal yet _living_, and present in the world.

## {{{ 20.553 }}} {{ 570 }} § 570.

2) For the _finite_ immediacy of the single subject, this objective totality is the _presupposition_ that exists in itself; to that subject it is therefore at first an _other_ and something _intuited_, though the intuition of the truth that is _in_ _itself_, and by this witness of spirit within it the subject, owing to its immediate nature, first determines itself for itself as the nugatory and the evil; and further, following the example of its truth, by way of faith in the unity of universal and single essentiality accomplished therein _in_ _itself_, it is also the movement of externalizing its immediate natural determinateness and its own will, of closing together with that example and its _in-itself_ in the pain of negativity, and thus of knowing itself as united with the essence, which 3) by this mediation brings itself about as dwelling in self-consciousness, and is the spirit's actual presence – the spirit that is in and for itself – as the universal.

## {{ 571 }} § 571.

The three syllogisms that together make up the one syllogism of spirit's absolute mediation with itself are spirit's revelation, which explicates its life in the cycle of concrete shapes of representation. As these shapes come apart and follow on one another in time and externality, the unfolding of the mediation gathers itself up, in its result, the closing-together of spirit with itself, not merely into the simplicity of faith and of devotion of feeling but also into _thinking_, in whose immanent simplicity the unfolding likewise finds its extension, though known as an inseparable connection, within itself, of the universal, simple and eternal spirit. Truth, in this form, is the object of _philosophy_.

::: remark
{{{ 20.554 }}} {{ 571 A }} If the result – the spirit _for_ _itself_, wherein every mediation has sublated itself – is understood in a merely _formal_, contentless sense, so that spirit is not at the same time known as _in_ _itself_ and as unfolding itself objectively, that infinite subjectivity is nothing but formal self-consciousness, knowing itself within itself as absolute, – _irony_; it knows how to reduce all objective import to nothing, to something _vain_, and is thus itself the lack of import and the vanity that gives itself, from out of itself, a contingent and arbitrary content as its determination, remains master of it, is not bound by it, and, assuring us that it stands on the highest peak of religion and philosophy, falls back rather into hollow arbitrary will. Only by laying aside the one-sidedness of the subjective, in which it is the vanity of thinking, is the pure infinite form, self-manifestation that is with itself, free thinking; such thinking has its infinite determination at once as absolute content, in and for itself, and has that content as its object, in which it is equally free. Thinking is to that extent itself only the formal side of the absolute content.
:::
