## {{{ 20.215 }}} C. The Idea

## {{ 213 }} § 213.

What is true _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ is the idea, _the_ _concept's_ _absolute_ _unity_ _with_ _objectivity_. The concept in its determinations is all that its ideal content consists of; its real content is merely the concept's presentation of itself, given by the concept in the form of external existence, so that, with this shape enclosed in the concept's ideality, in its power, the concept maintains itself in that shape.

::: remark
{{ 213 A }} To define _the_ _absolute_ as the _idea_ is by now itself an absolute definition. Every earlier definition goes back into it. – Truth is the _idea_; for truth means that objectivity answers to the concept, – and not that external things answer to my representations; such representations are merely _correct_ ones, held by _me_, this individual. The idea is not concerned with this individual, nor with representations, nor with external things. – Yet _everything_ actual, too, is the idea so far as it is true, and its truth is wholly owed to the idea and its force. Singular being is some side of the idea; so it needs further actualities, which likewise appear as subsisting on their own in particular, and the concept is realized solely in these together and in their relation. By itself the singular fails to correspond to its concept; this limitation of its existence is what constitutes its _finitude_ and its perishing.

One must not take the idea itself as the idea _of_ _some_ _thing_, any more than the concept as merely a determinate concept. As the universal and One idea, the absolute _judges_ and thus particularizes itself into the _system_ of determinate ideas, whose whole significance, however, is to return into the One idea, into their truth. What follows from this judgment is that the idea is _at_ _first_ just the One universal _substance_, whereas its developed, true actuality consists in its being _subject_, and thereby spirit.

{{{ 20.216 }}} Often the idea is held to be something merely formal and logical, on the ground that it has no _existence_ as its starting point and support. Such an opinion should be left to those standpoints from which the existing thing, and every further determination that has not yet broken through to the idea, still passes for a so-called _reality_ and genuine _actuality_. – No less mistaken is the notion that the idea is only the _abstract_. It is that, to be sure, to the extent that all _untruth_ is consumed within it; but in itself it is essentially _concrete_, being the concept that is free, determines itself and so determines itself to reality. It would be the formal-abstract only if its principle, the concept, were taken as abstract unity rather than in its actual character, as the _negative_ _return_ _into_ _itself_ and as _subjectivity_.
:::

## {{ 214 }} § 214.

Among the ways to grasp the idea are these: as _reason_ (the proper philosophical sense of _reason_), also as the _subject-object_, as _unity_ of the _ideal_ with the _real_, of the _finite_ with the _infinite_, of _soul_ with _body_, as the _possibility_ that _contains_ _its_ _actuality_ _in_ _itself_, as that whose _nature_ is _conceivable_ only as _existing_, and so forth; for all the relations of the understanding are contained in the idea, though in their _infinite_ return and identity within themselves.

::: remark
{{ 214 A }} For the understanding it is easy work to point out that whatever is said of the idea is _contradictory_ in itself. The same can be given back to it, or rather the idea has already carried this out; – a task that is the work of reason, and certainly not so easy as the understanding's own. The understanding argues that the idea contradicts itself – the subjective, for instance, being only subjective, with the objective instead set against it; being being something wholly different from the concept and hence not to be picked out of it; likewise the finite being only finite and just the opposite of the infinite, thus not identical with it; and likewise for every further determination; logic, rather, shows the reverse, that what is supposed to be only subjective, only finite, only infinite and so forth has no {{{ 20.217 }}} truth, contradicts itself and passes over into its opposite, so that this passing over, along with the unity in which the extremes are sublated, as a shining or as moments, proves to be their truth.

The understanding that takes up the idea falls into a double misunderstanding: _first_, it takes the idea's _extremes_ (however they may be expressed, so far as _they_ _are_ _in_ _their_ _unity_) still in the sense and determination which they have so far as they are _not_ in their concrete unity but are still _abstractions_ outside it. It fails just as much to recognize the _relation_, even when this has been expressly posited; it overlooks, for instance, even the nature of the _copula_ in the judgment, which says of the singular, the subject, that the singular is equally not singular but universal. – _Second_, the understanding regards _its_ reflection, that the idea identical with itself contains its own _negative_, contradiction, as an _external_ reflection falling outside the idea. Yet this is in truth no wisdom of the understanding's own; the idea itself is the dialectic that eternally holds apart the self-identical and the different – subjective and objective, finite and infinite, soul and body – and that is eternal creation, eternal vitality and eternal spirit only to that extent. Because the idea is itself the passage, or better the self-translation, into the _abstract_ _understanding_, it is eternally _reason_ as well; it is the dialectic that has this product of the understanding, this diverse item, come to understand once more its finite nature and the false shine of self-subsistence in what it produces, and that leads it back into unity. This doubled movement is neither temporal nor in any way separated and distinguished, – otherwise it would once again be just abstract understanding, – and it is, therefore, an eternal intuiting of itself in the other: the concept that _has_ carried _itself_ out within its own objectivity, an object that is essentially subjective, _inner_ _purposiveness_.

The _various_ _ways_ of grasping the idea, as the unity of ideal and real, of _finite_ and _infinite_, of _identity_ {{{ 20.218 }}} and _difference_ and the like, are more or less _formal_, since each marks off some stage of the _determinate_ _concept_. Freedom and genuine _universality_ belong only to the concept itself; in the idea, accordingly, its _determinateness_ is likewise just itself, an objectivity into which, as the universal, it continues, and in which it has solely what is its own, the total determinateness. As the _infinite_ _judgment_, the idea has sides that are each a self-subsistent totality, and just by each bringing itself to completion as such, each has equally passed over into the other. No other determined concept is a totality completed in both of its sides, except the _concept_ itself and _objectivity_.
:::

## {{ 215 }} § 215.

Essentially the idea is _process_, because its identity is the concept's absolute and free identity only to the extent that the idea is absolute negativity, hence dialectical. The idea runs its course thus: the concept, as universality that is singularity, determines itself to objectivity and to opposition against objectivity, and this externality, whose substance is the concept, brings itself back to _subjectivity_ by means of its immanent dialectic.

::: remark
{{ 215 A }} It is because the idea is a. _process_ that the expression for the absolute as the _unity_ of finite and infinite, of thinking and being and so forth is false, as has been recalled often; for unity expresses an abstract identity that persists _at_ _rest_. It is because the idea is _subjectivity_ that the same expression is false in addition, since that unity expresses the _in-itself_, the _substantial_ aspect of true unity. So the infinite looks as if it were merely _neutralized_ with the finite, the subjective with the objective, thinking with being. In the idea's _negative_ unity, however, the infinite reaches over the finite, thinking over being, subjectivity over objectivity. Subjectivity, thinking, infinity make up the idea's unity, and it is therefore essentially to be told apart from the idea as _substance_; and equally this _overreaching_ subjectivity, thinking, infinity is to be told apart from the _one-sided_ subjectivity, thinking, infinity to which it reduces itself when it judges and determines.
:::
