## {{{ 20.23 }}} Preface to the First Edition

The need to put into my listeners' hands a guide to my philosophical lectures is the immediate occasion for my letting this survey of the whole range of philosophy appear sooner than I had intended.

The nature of an outline excludes not only a more exhaustive elaboration of the ideas with respect to their _content_, but in particular also constrains the elaboration of their systematic derivation, which must contain what was otherwise understood by _proof_, and which is indispensable to a scientific philosophy. The title was meant to indicate partly the scope of a whole, partly the intention of reserving the particulars for oral delivery.

In an outline, however, what comes into consideration is then rather a merely _external_ _appropriateness_ of arrangement and organization, when the content to be presented in deliberate brevity is one already presupposed and familiar. Since the present exposition is not in this case, but sets forth a new treatment of philosophy according to a method that, as I hope, will still come to be recognized as the only true one, identical with the content, I might have considered it more advantageous for it in the eyes of the public if circumstances had allowed me to let a more detailed work on the other parts of philosophy precede it, of the kind I have given the public on the first part of the whole, the _Logic_. For the rest, although in the present exposition the side by which the content lies closer to _representation_ and to empirical acquaintance had to be restricted, I believe I have made enough noticeable, with regard to the transitions, which can only be a mediation brought about through the _concept_, that the methodical character of the progression is sufficiently distinguished both from the merely _external_ _order_ which the other sciences seek out, and from a _manner_ that has become customary in philosophical subjects, which _presupposes_ _one_ _schema_ {{{ 20.24 }}} and with it parallelizes the subject matters just as externally and still more arbitrarily than the first way does, and, through the strangest misunderstanding, wants the necessity of the concept to be satisfied by contingency and arbitrariness of connections.

We saw this same arbitrariness seize upon the content of philosophy, set out on adventures of thought, and for a time impress upright and honest striving, yet otherwise also be taken for a witlessness heightened even to derangement. In place of the imposing or the deranged, the import, more properly and more often, betrayed well-known trivialities, and the form the mere manner of a deliberate, methodical and easily acquired wit of baroque connections and a forced perversity, and in general, behind the mien of earnestness, a deceit toward oneself and toward the public. On the other side, by contrast, we saw shallowness stamp _the_ _lack_ _of_ _thoughts_ as a self-satisfied clever skepticism and a criticism modest about reason, and raise its conceit and vanity to the same degree as its emptiness of ideas. – These two tendencies of spirit mocked German earnestness for a considerable time, wearied its deeper philosophical need, and had as their consequence an indifference, indeed such a contempt for the science of philosophy that now even a self-styled modesty believes it may presume to have a say and pass dismissive judgment on the profoundest matters of philosophy, and to deny to it rational cognition, the form of which was formerly comprehended under _proving_.

Of the two phenomena just touched on, the first can in part be seen as the youthful high spirits of the new epoch, which has broken upon the realm of science just as upon that of politics. If this zest greeted the dawn of rejuvenated spirit with intoxication, and without deeper labor went straight to the enjoyment of the idea and for a while reveled in the hopes and prospects it offered, it more readily reconciles us to its excesses, because a kernel lies at its base, and the superficial haze it has poured around that kernel must disperse of itself. The other phenomenon, however, is more repugnant, because it betrays exhaustion and powerlessness and strives to cover them with a conceit that lords it over the philosophical spirits of all centuries, misjudging them and most of all itself.

{{{ 20.25 }}} All the more gratifying is it to perceive, and still to mention, how, in the face of both, the philosophical interest and the earnest love of _higher_ _cognition_ has maintained itself unprejudiced and without vanity. If this interest at times threw itself more upon the form of an _immediate_ _knowing_ and of _feeling_, it attests, on the other hand, the inner, further-reaching drive of rational insight, which alone gives human beings their dignity, all the more highly by this: that even that standpoint becomes for it only a _result_ of philosophical knowledge, and thus that which it seems to disdain is at least acknowledged by it as _condition_. – To this interest in _cognizing_ _the_ _truth_ I dedicate this attempt to supply an introduction or contribution toward its satisfaction; may such a purpose gain the attempt a favorable reception.

_Heidelberg_, _May_ 1817.
