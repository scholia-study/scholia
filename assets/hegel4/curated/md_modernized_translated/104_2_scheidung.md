## {{{ 20.339 }}} 2. Separation

## {{ 334 }} § 334.

In the dissolution of the neutral begins the return to the particular chemical bodies and on to the indifferent bodies, through a series of processes, on the one hand peculiar, but on the other hand every such separation is in general itself inseparably bound up with a union, and likewise the processes that have been stated as belonging to the course of union contain immediately, at the same time, the other moment, of separation. For the _peculiar_ _place_ that each particular form of the process occupies, and thus for what is specific among the products, the processes are to be considered from _concrete_ agents and likewise in the _concrete_ products. Abstract processes, where the agents are abstract (for instance mere water acting on metal, or, to go further, gases and so forth), do indeed contain _in_ _itself_ the totality of the process, but do not present its moments in an explicated manner.

::: remark
{{ 334 A }} In empirical chemistry the concern is chiefly with the _particularity_ of the _materials_ and _products_, which are arranged according to superficial abstract determinations, so that no order thereby enters into their particularity. In that arrangement metals, oxygen, hydrogen and so forth (formerly earths, now) metalloids, sulfur, phosphorus appear side by side on one line, as _simple_ chemical bodies. At once the so great physical diversity of these bodies must arouse an aversion to such coordinating; just as diverse, however, shows itself also their chemical origin, the process out of which they proceed. But with equal chaos more abstract and more real processes are set on the same level. If scientific form is to enter into this, then each product is to be determined according to the stage of the concrete, completely {{{ 20.340 }}} developed process from which it essentially proceeds and which gives it its peculiar significance; and for this it is equally essential to distinguish the stages of abstraction or reality of the process. _Animal_ and _vegetable_ substances belong in any case to a wholly different order; their nature can so little be understood from the chemical process that they are rather destroyed in it, and only the path of _their_ _death_ is grasped. These substances ought nevertheless to serve most to counteract the metaphysics that prevails in chemistry as in physics, namely the thoughts, or rather wild representations, of the _unalterability_ _of_ _materials_ under all circumstances, like the categories of the _composition_ and _consisting_ of bodies out of such materials. We see it admitted in general that the chemical materials in union lose the _properties_ that they show in separation, and yet the representation holds that they are, _without_ the properties, the same things that they are _with_ them, as well as that, as things _with_ these properties, they are not first products of the process. The still indifferent body, the metal, has its affirmative determination in such a physical way that its properties appear on it as _immediate_. But the further determined _bodies_ cannot be so presupposed that one then sees how they behave in the process; rather, they have their first, essential determination solely according to their place in the chemical process. A further matter is the empirical, quite special particularity according to the behavior of bodies toward all other particular bodies; for this knowledge each has to run through the same litany of behavior toward all agents. – Most striking in this respect is to see the four chemical elements (oxygen and so forth) listed as _materials_ on the same line with gold, silver and so forth, sulfur and so forth, as if they had such a self-subsistent existence as gold, sulfur and so forth have, or {{{ 20.341 }}} oxygen had such an existence as carbon has. From their place in the process there results their subordination and abstraction, through which they are, in genus, wholly different from metals and salts and by no means belong on the same line with such concrete bodies; this place is set out in § 328. At the _abstract_ middle term, which _is_ _broken_ _in_ _itself_ (compare § 208, Remark), to which therefore _two_ elements belong – water and air, which is sacrificed as a means – the real extremes of the syllogism take for themselves the _existence_ of their original difference, existing as yet only _in_ _itself_. This moment of difference, brought thus _for_ _itself_ to existence, constitutes the chemical element, as a perfectly abstract moment; instead of being basic materials, substantial foundations, as one at first imagines with the expression element, those matters are rather the most extreme peaks of difference.

Here, as in general, the chemical process is to be taken in its complete totality. To isolate particular parts, formal and abstract processes, leads to the abstract representation of the chemical process in general as merely the _action_ of one material upon an other, whereby the many other things that take place (as also everywhere abstract neutralization, the production of water, and abstract separation, the evolution of gas) appear as almost a side issue or accidental consequence, or at least as only externally connected, and are not considered as an essential moment in the relation of the whole. A complete exposition of the chemical process in its totality, however, would require more precisely that it be explicated as a real {{{ 20.342 }}} syllogism, at the same time as the _triad_ of syllogisms engaging most intimately with one another – syllogisms that are not merely a combination of their terms in general but, as activities, negations of their determinations (compare § 198), and which would have to present, in their connection, the union and separation linked in one process.
:::

## {{ 335 }} § 335.

The chemical process is, to be sure, in general _life_; the individual body is just as much _sublated_ as _produced_ in its immediacy, and thus the concept no longer remains inner necessity but comes to _appearance_. It is, however, through the _immediacy_ of the corporealities that enter into the chemical process that it is afflicted with separation in general; thereby its moments appear as external _conditions_, what separates falls apart into products indifferent to one another, the fire and the inspiriting expires in the neutral and does not kindle itself again within it; the _beginning_ and the _end_ of the process are different from one another; – this constitutes its finitude, which holds it off from life and distinguishes it from it.

::: remark
{{ 335 A }} Chemical phenomena, for instance that in the process an oxide is reduced to a lower degree of oxidation, at which it can combine with the acid acting upon it, while a part, on the other hand, is more strongly oxidized, have led chemistry to apply in the explanation the determination of _purposiveness_, of an initial self-determining {{{ 20.343 }}} of the concept out of itself in its realization, so that this is not determined solely by the _externally_ present conditions.
:::

## {{ 336 }} § 336.

But the chemical process itself is just this: to posit those immediate presuppositions, the basis of its externality and finitude, as negated, to alter on one stage the properties of bodies that appear as results of a particular stage of the process, and to reduce those conditions to products. What is thus posited in it, in general, is the _relativity_ of the immediate substances and properties. The indifferently subsisting corporeal is thereby posited only as a _moment_ of individuality, and the concept in _the_ _reality_ _corresponding_ _to_ _it_; the _concrete_ _unity_ with itself, produced in _one_ out of the particularization of the distinct corporealities, which is the activity of negating this its one-sided form of relation to itself, of _dirempting_ itself into the moments of the concept and particularizing, and equally of leading back into that unity – thus the infinite process kindling and sustaining itself, – _the_ _organism_.
