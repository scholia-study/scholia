## b. Existence

## {{ 89 }} § 89.

Being in becoming, as one with the nothing, and the nothing as one with being, are merely things that vanish; on account of its inner contradiction becoming falls together into the unity in which the two are sublated, and thus its _result_ is _existence_.

::: remark
{{{ 20.129 }}} {{ 89 A }} On this first example, one should be reminded once and for all of what § 82 and its Remark state: the one thing able to establish progress and development in knowing is to keep hold of results in their truth. Suppose a contradiction is shown up in some object or concept (– and there is _nowhere_ _at_ _all_ anything in which a contradiction, that is, opposed determinations, cannot and need not be shown up; – the understanding's abstracting is a violent clinging to a single determinateness, an effort to darken and push away the awareness of the other one that sits in it) – suppose, then, that the contradiction is recognized, the habit is to infer: _Therefore_ this object is _nothing_; this is how _Zeno_ first showed that motion contradicts itself and hence does not _exist_, and how the ancients declared _coming-to-be_ and _ceasing-to-be_, the two forms of becoming, untrue determinations, by saying that the _one_, which is the absolute, neither comes to be nor ceases. Dialectic of this kind lingers on the result's negative aspect alone, and abstracts from what is simultaneously present in actuality, a _determinate_ result, here a pure _nothing_, yet a _nothing_ with _being_ inside it, and likewise a being with the nothing inside it. Thus 1) existence is the unity of being and nothing, one in which the immediacy of the two determinations, and so their contradiction within their relation, has disappeared, – a unity in which they are now merely _moments_, 2) because the result is the contradiction as sublated, it has the form of a _simple_ _unity_ with itself, or is itself a _being_, though a being bearing negation or determinateness; becoming is posited in the form of _one_ of its moments, namely being.
:::

## {{ 90 }} § 90.

α) _Existence_ is being along with a _determinateness_, and this determinateness, as immediate determinateness or one that is, is _quality_. When existence is reflected _into_ _itself_ in this {{{ 20.130 }}} determinateness of its own, it is the _existent_, a _something_. – The categories that unfold in existence are to be listed only in summary.

## {{ 91 }} § 91.

As determinateness that _is_, set against the _negation_ it contains yet differs from, quality is _reality_. Negation is no longer the abstract nothing; being an existence and a _something_, it is only a form on the latter, and it is there as _otherness_. Since this otherness is quality's own determination, though to begin with different from it, quality – _is_ _being-for-other_, – an extension of existence, of the something. Against this relation to an other, the _being_ of quality as such is _being-in-itself_.

## {{ 92 }} § 92.

β) If being were held apart from determinateness, as being-<i>in-itself</i>, it would be no more than the empty abstraction of being. In existence determinateness is one with being, and it is at once posited as negation, as _limit_, as _restriction_. Otherness is therefore no indifferent outside of existence but is a moment belonging to it. It is by virtue of its quality that _something_ proves _finite_ in the first place and _alterable_ in the second, which means that finitude and alterability pertain to its very being.

## {{ 93 }} § 93.

Something turns into an other; but that other is itself a something, so it too turns into an other, and so on _into_ _infinity_.

## {{ 94 }} § 94.

The _infinity_ here is _bad_ _or_ _negative_ infinity, because it is only the finite's negation, and the finite equally rises {{{ 20.131 }}} again and so is no more sublated than before, – put otherwise, this infinity states merely the _ought_ of sublating the finite. The progress into infinity does not get beyond stating the contradiction in the finite, namely that it is both _something_ and its _other_, and it is an unending continuation of the alternation between these determinations, each of which calls up the other.

## {{ 95 }} § 95.

γ) What is really there is this: something becomes an other, and the other in general becomes an other. Standing in relation to an other, something is already itself an other relative to it; and so, since what it passes into is exactly what passes over, – each has no determination beyond one and the same, that of being an _other_, – something in passing over into an other merely joins up _with_ _itself_, and this self-relation within the passing over and within the other is _genuine_ _infinity_. Seen from the negative side: the thing that undergoes alteration is the _other_, and what it turns into is the _other_ of the _other_. Being thereby re-establishes itself, now as negation of negation, and this is _being-for-itself_.

::: remark
{{ 95 A }} Dualism, which declares the opposition of finite and infinite impossible to overcome, fails to notice the simple point that this makes the infinite at once just _one_ _of_ _the_ _pair_, so that it is turned into a merely _particular_ for which the finite is the remaining particular. An infinite that is merely a particular, and stands _next_ _to_ the finite, thereby possesses in the finite its restriction and limit; it is _not_ what it should be, not the infinite, but only _finite_. – Under this arrangement, in which the finite is set _over_ _here_ and the infinite _over_ _there_, the first _on_ _this_ _side_ and the other _beyond_, the finite is credited with the _same_ _worth_ of _subsisting_ and _self-subsistence_ as the infinite; the finite's being is turned into an absolute being; within such a dualism it stands firm on its own. If the infinite so much as touched it, {{{ 20.132 }}} it would be nullified; yet it is not to be touchable by the infinite at all, an abyss, an impassable gulf is to lie between the two, with the infinite staying absolutely over there and the finite _persisting_ over here. In imagining that its claim of the finite's firm persistence against the infinite has left all metaphysics behind, this claim stands wholly on the ground of the commonest metaphysics of the understanding. Here the same thing occurs that the infinite progress states: at one moment the finite is conceded not to be _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_, to have _no_ self-subsistent actuality and no _absolute_ being, to be merely transient; _on_ _another_ this is promptly forgotten, and the finite, set merely over against the infinite, wholly cut off from it and spared nullification, is pictured as persisting on its own, self-subsistent. – When thinking supposes in this way that it rises to the infinite, the reverse happens to it, – it arrives at an infinite that is only a finite, and it keeps hold, ever again, of the finite it believes it has left behind, making it an absolute.

After the reflection just carried out on the nullity of the understanding's opposition of finite and infinite (for which _Plato’s_ _Philebus_ may usefully be compared), it is easy to land here as well on the statement that infinite and finite are _one_, that what is true, genuine infinity, is to be defined and stated as the _unity_ of infinite and finite; such a statement does contain something right, but it is just as skewed and false as what was noted above about the _unity_ of being and nothing. A further consequence is the justified reproach that infinity is being made finite, that a finite infinite results. For in that statement the finite appears left as it was; it is not expressly stated to be _sublated_. – Or, reflecting that the finite, once posited as one with the infinite, could not stay what it was outside this unity and would at least suffer in its determination (as potash loses some of its properties when combined with acid), one would find that exactly this happens to the infinite too, which, being the negative, would for its part {{{ 20.133 }}} also be dulled by the other. In fact the understanding's abstract, one-sided infinite does undergo such a thing. The genuine infinite, though, does not simply behave like the one-sided acid; it maintains itself; negation of the negation is no neutralization; the infinite is the affirmative, and the finite alone is what is sublated.

With being-for-itself, the determination of _ideality_ has made its entry. When existence is understood to begin with only in terms of its being or affirmation, it has _reality_ (§ 91), and finitude accordingly stands at first under the determination of reality. The truth of the finite is, however, rather its _ideality_. Equally, the understanding's infinite, set be<i>side</i> the finite, is itself merely one of two finites, an untrue one, an _ideal_ one. That the finite is ideal is the principal thesis of philosophy, and for this reason every genuine philosophy is _idealism_. All depends on not taking as the infinite something that its very determination makes into a particular and finite. – This is why the difference has been pointed out here at some length; the basic concept of philosophy, the genuine infinite, hangs on it. The difference is settled by the very simple, and therefore perhaps inconspicuous, yet irrefutable reflections that the paragraph contains.
:::
