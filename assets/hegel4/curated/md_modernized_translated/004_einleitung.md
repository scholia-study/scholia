## {{{ 20.39 }}} Introduction

## {{ 1 }} § 1.

Philosophy lacks the advantage enjoyed by the other sciences, of being able to _presuppose_ its _objects_ as immediately granted by representation, and likewise the _method_ of cognition, for beginning and for progress, as already accepted. It does, to be sure, share its objects with religion at the outset. Both have _truth_ as their object, and in the highest sense, – in that _God_ is the truth and _alone_ is the truth. Both then go on to deal with the domain of the finite, with _nature_ and the _human_ _spirit_, with their relation to one another and to God as their truth. Philosophy can therefore certainly presuppose an _acquaintance_ with its objects, indeed it _must_ presuppose one, as it must an interest in them; – if only because consciousness forms _representations_ of objects earlier in time than _concepts_ of them, and the _thinking_ spirit even advances to thinking cognition and comprehension only _through_ representing and by turning _toward_ it.

But in thinking consideration it soon becomes apparent that it involves the demand to show the _necessity_ of its content, to _prove_ both the being and the determinations of its objects. That acquaintance with them thus appears insufficient, and making or accepting _presuppositions_ and _assurances_ inadmissible. The difficulty of making a _beginning_ arises at the same time, however, since a beginning, as something _immediate_, makes a presupposition or rather is itself one.

## {{{ 20.40 }}} {{ 2 }} § 2.

Philosophy can at first be determined in general as the _thinking_ _consideration_ of objects. But if it is correct (and it will surely be correct) that the _human_ _being_ is distinguished from the _animal_ by thinking, then everything human is human through this and only through this, that it is brought about by thinking. Yet because philosophy is a peculiar way of thinking, one by which thinking becomes cognition, indeed comprehending cognition, its thinking too will show a _diversity_ from the thinking at work in everything human, that indeed brings about the humanity of the human, however much it is identical with it and in itself only _One_ thinking. This difference is connected with the fact that the human import of consciousness, grounded through thinking, does not at first _appear_ _in_ _the_ _form_ _of_ _thought_, but as feeling, intuition, representation, – _forms_ that are to be distinguished from thinking _as_ _form_.

::: remark
{{ 2 A }} It is an old prejudice, a proposition grown trivial, that the human being is distinguished from the animal by thinking; it may seem trivial, but it ought also to seem odd if there were a need to recall such an old belief. Yet it can be held a need, given the prejudice of the present time, which so separates _feeling_ and _thinking_ that they are supposed to be opposed, indeed so hostile that feeling, especially religious feeling, is supposedly contaminated, perverted, even perhaps destroyed by thinking, and that religion and religiosity essentially have neither their root nor their place in thinking. In such a separation it is forgotten that only the human being is capable of religion, whereas the animal has no religion, just as right and morality do not pertain to it.

When that separation of religion from thinking is asserted, the thinking that is usually in view is the kind that can be designated as _reflective_ _thinking_, – the thinking that _reflects_, which has _thoughts_ as such for its _content_ and brings them to consciousness. The negligence in knowing and heeding the difference that philosophy has determinately stated with regard to thinking is what produces the crudest representations of philosophy and reproaches against it. Since religion, right and ethical life pertain only to the human being, and indeed {{{ 20.41 }}} only because the human being is a thinking being, thinking has not been idle at all in the religious, the right and the ethical, – be it feeling and faith or representation; its activity and its productions are _present_ and _contained_ in them. But it is a different matter to have such feelings and representations _determined_ and _permeated_ by _thinking_, – and to have _thoughts_ _about_ them. The thoughts produced through _reflective_ _thinking_ _about_ those modes of consciousness are what reflection, ratiocination and the like, and then philosophy too, comprise.

In this connection it has happened, and misunderstanding has often prevailed, that such _reflective_ _thinking_ has been asserted to be the condition, indeed the sole path, on which we arrive at the representation and acceptance as true of the eternal and the true. Thus, for instance, the (now rather _former_) _metaphysical_ _proofs_ of the _existence_ _of_ _God_ have been put forward as if, or to the effect that, belief and conviction in the existence of God could be essentially and solely produced by knowledge of them and conviction through them. Such an assertion would amount to saying that we could not eat before acquiring knowledge of the chemical, botanical or zoological determinations of foodstuffs, and would have to hold off digesting until we had finished studying anatomy and physiology. Were that the case, these sciences in their field, like philosophy in its own, would no doubt gain enormously in usefulness, their usefulness even rising to absolute and universal indispensability; instead of being indispensable, however, they would not exist at all.
:::

## {{ 3 }} § 3.

Whatever the kind of _content_ filling our consciousness, it makes up the _determinateness_ of feelings, intuitions, images, representations, purposes, duties and so forth, and of thoughts and concepts. Insofar, feeling, intuition, image and so forth are _the_ _forms_ of a content that stays _one_ _and_ {{{ 20.42 }}} _the_ _same_, no matter whether it is felt, intuited, represented or willed, and no matter whether it is _only_ felt, or felt, intuited and so forth with thoughts mingled in, or thought entirely _unmixed_. In one of these forms, or in a blend of several, the content is an _object_ of consciousness. In this objecthood, however, _the_ _determinatenesses_ _of_ _these_ _forms_ also _attach_ _themselves_ _to_ _the_ _content_; so that a particular object seems to arise according to each of these forms, and what is in itself the same can look like a different content.

::: remark
{{ 3 A }} Since the determinatenesses of feeling, intuition, desire, will and so forth, insofar as they are _known_ of, are generally called _representations_, one may say generally that what philosophy sets where representations stood are _thoughts_, _categories_, or more exactly _concepts_. Representations in general may be viewed as _metaphors_ of thoughts and concepts. But merely having representations does not yet mean knowing their significance for thinking, that is, their thoughts and concepts. Likewise, having thoughts and concepts is one thing, knowing the representations, intuitions and feelings that answer to them another. – This bears on one side of what gets called _the_ _unintelligibility_ of philosophy. The difficulty lies in part in an incapacity, which in itself is only _unaccustomedness_, to think abstractly, that is, to hold fast to pure thoughts and move within them. In our ordinary consciousness thoughts are clothed in, and united with, familiar sensuous and spiritual material, and in reflective thinking, reflecting and ratiocinating we _mix_ feelings, intuitions and representations with thoughts, (in every proposition of wholly sensuous content, such as: this leaf _is_ green, categories, _being_, _singularity_, are already mixed in). But it is another thing to make thoughts themselves, unmixed, into the object. – The other part of the unintelligibility is the impatience to want to have before oneself, in the manner of representation, what is in consciousness as thought and concept. The expression occurs that one does not know what one is supposed to _think_ in the case of a concept that has been grasped; in the case of a concept there is nothing further to think than the concept itself. The sense of that expression, however, is a longing for an already _known_, _familiar_ _representation_; it is as if consciousness, with {{{ 20.43 }}} the manner of representation, had the ground pulled out from under it on which it otherwise has its firm and homely footing. Once placed in the pure realm of concepts, it has no idea where in the world it stands. – The most _intelligible_ writers, preachers, orators and so forth are therefore found to be those who recite to their readers or listeners things that these already know by heart, that are familiar to them and that _go_ _without_ _saying_.
:::

## {{ 4 }} § 4.

With regard to our common consciousness, philosophy would first have to demonstrate, or even awaken, _the_ _need_ for its _peculiar_ mode of cognition. With regard to the objects of religion, however, and to _truth_ in general, it would have to prove the _capacity_ to cognize them from itself; with regard to a _diversity_ from the _religious_ representations that comes to light, it would have to _justify_ its deviating determinations.

## {{ 5 }} § 5.

For the purpose of a preliminary understanding about the difference stated and about the insight connected with it, that the true _content_ of our consciousness is _preserved_, indeed first put into its proper light, in being translated into the form of thought and concept, another _old_ _prejudice_ can be recalled, namely that, in order to find out what is _true_ in objects and events, also in feelings, intuitions, opinions, representations and so forth, _reflective_ _thinking_ is required. Reflective thinking, however, does at least this in every case: it transforms feelings, representations and so forth into _thoughts_.

::: remark
{{ 5 A }} Insofar as it is only _thinking_ that philosophy claims as the peculiar _form_ of its business, and every human being can think by nature, there arises by virtue of this abstraction, which leaves out the difference stated in § 3, the opposite of what was mentioned just before as a complaint about the _unintelligibility_ of philosophy. This science often suffers the contempt that even those who have not troubled themselves with it voice the conceit {{{ 20.44 }}} that they _understand_ from the outset what is at stake with philosophy, and, going and standing as they do in ordinary education, especially proceeding from religious feelings, are capable of philosophizing and of passing judgment on it. It is admitted that one must have studied the other sciences in order to know them, and that only by virtue of such knowledge is one entitled to have a judgment about them. It is admitted that in order to make a shoe one must have learned and practiced it, although everyone has the measure for it in their own foot, and in their hands the natural skill for the required business. Only for philosophizing itself is such study, learning and effort supposed not to be required. – This comfortable opinion has received its confirmation in the most recent times through the doctrine of immediate knowing, knowing by intuiting.
:::

## {{ 6 }} § 6.

On the other side it is just as important that philosophy be clear that its content is no other than the import originally produced, and producing itself, in the domain of living spirit, made into the _world_, the outer and inner world of consciousness, – that its content is _actuality_. The most immediate consciousness of this content we call _experience_. A thoughtful consideration of the world already distinguishes what, in the wide realm of outer and inner existence, is only _appearance_, transient and meaningless, and what in itself truly deserves the name of _actuality_. Since philosophy is distinguished from other becoming-conscious of this one and the same import only by form, its agreement with actuality and experience is necessary. Indeed one may take this agreement as at least an external touchstone for the truth of a philosophy; and equally as the supreme final purpose of science, to bring about, by knowing this agreement, the reconciliation of self-conscious reason with the reason that _is_, with actuality.

::: remark
{{ 6 A }} In the _preface_ to my _Philosophy_ _of_ _Right_ (p. XIX) are found the propositions:

_what_ _is_ _rational_, _that_ _is_ _actual_, _and_ _what_ _is_ _actual_, _that_ _is_ _rational_.

{{{ 20.45 }}} These simple propositions have struck many as remarkable and have met with hostility, and even from those who do not wish to deny possessing philosophy and, no doubt, religion as well. It will be unnecessary to cite religion in this connection, since its doctrines of divine world-governance state these propositions all too determinately. As for the philosophical sense, however, enough education is to be presupposed to know not only that God is actual, – that he is the most actual, that he alone is truly actual, – but also, with regard to the formal side, that existence in general is partly _appearance_ and only partly actuality. In common life one is liable to call every whim, error, evil and whatever belongs on that side, and every concrete existence however stunted and transient, casually an _actuality_. But even an ordinary feeling will not grant a contingent concrete existence the emphatic name of something actual; – the contingent is a concrete existence that has no greater value than that of something _possible_, that can just as well _not_ _be_ as it is. But when I spoke of actuality, one should of itself have thought about the sense in which I use this expression, since in an extended _Logic_ I also treated actuality and distinguished it precisely not only at once from the contingent, which after all also has concrete existence, but more closely from existence, concrete existence and other determinations. – The representation already opposes itself to the _actuality_ _of_ _the_ _rational_, both in holding that ideas and ideals are nothing further than chimeras and philosophy a system of such figments of the brain, and conversely in holding that ideas and ideals are far too excellent a thing to possess actuality, or just as much too impotent a thing to bring it about for themselves. But the separation of actuality from the idea is especially favored by the understanding, which holds the dreams of its abstractions to be something true, and is vain about the _ought_, which it likes to prescribe above all in the political field too, as though the world had waited on it for news of how it _ought_ to be, yet is not; and were it as it ought to be, where would its ought's smug cleverness be? If it turns, with the ought, against trivial, external and transient objects, institutions, conditions and so forth, which perhaps for a certain time, for particular circles, may {{{ 20.46 }}} have a great relative importance, it may well be right, and in such a case find much that does not correspond to generally correct determinations; who would not be clever enough to see in their surroundings much that in fact is not as it ought to be? But this cleverness is wrong to imagine that, with such objects and their ought, it finds itself within the interests of philosophical science. This has to do only with the idea, which is not so powerless as merely to be something that ought to be and not to be actual, and thus with an actuality of which those objects, institutions, conditions and so forth are only the superficial outer side.
:::

## {{ 7 }} § 7.

Since _reflective_ _thinking_ as such initially contains the principle (also in the sense of the beginning) of philosophy, and since it has bloomed again in its _self-subsistence_ in more recent times (after the times of the Lutheran Reformation), and, since from the very start it kept itself not merely abstract, as in the philosophizing beginnings of the Greeks, but at the same time threw itself upon the seemingly measureless material of the world of appearance, the name _philosophy_ has been given to all that knowledge that busies itself with the cognition of the fixed measure and of the _universal_ in the sea of empirical singularities, and of the _necessary_, of the _laws_, in the apparent disorder of the infinite multitude of the contingent, and has thereby at the same time taken its _content_ from its _own_ intuiting and perceiving of the outer and the inner, from _present_ nature, as from the _present_ spirit and the breast of the human being.

::: remark
{{ 7 A }} The principle of _experience_ contains the infinitely important determination that, for accepting and holding as true any content, one must be _present_ _at_ _it_ oneself, more precisely that one must find such content in unity and united with _the_ _certainty_ _of_ _oneself_. One must be present oneself, whether only with one's external senses or with one's deeper spirit, one's essential self-consciousness. – This {{{ 20.47 }}} principle is the same that has nowadays been called faith, immediate knowing, the revelation in the outer and above all in one's _own_ inner. We call those sciences that have been named _philosophy_ _empirical_ sciences, from the starting point that they take. But the essential thing they aim at and produce is _laws_, _universal_ _propositions_, a _theory_; the _thoughts_ of what is at hand. Hence _Newtonian_ physics got the name philosophy of nature, whereas _Hugo_ _Grotius_, to give an example, drew up universal principles, a theory that may be called philosophy of external public law, by collecting the historical dealings of peoples with one another and with the aid of ordinary ratiocination. – Among the English the name _philosophy_ still generally has this determination, _Newton_ continues to enjoy the fame of the greatest philosopher; down to the price lists of instrument makers, those instruments that are not placed under a special rubric of magnetic or electrical apparatus, the thermometers, barometers and so forth, are called _philosophical_ _instruments_; though surely not a composition of wood, iron and so forth but _thinking_ alone should be called the instrument of philosophy.[^1] – Thus in particular the science of political economy, which is owed to the most recent times, is also called philosophy, what we are accustomed to call _rational_ state economics, or perhaps state economics of _intelligence_.[^2]
:::

## {{{ 20.48 }}} {{ 8 }} § 8.

However satisfying this cognition is initially in its field, there shows itself _in_ _the_ _first_ _place_ yet another circle of _objects_ not comprised in it, – _freedom_, _spirit_, _God_. They are not to be found on that ground not because they are supposed not to belong to experience; they are not experienced sensuously, to be sure, but what is in consciousness at all is experienced, – this is even a tautological proposition, – but because these objects present themselves at once, as to their _content_, as infinite.

::: remark
{{ 8 A }} There is an old proposition, which is usually falsely ascribed to _Aristotle_ as if it were meant to express the standpoint of his philosophy: nihil est in intellectu, quod non fuerit in sensu; – there is nothing in thinking that has not been in the senses, in experience. It is to be regarded only as a misunderstanding if speculative philosophy were unwilling to admit this proposition. But conversely it will equally assert: nihil est in sensu, quod non fuerit in intellectu, – in the entirely general sense that the _nous_ and, in a deeper determination, _spirit_, is the cause of the world, and in the closer sense (see § 2) that the feeling of right, of the ethical and of the religious is a feeling and thus an experience {{{ 20.49 }}} of such a content as has its root and seat only in thinking.
:::

## {{ 9 }} § 9.

_In_ _the_ _second_ _place_, subjective reason demands further satisfaction _as_ _to_ _form_; this form is _necessity_ in general (see § 1). In that scientific manner, on the one hand, the _universal_ contained in it, the genus and so forth, is in itself indeterminate and not connected for itself with the _particular_, but both are external and contingent to one another, just as the combined particularities are likewise in themselves mutually external and contingent. On the other hand, the beginnings are everywhere _immediacies_, things _found_, _presuppositions_. In both the form of necessity is not satisfied. Reflective thinking, insofar as it is directed at satisfying this need, is the properly philosophical, _speculative_ _thinking_. As reflective thinking, then, which in its _commonality_ with that first reflective thinking is at the same time _different_ from it, it has, besides the common forms, also _peculiar_ _forms_, whose universal form is the _concept_.

::: remark
{{ 9 A }} The relation of speculative science to the other sciences is in this respect only this: that it does not leave the empirical content of the latter aside but recognizes and uses it, that it likewise recognizes the universal of these sciences, the laws, the genera and so forth, and employs it for its own content, but that it also further introduces other categories into these and makes them count. The difference in this respect relates solely to this alteration of the categories. The earlier logic and metaphysics are contained in speculative logic, which retains the same thought-forms, laws and objects while at once developing and reshaping them through additional categories.

What has usually been called concept is to be distinguished from the _concept_ in the speculative sense. It is in the latter, one-sided sense that the assertion has been put forward, repeated a thousand and a thousand times, and made into a prejudice, that the infinite cannot be grasped by concepts.
:::

## {{{ 20.50 }}} {{ 10 }} § 10.

This thinking of the philosophical mode of cognition itself needs to be grasped as to its necessity, and also to be justified as to its capacity to cognize the absolute objects. Such an insight, however, is itself philosophical cognition, which therefore falls only _within_ philosophy. Any _preliminary_ explication would accordingly be bound to be unphilosophical, amounting to nothing but a web of presuppositions, assurances and ratiocinations, – that is, of contingent assertions, to which the opposite ones could with the same right be asserted in reply.

::: remark
{{ 10 A }} A main point of view of _critical_ philosophy is that, before one sets about cognizing God, the essence of things and so forth, the _faculty_ _of_ _cognition_ itself must first be examined as to whether it is capable of achieving this; one must get to know the _instrument_ beforehand, before undertaking the work that is to be accomplished by means of it; if it were insufficient, all effort would otherwise be spent in vain. – This thought has seemed so _plausible_ that it has awakened the greatest admiration and assent, and has led cognition back from its interest in the _objects_ and its business with them to itself, to the formal. If one does not want to deceive oneself with words, however, it is easy to see that other instruments may well be examined and judged in some other way than by undertaking the peculiar work for which they are destined. But the examination of cognition cannot take place otherwise than _cognitively_; in the case of this so-called tool, to examine it is nothing other than to cognize it. To want to cognize _before_ one cognizes, however, is just as absurd as the wise resolution of that schoolman to learn to _swim_ _before_ _he_ _ventured_ _into_ _the_ _water_.

_Reinhold_, who recognized the confusion that reigns in such an undertaking, proposed as a remedy to begin provisionally with a _hypothetical_ and _problematic_ philosophizing, and to carry on in {{{ 20.51 }}} it, one does not know how, until it should perhaps emerge further on that one had arrived by such a path at the _original_ _truth_. Considered more closely, this path would come down to the ordinary, namely to the analysis of an empirical basis or of a provisional assumption brought into a definition. It cannot be denied that there lies a correct consciousness in declaring the ordinary course of presuppositions and provisionalities to be a hypothetical and problematic procedure. But this correct insight does not change the constitution of such a procedure, but rather at once states its insufficiency.
:::

## {{ 11 }} § 11.

More closely, the need of philosophy can be determined in this way: since spirit, as feeling and intuiting, has the sensuous, as fantasy, images, as will, purposes and so forth, as its objects, it procures satisfaction, in _opposition_ _or_ merely in _difference_ _from_ _these_ _forms_ of its existence and of its objects, for _thinking_, its highest inwardness, and wins thinking as its object. Thus it comes _to_ _itself_, in the deepest sense of the word, for its principle, its unmixed selfhood, is thinking. In this its business, however, it happens that thinking entangles itself in contradictions, that is, loses itself in the fixed non-identity of thoughts, thus does not reach itself, but rather remains caught in its opposite. The higher need turns against this outcome of merely understanding thinking; it has its ground in thinking's refusal to abandon itself: even in this conscious forfeiture of its being-with-itself it stays true to itself, »_so_ _that_ _it_ _may_ _overcome_,« and carries out within thinking itself the dissolution of its own contradictions.

::: remark
{{ 11 A }} To see that thinking is by its very nature dialectic, and that as understanding it is bound to land in its own negative, in contradiction, is a main aspect of logic. Thinking, despairing of its ability to resolve _out_ _of_ _itself_ the contradiction in which it has landed itself, turns back to the solutions and consolations that spirit has been granted in its other modes and forms. In this return, however, thinking would not have needed to fall {{{ 20.52 }}} into _misology_, of which _Plato_ already had experience, and to behave polemically toward itself, as happens in the assertion of so-called _immediate_ _knowing_ as the _exclusive_ form of the consciousness of truth.
:::

## {{ 12 }} § 12.

The _emergence_ of philosophy proceeding from the said need has _experience_, the immediate and ratiocinating consciousness, as its _starting_ _point_. Stimulated by this as by a spur, thinking essentially conducts itself in such a way that it _raises_ itself above natural, sensuous and ratiocinating consciousness into the unmixed element of itself, and thus at first gives itself a distancing, _negative_ _relation_ to that beginning. It thus finds its first satisfaction within itself, in the idea of the _universal_ essence of these appearances, an idea (the absolute, God,) that may be more or less abstract. Conversely, the empirical sciences bring with them the spur to overcome _the_ _form_ in which the wealth of their content is offered as something merely immediate and found, a multiplicity set _side_ _by_ _side_, and thus as _contingent_ in general, and to raise this content to necessity, – this spur tears thinking out of that universality and the satisfaction afforded only _in_ _itself_, and drives it to _development_ _out_ _of_ _itself_. This development is on the one hand only a taking up of the content and its given determinations, and on the other hand at the same time gives it the shape of proceeding freely, in the sense of original thinking, solely according to the necessity of the matter itself.

::: remark
{{ 12 A }} The relation of _immediacy_ and _mediation_ _in_ _consciousness_ is to be spoken of expressly and at greater length below. Here it need only be pointed out provisionally that, even if both moments _appear_ as distinguished, neither _of_ _the_ _two_ can _be_ _lacking_, and that they stand in _inseparable_ connection. – Thus {{{ 20.53 }}} the knowledge of God, like that of everything <i>super</i>sensible in general, essentially contains an _elevation_ above sensuous sensation or intuition; it thereby contains a _negative_ comportment toward this first, but in this the _mediation_. For mediation is a beginning and a having-proceeded to a second, so that this second is only insofar as it has been arrived at from something that is an other to it. But with this the knowledge of God is no less self-subsistent in relation to that empirical side; indeed it essentially gives itself its self-subsistence through this negation and elevation. – If mediation is made into conditionedness and one-sidedly singled out, one can say, though not much is said with it, that philosophy owes its first emergence to experience (the _a_ _posteriori_), – in fact thinking is essentially the negation of something immediately at hand, – just as much as one owes eating to foodstuffs, for without these one could not eat; eating, to be sure, is represented in this relation as ungrateful, for it is the consuming of that to which it is supposed to owe itself. Thinking is in this sense no less ungrateful.

The thinking's own _immediacy_, however, reflected into itself and therefore mediated in itself (the _a_ _priori_), is _universality_, its being-with-itself in general; in it it is satisfied in itself, and in this respect an indifference to _particularization_, and thus to its development, is innate to it. Just as religion, whether more developed or unformed, whether cultivated into scientific consciousness or held in unreflective faith and heart, possesses the same intensive nature of satisfaction and blessedness. If thinking remains at the _universality_ of ideas, – as is necessarily the case in the first philosophies (for instance the _being_ of the Eleatic school, the _becoming_ of Heraclitus, and the like), – it is rightly reproached with _formalism_; even in {{{ 20.54 }}} a developed philosophy it can happen that only the abstract propositions or determinations are grasped, such as that everything in the absolute is one, or the identity of the subjective and objective, and that these same ones are merely repeated in the particular. With regard to the first abstract universality of thinking, it has a correct and more fundamental sense that the _development_ of philosophy is to be owed to experience. The empirical sciences, on the one hand, do not stop at perceiving the _singularities_ of appearance, but, thinking, have worked toward meeting philosophy with the material by finding the universal determinations, genera and laws; in this way they ready that content of the particular for reception into philosophy. On the other side, they thereby carry the compulsion for thinking itself to proceed to these concrete determinations. The taking up of this content, in which thinking sublates the immediacy and givenness still clinging to it, is at once a _developing_ of thinking from within itself. Since philosophy thus owes its development to the empirical sciences, it gives their content the most essential shape of the _freedom_ (of the _a_ _priori_) of thinking and the _confirmation_ of _necessity_, in place of the authentication by finding and by the experienced fact, so that the fact becomes the presentation and reproduction of the original and perfectly self-subsistent activity of thinking.
:::

## {{ 13 }} § 13.

The emergence and development of philosophy is presented, in the peculiar shape of _external_ _history_, as the _history_ _of_ _this_ {{{ 20.55 }}} _science_. Under this shape the stages of the idea's development take on the form of a _contingent_ sequence, perhaps of mere _diversity_ among principles and the way the several philosophies carry them out. But the master-builder of this work of millennia is the One living spirit, whose thinking nature it is to bring _what_ _it_ _is_ to its consciousness, and, as this thus becomes an object, to be at the same time already raised above it and to be a higher stage in itself. Across the philosophies that look different, the _history_ _of_ _philosophy_ reveals in part only One philosophy at various stages of formation, in part that the particular _principles_, each of which underlay one system, are merely _branches_ of a single whole. The latest philosophy in time results from all the ones before it and so has to contain the principles of all of them; for this reason, provided it is philosophy at all, it is the most unfolded, richest and most concrete.

::: remark
{{ 13 A }} In view of the appearance of so many _different_ philosophies, the _universal_ and the _particular_ must be distinguished according to their proper determination. The universal, taken formally and placed _alongside_ the particular, itself also becomes something particular. Such a placing would of itself strike one as inappropriate and clumsy in the case of objects of common life, as when, for instance, someone who asked for fruit rejected cherries, pears, grapes and so forth because they are cherries, pears, grapes, _not_ however fruit. In regard to philosophy, however, one allows oneself to justify its disdain by saying that there are such different philosophies, and each is only _a_ philosophy, not _the_ philosophy, – as if cherries were not also fruit. It also happens that one whose principle is the universal is placed _alongside_ those whose principle is a particular one, indeed even alongside teachings affirming that no philosophy exists whatever, the idea being that the two are _only_ _different_ views of philosophy, much as if light and darkness were called only two _different_ kinds of light.
:::

## {{{ 20.56 }}} {{ 14 }} § 14.

In philosophy itself the very development of thinking that the history of philosophy lays out is laid out again, now released from that historical externality and _purely_ _in_ _the_ _element_ _of_ _thinking_. The free and true thought is _concrete_ in itself, and so it is _idea_, and in its whole universality _the_ idea or _the_ _absolute_. The science of it is essentially _system_, because the true, as _concrete_, is only as unfolding itself within itself and gathering and holding together in unity, that is, as _totality_, and can be the necessity of its differences and the freedom of the whole only through the differentiation and determination of its differences.

::: remark
{{ 14 A }} Philosophizing _without_ _system_ cannot be anything scientific; besides expressing, in itself, rather a subjective cast of mind, it is contingent in its content. A content has its justification solely as a moment of the whole, apart from it however an unfounded presupposition or subjective certainty; many philosophical writings confine themselves to expressing in this way merely _sentiments_ and _opinions_. – By a _system_ is wrongly understood a philosophy with a limited _principle_ distinguished from others; on the contrary, it is the principle of true philosophy to contain all particular principles within itself.
:::

## {{ 15 }} § 15.

Every part of philosophy is a philosophical whole, a circle that closes upon itself; yet in it the philosophical idea has a particular determinateness or element. Being totality in itself, the single circle therefore also bursts the restriction of its element and founds a further sphere; the whole thus shows itself as a circle of circles, each of them a necessary moment, with the system of their peculiar elements making up the whole idea, which likewise appears in every single one.

## {{{ 20.57 }}} {{ 16 }} § 16.

As _encyclopaedia_, science is not presented in the detailed development of its particularization but is to be confined to the beginnings and the basic concepts of the particular sciences.

::: remark
{{ 16 A }} How much of the particular parts it takes to constitute a particular science is indeterminate only insofar as the part, in order to be something true, must be not an isolated moment but itself a totality. The whole of philosophy therefore truly constitutes _One_ science, but it can also be regarded as a whole of several particular sciences. – The philosophical encyclopaedia is distinguished from another, ordinary encyclopaedia in that the latter is supposed to be an _aggregate_ of sciences taken up contingently and empirically, among which are also some that bear only the name of sciences, but are otherwise themselves a mere collection of items of knowledge. The unity into which the sciences are brought together in such an aggregate is, because they have been taken up externally, likewise an _external_ one, – _an_ _order_. For the same reason, and since the materials too are of a contingent nature, this must remain an _attempt_, and always show unsuitable sides. – Besides this, the philosophical encyclopaedia excludes 1) mere _aggregates_ of items of knowledge – as philology, for instance, at first appears to be – and likewise, of course, 2) those that have mere arbitrariness as their ground, as heraldry, for instance; sciences of the latter kind are those _positive_ _through_ _and_ _through_. 3) Other sciences too are called _positive_, which nevertheless have a rational ground and beginning. This component belongs to philosophy; the _positive_ _side_, however, remains peculiar to them. The positive element of the sciences is of various kinds; 1) their beginning, rational in itself, passes over into the contingent in that they have to bring the universal down into _empirical_ _singularity_ and _actuality_. In this field of changeability and contingency not the _concept_ but only _grounds_ can be made to count. The science of right {{{ 20.58 }}}, for instance, or the system of direct and indirect taxes, require _final_ _precise_ decisions that _lie_ _outside_ the _determinedness_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_ _of_ _the_ _concept_ and therefore allow a latitude for determination, which according to one ground can be grasped one way and according to another ground another way, and is capable of no secure ultimate. Likewise the idea of _nature_ loses itself in its isolation in contingencies, and _natural_ _history_, _geography_, _medicine_ and so forth get into determinations of concrete existence, into kinds and differences, that are determined by external chance and by play, not by reason. _History_ too belongs here insofar as the idea is its essence, but its appearance is in contingency and in the field of arbitrary will. 2) Such sciences are also _positive_ insofar as they do not cognize their determinations as _finite_, nor show the transition of them and of their whole sphere into a higher one, but accept them as _absolutely_ _valid_. With this finitude of _form_, as the first is the finitude of _material_, there is connected 3) that of the _ground_ _of_ _cognition_, which is in part ratiocination, in part feeling, faith, the authority of other people, and in general that of inner or outer intuition. Philosophy too, which wants to base itself on anthropology, facts of consciousness, inner intuition or outer experience, belongs here. It may also be that merely the _form_ _of_ _scientific_ _presentation_ is empirical, but meaningful intuition orders what are only appearances in the manner in which the inner sequence of the concept stands. It belongs to such empirical observation that, through the counterposition and manifoldness of the appearances compiled, the _external_, _contingent_ _circumstances_ of the conditions are sublated, whereby the _universal_ then comes before the senses. – A thoughtful experimental physics, history and so forth will in this way present the rational science of nature and of human events and deeds in an external image mirroring the concept.

## {{{ 20.59 }}} {{ 17 }} § 17.

For the _beginning_ that philosophy has to make, it seems in general to begin, like the other sciences, with a subjective presupposition, namely of having to make a particular object, as elsewhere space, number and so forth, so here _thinking_ the object of thinking. But this is the free act of thinking, of placing itself at the standpoint where it is for itself and thereby _itself_ _produces_ and _gives_ _its_ _object_. Furthermore, the standpoint that thus appears as _immediate_ must, within the science, make itself into the _result_, and indeed its last, where it regains its beginning and comes back to itself. Philosophy thus presents itself as a circle that runs back into itself, one that has no beginning in the sense of other sciences, so that the beginning has only a relation to the subject who wants to resolve to philosophize, but not to the science as such. – Put otherwise, the concept of science, and so the first, – which, being first, carries the separation of thinking standing as object for a (so to speak external) philosophizing subject, – has to be grasped by the science itself. Indeed that is its one purpose, activity and aim: to reach the concept of its concept and thereby its return and satisfaction.

## {{ 18 }} § 18.

A philosophy admits of no preliminary general representation, since the presentation of the idea is nothing less than the _whole_ of the science; so its _division_ too can only be conceived out of this whole, and is, like the whole from which it must be taken, something anticipated. The idea, however, proves to be thinking absolutely identical with itself, and this at the same time to be the activity of setting itself over against itself, in order to be for itself, and of being only with itself in {{{ 20.60 }}} this other. Thus the science divides into the three parts:

+ I. The _logic_, being the science of the idea in and for itself,
+ II\. The _philosophy_ _of_ _nature_, being the science of the idea in its otherness,
+ III\. The _philosophy_ _of_ _spirit_, as that of the idea that returns into itself from its otherness.

::: remark
{{ 18 A }} § 15 above noted that the particular philosophical sciences differ merely by determinations of the idea itself, and that the idea alone is what presents itself in these various elements. In nature it is nothing other than the idea that would be cognized, but it is in the form of _externalization_, just as in spirit it is the very same idea _as_ _being_ _for_ _itself_ and _becoming_ _in_ _and_ _for_ _itself_. Each such determination in which the idea appears is simultaneously a _fluid_ moment; hence the single science is just as much this, to cognize its content as an object that _is_, as also this, to cognize immediately in it its transition into its higher circle. The _representation_ of the _division_ therefore has the incorrectness that it sets the particular parts or sciences _side_ _by_ _side_, as though they were merely at rest and, in their distinction, substantial, like _species_.
