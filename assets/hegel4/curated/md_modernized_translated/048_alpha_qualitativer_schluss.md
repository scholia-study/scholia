## α) Qualitative Syllogism

## {{ 183 }} § 183.

The first syllogism is the _syllogism_ _of_ _existence_, or the _qualitative_ one, as given in the preceding §, 1) S – P – U: a subject as singular _is_ _closed_ _together_ through _one_ _quality_ with a _universal_ _determinateness_.

::: remark
{{ 183 A }} Here no account is taken of the fact that the subject (the minor term) has further determinations than singularity, or that the other extreme (the predicate of the conclusion, the major term) is further determined than merely to be a universal; only the forms by which they make the syllogism count.
:::

## {{{ 20.194 }}} {{ 184 }} § 184.

In its determinations this syllogism is α) wholly _contingent_: the middle, as abstract particularity, is _merely_ _some_ _determinateness_ of the subject, and since the subject, as _immediate_ and so empirically concrete, has several such, it can be joined with as _manifold_ _other_ universalities; likewise a _single_ particularity can in turn have various determinatenesses within itself, so that the subject can be referred through _the_ _same_ medius terminus to _different_ universals.

::: remark
{{ 184 A }} Formal syllogizing has gone out of fashion rather than having been seen through as incorrect, with its disuse justified on that ground. This § and the next record the nullity of such syllogizing for truth.

From the side given in the §, the most diverse things, as people put it, can be _proved_ by such syllogisms. All that is needed is to take the medius terminus by which the transition to the required determination can be made. By an [other] medius terminus, however, something else, even the opposite, can be _proved_. – The more concrete an object, the more sides it has belonging to it that can serve as media termini. Which of these sides is more essential than the others must in turn rest on such syllogizing, which holds to the single determinateness and can likewise readily discover for it some aspect and _respect_ in which it can be made to _count_ as _important_ and _necessary_.
:::

## {{ 185 }} § 185.

β) This syllogism is just as contingent through the form of the _relation_ that is in it. According to the concept of the syllogism, what is true is the relation {{{ 20.195 }}} of distinct terms through a middle, which is their unity. Relations of the extremes to the middle, however, (the so-called _premises_, the _major_ _premise_ and the _minor_ _premise_) are rather _immediate_ relations.

::: remark
{{ 185 A }} This contradiction of the syllogism is again expressed through an infinite _progress_, as the demand that the premises too each be proved through a syllogism; but since this has two just such immediate premises, this demand, and indeed an ever-doubling one, repeats itself _into_ _infinity_.
:::

## {{ 186 }} § 186.

The _deficiency_ of the syllogism that has been noted here (for the sake of its empirical importance), a syllogism to which in this form absolute correctness is attributed, must sublate itself of its own accord in the syllogism's further determination. Here, in the sphere of the concept as in the judgment, the _opposite_ determinateness is not merely present _in_ _itself_ but _posited_; hence for the syllogism's further determination one need take up only what the syllogism itself in each case posits.

The immediate syllogism S–P–U mediates the _singular_ with the universal, so that in this _conclusion_ the singular is posited as _universal_. The singular, [as] subject, thus itself universal, now becomes the unity of the two extremes and what mediates, which yields the _second_ _figure_ of the syllogism, 2) U–S–P. What it expresses is the truth of the first figure: the mediation has occurred in singularity and is therefore something contingent.

## {{{ 20.196 }}} {{ 187 }} § 187.

The second figure closes together the universal (which, determined through singularity out of the preceding conclusion, passes over here, and thus now takes the place of the immediate subject) with the particular. The _universal_ is thereby posited by this conclusion as particular, and so as what mediates the extremes, whose places the others now take; the _third_ _figure_ of the syllogism: 3) P–U–S.

::: remark
{{ 187 A }} The so-called _figures_ of the syllogism (Aristotle rightly knows only _three_ of them; the _fourth_ is a superfluous, indeed even tasteless addition of the moderns) are set side by side in the usual discussion of them, with not the least thought of showing their necessity, still less their meaning and worth. It is therefore no wonder that the figures were later treated as an empty formalism. They have, however, a quite thoroughgoing sense, which rests on the necessity for _every_ _moment_, as a determination of the concept, to become itself the _whole_ and the _mediating_ _ground_. – As for the further determinations the propositions may need, whether universal and so forth or negative, if a _correct_ syllogism is to result in the various figures, this is a _mechanical_ inquiry, rightly consigned to oblivion owing to its conceptless mechanism and inner insignificance. – Least of all can one appeal, for the importance of such investigation and of the syllogism of the understanding in general, to _Aristotle_, who admittedly described these as well as countless other forms of spirit and of nature, and sought out and stated their determinateness. In his metaphysical {{{ 20.197 }}} _concepts_ as in his _concepts_ of nature and of spirit, he was so far from wanting the understanding's syllogistic form as foundation and criterion that one could say not a single one of these concepts could have arisen or been left standing had it been made subject to the laws of the understanding. Amid the much that is descriptive and of the understanding which Aristotle essentially contributes in his manner, what rules with him is always the _speculative_ concept, and that syllogizing of the understanding, which he was the first to state so determinately, he does not let pass over into this sphere.
:::

## {{ 188 }} § 188.

Because each moment has passed through the place of the middle and of the extremes, their determinate _difference_ from one another has been _sublated_, and the syllogism, at first in this form where its moments are undifferentiated, has as its relation the understanding's external identity, _equality_; – the _quantitative_, or _mathematical_, syllogism. Things _equal_ to a third are equal to each other.

## {{ 189 }} § 189.

Through this, there has come about at first in the _form_: 1) each moment has assumed the determination and place of the _middle_, and so of the whole in general, thereby losing _in_ _itself_ the one-sidedness of its abstraction (§§ 182 and 184); 2) _the_ _mediation_ (§ 185) has been completed, likewise only _in_ _itself_, namely only as a _circle_ of mediations that presuppose one another. With the first figure S–P–U, the premises S–P and P–U are left unmediated; the former gets mediated by the third figure, the latter by the second. Yet each of the two figures, to mediate its premises, equally presupposes its two other figures.

Accordingly, the mediated unity of the concept is to be posited no longer as abstract particularity alone but as the _developed_ unity of singularity and {{{ 20.198 }}} universality, and at first as the _reflected_ unity of these determinations; singularity being determined _at_ _the_ _same_ _time_ as universality. Such a middle yields the _syllogism_ _of_ _reflection_.
