## b. Impact

## {{ 265 }} § 265.

The inert body, posited in motion externally, which is thereby finite, and so related to another, makes up with it for a moment one body, for they are masses of merely quantitative difference; motion is in this way _one_ motion of both bodies (communication of motion). But they equally offer resistance to each other, since each is likewise presupposed as {{{ 20.258 }}} an immediate one. This their _being-for-itself_ against one another, further particularized by the quantum of mass, is their relative _gravity_, – _weight_ as the _gravity_ of a quantitatively particular mass (extensively as a multitude of heavy parts, – intensively as determinate pressure, see § 103, Remark); – which as real determinateness makes up with the ideal one, the quantitative determinateness of motion, – velocity, – _one_ determinateness (quantitas motus), within which those two can reciprocally take each other's places, compare § 261, Remark.

## {{ 266 }} § 266.

This weight, as intensive magnitude concentrated in one point in the body itself, is its _center of gravity_, but the body, as heavy, is this: to posit and to have its center _outside_ _itself_. Impact and resistance, like the motion posited through them, therefore have a substantial foundation in a _center_ common to the single bodies and lying outside them, and their externally posited accidental motion passes over into _rest_ in this center. This rest is at the same time, since the center is outside matter, only a _striving_ toward the center, and, according to the relation of the matter particularized into bodies and striving jointly there, a _pressure_ of that matter on one another. This striving, in the relation of the body's _being-separated_ from the center of its gravity by a relatively empty space, is the _fall_, the _essential_ motion, into which that accidental one _passes_ over according to the concept, as it does into rest according to existence.

::: remark
{{ 266 A }} For _external_, finite motion it is the principle of mechanics that a body that rests would rest in eternity, and one that is in motion would continue in motion in eternity, _if_ _it_ _were_ _not_ transferred by an _external_ cause from the one state into the other {{{ 20.259 }}}. This says nothing other than motion and rest pronounced according to the _law_ _of_ _identity_ (§ 115): motion _is_ motion, and rest _is_ rest; the two determinations are external to each other. It is only these abstractions of motion by itself and of rest by itself that produce the empty assertion of a motion continuing _eternally_, _if_ _not_ – and so forth. The law of identity, which is their basis, has been shown in its nullity in its own place. That assertion has no _empirical_ ground; already impact as such is conditioned by gravity, that is, by the determination of falling. The _throw_ shows the _accidental_ motion against the _essential_ motion of fall; but the abstraction, the body qua body, is inseparably linked with its gravity, and so in the case of the throw this gravity of itself forces itself upon us as something that has to be taken into account. The throw, as separated, _existing_ _for_ _itself_, cannot be demonstrated. The example for the motion that is supposed to derive from the vis centrifuga is usually the stone in a sling, moved in a circle by the hand, which always shows the striving to move away from it, Newton phil. nat. princ. math\. Defin\. Vta. But what matters is not that such a direction _exist_, but that it exist _separated_ _from_ _gravity_ _for_ _itself_, as it is represented as made wholly self-subsistent in _force_. Newton assures us in the same place that a leaden ball in coelos abiret et motu abeundi pergeret in infinitum, _if_ (to be sure; _if_) one could _only_ impart the requisite velocity to it. Such a separation of the external and the essential motion belongs neither to experience nor to the concept, but only to abstracting reflection. It is one thing to _distinguish_ them, which is necessary, as well as mathematically to draw them as separated lines, to treat them as {{{ 20.260 }}} separated quantitative factors and so forth, and another to regard them as physically self-subsistent existences[^1].

But in the case of such a flying of the leaden ball into infinity, abstraction is supposed to be made also from the resistance of the air, from _friction_. That a perpetuum mobile, however correctly calculated and proved by theory, passes in its time, which is not long in coming, into rest, involves abstraction from gravity, and the phenomenon is ascribed wholly to _friction_. To this very obstacle are ascribed the gradual decrease of the _pendulum's motion_ and its eventual standstill; of the pendulum's motion, too, it is said that it would continue without cessation _if_ the friction could be removed. This resistance, which the body experiences in its accidental motion, certainly belongs to the necessary appearance of its non-self-subsistence. But just as the body finds obstacles in reaching the center of its central body without these obstacles removing its pressing, its gravity, so that resistance of friction checks the body's motion of throw without its gravity thereby having fallen away or the {{{ 20.261 }}} friction taking its place. Friction is an obstacle, but not the _essential_ check of the external, accidental motion. What remains is that finite motion is inseparably bound up with gravity, and, as accidental by itself, passes over into the direction of the latter, the substantial determination of matter, and succumbs to it.
:::

[^1]: _Newton_ (ibid. Defin. VIII.) says expressly: Voces Attractionis, Impulsus vel Propensionis cujuscunque in centrum, indifferenter et pro se mutuo promiscue usurpo; has vires non Physice sed Mathematice tantum considerando. Unde caveat lector, ne per hujusmodi voces cogitet me speciem vel modum actionis causamve aut rationem Physicam alicubi definiere, vel centris (quae sunt puncta Mathematica) vires vere et Physice tribuere; si forte aut centra trahere, aut vires centrorum esse dixero. Yet by introducing the representation of forces, _Newton_ moved the determinations away from physical actuality and made them _essentially_ self-subsistent. At the same time he himself spoke throughout of physical objects in these representations, and so too in the presentations of the so-called structure of the world, which are meant to be only _physical_ and not metaphysical, such _mutually_ _self-subsistent_ and independent forces, their attractions, impacts and the like are spoken of as physical existences, and treated on the basis of the law of identity.
