## {{{ 20.523 }}} β. External Public Law

## {{ 547 }} § 547.

War puts the self-subsistence of states at stake and, on one side, brings about the reciprocal recognition of the free individual peoples (§ 430); this universal recognition is then fixed, together with the particular entitlements the peoples have against one another, by means of _peace_ _settlements_ meant to last for ever. _External_ _public_ _law_ is based in part on these positive treaties, which in that regard contain only rights lacking genuine actuality (§ 545); in part on the so-called _law_ _of_ _nations_, whose universal principle is the presupposed _being-recognized_ of states, and which accordingly sets bounds to the otherwise unbound way they behave toward one another, so that peace stays possible; – it also distinguishes individuals as private persons from the state; and in general it is based [on] _customs_.
