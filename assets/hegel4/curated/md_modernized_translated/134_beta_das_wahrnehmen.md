## β) Perceiving

## {{ 420 }} § 420.

Consciousness, having passed out of sensibility, seeks to _take_ the object _truly_, that is, no longer as something merely immediate but as something mediated, reflected into itself, and universal. The object is thus a combination of the sensuous and of extended thought-determinations of concrete relations and connections. Consciousness's identity with the object is therefore not the abstract identity of _certainty_ any longer; it is a _determinate_ identity, a _knowing_.

::: remark
{{{ 20.426 }}} {{ 420 A }} The more precise stage of consciousness at which the _Kantian_ _philosophy_ grasps spirit is _perceiving_, which in general is the standpoint of our _ordinary_ _consciousness_ and, to a greater or lesser degree, of the _sciences_. One starts from sensuous certainties of single apperceptions or observations; these are to be raised to truth by being examined in their relation, reflected on, and, by being made, according to determinate categories, something necessary and universal at the same time, are to turn into _experiences_.
:::

## {{ 421 }} § 421.

This linking of the singular and the universal is a mixing, because the singular stays on as a being lying at the _ground_, and firm against the universal to which it is at the same time related. It is therefore the many-sided contradiction, – of the _single_ things of sensuous apperception, which are supposed to make up the _ground_ of universal experience, and of _universality_, which is rather meant to be the essence and the ground, – of _singularity_, which in its concrete content makes up _self-subsistence_, and of the manifold _properties_, which, freed from this negative bond and from one another, are rather self-subsistent _universal_ _matters_, (see § 123 ff.) and so forth. Here properly falls the contradiction of the finite through all the forms of the logical spheres, most concretely insofar as the something is determined as _object_ (§ 194 ff.).
