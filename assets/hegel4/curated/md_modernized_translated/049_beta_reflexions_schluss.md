## β) Syllogism of Reflection

## {{ 190 }} § 190.

Taken at first as 1) not merely the abstract _particular_ determinateness of the subject but at once as _all_ _singular_ _concrete_ subjects to which, among others, that determinateness also pertains, the middle gives the syllogism _of_ _allness_. Yet the major premise, which has for its subject the particular determinateness, the terminus medius, as allness, itself _presupposes_ the _conclusion_, which was meant to have that premise as its presupposition. Hence it rests 2) on _induction_, whose middle is the _complete_ singulars as such, a, b, c, d and so forth. But since immediate empirical singularity differs from universality and therefore can furnish no completeness, induction rests 3) on _analogy_, whose middle is a singular, though taken in the sense of its genus or essential determinateness, that is, its essential universality. – The first syllogism points to the second for its mediation, the second to the third; and this in turn demands a universality determined within itself, or singularity as genus, once the forms of external relation between singularity and universality have been gone through in the figures of the syllogism of reflection.

::: remark
{{ 190 A }} Through the syllogism of allness the deficiency pointed out in § 184 in the basic form of the syllogism of the understanding is remedied, but only in such a way that the new deficiency arises, namely that the major premise itself presupposes what ought to be the conclusion, as thus an _immediate_ proposition. – All human beings are mortal, _therefore_ Caius is mortal, – all metals are electrical conductors, _therefore_ also, for instance, copper. To be able to state those major premises, which express as _all_ the {{{ 20.199 }}} _immediate_ singulars and are supposed to be essentially _empirical_ propositions, the propositions about the _single_ Caius and the _single_ piece of copper must already have been established _beforehand_ as correct, each for itself. – What rightly strikes everyone in such syllogisms – All human beings are mortal, but now Caius is, and so on – is not merely the pedantry but the vacuous formalism.
:::
