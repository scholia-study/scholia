## α. Internal Constitutional Law

## {{ 537 }} § 537.

The state's essence is what is universal in and for itself, the rational element of the will, which, however, in knowing and activating itself, is simply subjectivity, and, as actuality, one individual. As regards the extreme of singularity, the multitude of individuals, the state's _work_ in general is twofold: _on_ _the_ _one_ _hand_ to maintain them as persons, thereby making _right_ into necessary actuality, and then to further their _welfare_, which each at first looks after for himself or herself but which simply has a universal side, to protect the family and to guide civil society; – _on_ _the_ _other_ _hand_ to draw both, along with the entire disposition and activity of the single one who strives to be a center for itself, back into universal substance's life, and so, as a free power, to curtail the spheres subordinate to it and hold them in substantial immanence.

## {{ 538 }} § 538.

The _laws_ declare the determinations of content belonging to objective freedom. First, for the immediate subject, for its self-subsistent arbitrary will and particular interest, they are restrictions. But _second_ they are the absolute _final_ _purpose_ and the universal _work_; as such they come about through the functions of the different _estates_, which single themselves out further from the universal particularization, together with the entire activity and private concern of the _single_ _ones_; and third, they are the substance of the single ones' willing, _free_ in them, and of their disposition, and are so presented as a valid _custom_.

## {{{ 20.509 }}} {{ 539 }} § 539.

The state, as living spirit, is simply an organized whole: it is differentiated into particular activities, and these arise from the one concept of the rational will, though not known as that concept, and ceaselessly produce this will as their result. The _constitution_ is this articulation of the _power_ _of_ _the_ _state_. It contains the determinations of how the rational will, which in the individuals is universal only _in_ _itself_, partly comes to consciousness and comprehension of itself and is _found_, and partly is set into actuality by the working of the government and its particular branches and is maintained there, and likewise protected against the contingent subjectivity of those branches as against that of the single ones. It is _justice_ as it exists, freedom's actuality in the unfolding of all of freedom's rational determinations.

::: remark
{{ 539 A }} _Freedom_ and _equality_ are the simple categories into which what ought to make up the constitution's basic determination, final goal and result has often been gathered. True as this is, the deficiency of these determinations lies first in their being wholly abstract; when they are held fast in this form of abstraction, it is they that either keep the concrete from arising or else destroy it – the concrete being an articulation of the state, a _constitution_ and government in general. With the state, inequality enters, the difference between governing powers and the governed, between magistrates, authorities, directorates and so forth. Followed through consistently, the principle of equality rejects all differences and thus lets no kind of state condition subsist. – Foundations of this sphere they certainly are, yet because they are the most abstract they are also the most superficial, and precisely for that reason they are readily the most current; it is therefore of interest to consider them a little {{{ 20.510 }}} more closely. As regards _equality_ first, the current proposition that _all_ _human_ _beings_ _are_ _equal_ _by_ _nature_ contains the misunderstanding of confusing what is natural with the concept; one must say that by _nature_ human beings are rather simply _unequal_. The _concept_ of freedom, however, as it first exists as such without further determination and development, is abstract subjectivity as _person_, capable of property, § 488; this single abstract determination, personality, is what makes up the actual _equality_ of human beings. That this equality is at hand, that it is _the_ _human_ _being_, and not, as in Greece, Rome and so forth, only _some_ human beings, who is recognized as a person and counts legally – this is so far from being by _nature_ that it is rather only the product and result of the consciousness of the deepest principle of spirit and of the universality and cultivation of this consciousness. – That citizens are _equal_ _before_ _the_ _law_ contains a high truth, which, however, expressed in this way, is a tautology; for it says no more than that the _lawful_ condition in general obtains, that laws rule. But with regard to the concrete, citizens are equal before the law, apart from personality, only in that in which they are otherwise equal _outside_ _it_. Only an equality of property, age, physical strength, talent, skill and so forth, or even of crimes and so forth, that is otherwise _contingently_ _present_, in whatever way, can and ought to justify an equal treatment of them before the law – with regard to taxes, military service, admission to public office and so forth – punishment and so forth – in the concrete. Unequal conditions are presupposed by the laws themselves, save where they touch that narrow circle of personality, and it is they that determine the unequal legal competences and duties arising from those conditions.

{{{ 20.511 }}} As regards _freedom_, it is taken most immediately partly in the _negative_ sense, as against foreign arbitrary will and lawless treatment, and partly in the _affirmative_ sense of _subjective_ freedom; to this freedom, however, wide latitude is given, both for one's own arbitrary will and activity toward one's particular purposes, and in respect of the claim of one's own insight and of busy participation in universal affairs. In former times the legally determined rights of a nation, city and so forth, private and public alike, went by the name of its _freedoms_. And indeed each genuine law is a freedom: it holds a determination of reason that belongs to objective spirit, and so a content of freedom. Against this, nothing has become more current than the notion that each must _limit_ his or her freedom in relation to the freedom of the others, and that the _state_ is the condition of this reciprocal limiting and the laws the limitations. In such notions freedom is grasped only as contingent liking and arbitrary will. – It has also been claimed that modern peoples are fit only, or rather more, for _equality_ than for _freedom_, and presumably for no other reason than that, with a determination of freedom that had been assumed (the participation of all in the affairs and actions of the state above all), one could not get along in actuality – actuality proving at once more rational and more powerful than abstract presuppositions. – Rather must it be said that it is just the high development and cultivation of modern states that produces the highest concrete _inequality_ of individuals in actuality, while the laws' deeper rationality and the consolidation of the lawful condition bring about a greater and better grounded freedom, which the state can admit and bear. Even the superficial distinction lying in the words freedom and equality points to the fact that the former is directed to inequality; conversely, though, the usual notions of freedom lead back only to equality. But the more freedom is consolidated as security of property, as possibility of developing one's talents and good qualities and bringing them to bear {{{ 20.512 }}} and so forth, the more it appears to _go_ _without_ _saying_; the consciousness and esteem of freedom then turn chiefly toward its _subjective_ sense. This, however, itself – the freedom of an activity that tries itself out on all sides and ranges at its own pleasure over particular and universal spiritual interests, the independence of individual particularity, like the inner freedom in which the subject has principles, insight and conviction of its own and thereby gains moral self-subsistence – on the one hand holds within itself the highest cultivation of that particularity wherein human beings are unequal and, by this cultivation, make themselves more unequal still, and on the other hand grows up only under the condition of that objective freedom, and only in modern states has been able to grow to this height. If with this cultivation of particularity the multitude of needs and the difficulty of satisfying them, the ratiocination and know-it-all attitude and its unsatisfied vanity, grow beyond all bounds, this belongs to abandoned particularity, which is left to generate all possible entanglements in its own sphere and to come to terms with them. This sphere is then, to be sure, at the same time the field of limitations, because freedom is caught up in naturalness, liking and arbitrary will and so has to limit itself, and indeed in part by the naturalness, liking and arbitrary will of the others, but chiefly and essentially by rational freedom.

As for _political_ freedom, however, namely in the sense of a formal participation in the public affairs of the state by the will and busyness of those individuals too who otherwise make the particular purposes and business of civil society their chief vocation, custom has to some extent come to reserve the name of constitution for that side of the state alone which concerns such a share of those individuals in universal affairs, and to see a state in which no such share formally occurs as a {{{ 20.513 }}} state without a constitution. Of this meaning it need first be said only that constitution means the determination of rights, that is, of _freedoms_ in general, together with the organization that realizes them, and that political freedom can in any case make up only a part of these; it will be discussed in the following §§.

## {{ 540 }} § 540.

A constitution's _guarantee_, that is, the necessity that the laws be rational and their realization secured, resides in the spirit of the whole people, namely in the determinateness by which that people possesses self-consciousness of its reason (religion being this consciousness in its absolute substantiality), – and then at once in the _actual_ _organization_ that corresponds to it, as the _development_ of that principle. The constitution presupposes that consciousness of spirit, and, conversely, spirit presupposes the constitution, for actual spirit itself has a determinate consciousness of its principles only in so far as these are present to it as existing.

::: remark
{{ 540 A }} The question to whom, to which authority and how organized, the power belongs of _making_ _a_ _constitution_ is the same as the question who is to make a people's spirit. If the notion of a constitution is severed from that of spirit, as though spirit could well exist, or have existed, without a constitution fitting it, that opinion proves only the shallowness of thinking about the connection of spirit, its consciousness of itself and its actuality. What is called _making_ a constitution has, because of this inseparability, never occurred in history, any more than the _making_ of a code of law; a constitution has _only_ _developed_ out of spirit, identical with spirit's own development, and has gone through, together with it, the stages of cultivation {{{ 20.514 }}} and changes that the concept makes necessary. It is the indwelling spirit and history – and history is only _its_ history – by which constitutions have been and are made.
:::

## {{ 541 }} § 541.

It is the _government_ that is the living totality, the preservation, which is to say the continual production, of the state in general and of its constitution. Naturally necessary organization means the arising of the _family_ and of the _estates_ of civil society. Within the constitution, the _universal_ part is the government; its intentional purpose is to preserve those parts, but at the same time it grasps and activates the universal purposes of the whole, purposes that stand above the determination of the family and of civil society. Likewise the government's organization consists in its differentiation into powers, whose peculiarities the concept determines but which, in the concept's subjectivity, interpenetrate into _actual_ unity.

::: remark
{{ 541 A }} The nearest categories of the concept being _universality_ and _singularity_, and their relation being the _subsumption_ of singularity under universality, a _legislative_ and an _executive_ power have come to be distinguished in the state, but in such a way that the former _exists_ _for_ _itself_ as the absolutely supreme one, while the latter divides again into a _governmental_ or administrative power and a _judicial_ power, according to whether the laws are applied to universal or to private affairs. The _division_ of these powers, in the sense of their _independence_ of one another in existence, though with the mentioned connection of the subsumption of the powers of the singular under the power of the {{{ 20.515 }}} universal, has been regarded as the essential relation. The elements of the concept are not to be missed in these determinations, but the understanding has joined them into a relation of unreason instead of into the self-concluding-with-itself of living spirit. That the business of the state's universal interests be organized, in its necessary difference, _separately_ _from_ _one_ _another_ as well: this division is the one absolute moment in freedom's depth and actuality; freedom has depth only in so far as it has unfolded into its differences and brought them to existence. But to turn the business of legislating (and the more so with the notion that a constitution and the fundamental laws would at some time first have to be made – in a condition into which an already present development of the differences is put) into a self-subsistent power, and indeed into the _first_ one, with the closer determination of the participation of all in it, and to turn the governmental power into one dependent on it and merely executive, – this presupposes that cognition is lacking of how the true idea, and with it living and spiritual actuality, is the self-concluding concept, and thereby _subjectivity_, which holds universality within itself as merely one moment. In the organization of the state, it is individuality that is the first and highest _pervading_ _determination_. The state is _one_ only through the governmental power and through its taking up into itself the particular business, to which even the itself particular, _abstract-for-itself_ business of legislation belongs. – As essential as everywhere, and alone true, is the rational relation of the logical, as against the external relation of the understanding, which gets no further than subsuming the single and particular under the universal. Whatever breaks up the unity of the logical-rational disorganizes actuality likewise.
:::

## {{ 542 }} § 542.

In the government as organic totality, a) the concept's _infinite_ unity _with_ _itself_ in its development, _subjectivity_, {{{ 20.516 }}} is the state's all-holding, resolving will, its highest peak as well as its all-pervading unity, – the _princely_ governmental power. The perfect form of the state, in which every moment of the concept has attained its free existence, shows this subjectivity not as a so-called _moral_ _person_, nor as a resolving _issuing_ _from_ _a_ _majority_ – forms in which the unity of the resolving will lacks _actual_ existence – but as individuality that is actual, the will of a single resolving individual; – _monarchy_. Reason that has _developed_ finds its constitution, therefore, in the monarchical one; every other constitution belongs to lower stages in the development and realization of reason.

::: remark
{{ 542 A }} To unite all the concrete powers of the state in one existence, as happens in the patriarchal condition, or, in the democratic constitution, by the participation of all in all business, runs of itself against the principle of the _division_ of powers, which is the moments of the idea come to developed freedom. But just as much must the division, the cultivation of the moments that has advanced to free totality, be led back into _ideal_ _unity_, that is, into _subjectivity_. What the cultivated differentiation, the realization of the idea, essentially involves is that this subjectivity has come into _actual_ existence as a _real_ moment; and this _actuality_ is solely the individuality of the monarch, – the subjectivity of abstract, final deciding, there in one person. To all those forms of a _common_ resolving and willing that is to issue and be reckoned out, democratically or aristocratically, from the atomistics of single wills, clings the unactuality of an _abstraction_. What matters is just the two determinations: the necessity of _one_ _moment_ _of_ _the_ _concept_, and the form of its _actuality_. The nature of the speculative concept alone can truly reach an understanding about this. – That subjectivity, being the moment of abstract deciding in general, advances partly to the determination that the monarch's name appears as the external bond and sanction under which everything in the government in general takes place, and partly, as simple relation to itself, has the determination of _immediacy_ and thereby of _nature_, so that _heredity_ fixes the determination of individuals for the dignity of the princely power.
:::

## {{{ 20.517 }}} {{ 543 }} § 543.

b) The _particular_ governmental power brings, on the one hand, the _division_ of state business into its branches, determined as they otherwise are – legislative power, administration of justice or judicial power, administrative and police power and so forth – and with it the _distribution_ of these among particular authorities, which are referred to the laws for their business, and for this reason possess independence in their activity while at the same time standing under higher supervision; – on the other hand it brings the participation of _several_ in the state's business: together they make up the universal estate (§ 528), in so far as they make universal purposes the essential vocation of their particular life, and a further condition for being able to take part in it, individually, is cultivation and skill for it.

## {{ 544 }} § 544.

c) The _estates_ authority concerns a participation in the governmental power, and indeed in legislation, namely in the _universal_ of the interests that do not concern the state's appearing and acting as an individual (like war and peace) and therefore do not belong merely to the nature of the princely power by itself, by all those who belong to civil society in general and are in so far private persons. By virtue of this participation, subjective freedom and conceit, together with their universal opinion, can show themselves in an existing activity and enjoy the satisfaction of counting for something.

::: remark
{{ 544 A }} The division of constitutions into _democracy_, _aristocracy_ and _monarchy_ still gives their difference in relation to state power in the most determinate way. At the same time they have to be seen as configurations necessary in the course of development, and so in the history of the state. To represent them as {{{ 20.518 }}} an object of _choice_ is therefore superficial and foolish. In so far as the pure forms of their necessity are finite and transitory, they are tied up partly with forms of their degeneration, ochlocracy and so forth, and partly with earlier transitional shapes; neither of these two sorts of form is to be mistaken for those genuine configurations. So it is that, on account of the equality in having the will of one individual at the head of the state, oriental despotism falls under the vague name of monarchy, and so does feudal monarchy too, to which the popular name of constitutional monarchy cannot even be denied. What truly distinguishes these forms from genuine monarchy rests on the import of the _valid_ _principles_ _of_ _right_ that have their actuality and guarantee in state power. These principles are the ones developed in the earlier spheres, those of the freedom of property and indeed of personal freedom, of civil society, its industry and the communities, and of the regulated activity of the particular authorities, dependent on the laws.

The question that has been most discussed is in what sense the participation of _private_ _persons_ in the affairs of the state is to be understood. Members of estates assemblies, after all, are first of all to be taken as _private_ _persons_, whether they count as individuals on their own or as representatives of _many_ or of the _people_. Commonly it is the aggregate of private persons that goes by the name of the _people_; as such an aggregate, though, it is vulgus, not populus; and in this respect it is the sole purpose of the state that a people _not_ _as_ _such_ _an_ _aggregate_ should come to existence, power and action. Such a condition of a people is a condition of wrongfulness, unethicalness, unreason in general; the people would be in it only a formless, wild, blind force, like that of the agitated, elemental sea, which, however, does not destroy itself, as the people, being a spiritual element, would do. One has often been able to hear such a condition represented as that of true freedom. For it to make sense to engage the question of private persons' participation in universal affairs, what must be presupposed is not what is unreasonable but an already organized people, that is, one in which a governmental power is present {{{ 20.519 }}}. – Yet the interest of such participation is to be sought neither in an advantage of particular insight in general which private persons are supposed to hold over state officials, – necessarily the opposite is the case; – nor in an advantage of good will for the universal best, – the members of civil society are rather, as such, people who make their particular interest, and, as chiefly in the feudal condition, that of their privileged corporation, their nearest vocation. Experience shows of _England_, for instance, whose constitution is regarded as the freest because private persons have a preponderant share in the business of the state, that this country is the furthest behind the other cultivated states of Europe in civil and criminal legislation, in the right and freedom of property, in arrangements for art and science and so forth, and that objective freedom, that is, rational right, is rather _sacrificed_ to formal freedom and to particular private interest (even in the arrangements and possessions that are supposed to be dedicated to religion). – The interest in a share of private persons in public affairs is in part to be located in the more concrete and therefore more pressing feeling of universal needs, but essentially in the right that the common spirit also attain the appearance of an _externally_ _universal_ will in an ordered and explicit activity for the public concern, receive from this satisfaction likewise an enlivening for itself, just as such an enlivening flows in upon the administrative authorities, who are thereby kept in present consciousness that, much as they have duties to demand, they equally have essential rights before them. In the state the citizens are the disproportionately larger multitude, and a multitude of persons recognized as persons. Willing reason therefore presents its existence in them as a plurality of free ones, or as their universality of reflection, to which actuality is granted in a share in state power. Yet it was already brought out as a moment of civil society {{{ 20.520 }}} (§§ 527, 534) that the single ones lift themselves out of external universality into substantial universality, and do so as a _particular_ kind, – _the_ _estates_; they enter upon that share not in the inorganic form of single ones as such (the _democratic_ manner of electing) but as organic moments, as estates; no power or activity in the state may ever show itself or act in a formless, inorganic shape, that is, on the principle of plurality and of the multitude.

Estates assemblies have wrongly been designated as _the_ _legislative_ _power_ already in the respect that they make up only one branch of this power, in which the particular governmental authorities have an essential share and the princely power the absolute share of the final decision. Moreover, in a cultivated state legislating can in any event be only a further forming of laws already there, and what are called new laws can only be details and particularities pushed to the extreme (compare § 529 Remark), the content of which the practice of the courts has already prepared, or even provisionally decided. – The _finance_ _law_, as it is called, is essentially _a_ _governmental_ _affair_ in so far as the estates come to codetermine it; the name of _law_ fits it only improperly, in the general sense that it embraces a wide range, indeed the entire range, of the government's external means. Finances concern, though as a complex, yet by their nature only the _particular_ and ever anew generated changeable needs. Were the chief component of the requirement taken as lasting – and it is – its determination would come nearer to the nature of a law; but for it to be a law, it would have to be laid down once and for all, rather than anew each year or every few years. The part that varies with time and circumstances in {{{ 20.521 }}} fact concerns the smallest portion of the sum, and for that reason its determination has still less the character of a law; yet only this small variable part is, and can be, open to discussion and subject to a variable, yearly determination, which therefore falsely bears the grand-sounding name of the _granting_ of the _budget_, that is, of the _whole_ of the finances. A law to be given for one year and annually strikes even common human sense as inappropriate, since it distinguishes what is universal in and for itself, as the content of a genuine law, from a universality of reflection which merely externally embraces what by its nature is many. Calling the annual fixing of the financial requirement a _law_ serves merely to uphold, under the presupposed separation of the legislative from the governmental power, the deception that this separation really takes place, and to conceal that in resolving on the finances the legislative power is in fact engaged in proper governmental business. – The interest, however, that is placed in the ability to grant the financial estimate again and again, namely that the estates assembly possess in it a _means_ _of_ _coercion_ against the government and so a guarantee against wrong and violence, – this interest is, on the one hand, a superficial shine, since the provision of finances necessary for the _subsistence_ of the state cannot be made conditional on any other circumstances, nor the state's subsistence be put in yearly doubt; any more than the government could grant and order the provision of the administration of justice only ever for a limited time, so as to keep for itself a means of coercion against private persons by threatening to suspend the activity of such an institution, with the fear of a robber-condition setting in. On the other hand, notions of a relation for which it might be useful and requisite to hold means of coercion rest in part on the false notion of a contractual relation between government and people, and in part presuppose the possibility of such a divergence of the spirit of the two that no thought can any longer be given to constitution and government. Should the empty possibility of _helping_ by such a means of coercion {{{ 20.522 }}} be imagined as come into being, such help would instead be the state's derangement and dissolution, a condition with no government any longer but only parties, whose remedy could be only force and the oppression of one party by another. – To represent the state's arrangement as a mere constitution of the understanding, that is, as the mechanism of an equilibrium of powers external to one another within it, runs against the basic idea of what a state is.
:::

## {{ 545 }} § 545.

The state has, finally, the side of being the immediate actuality of a _single_ and _naturally_ determined people. As a single individual it is _exclusive_ toward _other_ individuals of the same kind. In their _relation_ to one another arbitrary will and contingency hold sway, because the _universal_ of right is between them, by reason of the autonomous totality of these persons, only something that _ought_ to be, not something _actual_. This independence turns the dispute between them into a relation of force, a _condition_ _of_ _war_, for which the universal estate determines itself, with the particular purpose of preserving the state's self-subsistence against others, as the _estate_ _of_ _bravery_.

## {{ 546 }} § 546.

This condition shows the state's substance advancing, in its individuality, to abstract negativity; it is the power in which the single ones' particular self-subsistence, and the condition of their being sunk in natural life and in the external existence of possession, feels itself to be a _nullity_, and which mediates the preservation of the universal substance by means of the sacrificing, carried out in the substance's disposition, of this natural and particular existence, the frustration of what is vain by comparison.
