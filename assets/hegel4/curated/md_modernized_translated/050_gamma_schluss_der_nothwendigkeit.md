## γ) Syllogism of Necessity

## {{ 191 }} § 191.

Considered in its merely abstract determinations, this syllogism takes the _universal_ as its middle, as the syllogism of reflection takes _singularity_, – the latter following the second figure, the former the third (§ 187); – the universal being posited as essentially determined in itself. First, 1) the mediating determination is the _particular_, understood as determinate _genus_ or _species_, – _in_ _the_ _categorical_ syllogism. 2) Then comes the _singular_, understood as immediate being, which is to be mediating just as much as mediated, – _in_ _the_ _hypothetical_ syllogism. 3) Finally the mediating _universal_ is posited as the totality of its _particularizations_ and likewise as one _single_ particular, as excluding singularity, – _in_ _the_ _disjunctive_ syllogism; – whereby one and the same universal exists in these determinations merely as in forms of difference.

## {{ 192 }} § 192.

The syllogism has been examined by the differences it holds, and the outcome of going through them is, in general, that these differences, and the concept's being-outside-itself, sublate themselves within it. More precisely, 1) each of the moments has shown itself to be the _totality_ of the moments, hence a whole syllogism, and they are thus identical _in_ _themselves_; and 2) the _negation_ of their differences, together with the mediation of those, makes up {{{ 20.200 }}} _being-for-itself_; so that what exists in these forms is one and the same universal, which is thereby posited as their identity as well. From this ideality of the moments, syllogizing gains the determination that it essentially contains the _negation_ of the determinatenesses by which it runs its course, and so is a mediation by way of sublating mediation, a joining of the subject not with an _other_ but with an other that has been _sublated_, that is, _with_ _itself_.

## {{ 193 }} § 193.

This _realization_ of the concept, where the universal is the _one_ totality returned into itself, where the differences of that totality are likewise this very totality, and which by sublating mediation has determined itself as _immediate_ unity, – is the _object_.

::: remark
{{ 193 A }} That the subject, the concept in general and more closely the syllogism, should pass over into the object may seem alien at first glance, especially if one has before one only the syllogism of the understanding and syllogizing as something consciousness does; yet there can be no question of making this passage plausible to representation. All that can be done is to ask whether our everyday representation of what goes by the name _object_ roughly matches what constitutes the determination of the object here. By an object, however, one is wont to understand not merely an abstract being, or an existing thing, or something actual in general, but a concrete self-subsistent that is _complete_ in itself; such completeness is the _totality_ _of_ _the_ _concept_. How the _object_ comes also to be something _confronted_ and _external_ to an other will be settled later, when it enters into _opposition_ with the _subjective_; here, at first, as what the concept has passed over into out of its mediation, it is just the _immediate_, unprejudiced object, in the way that the concept too becomes determined as the _subjective_ only in the later opposition.

The _object_ in general, moreover, is the _one_ whole, still undetermined further in itself, the objective world in general, God, the absolute object. {{{ 20.201 }}} Yet difference belongs to the object just as well; it breaks up within itself into an indeterminate manifoldness (as the objective _world_), and every one of these _isolated_ items is again an object, a concrete, complete, self-subsistent existence in itself.

Just as objectivity has been set beside being, concrete existence and actuality, so the passage to concrete existence and actuality (being being the first, wholly abstract immediate) can be set beside the passage to objectivity. The _ground_ out of which concrete existence emerges, and the _relation_ of reflection that sublates itself into actuality, are nothing but the _concept_ still imperfectly _posited_, or abstract sides of it only, – the ground being merely its essential _unity_, the relation merely the connection of _real_ sides which are only supposed to be _reflected_ _into_ _themselves_; – the concept unites the two, and the object is a unity which is not merely essential but universal within itself, containing not just real differences but those same differences as totalities.

It is evident, besides, that in all these passages more is at stake than merely showing, in general, that the concept or thinking cannot be separated from being. It has often been observed that _being_ is nothing beyond simple relation to itself, and that this poor determination is anyway contained in the concept, or in thinking too. These passages do not mean to take up determinations just as they are _contained_ (as happens in the ontological argument for God's existence, with its proposition that being is _one_ _of_ the realities); what they mean is to take the concept as it is at first _meant_ to be determined for itself, as concept, which this remote abstraction of being, or of objectivity, would as yet have nothing to do with, and to see, by its determinateness alone as <i>concept</i>-determinateness, whether, and that, it passes over into a form differing from the determinateness that belongs to the concept and _shows_ _itself_ _in_ _it_.

Suppose the product of this passage, the object, is set in relation to the concept, which has vanished into it as regards {{{ 20.202 }}} its own peculiar form: the result can then be stated _correctly_ by saying that concept, or if one likes subjectivity, and object are _the_ _same_ _in_ _themselves_. Equally _correct_, though, is that they are _different_; and since the one statement is as correct as the other, the one is as incorrect as the other too; such a way of speaking cannot present the true state of affairs. That _in-itself_ is an abstraction, more one-sided still than the concept, whose one-sidedness altogether cancels itself by sublating itself into the object, the opposite one-sidedness. So that _in-itself_ too must, through the _negation_ of itself, come to determine itself as _being-for-itself_. Speculative identity, as everywhere, is not the trivial one of concept and object being identical in themselves; – a remark repeated often enough already, yet one that could never be repeated often enough, were the aim to end the insipid and quite malicious misunderstandings about this identity; which, however, in all good sense, is once more not to be hoped for.

Take that unity, further, quite generally, leaving aside the one-sided form of its _being-in-itself_: it is, as everyone knows, what the _ontological_ _proof_ of God's existence _presupposes_, namely as the _most_ _perfect_. With _Anselm_, in whom the highly remarkable thought of this proof first appears, the discussion is admittedly at first only about whether a content exists merely in _our_ _thinking_. His words are briefly these: Certe id, quo majus cogitari nequit, non potest esse, in intellectu solo. Si enim vel in solo intellectu est, potest cogitari esse _et_ _in_ _re_: quod majus est. Si ergo id, quo majus cogitari non potest, est in solo intellectu; id ipsum, quo majus cogitari non potest, est, quo majus cogitari potest. Sed certe hoc esse non potest. – By the determinations in which we stand here, the _finite_ things are marked by their objectivity's failing to agree with the thought of them, that is, with what universally determines them, their genus and purpose. Descartes and Spinoza and so forth stated this unity in a more objective fashion; the principle of immediate certainty or faith, however, grasps it after Anselm's more subjective manner, {{{ 20.203 }}} namely that the representation of God is inseparably joined to the determination of His being _in_ _our_ _consciousness_. Should the principle of this faith extend the representations of external finite things to the inseparability of our consciousness of them from their being, because _in_ _intuition_ they are joined with the determination of concrete existence, that is quite right. But it would be the height of thoughtlessness to suppose that concrete existence is tied in our consciousness to the representation of finite things in the same manner as to the representation of God; this would overlook that finite things are changeable and transient, that is, that concrete existence attaches to them only fleetingly, and that this tie is separable, not eternal. Anselm, therefore, was right to set aside the kind of tie found with finite things and to declare perfect only what exists not merely subjectively but at the same time objectively. All the lofty airs taken against the so-called ontological proof and this Anselmian determination of the perfect achieve nothing, since it lies as much in every unprejudiced human mind as it returns, against knowledge and will, in every philosophy itself, as in the principle of immediate faith.

The shortcoming of Anselm's argument, though, which Descartes and Spinoza share with it, as does the principle of immediate knowing, is that this _unity_, pronounced as the most perfect or, subjectively, as true knowing, is _presupposed_, that is, merely assumed as _in_ _itself_. Against this identity, abstract as it is, the _diversity_ of the two determinations is promptly held up, as was long since done against Anselm; that is, in fact, the representation and concrete existence of the _finite_ is held up against the infinite, for, as just remarked, the finite is an objectivity which at once fails to fit its purpose, its essence and concept, and differs from it, – or a representation, a subjective, which does not involve concrete existence. This objection and opposition is sublated {{{ 20.204 }}} solely by showing up the finite as untrue, these determinations as one-sided and null _for_ _themselves_, and identity accordingly as that into which they themselves pass over and where they are reconciled.
