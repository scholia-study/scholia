## a. The System of Needs

## {{ 524 }} § 524.

α) The particularity of the persons at first embraces their needs. The possibility of satisfying these is placed here in the social connection, which is the universal _wealth_ from which all {{{ 20.499 }}} obtain their satisfaction. The _immediate_ taking possession (§ 488) of external objects as means to this no longer or hardly takes place in the condition in which this standpoint of mediation is realized; the objects are property. Their acquisition is conditioned and mediated, on the one hand, by the will of the possessors, which as particular has the satisfaction of the manifoldly determined needs as its purpose, and, on the other, by the ever renewing production of exchangeable means through _one's_ _own_ _labor_; this mediation of satisfaction through the labor of all makes up the universal wealth.

## {{ 525 }} § 525.

β) Universality shines into the particularity of needs at first in such a way that the understanding differentiates in them and thereby multiplies both them and the means for these differences indefinitely, and makes both ever more abstract; this singularizing of the content through abstraction yields _the_ _division_ _of_ _labor_. The habit of this abstraction in enjoyment, acquaintance, knowledge and conduct makes up _culture_ in this sphere, – _formal_ _culture_ in general.

## {{ 526 }} § 526.

Labor, being thereby at the same time more abstract, leads on the one hand, through its uniformity, to the ease of labor and the increase of production, on the other to confinement to one skill and thereby to a more unconditional dependence on the social connection. Skill itself in this way becomes mechanical and acquires the capacity to let the machine take the place of human labor.

## {{ 527 }} § 527.

γ) The concrete division, however, of the universal wealth, which is likewise a universal business, into the particular masses {{{ 20.500 }}} determined according to the moments of the concept, which possess a peculiar basis of subsistence and, connected with it, corresponding ways of labor, of needs and of the means of their satisfaction, further of purposes and interests as well as of spiritual culture and habit, makes up the _difference_ _of_ _estates_. – The individuals allot themselves to these according to natural talent, skill, arbitrary will and chance. Belonging to such a determinate, fixed sphere, they have their actual concrete existence, which as concrete existence is essentially a particular one, and in it their _ethical_ _life_ as _rectitude_, their being-recognized and their _honor_.

::: remark
{{ 527 A }} Where civil society, and with it the state, is present, the estates enter in their difference; for the universal substance as living _exists_ only in so far as it _particularizes_ itself organically; the history of constitutions is the history of the formation of these estates, of the legal relations of individuals to them, and of the estates to one another and to their center.
:::

## {{ 528 }} § 528.

The _substantial_, _natural_ estate has in fertile _ground_ and _soil_ a natural and secure wealth; its activity receives its direction and content through natural determinations, and its ethical life is grounded on faith and trust. The _second_, the _reflected_ estate is referred to the wealth of society, to the element placed in mediation, representation and a concurrence of contingencies, and the individual to one's own subjective skill, talent, understanding and diligence. The _third_, _thinking_ estate has the universal interests as its business; like the second it has a subsistence mediated through its own skill, and like the first one secured, however, through the whole of society.
