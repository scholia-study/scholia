## c. Being-for-itself

## {{ 96 }} § 96.

α) Being-for-itself is _immediacy_ insofar as it refers to _itself_, and, insofar as the negative refers to itself, it is the entity that is for itself, the one, – which lacks any difference within itself and therefore excludes the other from itself.

## {{{ 20.134 }}} {{ 97 }} § 97.

β) The negative's reference to itself is a _negative_ reference, and so the one differentiates itself from itself; this is the one's _repulsion_, that is, a positing of _many_ _ones_. Because what is for itself is _immediate_, such a multitude consists of _beings_, and the repulsion exerted by the ones that are turns, to that extent, into a repulsion _of_ _each_ _against_ _the_ _others_ as things at hand, a mutual _shutting-out_.

## {{ 98 }} § 98.

γ) Yet the _many_ are each what the other is, every one being a one or a one among the many; so they are one and the same. If one considers repulsion in itself, the _behavior_ by which the many ones negatively face each other is, just as essentially, a _bearing_ they have on each other; and since what the one refers to as it repels are likewise ones, its reference in them is to itself. Repulsion is hence just as essentially _attraction_; and the one that shuts out, that is, being-for-itself, sublates itself. Qualitative determinateness, having attained in the one its determinedness in and for itself, has thereby passed into determinateness _as_ _sublated_, that is, into being as _quantity_.

::: remark
{{ 98 A }} The _atomistic_ philosophy occupies the standpoint from which the absolute gets fixed in its determination as being-for-itself, as a one, and as a plurality of ones. The repulsion that the concept of the one reveals has been adopted as the atoms' fundamental force; attraction has not, but _chance_, that is, the thoughtless, is to unite them. Because the one is held fixed as one, its joining with others has to be regarded as thoroughly external. – The _void_, adopted as the second principle beside the atoms, is repulsion itself, pictured as the nothing that _is_ between the atoms. – Recent atomism, – and physics keeps clinging to this principle, – has dropped the atoms in so far as it sticks to small particles, molecules; this brings it closer to sensuous imagining but leaves behind the {{{ 20.135 }}} determination of thought. – When, besides, an attractive force is set next to the repulsive one, the opposition does become _complete_, and much was made of having discovered this so-called force of nature. But how the two relate to each other, which is what constitutes their concrete and true character, still needs to be wrested out of the murky confusion in which even _Kant’s_ metaphysical _first_ _principles_ of _natural_ _science_ leave it. – The atomistic view has become still more weighty in modern times in the _political_ domain than in the physical. On that view the state has its principle in the will of _singular_ individuals as such; what draws them together is the particularity of their needs and inclinations, and the universal, the state itself, amounts to the external bond of a contract.
:::
