## b. Content and Form

## {{ 133 }} § 133.

The asunderness of the world of appearance is totality and is wholly contained in its _self-relation_. The self-relation of appearance is thus completely determined, has the _form_ within itself, and, because it is in this identity, essential subsistence. Thus the form is _content_, and, according to its developed determinateness, the _law_ of appearance. Into the _form_ as _not_ _reflected_ _into_ _itself_ falls the negative of appearance, the non-self-subsistent and changeable, – it is the indifferent, _external_ _form_.

::: remark
{{ 133 A }} In the opposition of form and content it is essential to hold fast that the content is not formless, but has the _form_ _in_ _itself_ just as much _as_ the form is _an_ _external_ one to it. There is a doubling of form, which in one case, as reflected into itself, is the content, and in the other, as not reflected into itself, is the external concrete existence indifferent to the content. _In-itself_ there is present here the absolute relation of content and form, namely their turning over into one another: the _content_ is nothing other than _form_ _turned_ _over_ _into_ content, {{{ 20.159 }}} and _form_ nothing other than _content_ _turned_ _over_ _into_ form. This turning over ranks among the weightiest determinations. It is _posited_, however, only in the _absolute_ _relation_.
:::

## {{ 134 }} § 134.

But _immediate_ concrete existence is a determinateness of subsistence itself and of form alike; it is accordingly as external to the determinateness of the content as that externality, which the content has by way of the moment of its subsistence, is essential to it. Appearance so posited is the _relation_ in which one and the same thing, the content, is as the developed form, as externality and _counterposition_ of self-subsistent concrete existences and their _identical_ relation; in that relation the distinct are alone what they are.
