## β) The Drives and Arbitrary Will

## {{ 473 }} § 473.

The practical _ought_ is a _real_ judgment. The _immediate_ adequacy, merely _found_ at hand, of the _existent_ determinateness to need is, for the <i>self</i>-determination of the will, a negation and inadequate to it. That the will, that is, the unity, _in_ _itself_ existing, of universality and determinateness, should satisfy itself, that is, be _for_ _itself_, the _adequacy_ of its inner determination and of existence is _to_ be posited by it. As to the form of the content, the will is at first still _natural_ will, immediately identical with its determinateness, – _drive_ and _inclination_, and, insofar as the totality of practical spirit lays itself into a single one of the _many_ _limited_ determinations posited with opposition generally, _passion_.

## {{{ 20.471 }}} {{ 474 }} § 474.

The content of inclinations and passions consists of the same determinations as that of the practical feelings, and they likewise have the rational nature of spirit as their foundation on the one hand; on the other, however, as belonging to the still subjective, singular will they are encumbered with contingency, and appear as particular ones, external to the individual as to one another, and hence as relating themselves according to unfree necessity.

::: remark
{{ 474 A }} _Passion_ is, by its determination, confined to one _particularity_ of the will's determination, and into this the entire subjectivity of the individual plunges, whatever the import of that determination may otherwise be. By reason of this formal aspect, however, passion is neither good nor evil; this form expresses only that a subject has laid the whole living interest of its spirit, talent, character, enjoyment into one content. Nothing great has been accomplished without passion, nor can it be accomplished without it. Only a morality that is dead, and indeed all too often hypocritical, rails at the form of passion as such.

Concerning the inclinations, however, the question arises at once which of them are _good_ and which _evil_, likewise to what _degree_ the good ones remain good, and, since they are particular ones over against one another and many, how they, since they are after all in One subject and, according to experience, cannot well all be satisfied, must at least limit [themselves] against one another. It is at first with these many drives and inclinations just as with the forces of the soul, whose collection theoretical spirit is supposed to be; – a collection which is now augmented with the _multitude_ of {{{ 20.472 }}} drives. The _formal_ rationality of drive and inclination consists only in their universal drive not to be as something subjective, but to be realized by the activity of the subject itself in sublating subjectivity. Their true rationality cannot emerge from a consideration of _external_ reflection, which presupposes _self-subsistent_ determinations of nature and _immediate_ drives, and thereby lacks the One principle and final purpose for them. It is spirit's own immanent reflection, however, that goes beyond their _particularity_ and also their natural _immediacy_ and gives their content rationality and objectivity, wherein they are _necessary_ relations, _rights_ and _duties_. This objectification it is, then, that shows their import, as well as their relation to one another, in general their truth; as _Plato_, with true sense, also insofar as he comprised under _the_ _right_ _of_ _spirit_ its whole nature, showed himself able to present what _justice_ is in and for itself solely in the _objective_ shape of justice, that is, by constructing the _state_ as _ethical_ life.

Which, then, the _good_, rational inclinations are, and what their subordination is, is transformed into the presentation of what relations spirit produces in developing itself as _objective_ spirit; – a development in which the _content_ of self-determination loses contingency or arbitrary will. The treatment of the drives, inclinations and passions according to their true import is therefore essentially the _doctrine_ of the juridical, moral and ethical _duties_.
:::

## {{ 475 }} § 475.

The _subject_ is the _activity_ of the satisfaction of the drives, of formal rationality, namely of the translation from the subjectivity of the content, which to that extent {{{ 20.473 }}} _is_ purpose, into objectivity, in which it closes together with itself. That, insofar as the content of the drive is distinguished as the matter from this its activity, the matter that has come about contains the moment of subjective singularity and its activity, is the _interest_. Nothing, therefore, comes about without interest.

::: remark
{{ 475 A }} An action is a purpose of the subject, and equally it is its activity that carries out this purpose; only through this, that the subject is in this way in the most disinterested action, that is, through its interest, is there acting at all. – Over against drives and passions one sets, on the one hand, the insipid dreaming of a natural happiness by which needs are supposed to find satisfaction without any activity of the subject to bring immediate existence and its inner determinations into adequacy. On the other hand, morality, duty for duty's sake, is set against them quite generally. But drive and passion is nothing other than the vitality of the subject, according to which it is itself in its purpose and its execution. The ethical concerns the content, which as such is the _universal_, something inactive, and has its activating element in the subject; this, that it is immanent in the latter, is interest, and, when it lays claim to the whole effective subjectivity, passion.
:::

## {{ 476 }} § 476.

The will, as thinking and free in itself, distinguishes itself from the _particularity_ of the drives and places itself, as the simple subjectivity of thinking, above their manifold content; thus it is _reflecting_ will.

## {{{ 20.474 }}} {{ 477 }} § 477.

Such a particularity of the drive is in this way no longer immediate, but is only _its_ _own_, in that the will closes together with it and thereby gives itself determinate singularity and actuality. It stands at the standpoint of _choosing_ among inclinations, and is _arbitrary_ _will_.

## {{ 478 }} § 478.

Will as arbitrary will is free _for_ _itself_, because it has reflected itself into itself, being the negativity of its merely immediate self-determining. Yet insofar as the content in which this its formal universality _resolves_ itself into actuality is still no other than that of the drives and inclinations, it is _actual_ only as _subjective_ and _contingent_ will. Being the _contradiction_ of realizing itself in a particularity which for it is simultaneously a nullity, and of finding a satisfaction therein from which it is simultaneously removed, it is at first the _process_ of dispersal and of the sublating of one inclination or enjoyment by another, and of the satisfaction, which equally is none, by yet another, _into_ _infinity_. What is true in the _particular_ satisfactions, however, is the _universal_ satisfaction, which the thinking will sets as its purpose under the name of _happiness_.
