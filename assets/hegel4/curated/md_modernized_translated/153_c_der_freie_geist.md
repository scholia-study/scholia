## {{{ 20.476 }}} c. Free Spirit

## {{ 481 }} § 481.

Practical and theoretical spirit are united in the actual free will; this is _free_ _will_ that exists _for_ _itself_ _as_ _free_ _will_, because the formalism, contingency and limitedness of the practical content up to now have been sublated. Through the sublating of the mediation that was contained in it, it is the _immediate_ _singularity_ posited through itself, which however is equally purified to the _universal_ determination, freedom itself. This _universal_ determination is the will's only as its object and purpose, so far as the will _thinks_ itself and knows this its concept; it is _will_ as free _intelligence_.

## {{ 482 }} § 482.

Spirit that knows itself as free and wills itself as this its object, that is, has its essence as determination and as purpose, is at first generally the rational will, or the idea _in_ _itself_, and for that reason only the _concept_ of absolute spirit. As an _abstract_ idea, it is again existent only in the _immediate_ will, is the side of the _existence_ of reason, the _single_ will as knowing of that determination of its own which constitutes its content and purpose and whose merely formal activity it is. The idea thus appears only in the will, which is a finite one, but is the _activity_ of developing it and of positing its unfolding content as existence, which, as existence of the idea, is _actuality_, – _objective_ _spirit_.

::: remark
{{ 482 A }} Of no idea does one know so generally that it is indeterminate, ambiguous and capable of the greatest misunderstandings, and for that reason actually subject to them, as [of] the idea of _freedom_, and none is so current with so little consciousness. Since free spirit is _actual_ spirit, the misunderstandings about it are of the most {{{ 20.477 }}} monstrous practical consequences, as nothing else, once individuals and peoples have taken the abstract concept of freedom existing for itself into their representation, has this irresistible strength, precisely because it is the very essence of spirit, and indeed as its actuality itself. Whole parts of the world, Africa and the Orient, have never had this idea and do not yet have it; the Greeks and Romans, Plato and Aristotle, the Stoics too, did not have it; what they knew, on the contrary, was only that the human being is actually free by birth (as an Athenian, Spartan and so forth citizen) or by strength of character and culture, through philosophy (even a slave in chains is free, if a sage). It was Christianity that brought this idea into the world; by its teaching the individual _as_ _such_ has an _infinite_ worth, being the object and purpose of God's love and destined to stand in absolute relation to God as spirit and to have this spirit dwell in it, which means that the human being is _in_ _itself_ destined for the highest freedom. If in religion as such the human being knows the relation to absolute spirit to be its essence, then the divine spirit is also present to it as stepping into the sphere of _worldly_ _existence_ and becoming the substance of the state, the family and the like. These relations are formed by that spirit and constituted in conformity with it, and through this concrete existence the disposition of ethical life takes up its abode in the individual, who within this sphere of particular existence, of present sensing and willing, is then _actually_ _free_.

That human beings know their essence, purpose and object to be freedom is a speculative knowing of the idea; but this idea as such is their actuality, something they do not _have_ but _are_. Christianity has made it the actuality of its adherents not to be slaves, for instance; were they enslaved, if the decision about their property were laid in arbitrary pleasure, not in laws and courts, they would find the substance of their existence violated. This willing of freedom is no longer a drive that demands its satisfaction, but character, – the spiritual consciousness that has become a _being_ free of drive. – This freedom, however, whose content and purpose is freedom itself, is at first only concept, principle of spirit and heart, and is destined to develop itself to objecthood, to juridical, ethical and religious as well as scientific actuality.
:::
