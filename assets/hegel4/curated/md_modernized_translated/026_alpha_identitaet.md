## α) Identity

## {{ 115 }} § 115.

Essence shines _in_ _itself_, or is pure reflection; so it is solely self-relation, not immediate but reflected, – _identity_ _with_ _itself_.

::: remark
{{ 115 A }} _Formal_ or _understanding-identity_ is this identity so long as it is clung to and _abstraction_ is made from difference. Or rather, _abstraction_ is what posits this formal identity, turning something concrete in itself into this form of simplicity – whether by _leaving_ _out_ part of the manifold present in the concrete (so-called _analyzing_) and singling out only _one_ aspect of it, or by _contracting_ the manifold determinatenesses into one while omitting their diversity.

Join identity to the absolute as the subject of a proposition, – and it runs: the _absolute_ _is_ _the_ _self-identical_. – True as the proposition is, it remains ambiguous whether it is meant in its truth; its expression, at least, is therefore incomplete: it is left undecided whether what is meant is the abstract identity of the understanding, that is, identity set against the other determinations of essence, – or identity as _concrete_ in itself; in the latter case it is, as will emerge, first the _ground_ and then, in higher truth, the _concept_. – The very word _absolute_ often means no more than _abstract_; _absolute_ space and _absolute_ time are just abstract space and abstract time.

{{{ 20.147 }}} If the determinations of essence are taken as _essential_ determinations, they become predicates of a presupposed subject that, since they are essential, is _everything_. The propositions that result have been announced as _the_ _universal_ _laws_ _of_ _thought_. _The_ _proposition_ _of_ _identity_ thus reads: _everything_ _[is]_ _identical_ _with_ _itself_; A = A; and negatively: A _cannot_ _be_ A _and_ _not_ A _at_ _once_. – Far from being a true law of thought, this proposition is merely the law of the _abstract_ _understanding_. The _form_ _of_ _the_ _proposition_ already refutes it, because a proposition promises a difference between subject and predicate as well, yet this one fails to supply what its form demands. Above all, the so-called laws of thought that follow, which turn the opposite of this law into laws, sublate it. – Some claim that this proposition cannot be proved yet that _every_ consciousness acts on it and, as experience shows, agrees with it the moment it hears it; against this alleged experience of the school one must set the universal experience that no consciousness thinks, forms representations, and so forth, or speaks according to this law, and that no concrete existence of any kind exists according to it. To speak according to this would-be law of truth (a planet is – a planet, magnetism is – magnetism, spirit is – a spirit) is rightly counted as silly; that is indeed universal experience. The school, where alone such laws are valid, has long since lost its credit, together with its logic that expounds them in earnest, with healthy common sense as with reason.
:::
