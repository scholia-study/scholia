## {{{ 20.254 }}} B. Matter and Motion. Finite Mechanics

## {{ 262 }} § 262.

Matter holds itself asunder against its identity with itself, through the moment of its negativity, of its abstract _isolation_; this is the _repulsion_ of matter. Just as essential, because these diverse items are one and the same, is the negative unity of this being-for-itself that is outside-one-another; matter is thus continuous, – this is its _attraction_. Matter is inseparably both, and the negative unity of these moments, singularity, yet as still _distinguished_ from the _immediate_ being-outside-one-another of matter and therefore itself still _not_ _posited_ as _material_, is _ideal_ singularity, _center_, – _gravity_.

::: remark
{{ 262 A }} Among other merits, Kant has that of having made, by his attempt at a so-called _construction_ of matter in his _metaphysical_ _first_ _principles_ _of_ _natural_ _science_, the beginning of a _concept_ of matter, and of having reawakened with this attempt the concept of a _philosophy_ _of_ _nature_. In doing so, however, he took the determinations of reflection called _attractive_ _force_ and _repulsive_ _force_ as fixed against one another, and, again, while _matter_ was supposed to emerge from them, he presupposed it as _ready-made_, so that it is already matter that is to be attracted and repelled. I have set out the confusion that prevails in this Kantian exposition at greater length in System of Logic, vol. 1, part 1, p. 119 ff. – Moreover, only heavy matter is the totality and the real in which attraction and repulsion can take place; it has the ideal moments of the concept, of singularity or subjectivity. They are therefore not to be taken as self-subsistent {{{ 20.255 }}} or as forces for themselves; matter results from them only as moments of the concept, but is what is presupposed for their appearance.

_Gravity_ is to be essentially distinguished from mere _attraction_. The latter is only, in general, the sublating of being-outside-one-another and yields mere continuity. Gravity, on the other hand, is the reduction of the particularity, which is asunder and just as continuous, to unity as negative relation to itself, to _singularity_, to _one_ (though still wholly abstract) _subjectivity_. In the sphere of the first _immediacy_ of nature, however, the continuity that is outside itself is still posited as what _subsists_; it is first in the physical sphere, in which the material reflection-into-itself begins. _Singularity_ is therefore indeed present as a determination of the idea, but here _outside_ _the_ _material_. Matter is therefore, first, itself essentially _heavy_; this is not an external property that could also be separated from it. Gravity constitutes the substantiality of matter, which itself is the striving toward the – though, – and this is the other essential determination, – _center_ that falls _outside_ _it_. One can say that matter is _attracted_ by the center, that is, that its continuous subsistence, which is outside itself, is negated; but if the center itself is represented [as] material, the attracting is only reciprocal, at the same time a being-attracted, and the center again something different from them. The center, however, is not to be taken as material; for the material is precisely this: to posit its center _outside_ _itself_. Not the center, but this striving toward it, is immanent in matter. Gravity is, so to speak, the confession of the nullity of matter's being-outside-itself in its being-for-itself, of its non-self-subsistence, of its contradiction.

One can also say that gravity is the _being-within-itself_ of matter, in this sense, that just insofar as it is not yet center, subjectivity, in {{{ 20.256 }}} itself, it is still indeterminate, undeveloped, unopened, the form not yet being material.

Where the center lies is determined by the heavy matter whose center it is; insofar as matter is mass, it is determinate, and so is its striving, which is the positing, and thus a determinate positing, of the center.
:::
