## C. The Animal Organism

## {{ 350 }} § 350.

Organic individuality exists as _subjectivity_ insofar as the shape's own externality is _idealized_ to members, and the organism in its outward process maintains the self-like unity within itself. This is the _animal_ nature, which, in the actuality and externality of immediate singularity, is equally, as against this, a self of _singularity_ _reflected_ _into_ _itself_, a _subjective_ universality existing _in_ _itself_ (§ 163).

## {{ 351 }} § 351.

The animal has contingent _self-motion_, because its subjectivity, like light the ideality wrested from gravity, is a free time, which, taken from real externality, _determines_ _itself_ _to_ _place_ out of itself according to inner contingency. Connected with this is that the animal has a _voice_, in that {{{ 20.353 }}} its _subjectivity_ as _actual_ ideality (soul) is the dominion of the abstract ideality of time and space, and presents its self-motion as a free trembling _within_ _itself_; – it has animal _heat_, as continuing _dissolution-process_ of the cohesion and of the self-subsistent subsistence of the parts in the continuing preservation of the shape; – further, _interrupted_ _intussusception_, as an individualizing relation to an individual inorganic nature, – above all, however, _feeling_, as the individuality that in determinateness is immediately _universal_, remains and preserves itself _simply_ with itself; the _existent_ ideality of being-determined.

## {{ 352 }} § 352.

The animal organism, as living universality, is the concept that runs its course through its three determinations as syllogisms, each of which is _in_ _itself_ the same _totality_ of substantial unity and at the same time, according to the determination of form, the _passing_ _over_ into the others, so that out of this process the totality _results_ as existing; only as this self-reproducing, not as being, does the living thing exist and _maintain_ _itself_; it is only in making itself into what it is; it is a purpose that precedes, which itself is only the result. – The organism is therefore to be considered a) as the individual idea, which in its process relates only _to_ _itself_ and closes together with itself within itself – the _shape_; b) as idea that relates to its _other_, its inorganic nature, and posits it ideally within itself – _assimilation_; c) the idea as relating to an other that is itself a living individual, and thereby, in the other, to itself – the _genus-process_.
