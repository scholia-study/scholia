## β) Recognizing Self-Consciousness

## {{ 430 }} § 430.

Self-consciousness here is for a self-consciousness, at first _immediately_ as an other for an _other_. I intuit in it, as I, immediately myself, but also therein an immediately existent other object, as I absolutely self-subsistent against myself; the sublating of the _singularity_ of self-consciousness was the first sublating; it is thereby determined only as _particular_. – This contradiction gives the drive to _show_ oneself as a free self, and to be _there_ as such for the other, – the process of _recognizing_.

## {{ 431 }} § 431.

It is a _struggle_; for I cannot know myself as myself in the other, insofar as the other is for me an immediate other existence; I am therefore directed to the sublation of this its immediacy. Equally I can be recognized not as immediate but only to the extent that I sublate immediacy in myself, and so lend existence to my freedom. Yet this immediacy is also the corporeality of self-consciousness, in which, as in its sign and tool, it has its own _self-feeling_ and its being _for_ _others_, and its relation mediating it with them.

## {{{ 20.431 }}} {{ 432 }} § 432.

The struggle of recognizing therefore goes to life and death; each of the two self-consciousnesses brings the life of the other into _danger_ and puts itself into it, but only _as_ _in_ _danger_, for each is equally directed to the preservation of its life, as the existence of its freedom. The death of the one, which resolves the contradiction on one side through the abstract, therefore crude negation of immediacy, is thus, on the essential side, namely the existence of recognizing, which is thereby at the same time sublated, a new contradiction, higher than the first.

## {{ 433 }} § 433.

Since life is as essential as freedom, the struggle ends at first, as _one-sided_ negation, with the inequality that one of the combatants prefers life, maintains itself as single self-consciousness but gives up its being-recognized, while the other holds fast to its relation to itself and is recognized by the first as the one subjugated: – the _relation_ of _mastery_ and _servitude_.

::: remark
{{ 433 A }} The struggle of recognizing and the subjection under a master is the _appearance_ in which the living together of human beings has emerged as a beginning of _states_. The _violence_ that is the ground in this appearance is not on that account the ground of _right_, although it is the _necessary_ and _justified_ moment in the transition from the _condition_ of self-consciousness sunk into desire and singularity into the condition of universal self-consciousness. It is the external or _appearing_ _beginning_ of states, not their _substantial_ _principle_.
:::

## {{ 434 }} § 434.

On the one hand, since the means of mastery, the servant, must likewise be maintained in his life, this relation is a _commonality_ of {{{ 20.432 }}} need and of the care for its satisfaction. In the place of the crude destruction of the immediate object steps the acquisition, preservation and forming of it as the mediating, in which the two extremes of self-subsistence and non-self-subsistence close together; – the form of universality in the satisfaction of need is a _lasting_ means and a provision that takes the future into account and secures it.

## {{ 435 }} § 435.

Secondly, in keeping with the difference, the master intuits, in the servant and his service, the validity of his _singular_ being-for-itself; and indeed by means of the sublation of immediate being-for-itself, which however falls in another. – The latter, the servant, however, works off in the service of the master his single will and self-will, sublates the inner immediacy of desire, and in this externalization and in fear of the master makes the beginning of wisdom, – the transition to _universal_ _self-consciousness_.
