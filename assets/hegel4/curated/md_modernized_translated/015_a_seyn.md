## a. Being

## {{ 86 }} § 86.

_Pure_ _being_ makes the beginning, because it is pure thought and also the simple, indeterminate immediate, while the first beginning cannot be anything mediated or further determined.

::: remark
{{ 86 A }} Whatever doubts and objections one might raise to opening the science with abstract, empty being get cleared away once one simply grasps what a beginning by its nature carries with it. One can define being as I=I, as _absolute_ _indifference_ or _identity_, and so forth. Anyone who feels the need to start either from something that is utterly _sure_, namely the self's own certainty, or from a definition or an intuition of the _absolutely_ _true_, may take these forms, and others like them, to be what has to come first. Yet because each of these forms already has _mediation_ inside it, none is truly first; mediation means that one has moved on from a first to a second and that something has come forth out of differentiated terms. Should I=I, or intellectual {{{ 20.123 }}} intuition, genuinely be understood merely as what comes first, then in that pure immediacy it amounts to nothing but _being_; and in turn pure being, once it is no longer this abstract being but one that holds mediation in itself, is pure thinking or intuiting.

When _being_ is predicated of the absolute, the first definition of the latter results: _The_ _absolute_ _is_ _being_. In thought it is the absolutely initial definition, the most abstract and the poorest of all. The _Eleatics_ gave it, and it is at the same time the well-known claim that _God_ _is_ _the_ _totality_ _of_ _all_ _realities_. What is meant is that one should abstract from the restrictedness attaching to every reality, so that God remains solely the _real_ element in all reality, the _all-real_. As reality already includes reflection, a more immediate statement of this is found in Jacobi's remark about Spinoza's God, that this God is the principium _of_ _being_ _in_ _all_ _existence_.
:::

## {{ 87 }} § 87.

This pure being is now _pure_ _abstraction_, and hence the _absolutely-negative_, which, taken just as immediately, is the _nothing_.

::: remark
{{ 87 A }} 1) A second definition of the absolute, that it is the _nothing_, would follow from this; and it is in fact implied whenever the thing-in-itself is called the undetermined, that which is utterly without form and so without content; – likewise when God is said to _be_ _merely_ _the_ _supreme_ _essence_ and nothing besides, since that expresses the very same negativity; and the nothing that the _Buddhists_ posit as the principle of everything, and as the ultimate end and aim of all things, is this same abstraction. – 2) Once the opposition is stated in this immediacy as _being_ and _nothing_, its nullity looks too glaring for anyone to refrain from trying to pin being down and secure it against the transition. Here reflective thinking is driven to look for a fixed determination in being that would set it apart from the nothing. One grasps it, say, as what endures amid all {{{ 20.124 }}} change, as the infinitely determinable _matter_ and the like, or, with no reflection at all, as any _single_ concrete existence, whatever sensible or spiritual item lies closest. All such further, more concrete determinations, however, no longer leave being as the _pure_ _being_ that it is here, directly at the outset. It is _nothing_ only through and because of this pure indeterminateness; – an _unsayable_; that it differs from the nothing is mere _opinion_. – The whole point is simply to become conscious of these beginnings, that is, to see that they are no more than empty abstractions, each as empty as its counterpart; the _urge_ to find a firm meaning in being, or in the two, is this very _necessity_ that leads being and nothing onward and endows them with a true, that is, concrete, meaning. This advance is the logical carrying-out, and the course taken in what follows presents it. _Reflective_ _thinking_, which _finds_ deeper determinations for them, is logical thinking, and through it such determinations get produced, though in a necessary manner, not an accidental one. – Whatever meaning they subsequently acquire has therefore to be seen merely as a _more_ _precise_ _determination_ and _truer_ _definition_ of the _absolute_; such a definition is then not an empty abstraction like being and nothing, but a concrete in which being and nothing both figure as moments. – _Freedom_ would be the highest form of the nothing taken for itself, but it is negativity in so far as it sinks to the greatest intensity within itself and is itself, indeed, absolute affirmation.
:::

## {{ 88 }} § 88.

As this immediate and self-identical thing, the _nothing_ is, conversely, _the_ _same_ as what _being_ is. Both being and the nothing therefore have their truth in the _unity_ of the two; and that unity is _becoming_.

::: remark
{{ 88 A }} 1) To _representation_, or the understanding, the proposition: _Being_ _and_ _nothing_ _are_ _the_ _same_ seems so paradoxical that perhaps {{{ 20.125 }}} it is not taken to be meant in earnest. And in truth it ranks among the hardest things that thinking asks of itself; being and nothing form the opposition in all its _immediacy_, that is, with no determination yet _posited_ in either that would hold its relation to the other. As the preceding paragraph showed, however, they _do_ hold such a determination, and it is exactly the same in each. To that extent the derivation of their unity is wholly _analytic_, just as the entire course of philosophizing, so far as it proceeds methodically, that is, as _necessary_, is merely a _positing_ of what a concept already contains. – It is, however, every bit as true as their unity that _they_ _are_ _utterly_ _different_, – that the one is _not_ what the other is. But the difference has not yet taken shape here, since being and nothing are still the immediate; hence, as it exists in them, it is the _unsayable_, mere _opinion_. –

2) Ridiculing the proposition that being and nothing are identical costs no great cleverness; the same goes for producing absurdities and wrongly declaring them to be its consequences and applications; for instance, that it would follow that it makes no difference whether my house, my property, the air I breathe, this city, the sun, right, spirit or God _is_ or _is_ _not_. Examples of that sort partly introduce _particular_ _purposes_, the _usefulness_ something has for _me_, and ask whether it would be all one to _me_ if the useful thing were there or not. Indeed philosophy is precisely the teaching that frees human beings from an endless multitude of finite purposes and aims, making them indifferent to these, so that it truly is all one to them whether such things are or are not. But as soon as a _content_ is under discussion, a connection with _other_ concrete existences, purposes and so on is given along with it, and these are _presupposed_ as valid; whether the being or non-being of _a_ _determinate_ _content_ is _the_ _same_ or _not_ then depends on _such_ _presuppositions_. Thus a difference _full_ _of_ _content_ {{{ 20.126 }}} is substituted for the empty difference between being and nothing. – In part, though, the items concerned are purposes that are essential in themselves, absolute concrete existences and ideas, which are merely placed under the determination of _being_ or non-being. Concrete objects of that kind are something quite different from merely _being_ or _not-being_; wretched abstractions such as being and nothing, – and since they are only the determinations of the beginning, they are the most wretched there can be, – do not at all suit the nature of those objects; true content has long ago left these abstractions and their opposition behind. – Whenever some concrete is substituted for being and nothing, thoughtlessness behaves as it usually does, picturing and discussing something quite other than what is at issue, whereas here the issue is solely abstract being and nothing. – 3) It is easy to say that one cannot _comprehend_ the unity of being and nothing. Yet their concept has been set out in the preceding paragraphs, and it is nothing more than what was set out there; to comprehend them is just to take this in. People, however, mean something beyond the concept proper by comprehending; they ask for a richer, more varied consciousness, a representation, so that the concept is put before them as a concrete case with which thinking is better acquainted in its customary practice. If this inability to comprehend only voices a lack of habit in holding abstract thoughts fast, free of any sensuous admixture, and in grasping speculative propositions, there is nothing to add except that philosophical knowing does of course differ in kind from the knowing familiar in everyday life, and from the kind that holds sway in the other sciences. If, though, not comprehending merely means that one is unable to _represent_ the unity {{{ 20.127 }}} of being and nothing, then this is so far from being so that everyone in fact has countless representations of that unity, and to say one has no such representation can only mean that one fails to recognize the concept in question in any of those representations or to know them as an instance of it. The nearest instance is _becoming_. Everyone has a representation of becoming and will readily admit that it is _one_ representation; also that analysis finds in it the determination of _being_ and likewise of being's utter other, the _nothing_; and that both determinations are present in this _one_ representation without being divided; so that becoming is the unity of being and nothing. – Another instance close at hand is the _beginning_; in its beginning the matter _is_ _not_ _yet_, but the beginning is not simply its _nothing_, for its _being_ is already within it too. The beginning is itself becoming as well, except that it already points ahead to further progress. – To suit the more usual procedure of the sciences, one might open the logic with the representation of the beginning as purely thought, that is, of the beginning as such, and analyze that representation; perhaps one would then be readier to accept, as the outcome of the analysis, that being and nothing turn out to be undivided within one.

4) A further remark: the utterance “being and nothing is _the_ _same_”, or “_the_ _unity_ of being and nothing” – and likewise any other such _unity_, of subject and object and so on – rightly causes offense, since what is skewed and incorrect in it is that the _unity_ is singled out while the diversity, though present in it (for it is being and nothing whose unity is posited), goes unvoiced and unacknowledged, so that it seems to be improperly disregarded and not thought of. A speculative determination cannot in truth be expressed correctly in a proposition of this form; the unity is to be grasped within a diversity that is at once _present_ and {{{ 20.128 }}} _posited_. _Becoming_ is the true statement of what being and nothing result in, as their unity; it is not just the _unity_ of being and nothing but _unrest_ in itself, – a unity that is not at rest in mere self-relation but, owing to the diversity of being and nothing that it holds, stands within itself against itself. – _Existence_, in contrast, is this _unity_, or becoming in this form of unity; for that reason existence is _one-sided_ and _finite_. The opposition looks as though it had disappeared; the unity contains it only _in_ _itself_ and it is not _posited_ in it.

5) Against the proposition that being passes over into the nothing and the nothing into being, – the proposition of _becoming_, one finds set the proposition _Nothing_ _comes_ _from_ _nothing_, something comes only from something, the proposition of matter's eternity, of pantheism. The ancients saw by simple reflection that saying something comes from something, or nothing from nothing, actually does away with becoming, because what it comes from and what comes to be are one and the same; all that is left is the proposition of the understanding's abstract identity. It is astonishing, however, that in our own day the propositions, nothing comes from nothing, something only from something, are advanced with complete unconcern, with no awareness that they ground pantheism and no knowledge that the ancients exhausted what there is to say about them.
:::
