## a. Relation of Substantiality

## {{ 150 }} § 150.

The necessary is in itself _absolute_ _relation_; that is to say, it is the process that the preceding sections developed, a process in which the relation equally sublates itself into absolute identity.

Its immediate form is the relation of _substantiality_ and _accidentality_. This relation's absolute identity with itself is _substance_ as such; as necessity, however, substance is the negativity of this form {{{ 20.170 }}} of inwardness, and so posits itself as _actuality_, but it is equally the _negativity_ of this externality, in accordance with which the actual, as immediate, is merely something _accidental_ that, by virtue of its mere possibility, goes over into a different actuality; this _passing_ _over_ is the substantial identity as _form-activity_ (§§ 148, 149.).

## {{ 151 }} § 151.

Substance is accordingly the totality of the accidents, revealing itself in them as their absolute negativity, that is, as _absolute_ _power_ and at once as the _riches_ _of_ _all_ _content_. Yet this content is _nothing_ _other_ _than_ _this_ _manifestation_ _itself_: determinateness, reflected in itself into content, is only a moment of the form and passes over into the _power_ of substance. Substantiality is absolute form-activity and the power of necessity; all content is merely a moment belonging to this process alone, the absolute turning of form and content into one another.

## {{ 152 }} § 152.

Given the moment that substance, as absolute power, is the power that _refers_ _itself_ _to_ _itself_ as to a merely inner possibility and thereby determines itself into accidentality, and that the externality thus posited is something distinct from it, substance is relation proper, in the way it is substance in the first form of necessity, – the _relation_ _of_ _causality_.
