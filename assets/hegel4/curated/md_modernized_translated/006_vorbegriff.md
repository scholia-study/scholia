## Preliminary Conception

## {{ 19 }} § 19.

Logic is the science _of_ _the_ _pure_ _idea_, which is to say of the idea as it stands within the abstract element of _thinking_.

::: remark
{{ 19 A }} What holds of this determination, as of the other determinations contained in this preliminary conception, is the same as what holds of the concepts advanced beforehand about philosophy in general: that they are determinations drawn _from_ and _after_ an overview of the whole.

It may well be said that logic _is_ the science of _thinking_, with its _determinations_ and _laws_; yet thinking as such supplies merely the _universal_ _determinateness_, the _element_ within which the idea exists as logical. The idea is not thinking in a formal sense but the self-unfolding totality of thinking's own determinations and laws, which it gives itself, rather than _having_ them already and finding them in itself.

In one respect logic is the _hardest_ of the sciences, since it deals not with intuitions, nor even, as geometry does, with abstract sensuous representations, but with pure abstractions, and demands both strength and practice in withdrawing into pure thought, holding it firm, and moving about in it. On the other side it could be regarded as the _easiest_, because its content is nothing but our own thinking and its familiar determinations, and {{{ 20.62 }}} these are at the same time the _simplest_ and the _elementary_. They are also the _best known_: being, nothing, and so forth; determinateness, magnitude, and so forth; being-in-itself, being-for-itself, one, many, and so forth. This acquaintance, however, makes logical study rather harder; on the one hand it is easily thought not worth the trouble to occupy oneself still with what is so well known; on the other hand the point is to become acquainted with it in a quite different, indeed even opposite way from the one in which one already is.

The _usefulness_ of logic concerns the relation to the subject, insofar as the subject gives itself a certain culture for other purposes. The subject's culture through logic consists in its being practiced in thinking, since this science is thinking of thinking, and in its getting the thoughts, and also as thoughts, into its head. – But insofar as the logical is the absolute form of truth and, more than this, even pure truth itself, it is something quite other than merely something _useful_. But as the most excellent, the freest and the most self-subsistent is also the most useful, so the logical too can be grasped in this way. Its usefulness is then to be reckoned otherwise than as merely the formal practice of thinking.
:::

## {{ 20 }} § 20.

If we take up thinking in its most immediate representation, it appears α) first in its ordinary subjective meaning, as one of the spiritual activities or faculties _alongside_ others, sensibility, intuition, fantasy and so forth, desiring, willing and so forth. What it _produces_, the determinateness or form of thought, is the _universal_, the abstract as such. _Thinking_ as _activity_ is accordingly the _active_ universal, and in fact the universal that sets _itself_ in action, since the deed, the thing brought forth, is nothing but the universal. Thinking represented as _subject_ is a _thinker_, and the simple expression of the concretely existing subject as thinking is _I_.

::: remark
{{ 20 A }} The determinations stated here and in the next few §§ are not to be taken as assertions or as my own _opinions_ concerning thinking; but since in this preliminary fashion neither derivation nor {{{ 20.63 }}} proof can occur, they may pass as _facta_, in the sense that anyone, on having thoughts and examining them, will find empirically in their own consciousness that the character of universality, and likewise the determinations that follow, are present in them. A culture of attention and of abstraction that is already present is certainly required for observing facts of one's consciousness and of one's representations.

Already in this preliminary exposition the difference between the sensuous, representation and thoughts comes up for discussion; it is decisive for grasping the nature and the kinds of cognition, and it will therefore serve as clarification to make this difference noticeable here already. – For the _sensuous_, its external origin, the senses or sense-organs, is taken first as the explanation. But naming the organ gives no determination of what is grasped with it. The difference of the _sensuous_ from thought is to be put in this: that the determination of the former is _singularity_, and since the singular (quite abstractly, the atom) also stands in connection, the sensuous is an _asunderness_, whose closer abstract forms are _side-by-side_ and _one-after-another_. – _Representing_ has such sensuous material as content, but posited in the determination of the _mine_, that such content is in _me_, and of _universality_, of relation-to-itself, of _simplicity_. – Besides the sensuous, however, representation also has as content material that has sprung from self-conscious thinking, such as the representations of what is right, ethical, religious, also of thinking itself, and it is not so easy to see where the difference of such _representations_ from the _thoughts_ of such content is to be put. Here both the content is thought and the form of universality is present, which already belongs to a content's being in _me_, to its being representation at all. But the peculiarity of representation is, in general and in this respect too, to be put in this: that in it such content likewise stands isolated. Right, determinations of right and the like do not, to be sure, stand in the sensuous asunderness {{{ 20.64 }}} of _space_. In time they perhaps appear one after another, but their content itself is not represented as afflicted by time, as passing and changeable in it. Yet such determinations, spiritual in themselves, likewise stand _isolated_ on the broad ground of the inner abstract universality of representing in general. Isolated in this way, they are _simple_: right, duty, God. Representation now either stops at this, that right is right, God is God, – or, more cultivated, it states determinations, for instance that God is creator of the world, all-wise, almighty and so forth; here likewise several isolated simple determinations are strung together, which, in spite of the connection assigned to them in their subject, remain outside one another. Representation here coincides with the _understanding_, which is distinguished from it only in that it posits relations of the universal and the particular, or of cause and effect and so forth, and thereby relations of necessity among the isolated determinations of representation, whereas representation leaves them in its indeterminate space, connected by the mere _also_, _side_ _by_ _side_. – The difference between representation and thoughts has the closer importance because it can in general be said that philosophy does nothing other than transform representations into thoughts, – but, of course, further the mere thought into the concept.

Moreover, if the determinations of _singularity_ _and_ _asunderness_ have been given for the sensuous, it can still be added that these too are in turn thoughts and universals; in logic it will be shown that the thought and the universal is precisely this, that it is itself and its other, reaches over this, and that nothing escapes it. Since _language_ is the work {{{ 20.65 }}} of thought, nothing can be said in it either that is not universal. What I merely _suppose_ is _mine_, pertains to me as this particular individual; but if language can voice only the universal, I cannot say what I merely _suppose_. And the _unsayable_, feeling, sensation, is not the most excellent, the truest, but the most insignificant, the most untrue. If I say: the _singular_, _this_ singular, here, now, these are all universalities; _everything_ and _each_ is a singular, a this, even if it is sensuous, a here, a now. Likewise when I say: _I_; I suppose _myself_ as _this_ one excluding all others, but what I say, I, is precisely everyone; an I that excludes all others from itself. – _Kant_ made use of the awkward expression that I _accompanies_ all my representations, also sensations, desires, actions and so forth. I is the in and for itself universal, and being in common is also a form of universality, but an external one. All other human beings have it in common with me to be I, as it is common to all _my_ sensations, representations and so forth to be _mine_. But _I_, abstractly as such, is the pure relation to itself, in which there is abstraction from representing, from sensing, from every state, as from every particularity of nature, of talent, of experience and so forth. I is to this extent the concrete existence of the wholly _abstract_ universality, the abstractly _free_. For this reason the I is _thinking_ as _subject_, and because I am at one and the same time in all my sensations, representations, states and so forth, thought is present everywhere and runs through all these determinations as category.
:::

## {{ 21 }} § 21.

β) Where thinking is understood as active with respect to objects – as _reflective_ _thinking_ on something – the universal, as the product {{{ 20.66 }}} of this activity, carries the weight of the _matter_, of the _essential_, the _inner_, the _true_.

::: remark
{{ 21 A }} In § 5 the old belief was cited, that what is truly the case in objects, constitutions and events, the inner, the essential, the matter that counts, is not found _immediately_ in consciousness, is not already what first appearance and first notion offer, but that one must first engage in _reflective_ _thinking_ on it to reach the object's genuine constitution, and that such reflective thinking secures this.
:::

## {{ 22 }} § 22.

γ) Through reflective thinking, something is _altered_ in the way the content is at first in sensation, intuition, representation; it is thus only _by means_ _of_ an alteration that _the_ _true_ nature of the _object_ comes to consciousness.

## {{ 23 }} § 23.

δ) Since in reflective thinking the true nature comes to light just as much as this thinking is _my_ activity, the former is just as much the _product_ of _my_ spirit, namely as thinking subject, of me according to my simple universality, as the I that absolutely _is_ _with_ _itself_, – or of my _freedom_.

::: remark
{{ 23 A }} One often hears the expression _thinking_ _for_ _oneself_, as if something significant were said with it. In fact no one can think for another, any more than eat and drink for another; that expression is therefore a pleonasm. – Freedom lies immediately in thinking, because it is the activity of the universal, hence an abstract relating-to-itself, a being-with-itself that is undetermined as to subjectivity, and that as to _content_ is at the same time only in the _matter_ and its determinations. If {{{ 20.67 }}} there is talk, therefore, of humility or modesty, and of arrogance, in relation to philosophizing, and humility or modesty consists in ascribing to one's subjectivity nothing _particular_ in trait and doing, philosophizing is at least to be pronounced free of arrogance, since thinking as to content is true only insofar as it is immersed in the _matter_, and as to form is not a _particular_ being or doing of the subject, but is precisely this: that consciousness, as abstract I, comports itself as _liberated_ from _all_ _particularity_ of other traits, states and so forth, and does only the universal, in which it is identical with all individuals. – If Aristotle calls on us to hold ourselves _worthy_ of such a comportment, the worthiness that consciousness gives itself consists precisely in letting go of _particular_ supposing and deeming and in letting the _matter_ prevail within itself.
:::

## {{ 24 }} § 24.

By these determinations the thoughts can be called _objective_ thoughts, among which are also to be counted the forms that are first considered in ordinary logic and are usually taken only as forms _of_ _conscious_ _thinking_. _Logic_ therefore coincides with _metaphysics_, the science of _things_ grasped _in_ _thoughts_, which were held to express the _essentialities_ of _things_.

::: remark
{{ 24 A }} The relation of such forms as concept, judgment and syllogism to others, such as causality and so forth, can emerge only within logic itself. But this much can be seen in advance as well: when thought tries to arrive at a _concept_ of things, that concept (and with it its most immediate forms, judgment and syllogism) cannot be made up of determinations and relations foreign and external to the things. Reflective thinking, it was said above, leads to the _universal_ of _things_; but this is itself one of the moments of the concept. That understanding, reason, is in the world says the same as what the expression objective thought contains. But this expression is inconvenient precisely because {{{ 20.68 }}} _thought_ is usually used only of what belongs to spirit, to consciousness, and likewise the objective is at first used only of the non-spiritual.
:::

## {{ 25 }} § 25.

The expression _objective_ _thoughts_ designates the _truth_, which is supposed to be the absolute _object_ of philosophy, not merely its _goal_. But it points at once to an opposition in general, and indeed to the one on whose determination and validity the interest of the philosophical standpoint of the present time, and the question of _truth_ and of cognition of it, turn. If the determinations of thought are burdened with a fixed opposition, that is, if they are merely of _finite_ nature, then they do not suit the truth that exists absolutely in and for itself, and truth cannot enter into thinking. Thinking that produces only _finite_ determinations and moves within them is called _understanding_ (in the stricter sense of the word). More precisely, the _finitude_ of the determinations of thought is to be grasped in a twofold way: the one, that they are _only_ _subjective_ and have the permanent opposition in the objective; the other, that as being of _limited_ _content_ in general they persist in opposition both toward one another and still more toward the absolute. The _positions_ _given_ _to_ _thinking_ _with_ _respect_ _to_ _objectivity_ are now to be considered as a closer introduction, in order to clarify and bring about the significance and the standpoint that is given to logic here.

::: remark
{{ 25 A }} In my _Phenomenology_ _of_ _Spirit_, which for that reason was designated at its publication as the first part of the system of science, the course taken is to begin from the first, simplest appearance of spirit, _immediate_ _consciousness_, and to develop its dialectic as far as the standpoint of philosophical science, whose necessity this very progression demonstrates. For this, however, it was not possible to stop at the formal aspect of mere consciousness; for the standpoint of philosophical {{{ 20.69 }}} knowledge is at the same time in itself the richest in import and the most concrete, and so, emerging as a result, it presupposed also the concrete shapes of consciousness, such as, for instance, morality, ethical life, art, religion. The development of the _import_, of the objects of the proper parts of philosophical science, therefore falls at the same time within that development of consciousness, which seems at first limited only to the formal; behind the back of consciousness that development must, so to speak, proceed, insofar as the content relates to consciousness as the _in-itself_. The presentation is thereby made more involved, and what belongs to the concrete parts already partly falls into that introduction. – The consideration to be undertaken here has the further inconvenience of being able to proceed only historically and by ratiocination; but it is meant above all to contribute to the insight that the questions one has before one in representation about the nature of _cognition_, about _faith_ and so forth, and holds to be entirely _concrete_, are in fact reducible to _simple_ determinations of thought, which, however, receive their true settlement only in logic.
:::
