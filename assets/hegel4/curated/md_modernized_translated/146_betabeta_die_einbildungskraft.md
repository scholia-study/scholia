## ββ) Imagination

## {{ 455 }} § 455.

1) Intelligence, active in this possession, is _reproductive_ _imagination_, the _emergence_ of the images from the I's own inwardness, which is now their power. The nearest _relation_ of the images is that of their externally immediate space and time, preserved along with them. – But the image has, in the subject in which it is preserved, only the individuality in which the determinations of its content are knotted together; its immediate concretion, that is, at first only spatial and temporal, which [it] has as a one in intuiting, is by contrast dissolved. The reproduced content, belonging as such to the self-identical unity of intelligence and brought forth from its universal shaft, possesses a _universal_ representation that serves as the _associating_ _relation_ among the images, the representations, more abstract or more concrete as other circumstances may determine.

::: remark
{{ 455 A }} Great interest has been taken in the so-called _laws_ _of_ _the_ _association_ _of_ _ideas_, above all in the heyday of empirical psychology, which went together with philosophy's decline. In the first place, it is not _ideas_ that are associated. In the second place, these ways of relating are no _laws_, precisely because _so_ _many_ laws concern one and the same matter, whereby arbitrariness and contingency, the opposite of a law, rather take place; it is contingent whether what connects is something pictorial or a category of the understanding, likeness and unlikeness, ground and consequence {{{ 20.449 }}} and so forth. The progression along images and representations according to associating imagination is on the whole the play of a thoughtless representing, in which the determination of intelligence is still formal universality generally, while the content is the one given in the images. – Image and representation, if one abstracts from the more exact determination of form indicated, differ in content in that the former is the more sensuously concrete representation; representation, whether its content be something pictorial or concept and idea, has generally the character of being, though belonging to intelligence, yet given and immediate as to its content. _Being_, the _finding-itself-determined_ of intelligence, still clings to representation, and the universality that that material receives through representing is still the abstract one. In the syllogism of intelligence's raising, representation is the middle term, the linking of the _two_ _meanings_ _of_ _relation-to-self_, _being_ and _universality_, which in consciousness are determined as object and subject. To what is _found_ intelligence adds the meaning of universality, and to what is its own and inner it adds that of a being, one that nevertheless is posited by it. – On the difference between representations and thoughts see the Introduction, § 20, Remark.

The abstraction that takes place in the activity of representing, whereby _universal_ _representations_ are produced, and the representations as such already have the form of universality on them, is often expressed as a _falling_ _upon_ _one_ _another_ of many _similar_ images, and is supposed to become comprehensible in this way. For this _falling_ _upon_ _one_ _another_ not to be wholly _chance_, the conceptless, a _force_ _of_ _attraction_ of the similar images or the like would have to be assumed, which would at the same time be the negative power of rubbing off against one another what is still dissimilar in them. In fact this force is intelligence itself, the I identical with itself; through its recollection the I gives the images universality at once, and _subsumes_ the singular intuition beneath the image that has already been made inward. (§ 453.)
:::

## {{{ 20.450 }}} {{ 456 }} § 456.

Hence the association of representations too is to be grasped as the _subsumption_ of the singular ones under a _universal_ one that constitutes their connection. But intelligence in it is not only universal form; its inwardness is _concrete_ subjectivity _determined_ _in_ _itself_, with an import of its own, which stems from some interest, from a concept or idea that is in itself, insofar as one can speak of such a content anticipatorily. Intelligence is the power over the store of images and representations belonging to it, and so 2) the free connecting and subsuming of this store under its own peculiar content. Thus it is, in the former, recollected as _determined_ in itself, and forming the former into this its content, – _fantasy_, _symbolizing_, _allegorizing_ or _poetizing_ imagination. These more or less concrete, individualized formations are still syntheses, insofar as the material in which the subjective import gives [itself] an existence of representation comes from what is found in intuition.

## {{ 457 }} § 457.

In fantasy, intelligence is completed to self-intuition within it insofar as its import, taken from itself, has pictorial existence. This formation of its self-intuiting is something subjective, and the moment of the _existent_ is still missing. But in the unity of the inner import and the material, intelligence has just as much returned to identical relation to itself as immediacy _in_ _itself_. As it starts, as reason, from appropriating to itself the immediate found within it (§ 445; compare § 455, Remark), that is, from determining it as a _universal_, so its doing as reason (§ 438) from the present {{{ 20.451 }}} point is to determine what in it has been completed to concrete self-intuition as _existent_, that is, to make itself into _being_, into the _matter_. Active in this determination, it is _externalizing_ itself, producing _intuition_, – 3) _sign-making_ _fantasy_.

::: remark
{{ 457 A }} Fantasy is the central point in which the universal and being, what is one's own and what is found, the inner and the outer, are created perfectly into one. The syntheses that precede, of intuition, recollection and so forth, unite the same moments, yet they remain syntheses; only in fantasy is intelligence not the indeterminate shaft and the universal, but singularity, that is, concrete subjectivity, in which relation to self is determined just as much to being as to universality. For such unions of what is own and inner to spirit with the _intuitable_, the formations of fantasy are everywhere acknowledged; their further determinate content belongs to other domains. Here this inner workshop is to be grasped only according to those abstract moments. – As the activity of this uniting, fantasy is reason, but only _formal_ reason, insofar as the _import_ of fantasy as such is indifferent, while reason as such determines the _content_ as well, to _truth_.

It is still to be emphasized in particular that, when fantasy brings the inner import to image and intuition, and one expresses this by saying that it determines it as _existent_, the expression, that intelligence makes itself _existent_, makes itself into the _matter_, need not seem striking either; for its import is itself, and so too is the determination given to it by it. The image produced by fantasy is intuitable only subjectively; in the _sign_ it adds actual intuitability; in _mechanical_ memory it completes this form of _being_ on it.
:::

## {{ 458 }} § 458.

In this unity, issuing from intelligence, of _self-subsistent_ _representation_ and an _intuition_, the matter of the latter is at first indeed {{{ 20.452 }}} something taken up, something immediate or given (for instance, the color of the cockade and the like). But in this identity the _intuition_ counts not as positive and itself, but as representing _something_ _else_. What we have here is an image into which a _self-subsistent_ representation of intelligence, the image's _meaning_, has been received as its soul. This intuition is the _sign_.

::: remark
{{ 458 A }} The _sign_ is any immediate intuition that represents a wholly different content from the one it has for itself; – the _pyramid_, in which a foreign soul is placed and preserved. The _sign_ differs from the _symbol_, an intuition whose _own_ determinateness, in its essence and concept, is more or less the content that it expresses as symbol; with the sign as such, on the other hand, the content belonging to the intuition itself and that of which it is the sign have nothing to do with one another. As _designating_, therefore, intelligence proves a freer arbitrary will and mastery in the use of intuition than as symbolizing.

Usually the _sign_ and _language_ are inserted somewhere in psychology, or also in logic, as an _appendix_, with no thought given to their necessity and their connection within the system of intelligence's activity. The true place of the sign is the one indicated: intelligence, which as intuiting generates the form of time and space, but appears as taking up the sensuous content and forming representations out of this material, now gives its self-subsistent representations a determinate existence out of itself, _makes_ _use_ of the filled space and time, the intuition, _as_ _its_ _own_, wipes out the intuition's immediate and peculiar content, and gives it another content to serve as its meaning and soul. – This sign-creating activity can be called, pre-eminently, _productive_ memory (the at first abstract Mnemosyne), since memory, which in common life is often confused with recollection, also with representation and imagination {{{ 20.453 }}} and used synonymously with them, has to do generally only with signs.
:::

## {{ 459 }} § 459.

Intuition as immediate, at first something given and spatial, receives, insofar as it is used for a sign, the essential determination of being only as sublated. Intelligence is this its negativity; thus the truer shape of intuition that is a sign is an existence in _time_, – a vanishing of existence in that it is, and, according to its further external, psychical determinateness, a _positedness_ proceeding from intelligence out of its own (anthropological) naturalness, – the _tone_, the filled expression of inwardness making itself known. The tone, articulating itself further for the determinate representations, _speech_ and its system, _language_, gives to sensations, intuitions and representations a second existence, higher than their immediate one, generally an existence that holds in the _realm_ _of_ _representing_.

::: remark
{{ 459 A }} Language comes into consideration here only in its peculiar determinateness as the product of intelligence, to manifest its representations in an external element. If language were to be treated in a concrete way, the anthropological, more closely the psychical-physiological, standpoint (§ 401) would have to be recalled for its _material_ (the lexical), and that of the understanding anticipated for its _form_ (grammar). As regards the _elemental_ _material_ of language, on the one hand the notion of mere contingency has been lost, on the other the principle of imitation has been limited to its small scope, sounding objects. Yet people can be heard praising the German language as rich because of the many particular expressions it has for particular sounds (rustling, whistling, creaking and so forth, one {{{ 20.454 }}} has perhaps collected more than a hundred of them; the momentary whim creates new ones when it pleases); a profusion of this sort in the sensuous and insignificant cannot count toward what should make up the wealth of a cultivated language. What is peculiarly elemental rests not so much on a symbolism related to external objects as on an inner one, namely on anthropological articulation, as it were as a _gesture_ of the bodily speech-expression. One has accordingly sought for each vowel and consonant, as for their more abstract elements (gesture of the lips, of the palate, of the tongue), and then for their compositions, the peculiar meaning. But these unconscious, dull beginnings are modified by further externalities, as well as needs of culture, into inconspicuousness and insignificance, essentially in that, as sensuous intuitions, they are themselves reduced to signs, and their own original meaning is thereby stunted and effaced. The _formal_ side of language, however, is the work of the understanding, which forms its categories into it; this logical instinct produces its grammatical aspect. The study of languages that have remained original, which only in recent times has begun to be known thoroughly, has shown in this regard that they contain a grammar developed very much in detail and express differences that are lacking in the languages of more cultivated peoples or have been blurred; it seems that the language of the most cultivated peoples has the more imperfect grammar, and the same language, in a less cultivated condition of its people, a more perfect one than in the more highly cultivated. Compare Mr. W. von _Humboldt_, On the Dual, I. 10. 11.

In the case of spoken language, as the original, written language too may be mentioned here only in passing; it is only a further development in the _particular_ domain of language, which takes an {{{ 20.455 }}} externally practical activity to its aid. Written language proceeds to the field of immediate spatial intuiting, in which it takes and produces the signs (§ 454). More closely, _hieroglyphic_ _writing_ designates _representations_ by spatial figures, while _alphabetic_ _writing_ designates _tones_, which are themselves already signs. The latter is thus made up of signs of signs, in that it breaks the concrete signs of spoken language, the words, down into their simple elements and gives these elements their designation. – _Leibniz_ let himself be led astray by his understanding into holding it very desirable that a complete written language be formed in hieroglyphic fashion (which also takes place partially even with alphabetic writing, as in our signs for numbers, planets, chemical substances and the like), as a general written language for the intercourse of peoples and in particular of scholars. But one may hold that it was rather the intercourse of peoples (as was perhaps the case in Phoenicia, and happens at present in Canton – see Staunton's account of _Macartney’s_ journey) that brought about the need for alphabetic writing and its origin. Besides, one cannot think of a comprehensive, _ready-made_ hieroglyphic language; sensuous objects are indeed capable of fixed signs, but for signs of what is spiritual, the progress of the formation of thought, the advancing logical development, brings with it altered views of their inner relations and thereby of their nature, so that another hieroglyphic determination too would then enter. This already happens, after all, with sensuous objects, in that their signs in spoken language, their names, are often changed, as for instance with the chemical and also mineralogical ones. Since people have forgotten what names as such are, namely, _senseless_ _externalities_ in themselves, which have a meaning only as _signs_, since, instead of proper names, one demands the expression of a kind of definition and even forms it, often, again arbitrarily and by chance, the naming changes, that is, only the composition out of signs of its {{{ 20.456 }}} generic determination or of other properties supposed to be characteristic, according to the difference in the view one takes of the genus or of some other property supposed to be specific. – Only to the stationary character of Chinese intellectual culture is the hieroglyphic written language of this people suited; this kind of written language can in any case be the lot only of the lesser part of a people, which holds itself in exclusive possession of spiritual culture. – The development of spoken language is connected at the same time most closely with the habit of alphabetic writing, and only through it does spoken language attain determinateness and purity in its articulation. How imperfect the Chinese spoken language is, is well known; a multitude of its words have several wholly different meanings, even up to ten, indeed twenty, so that in speaking the difference is made noticeable merely by the intonation, intensity, softer speaking or shouting. Europeans who begin to speak Chinese before they have made these absurd subtleties of accentuation their own fall into the most ridiculous misunderstandings. Perfection here consists in the opposite of the parler sans accent, which is rightly demanded in Europe for cultivated speech. On account of the hieroglyphic written language, the Chinese spoken language lacks the objective determinateness that is gained in articulation through alphabetic writing.

Alphabetic writing is in and for itself the more intelligent; in it the _word_, the worthiest manner, peculiar to intelligence, of expressing its representations, is brought to consciousness, made an object of reflection. In this occupation of intelligence with it, it is analyzed, that is, this sign-making is reduced to its simple, few elements (the primal gestures of articulating); they are the sensuous side of speech brought to the form of universality, which in this elemental way at the same time attains complete determinateness and purity. Alphabetic writing thereby also retains the advantage of spoken language, that in it, as in the latter, representations have proper names; the name is the simple sign for the proper, that is, _simple_, representation, not dissolved into its determinations and composed from them. Hieroglyphic language arises not from the immediate {{{ 20.457 }}} analysis of sensuous signs, as alphabetic writing does, but from the antecedent analysis of the representations, whence the thought is then easily conceived that all representations could be reduced to their elements, to the simple logical determinations, so that from the elementary signs chosen for this (such as the Chinese Kua, the simple straight stroke and the stroke broken in two) a hieroglyphic language could be produced through combining them. This circumstance of the analytic designation of representations in hieroglyphic writing, which led _Leibniz_ astray into holding it superior to alphabetic writing, is rather what contradicts the basic need of language generally, the name: to have, for the immediate representation, which, however richly its content may be comprehended within it, is simple for spirit in the name, also a simple immediate sign, which as a being for itself gives nothing to think, and has only the determination of signifying and sensuously representing the simple representation as such. Representing intelligence does not only do this, namely both to dwell on the simplicity of representations and to gather them together again out of the more abstract moments into which they have been analyzed, but thinking also resumes the concrete content from the analysis, where it has turned into a combination of many determinations, back into the form of a simple thought. For both it is a need to have also such signs, simple with regard to meaning, which consist of several letters or syllables and, even when divided into these, still do not present a combination of several representations. – What has been adduced constitutes the basic determination for deciding the value of these written languages. It then also follows that, with hieroglyphic writing, the relations of concrete spiritual representations must necessarily become entangled and confused, and the analysis of them, whose nearest products are in turn to be analyzed, appears in any case possible in the most manifold and divergent ways. Every divergence in the analysis would bring forth another formation of the written name, as in recent times, according to the remark made just before, hydrochloric acid has changed its name in several ways even in the sensuous domain. For a hieroglyphic written language, a philosophy would be needed that stood as still as Chinese culture does as a whole.

{{{ 20.458 }}} What has been said further implies that learning to read and write in an alphabetic script deserves esteem as an infinite means of culture, valued far too little, in that it leads spirit from the sensuously concrete to attention to the more formal, the sounding word and its abstract elements, and does something essential toward establishing and purifying the ground of inwardness in the subject. – Acquired habit later effaces even the peculiarity of alphabetic writing, of appearing, in the interest of seeing, as a detour through audibility to the representations, and makes it for us into hieroglyphic writing, so that when we use it the mediation of the tones need not be present to our consciousness; people, by contrast, who have little habit of reading, recite [to themselves] aloud what they read, in order to understand it in its sounds. Besides the fact that, in that skill that turns alphabetic writing into hieroglyphs, the capacity for abstraction gained through that first training remains, reading in hieroglyphs is of itself a reading that is deaf, and a writing that is mute; the audible or temporal and the visible or spatial each have indeed their own basis, at first of equal validity with the other; with alphabetic writing, however, there is only one basis, and indeed in the right relation, that visible language relates to sounding language only as sign; intelligence expresses itself immediately and unconditionally by speaking. – The mediation of representations through the less sensuous element of tones shows itself further, for the following transition from representing to thinking, – memory, – in its peculiar essentiality.
:::

## {{{ 20.459 }}} {{ 460 }} § 460.

The name, as the connection of the intuition produced by intelligence and its meaning, is at first a _single_ transitory production, and the connection of the representation as an inner with the intuition as an external one is itself _external_. The recollection of this externality is _memory_.
