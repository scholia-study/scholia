## {{{ 20.466 }}} b. Practical Spirit

## {{ 469 }} § 469.

Spirit as will knows itself as resolving within itself, and as fulfilling itself out of itself. This fulfilled _being-for-itself_ or _singularity_ constitutes the side of concrete existence or _reality_ of the _idea_ of spirit; as will, spirit enters into actuality, as knowing it is in the ground of the universality of the concept. – As giving itself the content, the will is _with_ _itself_, _free_ generally; this is its determinate concept. – Its finitude consists in the _formalism_ of this, that its being fulfilled through itself, the _abstract_ determinateness, the _its_ _own_ generally, is not identified with developed _reason_. The determination of the will that _is_ _in_ _itself_ is to bring freedom in the formal will to concrete existence, and thereby the purpose of the latter, to fulfil itself with its concept, that is, to make freedom its determinateness, its content and purpose, as well as its existence. This concept, freedom, is essentially only as thinking; the path of the will to make itself into _objective_ spirit is to raise itself to the thinking will, – to give itself the content that it can have only as thinking itself.

::: remark
{{ 469 A }} _True_ freedom, as ethical life, is this: that the will has as its purposes not subjective, that is, self-seeking, but universal content; yet such content is present only in thinking and by means of it; to wish to shut thinking out of ethical life, religiosity, uprightness and the like is thoroughly absurd.
:::

## {{{ 20.467 }}} {{ 470 }} § 470.

At first, as formal or immediate will, practical spirit contains a twofold _ought_, 1) in the opposition of the determinateness posited out of it to the _immediate_ being-determined that re-enters with it, to its _existence_ and _condition_, which in consciousness develops at the same time in relation to external objects. 2) That first self-determination, being itself immediate, is at first not raised to the universality of thinking, which therefore constitutes _in_ _itself_ the _ought_ against it, as to form, and can constitute it as to content; – an opposition that at first is only for us.
