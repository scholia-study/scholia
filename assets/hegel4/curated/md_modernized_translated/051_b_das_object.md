## B. The Object

## {{ 194 }} § 194.

Through its indifference toward the difference, which has been sublated within it, the object is immediate being and a totality in itself; yet because this identity belongs to the moments only _in_ _itself_, the object is just as indifferent toward its own immediate unity, and is a disintegration into distinct items, every one of which is itself the totality. What the object is, therefore, is the absolute _contradiction_ of the manifold's perfect self-subsistence with its equally perfect lack of self-subsistence.

::: remark
{{ 194 A }} The _Leibnizian_ _monad_ contains the definition "_the_ _absolute_ _is_ _the_ _object_" in its most determinate form: it is meant to be an object, yet one that is _in_ _itself_ representing, and moreover to be the totality of the world's representation, within whose simple unity every difference is merely something ideal and without self-subsistence. From outside nothing comes into the monad; the whole concept is in it, and monads differ solely by how far that concept has been developed in each. Equally, this simple totality breaks up into an absolute plurality of differences, and these are self-subsistent monads. Yet with the monad of monads, and with the pre-established harmony among their inner developments, these substances are once again brought down to non-self-subsistence and ideality. Thus the Leibnizian philosophy stands as the _contradiction_ in its full development.
:::
