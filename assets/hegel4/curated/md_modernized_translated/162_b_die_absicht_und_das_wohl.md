## b. Intention and Welfare

## {{ 505 }} § 505.

1) The action, in its empirically concrete _content_, possesses a manifold of _particular_ sides and connections; as to form, the subject must have known and willed it in its _essential_ determination, which embraces these singularities; – the _right_ of _intention_. – Resolve bears only on immediate existence, whereas intention bears on the substantial element and its purpose. 2) The subject has the right, too, that the action's _particularity_ of content be not alien to it so far as matter goes, but include its own particularity, its needs, {{{ 20.491 }}} interests and purposes, which, gathered together as well into a single purpose, as in happiness (§ 479), constitute its _welfare_; – the _right_ _of_ _welfare_. Happiness is distinguished from welfare only by this: the former is represented as immediate existence in general, the latter as entitled in relation to morality.

## {{ 506 }} § 506.

The essentiality of intention is, however, at first the abstract form of universality; in the empirically concrete action, reflection is able to place this or that particular side in that form and so make it essential to the intention, or confine the intention to it, as a result of which the essentiality that is merely supposed for the intention and the true essentiality of the action may be set in the sharpest contradiction (as when a good intention accompanies a crime). – Welfare too is abstract, and may be located in this or that; as belonging to _this_ subject it is something particular in general.
