## δ) The Process in Its Totality

## {{ 333 }} § 333.

These neutral bodies, entering again into relation to one another, form the _completely_ _real_ chemical process, since it has such real bodies for its sides. For their mediation they require water, as the abstract medium of neutrality. But both, as neutral for themselves, are in no difference toward one another. Here there sets in the _particularization_ of the universal neutrality, and with it just as much the particularization of the differences of the chemically inspirited bodies against one another – the so-called _elective_ _affinity_ – formation of other particular neutralities through the separation of those present.

::: remark
{{ 333 A }} The most important step toward the simplification of the particularities in the elective affinities was made by the law found by _Richter_ and _Guiton_ _Morveau_, that neutral combinations suffer _no_ _alteration_ with regard to the _state_ _of_ _saturation_ when they are mixed by dissolution and the acids exchange their bases {{{ 20.338 }}} with one another. Connected with this is the scale of quantities of acids and alkalis, according to which each single acid has, for its saturation, a particular ratio to every alkaline, and if now for one acid in a definite quantum the series of the alkalis is set out according to the quantities in which they saturate the same quantum of that acid, then _for_ _every_ _other_ _acid_ the _alkalis_ retain among themselves _the_ _same_ _ratio_ to its saturation as to that of the first, and only the quantitative unit of the acids, with which they combine with that constant series, is different. In the same way the acids have a constant ratio among themselves toward each different alkaline.

Moreover, elective affinity itself is only the _abstract_ relation of the acid to the base. The chemical body in general, and in particular the neutral one, is at the same time a concrete physical body of determinate specific gravity, cohesion, temperature and so forth. These properly physical properties and their alterations in the process (§ 328) enter into relation to the chemical moments of the process, and make its efficacy harder, hinder or facilitate it, modify it. _Berthollet_, in his famous work Statique chimique, while fully recognizing the series of affinity, has compiled and investigated the circumstances that bring an alteration into the results of the chemical action, results that are frequently determined only according to the one-sided condition of elective affinity. He says, “the superficiality that science obtains through these explanations is regarded chiefly as progress.”
:::
